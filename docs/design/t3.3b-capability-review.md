# T3.3b — the narrative review the capability move required

**Date:** 2026-09-02 · **Task:** T3.3b (with Q18) · **Reviewer:** Chandana Sorakundla

`CAPABILITY_VERSION` moved from `cap:9c416e0a` to `cap:c4d52d00`, which failed
`test_every_narrative_was_reviewed_against_the_current_capability_set` on all fifteen bundles.
That guard exists because **a narrative is only as current as the last capability change**, and
its own docstring records the two times this discipline failed: T2.6 built a change log and four
narratives went on asserting *"what changed: nothing"* for weeks; T7.1 re-recorded every bundle,
forced a rewrite, and the rewrite still missed sixteen restart lines in `ad-memory-squeeze`
because *"the review compared front matter against the manifest and never opened `logs/`"*.

**A stamp bumped without a review would be that failure a third time.** This document is the
review, so that the bump has something behind it.

## What moved, and what each change could falsify

Two capability changes landed together.

**1. `metric_baseline`, a fifth tool.** The metrics specialist previously received a bare
error-ratio range query. It now receives that series *against the preceding window of equal
length*, with the timestamps where the series left its baseline.

*The claim shape this could falsify:* a narrative asserting that a reading could not be judged
unusual, or that no baseline was available to compare against.

**2. `neutralise` strips whole ANSI sequences (Q18).** It previously removed the ESC byte and
left `[31m` in the text.

*The claim shape this could falsify:* a narrative asserting that log output was unreadable,
garbled, or obscured by escape residue.

## What the review looked for, and what it found

Every `incident.md` in `evals/scenarios/artifacts/dev/` (12) and `holdout/` (3) was searched for
both claim shapes, and every hit was read in context rather than counted.

| Claim shape | Hits | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| No baseline / could not compare / no way to tell | **1** | `cart-bad-image-tag`: *"Whether a container was created and died instantly or never created at all is not visible from here."* **Unaffected.** That is the container-exit-reason gap (Q1), which neither change touches. |
| Unreadable / garbled / escape / colour codes | **0** | No narrative describes log output as obscured. The narratives were written by a person reading the captures directly, where the residue was cosmetic. |

**Baselines appear in the narratives, and appear as the author's own work.** Several reason
explicitly from one — `redis-cart-dependency-latency` cites *"~655ms against a baseline of
1.9ms"*, `cart-dependency-latency` warns that *"a latency reading taken shortly after a restart
is not a baseline reading"*. Those are statements about **the world**, and they remain true.
What changed is that the *agent* can now reach the comparison the author made by hand; nothing
in the prose claims it could not.

## The conclusion, stated as a limit rather than as a pass

**No claim in any of the fifteen narratives is falsified by either change.** The stamps are
updated to `cap:c4d52d00` on that basis.

**What this review did not do**, said plainly because the guard's own message says a green run
is not a proof: it did not re-verify each narrative's findings against its captures. It asked
two specific questions derived from the two specific capability changes and answered them. A
claim falsified by something *other* than a baseline comparison or an escape sequence would not
have been caught here, and the previous two failures of this discipline were both of that
kind — which is why the questions are written down above, and why a future reviewer can see
exactly what was and was not asked.
