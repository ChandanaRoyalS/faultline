# T6.1 — the narrative review the capability move required

**Date:** 2026-09-08 · **Task:** T6.1 (with Q27, Q29, Q30) · **Reviewer:** Chandana Sorakundla

`CAPABILITY_VERSION` moved from `cap:c4d52d00` to `cap:dd651ccc`, which failed
`test_every_narrative_was_reviewed_against_the_current_capability_set` on all fifteen bundles, as
it did at T3.3b. The guard's own message says a green run is not a proof; this document is the
review that the re-stamp rests on, in the shape `docs/design/t3.3b-capability-review.md` set.

## What moved, and what each change could falsify

One capability input moved: `TOOL_BEHAVIOUR_REVISION` **2 → 3**. The tool surface is unchanged
(`trace_query` keeps its name and signature) and `CAPTURE_SET` is unchanged. Three behaviour
changes sit behind the one bump.

**1. `trace_query` reads Tempo, not Jaeger.** Same spans, arriving through a second exporter on
the demo collector's traces pipeline, stored for 48 hours in a store that answers a search by
service and window and then a whole trace by id. (Retention was 48 h when this was
written and is 24 h as shipped — bounded with Tempo's memory before the first recording.)

*The claim shape this could falsify:* a narrative asserting that a trace could not be found, was
not retained, or that the trace store was unavailable during the incident.

**2. The span tree (Q30).** Each trace is rendered as a tree with the span's offset from the
trace's root, its duration, its self-time, its status and its parent - and the tool names the
**degrading hop** by a stated rule (`faultline.tools.spantree`). Before this the tool flattened
every trace into a list with neither start time nor parent, and three sweep-11 verdicts said so.

*The claim shape this could falsify:* a narrative asserting that the slow or failing hop could
not be placed from traces, that spans carried no timing or status, or that a call's position in
the request path was not visible.

**3. `redis-cart` and `kafka` join the service catalog (Q27).** Not a capability input - the
catalog is not in the digest - but it lands in the same PR and changes what a verdict may name,
so it is reviewed here rather than nowhere.

*The claim shape this could falsify:* a narrative asserting that the system does not know these
datastores exist, or that nothing could name them.

## What the review looked for, and what it found

Every `incident.md` in `evals/scenarios/artifacts/dev/` (12) and `holdout/` (3) was searched for
`trace`, `span`, `jaeger`, `tempo`, `hop`, `which call`, `where the time`, `not visible`, `cannot
tell`, `could not`, and every hit was read in context rather than counted.

| Claim shape | Hits | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Trace unavailable / not found / not retained | **0** | No narrative describes the trace store as missing or empty. `payment-telemetry-blackout` describes *one service's spans* stopping, which is the fault, and is what Tempo would also show. |
| Hop could not be placed / spans lacked timing or status | **0** | Every trace passage names the hop it saw: *"Checkout spans failing on their call to cart"* (`cart-bad-image-tag`, `cart-redis-misconfig`), *"Checkout's client spans to paymentservice continued … and continued to return normally"* (`payment-telemetry-blackout`), *"Which call was failing. Order confirmation."* (`email-wrong-image`). These are statements about **the world**, made by a person reading the captures, and they remain true. What changed is that the *agent* can now reach the placement the author made by hand; nothing in the prose claims it could not. |
| Datastore unknown / unnameable | **1** | `redis-cart-dependency-latency`: *"Redis is not an instrumented service - it emits no spans, exports no runtime metrics and has no service-level series of any kind. No metric in the system mentions it."* **Unaffected, and still exactly true.** Q27 puts `redis-cart` in the *catalog* as an `INFRASTRUCTURE` node with no edges and no telemetry; it does not instrument Redis, and Tempo receives no span from it. The title *"the datastore has no spans"* stands. The narrative's own reasoning - cart's client-side p95 carried the time - is what the degrading-hop rule now computes (`cartservice/GetCart -> cartservice/HGET`), which is the point of the change and not a contradiction of the prose. |
| Per-hop arithmetic | **3** | `cart-dependency-latency`, `productcatalog-dependency-latency`, `shipping-quote-misconfig` reason about *"the added per-hop delay"* and *"one hop away"*. Statements about the injected fault's geometry, unchanged by any tool. |

`product-catalog-flag-failure`'s *"not a dashboard, not an alert, not a trace attribute"* was
read too: it says the flag's value is in no telemetry, which the span tree does not change - the
flag-service call is a span, its *value* is not an attribute the demo emits.

## The conclusion, stated as a limit rather than as a pass

**No claim in any of the fifteen narratives is falsified by the three changes.** The stamps are
updated to `cap:dd651ccc` on that basis, and the narratives are otherwise untouched: they are
corpus material and a record of what the fault did to the world, and T7.28 established that a
tool change and a re-record replace captures, not prose.

**What this review did not do**, in the same words as last time because they are still the right
ones: it did not re-verify each narrative's findings against its captures. It asked three specific
questions derived from three specific changes and answered them. A claim falsified by something
*other* than trace availability, hop placement or the catalog would not have been caught here.

**What it will have to do again.** The re-record that follows this PR moves `recorded_from` on
every bundle and will ask for a second review against the new captures - `seconds_to_alert` in
particular, which `PREREGISTRATION-T6.1.md` prediction 1 says must stay within T7.28's spread.
**That review is the addendum below**, written the same day the recordings landed.

## Addendum — the second review, against the new captures (2026-09-08)

The review above asked what the *capability* change could falsify. The re-record asks a different
question — what the *captures* now say — and this is that pass, in T7.28 stage 3's shape.

**Eleven of thirteen narratives carried a claim the new captures contradict.** Every one is a
statement about what this recording of the fault did, not about the diagnosis, and each was
corrected against the manifest rather than reworded around.

| `cart-bad-image-tag` | seven services quiet **"together"** at T+6m15s; **"ten alerts"** | four at T+6m00s, three at T+6m15s; **eleven** alerts across ten services, the eleventh after the revert |
| `cart-dependency-latency` | page named **three** services | **cartservice alone**; the slow service alerted first, which the other latency scenarios here do not do |

**Two narratives were checked and needed nothing.** `cart-redis-misconfig`'s seven-quiet-services
passage (six together at T+6m00s, cartservice fifteen seconds behind) matches the new capture
exactly, and `shipping-quote-misconfig`'s evidence passage — *"no error line, no retry"* — still
holds: **zero** error lines in 336, `GetQuoteRequest` throughout. `ad-memory-squeeze`'s runtime-series
argument also survives: the JVM series still stop at T+4m30s and resume at T+10m00s, so *"the series
answer whether, the logs answer when"* is unchanged.

**One claim that reads as a finding rather than a correction.** `shipping-wrong-image` previously
supported *"dilution is worth nearly four minutes"* with frontend crossing at T+6m30s. In this
recording frontend and loadgenerator **never cross**. The mechanism is the same and its magnitude is
not stable enough to quote a delay from, so the prose now says that instead of a number.

## What the re-record measured about the world itself

`PREREGISTRATION-T6.1.md` prediction 1 asked whether alert timing moved. **Twelve of thirteen sat
inside their historical spread; `cart-bad-image-tag` read 181 s against a history of 301, 242, 286.**
Total request rate was unchanged across all thirteen (0.93–1.08× against their archives), so a
busier or quieter world does not explain it. A confirmation re-record — rule fixed before it ran:
keep whatever it produces, publish both readings — returned **272 s**, inside the spread. So 181 was
an outlier, and **the 91-second spread between two recordings of one scenario hours apart is itself
the finding**: it is direct evidence for the R = 3 the sweep is registered at, and against reading
any single R = 1 timing as a property of the scenario.

## Addendum 2 — the third review, and the finding that came out of doing this three times (2026-09-09)

The bundles were recorded again, six hours after the second pass, because the first Tempo
configuration left the trace store's search blind to the last 0-5 minutes (`compose/tempo.yaml`
holds the measurement; ADR-0037 §2 the argument). The prose therefore needed a third pass, and
**that repetition is itself the most useful thing in this document.**

### Three readings of the same thirteen scenarios, on the same compose world

`seconds_to_alert`, in the order recorded:

| scenario | 09-08 early | 09-08 late | 09-09 | spread |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `cart-bad-image-tag` | 181 | 272 | 286 | **105 s** |
| `ad-memory-squeeze` | 196 | — | 256 | **60 s** |
| `recommendation-memory-squeeze` | 241 | — | 225 | 16 s |
| `product-catalog-flag-failure` | 199 | — | 184 | 15 s |
| `shipping-quote-misconfig` | 199 | — | 184 | 15 s |
| `payment-telemetry-blackout` | 377 | — | 361 | 16 s |
| six others | | | | **0-16 s** |

Most scenarios move by one scrape interval or not at all. Two move by minutes. **Onset timing is
not a property of a scenario on this world; it is a property of a run**, and a single reading of
it is an observation rather than a figure - which is the argument `PREREGISTRATION-T6.1.md` makes
for R = 3, arriving from the recorder rather than from the sweep.

### The fan-out is less stable than the timing, and the narratives kept saying otherwise

| narrative | what changed between recordings |
|---|---|
| `shipping-wrong-image` | frontend and loadgenerator crossed the error threshold **four minutes late** in older recordings, **not at all** in the second, and **two and a half minutes late** in this one. Six alerts, then six, then **eight** |
| `product-catalog-flag-failure` | the page named **one** service, then **one**, then **three in the same evaluation** |
| `productcatalog-dependency-latency` | the page named **one**, then **one**, then **four** - with the slow service itself arriving *last* |
| `redis-cart-dependency-latency` | **one** on the page, then **all four together**, then **two** |
| `cart-bad-image-tag` | **three**, then **checkoutservice alone**, then **checkout and loadgenerator together** |
| `ad-memory-squeeze` | three alerts, then three, then **five** - a latency pair arrived at T+8m15s that no previous recording had |

Every one of these was corrected against its manifest, as the addendum above corrected the
previous set. But a claim that has been rewritten three times in two days is not a claim about the
fault - it is a claim about one run of it. **The prose now says so where it matters**:
`shipping-wrong-image` states that how far behind the edge alerts arrive *is not stable* and gives
all three readings rather than a delay; `redis-cart-dependency-latency`'s alert table says the
page-time radius is a sample rather than a clue to ordering.

### What this does not license

It does not license leaving a narrative stale. Each one is pinned to its recording by
`recorded_from` and must describe *that* recording, which is why the third pass happened rather
than being argued away. What it licenses is **writing the stable thing as a claim and the unstable
thing as an observation** - the mechanism as fact, the fan-out as this run's.

### The capability question, asked again and answered the same way

`TOOL_BEHAVIOUR_REVISION` did not move: the tool surface, `CAPTURE_SET` and the tool's behaviour
are all unchanged since the second review. **No bundle holds a trace**, so how fresh Tempo's search
index is cannot falsify a claim in any narrative - it changes what the *agent* reaches at runtime,
which dev sweep 12 measures directly. The stamps stay at `cap:dd651ccc`.
