# T7.1 — the v2 candidates, and the order they fill SPLIT-V2.md's slots

**Written 2026-09-24, after `SPLIT-V2.md` was committed (#481) and before any v2 scenario exists.**
ADR-0008 makes fault selection *"the separate, earlier decision"*: the allocation was argued with
no candidate in view, and this note chooses the candidates with the allocation fixed. Nothing here
is rehearsed or run. It fixes, per row, which designs are tried, in what order, and why each is
distinct from its row-mates - so the order in which slots fill, and therefore which scenarios
become holdout, is written down before any evidence exists.

Read against the v2 compose at tag 2.2.0 (`world-v2/docker-compose.yml`, 2026-09-24): images are
`${IMAGE_NAME}:${DEMO_VERSION}-<service>`; address variables are `<SERVICE>_ADDR`
(`VALKEY_ADDR`, `QUOTE_ADDR`, `CURRENCY_ADDR`, `KAFKA_ADDR`, …); memory limits are cart 160M,
checkout 20M, shipping 20M, payment 140M, accounting 160M, fraud-detection 300M, recommendation
500M, ad 300M, email 100M, currency 20M, product-catalog 20M, frontend 250M, quote 40M, kafka 620M,
valkey-cart 20M, postgresql 80M.

## 1. The eligibility rule — which designs may ever be holdout

**A holdout scenario must be one that nothing tuned or retrieved has already seen.** On v2 that
is not automatic, because two kinds of design arrive already seen:

- **v1 dev designs** — their bundles and narratives are in the retrieval corpus (T1.5's rehearsals
  feed T2.4b's past-incident store) and prompts were tuned against them. Re-authored on v2 with a
  renamed service they are the same fault; in a v2 holdout slot they would be retrievable by the
  agent being scored on them.
- **The five mechanisms rehearsed today (R1–R5) and attempted before them (A1–A8b)** — every page,
  log line and span is published in `evals/attempts/`, read line by line while building the
  injector and the tools.

So: **a seen design is dev-only**, and symmetrically **a v1 holdout design is holdout-only** —
`email-wrong-image`, `productcatalog-dependency-latency`, `recommendation-memory-squeeze` were
never tuned against, and spending one in a v2 dev slot would throw away the one property that
makes it worth carrying.

**The fill order that follows, mechanical within each row:** (1) dev-only designs, alphabetical by
v2 fault id; (2) new designs, alphabetical; (3) v1 holdout designs last. Because holdout takes a
row's highest-numbered slots, this puts seen designs in dev and v1 holdout designs in holdout
without anyone choosing a side. A candidate whose eligibility forbids the lowest free slot waits
for its slot; a reserve fills the one it cannot take. A `blocked` candidate releases its slot to
the next in order.

**2026-09-24, at rehearsal: `v2-cart-valkey-misconfig` is blocked by the same rule.** v2's cart connects to its store at startup and exits when it cannot (`Wasn't able to connect to redis`). A wrong `VALKEY_ADDR` is therefore a crashloop: callers refused, cart silent. By the fill rule, its slot passes to the next in order: `bad_config-2` is `v2-payment-telemetry-blackout`, `bad_config-3` is `v2-shipping-quote-misconfig`, and `bad_config-4` takes the first reserve that rehearses. Each is still checked at its own rehearsal against this rule: a misconfigured *dependency address* is exactly the case where v2's services may exit rather than fail per call.

**2026-09-26, at rehearsal: `v2-shipping-quote-misconfig` fills `v2/bad_config-3`, and it changed how the rest of this list must be checked.** v2's shipping reads `QUOTE_ADDR` per request, so it does not crash, and the cart's rule does not apply. A v2 target can therefore have no reachable logs even when its source logs plenty, and v1's logs-based evidence does not carry over by default. **Before a remaining candidate is authored, check its target in Loki's `service` label values over 24 hours, and read its source for a stdout log layer**, then declare `answers_idle_or_absent` from that, not from v1. Of the next reserves, fraud-detection and frontend both have Loki streams. The shipping scenario was recorded as a deliberate zero-class scenario, because its own error spans show it running.

**2026-09-26, by decision: `bad_config-4` goes to the second reserve, `v2-frontend-cart-misconfig`, and the first is passed over.** `v2-fraud-detection-kafka-misconfig` is the same mechanism as holdout row 5, `v2-accounting-kafka-misconfig`: a consumer cut from its broker by a wrong `KAFKA_ADDR`. A dev narrative for it would sit in the retrieval corpus beside the holdout design and hand over its answer, which is what §1 exists to prevent. So the mechanical order was overridden, for §1's own reason, and written here before anything was recorded. fraud-detection-kafka stays a reserve for a case where it is holdout-safe. The frontend design shares no mechanism with either holdout row, and it is distinct in the row: the service that changed is the edge application, and the dependency its errors name (cart) is healthy and busy with checkout.

**2026-09-26, by decision, before authoring: row 5's value is `kafka:9094`, not the table's `kafka:9093`.** The table's value was carried from v1, where nothing listened on 9093. A consumer pointed at 9093 would reach a live listener that serves a different set of APIs, with an unmeasured result, which is not the row's mechanism, *a consumer cut from its broker*, nor v1's *a port nothing listens on*. 9094 keeps both. The design and its slot are unchanged. T7.56's rule is read against v2's source before the recording: accounting is .NET, subscribes without connecting, and blocks in `Consume()` while the client retries, so no exit is expected. The recording checks it.

**2026-09-26, before authoring row 6: `v2-checkout-currency-misconfig` is kept, and its comparator is named.** It is close to the recorded dev scenario `v2-shipping-quote-misconfig`: both are a service holding an address for a leaf that does not resolve. It is not a seen design under §1 (v1's spare, never recorded), and it was pre-registered for this slot before any evidence existed, so swapping it after seeing shipping's result would be the choosing ADR-0008 forbids. Distinctness is decided at rehearsal, by the registered test (PREREGISTRATION-T7.0: (a) rules on services, (b) change recorded, (c) the target's log signature, (d) the target's spans), **against `v2-shipping-quote-misconfig`**, named here before recording. Not distinct on (b) or (d), and (c) cannot separate them because neither target has a reachable log. If the recording pages the same rules on the same services, the scenario is blocked and releases the slot. **2026-09-26, at rehearsal: row 6 is distinct on (a), as predicted, and fills `v2/bad_config-6`.** The page was `ServiceHighErrorRate` on checkout alone; shipping did not page and went quiet (`ServiceNoTraffic`) with currency, quote, payment, email, accounting and fraud-detection. **2026-09-26, before authoring row 2: `cartFailure` is narrower than "errors on the cart write path".** Read at source (`world-v2/src/cart`): the flag switches only `EmptyCart` to a second store built on `badhost:1234`; `AddItem` and `GetCart` keep the real one. Checkout calls `EmptyCart` after an order is paid and shipped, and discards its error, so orders should complete. Measured at rest the same day: `EmptyCart` is 0.133 of cart's 1.28 server spans a second, so cart's own error ratio is bounded to a few percent of all its spans and may stay under the 5% line; checkout's client span to it is roughly one in thirteen of checkout's spans, so checkout may page on errors while its orders succeed. The bad store tries to connect on every call under a lock with 30 connect retries, so each failing call may also be slow, and a latency page is possible. None of this is predicted as a number. **The rule for it, written before recording:** a page of any shape is a scenario, recorded as it comes; no page at all blocks it, as A5's flood was excluded, and the slot passes to the next candidate.

**2026-09-26, before authoring row 3: `kafkaQueueProblems` has two halves, and the assessment recorded one.** Read at source: fraud-detection sleeps one second before each record it reads (`main.kt:60`), and checkout, after publishing an order, publishes the same message `value` more times (`main.go:664`; the flag's `on` is 100). At about 0.3 orders a second that is roughly 30 messages a second into a consumer that can do one. Accounting reads the same topic without the sleep and receives every duplicate; its code keys orders by id on one long-lived database context, so a duplicate should be refused in its own code and logged, without an error span. Measured at rest the same day: fraud-detection's spans are `orders receive` (p95 98ms) and a per-record `orders process` (p95 1.9ms), both consumer spans, which the latency rule does not exclude; if `orders process` covers the sleep, fraud-detection's p95 goes past 250ms and it pages on latency. Not predicted beyond that. **Same rule as row 2, written before recording:** a page of any shape is a scenario; no page blocks it and the slot passes to the next candidate.

**2026-09-26, at rehearsal: row 3 paged, and its revert does not restore the world.** The first recording paged `ServiceHighLatency` on fraud-detection alone at 4m16s, so by the rule above it is a scenario. It also showed what the source reading had not. Accounting, the topic's other consumer, refused every order from seconds after onset (`Order parsing failed`, an identity conflict on the duplicates, then `Unexpected entry.EntityState: Detached` from `SaveChanges` on its long-lived database session), raised no error span and no alert, and **kept refusing every new order after the flag was off** until it was restarted by hand eight minutes later; the refused orders' offsets were committed, so they are lost. The ground truth's "turning it off fixes it" was false, so it is corrected, which moves the fingerprint, and the scenario is re-recorded (decided). The injector's revert stays flag-only, which is what an operator would do, so the bundle shows accounting still failing after the fix; the recording routine restarts accounting after the recorder and checks it recovered. Q106 records that the recorder could not see this.

**2026-09-26, by decision: row 4, `v2-image-provider-flag-slow-load`, is blocked on its verification, and the slot passes to the reserve `adFailure`, passing over `paymentFailure`.** The table marked row 4 "flag not located at source; verify first". Located: `imageSlowLoad` is read in the storefront's browser code (`frontend/components/ProductCard/ProductCard.tsx:32`), which adds `x-envoy-fault-delay-request` to image fetches the browser makes, and frontend-proxy's fault filter honours it (`envoy.tmpl.yaml`, `header_delay`). Image-provider does nothing differently. Measured on the running world the same day: the load generator produced no browser spans and no browser log lines in 10 minutes (its Playwright users are not running, though the compose sets `LOCUST_BROWSER_TRAFFIC_ENABLED=true`), and frontend-proxy's only spans were `ingress` and `router frontend egress`, with no image route at all. Nothing on this world sends the header, so the flag would inject nothing; it is blocked without a recording, as A5's flood was excluded, and its measurement is here. The slot goes to the first reserve that rehearses, as `bad_config-4` did, except that `paymentFailure` is passed over for §1's reason: it is a flag on the charge step, beside holdout row 5, `v2-payment-flag-unreachable`, also a flag on the charge step, and a dev narrative for it would sit in the corpus and hint at the holdout's answer. The next reserve is `adFailure`, authored as `v2-ad-flag-failure`.

**Before recording row 4's replacement: its page is predicted to be marginal.** Read at source, `adFailure` fails one `GetAds` in ten (`random.nextInt(10) == 0`). Measured at rest: ad makes 0.472 spans a second, 0.220 of them `GetAds`, so the expected error ratio is about 0.022 / 0.472, **4.7%, just under the 5% line**; the frontend's three ad spans a request are 0.67 of its 11.6 spans a second, so its ratio would be about 0.6%. **2026-09-26, before authoring row 5 (holdout): `paymentUnreachable` acts in checkout, so the target is checkout.** Read at source (`checkout/main.go`, `chargeCard`): with the flag on, checkout builds a client on `badAddress:50051` for each charge instead of using payment's, so every order fails at the charge with `failed to charge card: could not charge the card: ...`, after the cart, the product lookups, the currency conversions and the shipping quote have all succeeded. Distinct from its row-mates on (a) by prediction: checkout pages while the steps before the charge (cart, catalog, currency, shipping, quote) stay busy and the steps after it (payment, email, accounting, fraud-detection) go quiet; and from `v2-checkout-currency-misconfig` on (b), because a flag leaves no change record.

**2026-09-26, at rehearsal: row 5 is distinct in the row on (a), as predicted, and fills `v2/feature_flag-5`.** The page was `ServiceHighErrorRate` on checkout alone at 4m00s, and a minute before the fix `ServiceNoTraffic` fired on payment, email, accounting and fraud-detection; cart, product-catalog, currency and quote kept their traffic, and shipping kept its quotes. **2026-09-26, by decision: row 6, `v2-recommendation-flag-cache-leak`, is blocked on its verification, and the slot passes to the reserve `emailMemoryLeak`.** The assessment read `recommendationCacheFailure` at line 78 as unbounded `cached_ids` growth. Read whole, and confirmed in the running image (`2.2.0-recommendation`, the same file): on a cache miss, half the calls and always the first, the flag calls `product_catalog_stub.GetProduct(demo_pb2.Empty())` (line 84). `Empty` serialises to `b''` (measured in the container), which the catalog reads as a `GetProductRequest` with an empty id and answers `NotFound`, so the call raises before `cached_ids` is extended (line 86); on a hit `cached_ids` is empty and the answer is an empty list. The cache never fills, so the flag leaks nothing: it fails about half of `ListRecommendations` and empties the rest. The row's purpose, memory growth as `resource_exhaustion`'s look-alike, does not exist on this world, and recording what the flag does instead would put a holdout beside the recorded dev ad flag (a random share failing) and catalog flag (a catalog `GetProduct` refused), which §1 exists to prevent. Blocked without a recording, as row 4 was. The reserves in list order: `paymentFailure` stays passed over for §1's reason, `adFailure` filled row 4, and `emailMemoryLeak` is next; it keeps the row's purpose. Authored as `v2-email-flag-memory-leak`, target email, whose code reads the flag.

**Before authoring row 6's replacement: `emailMemoryLeak`'s variant is chosen by measurement, once, and its page is not predicted.** Read at source (`email/email_server.rb`, `send_email`): the flag is a multiplier (`off` 0, `1x` to `10000x`); at 1 or more, `Mail::TestMailer.deliveries` is never cleared and each body is padded to (multiplier - 1) times its length. Measured on the running world the same day: email at 46.5-48.9 MiB of its 100M limit; 2 to 11 confirmation emails a minute (0.10 a second on spans); in a separate process in the container, one kept email cost 17.5KB at 1x, 26.7KB at 10x and 190KB at 100x (about 1.9KB times the multiplier), and 11-17ms to build at 1000x. At 100x the limit is about 45 minutes away and nothing could page inside the 900 s timeout; at 1000x it fills in about five minutes and each OOM kill loses about one email; **at 10000x, about 19MB an email, email should be killed within one to three emails and restart over and over (decided)**, the closest look-alike of a memory squeeze. 10000x was not measured in the container, because it could kill the live server. v2 has no memory rule and checkout only logs a warning when an email fails, so a page can come only from email dying: checkout's email call is about 5-6% of its spans, near the line, and email's spans may stop. Email has no runtime series and a Loki stream (4 to 22 lines a minute at rest), so `[logs]`; the recording reads its restart count and OOM flag. **The rule, written before recording:** a page of any shape is a scenario; no page blocks it and the slot passes to the next reserve, `llmRateLimitError`. The variant is not re-tried to get a page.

**2026-09-26, at rehearsal: row 6's replacement paged, on latency, and fills `v2/feature_flag-6`.** `ServiceHighLatency` on email alone at 4m16s, then on checkout at +6:00; nothing else, and none after the fix. The guessed shape (errors on checkout, a quiet email) was wrong, and so was the reason given for doubting a latency page: one email took 11-17ms at 1000x in a separate process, but at 10000x, in a process near its limit, each took 0.28-0.97s, so email's p95 went to 870-960ms and checkout, which waits for the confirmation before publishing the order, to 550-590ms. Email restarted 24 times in the 9m16s the flag was on, after every one or two emails, its memory at 99.2-99.8% of 100M before each restart; it raised no error span, and checkout's email calls ended in `EOF` at 1.6-3.6% of its spans, under the line. No restarts after the fix, and memory back to its resting 47-50% within two minutes. Distinct in the row on (a): no other row-6 sibling pages email, or latency on a service it kills. **For the `resource_exhaustion` row ahead: Docker did not mark these exits as OOM kills (`State.OOMKilled` false in every probe) and `docker events` returned nothing for the window, so an OOM kill on this host is read from memory at the limit and a silent restart, not from the runtime's flag.**

**2026-09-27, a correction to the note above, found by row 2 of `bad_deploy`.** "Docker did not mark these exits as OOM kills" was a sampling artifact, not the host's behaviour. `State.OOMKilled` and `State.ExitCode` describe the last exit and are cleared when the container starts again; `v2-shipping-wrong-image`'s probe caught `exit=137 oom=true` in every sample taken while the container waited in `restarting`, because the runtime backed off for seconds to a minute. Email's Ruby server was restarted almost at once, so a 20-second probe nearly always found it `running` with the flag already cleared. An OOM kill on this host is recorded; reading it needs a sample between the kill and the next start (`docker events` still returned nothing for either window). The email narrative's wording - the kill read from memory at the limit and a silent restart - stays true of what that recording captured.
