# T7.1 — the v2 candidates, and the order they fill SPLIT-V2.md's slots

**Written 2026-09-24, after `SPLIT-V2.md` was committed (#481) and before any v2 scenario exists.**
ADR-0008 makes fault selection *"the separate, earlier decision"*: the allocation was argued with
no candidate in view, and this note chooses the candidates with the allocation fixed. Nothing here
is rehearsed or run. It fixes, per row, which designs are tried, in what order, and why each is
distinct from its row-mates - so the order in which slots fill, and therefore which scenarios
become holdout, is written down before any evidence exists.

Read against the v2 compose at tag 2.2.0 (`world-v2/docker-compose.yml`, 2026-09-24): images are
`${IMAGE_NAME}:${DEMO_VERSION}-<service>`; address variables are `<SERVICE>_ADDR`
(`VALKEY_ADDR`, `QUOTE_ADDR`, `CURRENCY_ADDR`, `KAFKA_ADDR`, …); memory limits are cart 160M,
checkout 20M, shipping 20M, payment 140M, accounting 160M, fraud-detection 300M, recommendation
500M, ad 300M, email 100M, currency 20M, product-catalog 20M, frontend 250M, quote 40M, kafka 620M,
valkey-cart 20M, postgresql 80M.

## 1. The eligibility rule — which designs may ever be holdout

**A holdout scenario must be one that nothing tuned or retrieved has already seen.** On v2 that
is not automatic, because two kinds of design arrive already seen:

- **v1 dev designs** — their bundles and narratives are in the retrieval corpus (T1.5's rehearsals
  feed T2.4b's past-incident store) and prompts were tuned against them. Re-authored on v2 with a
  renamed service they are the same fault; in a v2 holdout slot they would be retrievable by the
  agent being scored on them.
- **The five mechanisms rehearsed today (R1–R5) and attempted before them (A1–A8b)** — every page,
  log line and span is published in `evals/attempts/`, read line by line while building the
  injector and the tools.

So: **a seen design is dev-only**, and symmetrically **a v1 holdout design is holdout-only** —
`email-wrong-image`, `productcatalog-dependency-latency`, `recommendation-memory-squeeze` were
never tuned against, and spending one in a v2 dev slot would throw away the one property that
makes it worth carrying.

**The fill order that follows, mechanical within each row:** (1) dev-only designs, alphabetical by
v2 fault id; (2) new designs, alphabetical; (3) v1 holdout designs last. Because holdout takes a
row's highest-numbered slots, this puts seen designs in dev and v1 holdout designs in holdout
without anyone choosing a side. A candidate whose eligibility forbids the lowest free slot waits
for its slot; a reserve fills the one it cannot take. A `blocked` candidate releases its slot to
the next in order.

## 2. Row by row

Ids are the v2 injector definition ids and the scenario ids (the same string, as on v1).
*Dev-only*, *new* and *v1 holdout* are the eligibility of §1.

### `bad_config` — 4 dev, 2 holdout

| order | id | eligibility | mechanism on v2 | distinct because |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `v2-accounting-bad-credential` | dev-only (A6) | accounting's database password wrong | the one service fails every write, loudly; nothing upstream notices |
| 2 | `v2-cart-valkey-misconfig` | dev-only (v1 `cart-redis-misconfig`) | cart `VALKEY_ADDR=valkey-cart:6380` | wrong backing store: the cart path errors end to end |
| 3 | `v2-payment-telemetry-blackout` | dev-only (v1) | payment `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` to a dead address | a healthy service goes *silent*: `ServiceNoTraffic` on a working payment |
| 4 | `v2-shipping-quote-misconfig` | dev-only (v1) | shipping `QUOTE_ADDR` to a missing host | the caller pages; the faulty service reports no errors of its own |
| 5 (holdout) | `v2-accounting-kafka-misconfig` | new (v1 attempt produced no artifact) | accounting `KAFKA_ADDR=kafka:9093` | a consumer cut from its broker: silence downstream of checkout, not errors |
| 6 (holdout) | `v2-checkout-currency-misconfig` | new (the v1 spare, never recorded) | checkout `CURRENCY_ADDR` to a missing host | the orchestrator itself misconfigured: checkout errors on a healthy currency |
| reserve | `v2-fraud-detection-kafka-misconfig`, `v2-frontend-cart-misconfig` | new | | |

T7.56's disqualification for `accounting-kafka-misconfig` (a consumer that exits fatally and
crashloops is `bad_deploy`'s page, not `bad_config`'s) carries over unchanged; v2's accounting is
.NET where v1's was not, so its reaction is unmeasured, not assumed.

**2026-09-24, at rehearsal: `v2-cart-valkey-misconfig` is blocked by the same rule.** v2's cart connects to its store at startup and exits when it cannot (`Wasn't able to connect to redis`). A wrong `VALKEY_ADDR` is therefore a crashloop: callers refused, cart silent. That is `bad_deploy`'s page, and near `v2-cart-bad-image-tag`'s on the same target. v1's lazily connecting cart failed per call instead. By the fill rule, its slot passes to the next in order: `bad_config-2` is `v2-payment-telemetry-blackout`, `bad_config-3` is `v2-shipping-quote-misconfig`, and `bad_config-4` takes the first reserve that rehearses. Each is still checked at its own rehearsal against this rule: a misconfigured *dependency address* is exactly the case where v2's services may exit rather than fail per call.

**2026-09-26, at rehearsal: `v2-shipping-quote-misconfig` fills `v2/bad_config-3`, and it changed how the rest of this list must be checked.** v2's shipping reads `QUOTE_ADDR` per request, so it does not crash, and the cart's rule does not apply. But it logs only over OTLP, and the collector sends OTLP logs to OpenSearch, which no tool reads. Loki had no `shipping` stream in 24 hours, and no `checkout` stream either. A v2 target can therefore have no reachable logs even when its source logs plenty, and v1's logs-based evidence does not carry over by default. **Before a remaining candidate is authored, check its target in Loki's `service` label values over 24 hours, and read its source for a stdout log layer**, then declare `answers_idle_or_absent` from that, not from v1. Of the next reserves, fraud-detection and frontend both have Loki streams. The shipping scenario was recorded as a deliberate zero-class scenario, because its own error spans show it running.

**2026-09-26, by decision: `bad_config-4` goes to the second reserve, `v2-frontend-cart-misconfig`, and the first is passed over.** `v2-fraud-detection-kafka-misconfig` is the same mechanism as holdout row 5, `v2-accounting-kafka-misconfig`: a consumer cut from its broker by a wrong `KAFKA_ADDR`. A dev narrative for it would sit in the retrieval corpus beside the holdout design and hand over its answer, which is what §1 exists to prevent. So the mechanical order was overridden, for §1's own reason, and written here before anything was recorded. fraud-detection-kafka stays a reserve for a case where it is holdout-safe. The frontend design shares no mechanism with either holdout row, and it is distinct in the row: the service that changed is the edge application, and the dependency its errors name (cart) is healthy and busy with checkout.

**2026-09-26, by decision, before authoring: row 5's value is `kafka:9094`, not the table's `kafka:9093`.** The table's value was carried from v1, where nothing listened on 9093. On v2 9093 is Kafka's own KRaft controller listener (`KAFKA_LISTENERS=...,CONTROLLER://kafka:9093`), and `nc -z kafka 9093` from the kafka container succeeds while 9094 fails. A consumer pointed at 9093 would reach a live listener that serves a different set of APIs, with an unmeasured result, which is not the row's mechanism, *a consumer cut from its broker*, nor v1's *a port nothing listens on*. 9094 keeps both. The design and its slot are unchanged. T7.56's rule is read against v2's source before the recording: accounting is .NET, subscribes without connecting, and blocks in `Consume()` while the client retries, so no exit is expected. The recording checks it.

## 4. The count this makes reachable

Forty-four slots, forty-three named candidates (disk_fill's holdout has none), thirteen reserves.
At the record's 23–35 % failure rate, twenty-eight to thirty-three valid scenarios - the plan's 30+
reachable but not assured, which is why the reserves are named now rather than invented when a
slot empties.

## 5. The order of work

1. **The old handlers on v2, once each.** `_ComposeOverrideFault` (bad_config, resource), the image
   swap, and the pumba delay have never run against v2. One inject/restore smoke per handler on a
   dev-only candidate, to T1.4's bar, before any of their scenarios is authored.
2. **The first v2 bundle.** No scenario bundle has been recorded on v2; the recorder's v2 path is
   proven by the first one. Start with `v2-product-catalog-freeze` (slot `v2/process_freeze-1`):
   rehearsed twice already, dev-only, its page known to the second.
3. **Then row by row, dev slots before holdout**, one scenario rehearsed the day it is authored,
   each against ADR-0042's distinctness criterion.
4. **Injector work** for datastore_corruption 2–4 and disk_fill 2–3, and **harness work** for
   `kind: injection`, each when its row reaches it.
