# Critical alert at the edge with a near-silent telemetry board

## What the responder saw first

The page named nginx-web-server, severity critical, with a declared blast radius of four services: the nginx front end plus compose-post-service, home-timeline-service and user-timeline-service. Triage crossed three edges that were never themselves measured, so the downstream list should be read as an assumption about topology, not as an observation. Nothing on the board reproduces the condition that fired the alert. That is the single most important thing to carry forward: by the time anyone looked, there was no alertable signal left to inspect, and no record of which series the alert evaluated.

## The one signal that was not empty

The only metric that returned anything at all was the span-status error ratio for nginx-web-server over the half hour ending at the alert. Five samples, mean around 1%, a single peak just under 5%, and the comparison tool reported no sustained departure. Three of the five samples had no defined value, which the tool reads as intervals with no traffic through the front end at all. So the shape is not a wave of errors; it is a trickle of requests with essentially one interval carrying the elevated ratio. Crucially, the preceding thirty minutes produced zero samples, so there is no before to compare the after against. A responder reading the chart alone could easily mistake "rose from nothing" for "regressed from healthy." It did not; the baseline is absent, not flat.

## The change record, which is where the story actually turns

A twenty-four hour lookback on nginx-web-server contains exactly three entries, and all three land in a roughly two-second span about three minutes before onset (T-3m). They are creations, not edits: a Service and a ConfigMap named for nginx-thrift, then first creation of the workload container. compose-post-service shows the identical pattern — Service and ConfigMap in the same second, container one second later, also around T-3m, also first creation. Every entry on both services is attributed to platform-automation; no human actor appears anywhere in the window. This is the signature of an environment being stood up from nothing, not of a change to something that was already serving.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Several plausible stories were killed and should not be re-walked. A configuration edit regressing nginx: there are no prior versions of those objects in twenty-four hours, so there is nothing to have been edited away from. A version or image bump: no version-change event of any kind is recorded. A rollback as remediation: there is no previous revision of either workload to roll back to. Drift from a change made hours earlier: the log is empty until the final three minutes. An ad-hoc human action: the actor column is uniformly automation. On the telemetry side, a crash loop at compose-post-service would have left startup and termination banners; readiness churn would have left probe lines; a hanging downstream call would normally leave unterminated spans at the front end. None of those signatures are present. Equally, the absence of downstream span metrics cannot be read as those services going quiet at onset, because the pre-incident window is just as empty — the gap predates the event.

## What this record concludes, and what it refuses to conclude

The evidence does not establish a failing mechanism, and naming a culprit service here would be invention. The most defensible reading is that this environment was being brought up from scratch at roughly T-3m and alerted at T+0 before the stack had stabilised or started emitting telemetry. The specialists found no running-state failure to attribute. Confidence is low and no fix class applies. Treat the empty results as unresolved ambiguity rather than as negative findings: an empty answer is not a clean bill of health, and at least two of them were flagged truncated.

## What the next responder should do first

Query the orchestrator. No dispatch ever asked whether the newly created workloads reached Running and Ready, or whether they were stuck pulling images, pending scheduling, or restarting before a first log line existed. That gap alone could explain every empty panel on this board. Second, confirm the Loki and Tempo emptiness with broader selectors — drop the pod-name regex and query by namespace, since a label or naming mismatch would present identically to genuine silence. Third, note that only the error-ratio template was ever run; latency and saturation were never measured for any of the four services, so a slow-dependency or resource-pressure mechanism remains entirely untested. Fourth, establish which signal the critical alert actually evaluated. Finally, the three unmeasured edges crossed during triage may conceal a backend — post storage, a cache or document store, or a service-discovery layer — that no specialist was ever dispatched against.
