# Protocol — the manual-RCA reference, blind within five (T4.7)

**Written and committed before the first draw.** Same discipline as a sweep pre-registration and
for the same reason: a procedure written down after the timings exist is a procedure chosen to
suit them.

T4.7's third deliverable: *"The MTTR claim gets its missing left-hand side the honest way:
self-timed manual RCA on five dev scenarios, reported as **n=5, self-timed, indicative** — an
unsourced number next to a rigorously sourced one damages the rigorous one."*

---

## 1. What this can and cannot measure

**The responder authored every scenario in this catalog.** She wrote the faults, the injections
and the recorded narratives. No procedure removes that, and none is claimed to.

What the blind draw changes is narrower and worth stating exactly:

| design | what the timing measures |
|---|---|
| unblinded — she picks and injects | how long it takes to **confirm** a known answer |
| **this protocol** — drawn from a sealed pool of five | how long it takes to **recognise** one of a known set |
| a working responder | how long it takes to **find** an answer they did not have |

The second is not the third. It is closer to it than the first, it costs nothing, and it is the
only reduction available: a second responder is not available, a holdout scenario does not help
because she authored those too, and waiting for forgetting is not a method.

**The number is a floor on human time, produced by the most advantaged possible responder.** A
pipeline that beats it has beaten a fully-informed expert, which is a weaker claim than it sounds
and is the claim the rendering makes.

## 3. The procedure, per attempt

1. **Confirm the world is quiet.** The same baseline gate every scored run passes. A world with an
   open incident is a world where the draw is not the only thing wrong with it.
2. **Draw.** One scenario is picked uniformly from what remains of the pool, written to
   `DO-NOT-OPEN-until-answered.json`, and injected. **You are not told which.**
3. **Wait 90 seconds after the incident opens**, before the clock starts. This is the same settle
   the harness takes: the first alert opens the incident and the blast radius fills over the
   following minute or two. The pipeline's latency figure does not include this wait, so neither
   does yours.
4. **You are handed an incident id, and the clock starts.**
5. **Investigate.** Same evidence the pipeline had and nothing else — Prometheus, Loki, Jaeger,
   the change log, the platform UI. **Not** the scenario bundle, **not** `incident.md`, **not**
   any run directory, **not** the seal.
6. **Answer**: a `fault_class` and a `service`. Both, because T4.2 made *which service broke* a
   scored axis and a reference that answers only the mechanism cannot be compared on it.
7. **Or give up.** `--give-up` is a first-class outcome. An investigation abandoned after twenty
   minutes is data about difficulty, and dropping it would make the median a median over the easy
   ones.
8. **The clock stops when the answer is recorded**, and only then is the seal opened and the
   fault reverted.
9. **Wait 300 seconds** before the next draw — the orchestrator's settle window. A firing inside
   it reopens the previous incident rather than opening a new one.

## 4. Rules fixed in advance

**No re-runs.** An attempt that goes badly is recorded and stands. ADR-0022 §3.3 forbids re-running
a scored run to improve a number, and a self-timed number is the one most vulnerable to it: the
second attempt at a scenario is not an investigation, it is a recollection.

**No pausing the clock.** Wall clock between draw and answer, including interruptions. A clock the
responder can stop when the phone rings is a clock that measures attention rather than elapsed
time, and the pipeline's does not stop either.

**Answer before reveal, always.** The seal is opened by the tool after the attempt is written, not
before. `--report` is safe to run at any point; it prints no per-scenario truth.

**A wrong answer is recorded as an answer.** `Reference.correct` reports how many matched, and
**the timings are not filtered by it** — excluding wrong answers would time only the
investigations that went well.

**Abandoned attempts count in the denominator** of the correctness figure and are excluded from
the median only by having no conclusion to time to. The rendering says how many there were.

## 5. What is predicted, before any of it runs

Registered so that a surprise is visible as one.

1. **The median lands between 4 and 15 minutes.** The pipeline's median on these five is 237s
   (3.95 min). A responder who wrote the scenarios and knows the answer set should be *slower*
   than that on most and faster on none by much — recognition still costs a dashboard load.
2. **`frauddetection-memory-squeeze` is the fastest.** One alert, one service, no callers, and the
   change log names the cause outright. Its pipeline run was also the second fastest at 183.3s.
3. **`cart-redis-misconfig` or `ad-memory-squeeze` is the slowest.** Both put the culprit one hop
   behind the loudest service, which is the shape that costs a responder time even when she knows
   it is coming.
4. **Five of five reach a conclusion.** A give-up on a scenario she authored would say something
   about the observability rather than the responder, and would be the most interesting outcome
   in the set.
5. **Service accuracy is 5 of 5; fault-class accuracy is 5 of 5.** She wrote them. **If either is
   below 5, that is a finding about how much the recorded narrative depends on knowledge the
   telemetry does not carry**, and it belongs in the write-up above the timing.

---

## 6. Cost

**$0.00.** No model is called at any point — the pipeline is not run, the judge is not run. What
it costs is world time and attention: five injections, five settles, and however long the five
investigations take. Budget two to three hours including the waits.

---

## 7. What gets written afterwards

`faultline-manual-rca --report`, which prints the contamination above the figure, the label
`n=5, self-timed, indicative`, a median and a range, and **no confidence interval** — five
self-timed observations from one contaminated rater have no sampling model, and a CI would
manufacture exactly the appearance of rigour that damages the figures earning it properly.

Beside it, the comparison T4.7 exists to enable, with both halves labelled for what they are:

| | pipeline, dev sweep 9 | manual, this protocol |
|---|---|---|
| median | 237s | *to be measured* |
| n | 5 runs, R=1 | 5 attempts, self-timed |
| the responder | `claude-opus-5`, no prior knowledge of the scenario | the author of the scenario, blind within five |
