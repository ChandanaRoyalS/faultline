## 2. Why prediction 5 was wrong, and why that is the useful result

The reasoning was that the synthesizer's `recall@3` is one query of twenty-two, so a single tie
flip is its whole number. It is the wrong way round. **A tie only changes recall when the two
tied rows differ in whether one of them is the labelled answer.** The synthesizer's
twenty-one failures are not queries whose answer sits at the boundary — they are queries whose
answer is nowhere near it. Ties never touch them, so the number is immovable.

**So the noise is concentrated where the instrument is already nearly right.** Planner `recall@3`
carries the entire spread: 0.571 / 0.619 / 0.667, two queries of twenty-one. A figure that is
close to zero is stable *because* it is close to zero, and its stability is not reliability —
which is exactly the reading a stable-looking synthesizer number invites.

`recall@5` is the other side of the same point: **0.372 at all seven readings, range zero**,
while `MRR@5` moves across 0.241–0.249. At k=5 the ties do not flip membership, only order
within the window. So the two metrics have different widths and neither states one.

## 3. What this says about the record

**Every retrieval figure in this repository is one draw from a distribution nobody sampled.**
`recall@3 = 0.395` (T6.4, and `GATE_RECALL_AT_3`), `recall@5 = 0.442`, and all seven rows of
[Q49's flag table](RETRIEVAL-2026-09-14-q49.md) were each produced by one seeding of one corpus.

**Q49's decision survives, and its rule was too narrow by half.** §4 registered a **one-query**
band as *"no measured difference"*. The measured width of `recall@3` is **two queries**. The
decision itself is unaffected — flag 1 read 0.395 against flag 0's 0.395, a difference of zero
under any band — and flag 2's `recall@5` = 0.605 against 0.442 is seven queries on the metric
this measurement finds **stable at range zero**, so that observation is if anything firmer than
it was. What does not survive is any reading of that table in which a one-query gap meant
something.

**No figure is restated here.** The measurement says how wide the uncertainty is, not which
value inside it was right, because none of them was: a tie has no right answer, only an
arbitrary one. A figure quoted after this carries the range beside it, or comes from a run made
after §4.

## 4. The repair, and what it does not do

`, id` on both arms — landed in this commit, with a test that reads the SQL literals rather than
the source text, over every `ORDER BY` the module sends rather than the two known today.

`id` is `document_id#section_index`, so the order it imposes is alphabetical and arbitrary, and
**that is the point**. A tiebreaker that preferred shorter chunks, or authored documents, would
be a ranking change wearing a determinism repair and would move figures for a reason nobody
registered.

**It makes the instrument reproducible. It does not make it correct**, and it does not recover
any past figure — those were drawn before a tiebreaker existed and the draw was not recorded.

**One exposure it leaves open.** This changes what the *pipeline* retrieves in scored runs, not
only what the benchmark scores, and `runtime_version` hashes prompts and contracts — it records
no retrieval change at all. Two sweeps either side of this commit are pooled by every grouping
this repository has. That is Q2 and Q31's territory and it is not closed here; what this commit
buys is that the change is on one side of a measurement rather than in the middle of one.
