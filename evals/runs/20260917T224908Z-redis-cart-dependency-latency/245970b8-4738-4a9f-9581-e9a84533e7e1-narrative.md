# Cart path latency with no failing component: unexplained time sits on the caller side

## What we saw, in order

The page was a warning-severity degradation across twelve services, entry point cartservice, with cartservice, checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator all alerting and four edges in the blast radius never measured. Because cartservice was the named start, nearly all the work went there — a reasonable default and, in hindsight, the wrong centre of gravity.

The change record for cartservice covering the nineteen hours before onset was busy: thirty-two entries, all from a single automated actor, no human or manual push. They formed a repeating cycle run about five times — an image reference moved to a hotfix tag and back, a traffic-shaping sidecar attached to the cart network namespace with a fixed egress delay and then detached, and a REDIS_ADDR variable pointed at an alternate Redis port and reverted. Every mutation had a matching revert, so the recorded state at onset was baseline, and the last event of any kind was the REDIS_ADDR revert about 1.3 hours before onset — a removal, not an introduction, followed by a clean quiet gap. One loose thread: the final cycle was compressed, about forty-five minutes starting two hours before onset, and is the only one whose tail lands close to the incident.

cartservice's own logs over the six hours to onset were routine info-level add-item, get-cart and empty-cart handling — no errors, no panics, no restart banners, no cache-connection messages, and continuous logging up to about two seconds before the window edge. The one substantive signal was timing: the interval between an add-item call and its paired get-cart call grew from a few milliseconds early in the window to roughly half a second late. Something slowed without anything failing.

Ten sampled traces then located that time. cartservice server spans were 0.4–2.2ms with 0.1–0.6ms self time, and their only children were Redis commands at 0.2–1.1ms each, no retries. In eight of ten traces the degrading hop was the frontend's client span for the cart call, whose self time (0.7–4.6ms) exceeds the entire downstream server span; start offsets of 0.7–2.2ms appear before the server span begins. checkoutservice showed the same shape from a different angle — full PlaceOrder flows completing at both ends of the window with queue offsets advancing steadily, but per-order wall clock growing from roughly twenty milliseconds to roughly two-thirds of a second, spread across payment, email and queue steps rather than concentrated in one.

## Dead ends worth not repeating

The change log looked like the whole answer for about fifteen minutes and was not. A fresh rollout breaking cartservice: the last image change was a revert 1.9 hours before onset, nothing in flight at onset. Lingering network delay on the cart interface: the shaping container was detached 1.6 hours before onset and never re-attached. A wrong Redis endpoint starving the cache: reverted 1.3 hours before onset, back to unset. An untracked manual edit: every entry carries the same automation actor. Accumulated drift: every mutation pairs with a revert across all five cycles. Note this source was consumed untrusted — it says what was recorded, not what was true on the node.

Metrics were a larger dead end. The only expression ever run was a span-derived error ratio for cartservice, and it returned nothing — not zero, empty — in the incident window and equally empty in both baseline windows. Numerator and denominator both absent means the series does not exist under those labels, an instrumentation or collection gap rather than a behaviour change. Crucially this is not evidence that throughput fell to zero at onset, not evidence for or against an error spike, and useless for distinguishing 'erroring but serving' from 'serving nothing'. Do not cite it in either direction.

cartservice's own logs killed four more: not crash-looping (continuous, no startup lines), not disconnected from its cache, not the origin of user-visible errors, not starved of traffic. Traces killed four more inside cartservice: no handler-time inflation, no slow cache, no other downstream fan-out, no retry storms. checkout logs killed the hard-failure stories: not a total outage, payment not globally down, email path working, queue writes succeeding.

## Where this landed, and what is still open

Conclusion, held at low confidence: cartservice alerted but is not the failing component. Its spans are fast, its cache calls are fast, its logs are clean and continuous, and its recorded configuration at onset is baseline. The unexplained time sits on the caller side of the frontend-to-cart hop, and checkoutservice shows the same broad, error-free, many-hops-slower shape. That pattern is most consistent with the client-side call path waiting — transport, connection pool or queueing saturation — rather than any single downstream being slow. No fix class was identified.

Four gaps the next responder should close first. No resource series were ever collected for frontend or cartservice — no CPU throttling, memory, connection pool, GC or restart counts — so the saturation story is inferred, never measured; start here. Trace sampling ended about an hour before onset, so no traces from the incident window itself were examined. The checkout log result was truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, so any error lines in the middle of the window — where a spike would most plausibly sit — are unobserved rather than absent. And frontend, loadgenerator and the other eight services in the blast radius were never queried directly; the shipping-to-quote hop flagged in one checkout trace is also unexplored.
