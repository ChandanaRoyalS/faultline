# Checkout orders stop completing at the cart fetch

## What was visible first, and the dead ends

The page named frontend, loadgenerator and checkoutservice at once, with the frontend handed over as the starting point. That framing cost the first third of the investigation: the frontend was where the failure became visible, not where anything was wrong.

The first move was comparing the frontend span error ratio against the preceding half hour. It did not rise; it fell slightly, to about 0.95x baseline. Both windows carried intermittent spikes near thirty-odd percent, and the largest spike in the comparison sat in the baseline period, before anything was wrong. Useful negatives: the alert was not driven by frontend error volume, and no dependency serving meaningful traffic went hard-down. The trap is that this query aggregates by service name only, with no downstream dimension, so it cannot say which dependency drives the curve. A flat ratio here means "cannot see", not "nothing wrong".

The same metric one hop in, on checkoutservice, produced the same non-answer more strongly: roughly 7.1% during the incident against 13.3% at baseline, with two-thirds peaks in both windows. That should have raised an alarm rather than closed a door — the ratio halving while orders were demonstrably aborting is only possible if the denominator moved, and total call volume was never measured.

In parallel, change history was queried for checkoutservice and later paymentservice across the preceding day. Both came back completely empty, which genuinely retires the same-service rollback path for those two. But both were scoped to the seed service at zero hops, so cartservice and the other dependencies were never covered. Given where this landed, that scoping is the largest procedural gap in the record.

## The first real signal, and the payment red herring

Checkoutservice logs carried no error-level lines at all across the window — every entry info severity, nothing naming a failing dependency, no timeout, no rejected payload. That absence is why metrics and logs both looked calm.

What changed was the shape of order handling. Before roughly T-2m each order produced a full lifecycle: order start, payment confirmation, confirmation email, downstream message write with an advancing offset. After that, only the order-start line appears, with nothing following. The offset stops advancing after the last good order at about T-2.5m. Requests kept arriving and being accepted through T+2m, each with a parsed user and currency. The service was up, logging and taking work; it just stopped producing completions. That kills the crash, restart, edge-rejection, recorded-timeout and fail-at-first-step stories in one pass, and places the break after order start and before payment confirmation.

Paymentservice was the natural next suspect, and its log stream stops dead at T-2.5m with five silent minutes after. A stream that stops looks like a service that died. It was not. Every charge is paired with a completion within about half a millisecond, including the last one; no dangling request line, no widening gap, no errors, no gradual thinning — steady arrivals, then nothing. That is traffic ceasing to arrive, not work hanging. Payment stopped being called; it did not stop working.

## Where traces put it, and what remains unknown

Traces settled it. Every failing checkout trace terminates at PlaceOrder into prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart into CartService/GetCart, with that child marked ERROR and the error propagating upward intact to the frontend HTTP POST. No payment, email, shipping, currency or EmptyCart children exist in any failing trace — not slow, not hung, simply never created, because the cart fetch aborts the flow first.

A premise the responder carried in was wrong and is worth keeping: nothing hangs. Every span has a finite duration, and the failing PlaceOrder spans run two to six milliseconds with the GetCart child two to four and a half — comparable to or shorter than healthy traces. This is fail-fast, not latency accumulation, so any hypothesis resting on a slow or unterminated child dies here. Notably prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart is not itself ERROR, placing the origin at the dependency boundary rather than in checkout's own logic, and the frontend does reach checkout in every failing trace.

Conclusion: orders stop completing because GetCart began returning errors immediately over roughly a minute starting near T-2.5m. Confidence is medium and no fix class is named, for a blunt reason — no dispatch ever examined cartservice. Its logs, resource metrics, change history and backing-store health are unmeasured; four edges were crossed without measurement. Open for the next responder: cartservice logs and change history including its datastore around onset; the exact status and message GetCart returned, which separates a connection failure from a rejected request; and the unexplained drop in aggregate error ratios, likely a volume effect never measured.
