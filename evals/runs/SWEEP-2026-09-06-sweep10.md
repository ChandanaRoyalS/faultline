## 1. The result that changes how every figure here must be read

**`cart-bad-image-tag` was scored correct on fault class at this exact stamp four hours before this
sweep, and came back wrong.**

| run | stamp | fault class | service | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `20260905T203936Z` (Q25b) | `b6837dd449ca` | `bad_deploy` — **correct** | `cartservice` ✓ | \$0.7349 |
| `20260906T021101Z` (this sweep) | `b6837dd449ca` | `resource_exhaustion` — **wrong** | `cartservice` ✓ | \$0.8203 |

Same scenario, same prompt digest, same capability, same world generation. Opposite answers.

**And the second run did not merely rank the truth low. It never considered it.** Its three
candidates were `resource_exhaustion > bad_config > unknown`; `bad_deploy` appears nowhere.

**Every figure this project has ever published is R=1.** `repeat_count: 1` and `tier: manual`
appear on every run in the record; the variance protocol's `weekly` (R=3) and `published` (R=5)
tiers have never been exercised. So run-to-run variance at a fixed stamp has never been measured —
and here it is, at n=2, arriving by accident, on the one scenario whose history the project has
been reasoning about most carefully.

**What follows, and what does not.**

- **It does not invalidate anything below.** These are five real scored runs and their numbers are
  what they are.
- **It does establish that a single run at this stamp is not reproducible to ±1 scenario on fault
  class**, which is a statement about all five figures and about dev sweep 9's four, and about
  every per-scenario comparison anyone draws between them.
- **It makes the `cart-redis-misconfig` result unreadable in the way it was registered.**
  Prediction 4 said that scenario would repeat its `bad_config` → `resource_exhaustion` mistake,
  with the registered consequence that a repeat *"makes it a property of the scenario rather than
  an instance"*. It did not repeat: it abstained, correctly named `cartservice`, and stated which
  queries it had not run. That looks like better behaviour. **Given the row above, it cannot be
  distinguished from the same variance in a different costume**, and this document does not claim
  otherwise.

**The cheapest thing that would settle it is R=3 on these five, priced in `docs/PLAN.md` at about
\$9.** That is now the highest-value unspent money in this project, and it was not before today.

### Prediction 4's failure, and why the registered consequence does not fire

Registered: *"`cart-redis-misconfig` wrong again, most likely `resource_exhaustion`… **A repeat
makes it a property of the scenario rather than an instance**, and the right response is an
investigation of whether the telemetry distinguishes the two at all, not a prompt edit."*

It abstained instead. `resource_exhaustion` was its rank-2 candidate, so the *shape* of the
registered guess is visible in the ranking — but the primary answer was `unknown`, and an
abstention is not a repeat. **The consequence does not fire, and §1 says why it could not have
fired cleanly either way.**

## 4. Top-3 becomes publishable, with a condition

Dev sweep 9 registered: *"Top-3 must not be published as a column at this stamp. Three of four
verdicts carried no alternative, so top-3 equals top-1 by construction."*

**That no longer describes this stamp.** Four of five pipeline verdicts carry three candidates.

**And ranking earned something for the first time in this project's record.**
`cart-redis-misconfig` scored `gained_by_ranking: true`: top-1 `unknown`, rank 2
`resource_exhaustion`, **rank 3 `bad_config` — the truth.** Every prior `gained_by_ranking` in the
record is false.

**The condition, and it is not a formality: depth travels with the rate, and B0.2's depth is 1 on
every run.** A top-3 column comparing this arm against that one compares a ranking arm with an arm
that cannot rank, and the gap would read as accuracy. Any published top-3 figure carries **4 of 5,
depth 3 on four of five, ranking gained one scenario** — all four numbers or none.

## 6. Two things that cost nothing and were found by running

**Q25b's contract policy paid for itself on an arm it was not written for.** All five B0.2 runs
emitted `summary` on the verdict, and `cart-redis-misconfig` emitted `confidence` on **both**
alternatives. Under the `extra="forbid"` policy in force before Q25b, each of those would have
destroyed the whole verdict — the failure measured twice at \$0.7323. `REPORTED` accepted and
logged them: `unexpected_fields` carries `{'alternatives[0]': ['confidence'], 'alternatives[1]':
['confidence']}` in that manifest.

**The judge's `different` pool gained a row of a kind it did not have.** Of the 19 `different`
verdicts in the record before today, **18 were abstentions** — which is why the calibration pool
could only ever test the `adjacent` boundary. `cart-bad-image-tag` is a `different` verdict on a
confident wrong answer, not an abstention.

## 7. What this sweep does not establish

- **No variance component and no A/A check.** R=1. §1 is the argument that this is now the binding
  constraint rather than a standing caveat.
- **No between-arm separation on fault class.** n=5 against an MDE of roughly 28pp at n=10. 3-of-5
  against 1-of-5 does not resolve and is not claimed to.
- **No per-class rate.** 0–2 scenarios per fault class.
- **No confidence interval on anything here**, for the reasons above.
- **`bad_deploy` is unsettled and got worse.** Two attempts at this stamp on the only `bad_deploy`
  scenario: one correct, one wrong with the truth absent from three candidates.
