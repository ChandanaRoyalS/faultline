# Checkout failures traced to unreadable cart storage payloads

## What was visible, in order

The page came from checkout: nine services in the blast radius, severity critical, eight unmeasured edges between the seed and the eventual answer. The first three looks all pointed away from the truth. Checkout's error ratio in the incident window ran at roughly a fifth of its own pre-onset baseline, with no sustained departure flagged — the panel a responder would naturally open says checkout is healthier than normal while users are failing to check out. Both series are highly dispersed, standard deviation at or above the mean, which is the shape of low or spiky call volume rather than a steady error signal. The same template was queried twice with the same result, and neither run returned latency percentiles or request rate, so the latency-versus-errors ordering at onset was never actually established. Checkout's change log was empty across a full 24 hours — no deploy, no config push, no flag flip — which kills the deploy-regression reading and also means there is nothing on checkout to roll back. Then checkout's log stream returned zero lines of any level, not merely zero errors. That silence is unresolved and must not be read as an all-clear: it is indistinguishable between a healthy-but-unlogged process, a stopped one, and a broken shipping path or mismatched label. Narrowing that selector further is pointless.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Four sampled checkout traces produced a convincing latency story that led nowhere. The slowest had a 106.8ms root and a 67.5ms PlaceOrder span, and the tooling flagged the frontend→checkout hop as the degrading edge at 63% of self-time. But the time sits in checkout's own self-time — about 30ms in the server span alone — while every downstream server span stays in single-digit milliseconds. Email's confirmation handler is the only consistently non-trivial child, and it is absent from the slowest trace, so it tracks nothing. The quote service's child spans report negative offsets of -65ms to -107ms against their shipping parent; that is clock skew on the quote host, and someone will rediscover it and think it matters. None of the 200 spans carries an error status and all four traces complete the full path — these were successful checkouts, not evidence that checkouts were succeeding. The genuinely load-bearing detail from the traces is the backing-store leaves: HGET, HMSET and EXPIRE all at or below ~2ms even in the slow trace, which rules out a sluggish datastore. Widening the change query to cart surfaced 16 image-reference events from platform automation — eight applies of a hotfix image, eight reverts, flapping for over 11 hours. The last action before onset was a revert, so cart was not on the hotfix build when the incident began.

## What it turned out to be, and what is still open

The answer came from the frontend log stream, not checkout's. Frontend records a gRPC INTERNAL from a checkout call naming a cart failure getting the user cart during checkout, wrapping an upstream FAILED_PRECONDITION about inaccessible cart storage; underneath both is a protobuf deserialization failure inside cart's own Valkey-backed store on both the get-cart and add-item paths. The word "inaccessible" misleads — the store answered and returned bytes, and its leaf operations are fast. This is corrupt or truncated stored content, not an outage, not a slow dependency, not a checkout-side defect; checkout is relaying. Cart is the only downstream named anywhere in the text. Fix class is restoring the data. Confidence is medium, for these reasons: cart was never dispatched directly, so there are no cart logs, cart metrics, or store-side evidence, and the whole attribution rests on error text relayed through frontend. Onset is unexplained — the same parse-failure signature appears about 25 minutes before the stated onset and again two minutes after, so the failure straddles it and nothing identifies what crossed a threshold at that minute. The frontend result was truncated with the onset minute itself dropped. The link from the image flapping to the bad bytes is inferred, never shown. And scope is unknown: whether all cart keys are affected or a subset, and whether writes are still producing bad payloads, decides whether a flush suffices or the corruption returns.
