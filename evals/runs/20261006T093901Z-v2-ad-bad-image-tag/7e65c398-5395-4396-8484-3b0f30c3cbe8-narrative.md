# Ad service unreachable after image rollout; /api/data failing through frontend

## Summary

Critical incident, nine-service blast radius, pages on frontend, frontend-proxy and ad. Every /api/data request failed upward through frontend-proxy and frontend. The origin was a single hop: ad's gRPC endpoint was not reachable at all. Platform automation updated ad's container image reference to a hotfix-tagged build at what this record calls T+0; the running instance shut down one second earlier and nothing came back up. Confidence high. Fix class: rollback. All offsets are relative to that image update.

## What was visible, in order

T-1s: ad's log stream ends on an orderly JVM-driven gRPC server shutdown plus a clean shutdown confirmation. No exception, no stack trace. The ~90 seconds before that are ordinary request handling at a steady rate.

T+0: the change log holds exactly one change against ad in 24 hours, a container image reference update applied by platform automation.

T+40s: frontend starts erroring and never stops. Every retained error line is the same shape: gRPC status 14, no connection established, connect EHOSTUNREACH to one peer address on the ad port. The embedded last-error timestamps advance, so the client kept retrying and kept failing at connect.

T+40s to end of window: ad logs nothing at all.

Traces agree from the other side. Of ten sampled traces (157 spans), the only erroring client spans are frontend's calls to AdService/GetAds on /api/data. Those spans are childless leaves with no ad-side server span beneath them, and they die in roughly half a millisecond. The error then propagates up through the frontend route, frontend-proxy and the load generator.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Ad's error ratio, queried three times across three window lengths, came back unremarkable every time: sub-1% mean, sub-2% peak, and in the tightest window below baseline. This repeatedly pointed away from ad. A service that is not running emits no error spans, so a ratio metric cannot see it. The one real signal there was 15 of 115 intervals with an undefined ratio against 0 of 46 in baseline, i.e. missing traffic rather than failed traffic, and it was under-weighted at the time.

Frontend's aggregate error ratio showed only ~5.7% mean, under 9% peak, and was briefly read as proof that no dependency was hard-down. It aggregates by service only, with no per-dependency breakdown, and ad carries too little of frontend's volume to move it. Worse, the frontend baseline window returned zero samples, so the tool's no-sustained-departure verdict rests on a comparison it never performed. Treat it as void.

Slow dependency, DNS/discovery, TLS or auth, and application-level errors from ad were all ruled out by the error shape: connect-level host-unreachable with a concrete resolved address, failing before handshake, well under any deadline. Broad mesh outage, product-catalog and its SQL children, the full checkout chain, and frontend-proxy as origin were all ruled out by traces showing those paths clean in the same window.

## Mechanism

The artifact deployed to ad was not serving. The rollout stopped the running instance cleanly, which is why ad's final lines are an orderly shutdown with no error, and the replacement never established a listening endpoint. From then on frontend's gRPC client failed at connect, immediately and repeatedly, on every /api/data request, and the error surfaced through frontend-proxy to the caller. Every other frontend dependency in the same traces stayed healthy. The ordering carries the argument: ad's last log line precedes the recorded image update by one second, frontend's first connect failure follows it by forty, and nothing else changed in the window.

## Still open

No container-level evidence was gathered for ad at all: no restart counts, exit codes, OOM kills or image-pull results. Whether the new image crash-looped, failed to pull, or started and died silently is unestablished. This is the biggest hole.

The prior image reference is recorded as absent, so this reads as a first-recorded pin rather than a diffable bump; the evidence does not name the artifact to roll back to. Ad's log result also returned only the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines, leaving roughly 23 minutes mid-window unexamined, so an earlier degradation or an intervening restart attempt cannot be excluded.

The peer address in frontend's errors was never confirmed to belong to ad by any result; the trace output exposed no span attributes, so the address-to-service mapping is inferred from the failing logical dependency. Ad latency, memory, CPU and restart metrics were never queried, and six of the nine services in the blast radius were never dispatched on, so other silent consumers of ad are unknown.
