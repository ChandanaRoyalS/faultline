# Cart service latency floor propagating through checkout and frontend

## What was visible, and the change log

The page arrived as a warning across twelve services rooted at cartservice, with checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator alerting within seconds. Four edges in the path were unmeasured. The misleading first impression was that nothing was down: orders kept completing, no crash text, no restarts. Everything touching the cart was simply slow, and the slowness arrived everywhere at once.

The change history for cartservice over a full 24 hours held exactly three entries, all from the same platform automation actor, all inside the twenty minutes before onset, and nothing in the preceding day. Two were an image pair: a hotfix tag applied near T-21m and reverted near T-12m, so the pre-hotfix reference was running at onset. The third, at about T-3m, attached a traffic-shaping container to cartservice's network namespace with a fixed, jitter-free egress delay on eth0. That was the only change still in force when symptoms began. The same record closed out config and environment edits (none recorded), human action (none), and any older slow-burn change (nothing before the cluster).

## Traces gave the mechanism

Baseline traces near T-6m show cartservice fast: server spans under a millisecond, Redis children around two tenths of a millisecond. From about T+0 every cartservice Redis round trip — reads and writes, all operations, any caller — sits at a near-constant 300-310ms while the gRPC handler's own self-time stays around one millisecond. It is a fixed floor, not a proportional cost: single-key reads, writes and cart emptying all pay the same amount, so cart size and payload are not the variable.

Callers absorb integer multiples of the same quantum in their own self-time — frontend roughly 302-309ms above the cart child, checkoutservice roughly 905ms above a ~302ms child — so the delay is paid on the client side of the hop too. Two orphan traces rooted directly at cartservice show the same ~301-303ms with no parent, confirming the floor is caller-independent. A T+0 checkout trace runs ~2.47s end to end with the cart hop alone contributing ~1.21s.

## Dead ends worth keeping

The hotfix image is the most tempting thing to blame and was live earlier in the window, but it was reverted about twelve minutes before onset. gRPC deadlines on the checkout-to-cart hop: every cart span completes and the full downstream sequence follows in the same trace, with no truncation at a round deadline value and no abort marker — nothing times out, things just wait. A different downstream: currency, catalog, payment, shipping/quote and email all stay between 0.1 and 24ms in the same slow traces, though the automatic worst-hop attribution pointed at the shipping-to-quote hop in the baseline and briefly sent us there.

Cartservice metrics were the biggest time sink. The only series returned was error ratio, and it had zero samples in both the incident window and the six-hour baseline. That looks alarming until you see the baseline is equally empty — the gap predates the incident and is a metric-name, label or scrape-target problem, not a symptom. Cart logs across the window are routine cart operations with no error or startup text, dense right up to the window edge.

## How it reached the alerting services, and the fix

Checkout logs explain the downstream alerts with no errors at all: every returned line is info-severity normal order flow, with queue offsets advancing and orders completing to the end of the window. What changed is spacing — the oldest lines separate order placement, payment and confirmation by tens of milliseconds; the newest separate each step by roughly 1.2 seconds. That is the ~300ms quantum paid several times per order, propagating to checkout, frontend and the load generator, which is why they alerted seconds later while still succeeding.

The mechanism is waiting on a deliberately slowed call path. Fix class is a configuration revert: detach the shaping container. Confidence high.

## Still open

One thing does not fit. Checkoutservice's error ratio has a single change point about twenty minutes before the named onset, rising from an exactly-zero baseline (129 samples, zero variance) to a mean of 0.171 with excursions near two-thirds of calls failing — sustained but highly variable. That is roughly seventeen minutes before the delay was attached, so the added latency cannot explain it, and the errors did not clear after the image revert. This is a second, unexplained failure and the thread to pick up next.

Why we stopped: both log results are truncated to the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines, leaving roughly T-30m to T+1m unreturned for both services. The error onset, and any burst or brief restart in that gap, sit inside the unexamined middle. Separately, cartservice has no metric samples in either window, so its request rate, latency, restarts and saturation are entirely unmeasured.
