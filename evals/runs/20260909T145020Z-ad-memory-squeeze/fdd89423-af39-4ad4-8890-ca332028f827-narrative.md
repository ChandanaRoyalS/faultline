# Frontend shipping-quote calls fail at connection setup; wrong artifact in the shippingservice slot

## What was visible, in order

The page came from frontend and loadgenerator together; blast radius seven services, severity critical, entry point frontend.

The frontend error ratio was the first thing reached for, and it was underwhelming: about 2.6% against a baseline mean near 1.6%, with the standard deviation comparable to the mean and no sustained departure from baseline. Peak in the window was ~7.3% versus ~6.3% in the baseline. If you judge this incident by that graph alone you will conclude nothing is wrong. Two useful things follow: the small magnitude excludes broad-based failure across all frontend traffic (a minority slice was failing), and because the series was grouped only by service_name with no peer or route dimension, it could not tell us which slice. No latency series was returned at all.

The logs gave the shape. Frontend emitted gRPC client errors across the whole window. The oldest kept lines (~14:24:18) show status 13 INTERNAL wrapping a shipping-quote failure, and beneath it a status 14 transport dial failure: connection refused against 172.18.0.7:50050. The newest lines, just after onset (14:55:42 and two within a millisecond at 14:55:46), are bare status 14 UNAVAILABLE, detail indicating no connection was ever established, empty metadata. The signature shifted from a port actively refusing to an endpoint unreachable. Failures a millisecond apart read as concurrent or retried calls on the same dependency.

Those logs ruled out a great deal. Frontend is not the broken component: every error is raised from the grpc-js client stack on a status from a peer, with no panics, OOMs, or handler exceptions. It is not slowness or overload: no DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, no RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED, and the failures occur at connection setup before any request is served. Nothing was rejected for auth or business reasons; no TLS or certificate errors. And it was not a blip — the same failure class bookends more than half an hour. Caveat: the late lines carry no service name, method, or address, so the callee at onset is inferred from the earlier addressed lines, not identified.

## The dead end: traces, and the empty frontend change log

Traces for frontend over the same window returned 10 traces / 96 spans with zero errored spans, every outbound edge in single-digit milliseconds. The peers actually exercised were CartService (AddItem, GetCart), ProductCatalogService (GetProduct), and CheckoutService (PlaceOrder). The deepest fan-out — frontend to checkout to shipping/currency/payment/email/cart/product/quote — finished in about 25ms, the largest hop being shipping to quote at roughly 5ms. Read straight, this rules out a continuously hard-failing outbound edge, rules out downstream latency from cart/product/checkout (attribution kept landing on frontend self-time), and rules out the quote dependency behind checkout erroring. In loadgenerator-rooted traces the frontend span was a leaf with all 4.7–11.2ms as self-time and no children, so the slow spans were not fan-out wait.

Why this is a dead end rather than an answer: the sampled spans cluster only between roughly 14:55:25 and 14:55:50, leaving most of the window unrepresented, and the returned view carried no span attributes at all — no peer address, no port, no status codes. A clean 25-second sample does not overturn thirty minutes of refused dials in the logs, but the tension was never resolved. Do not spend your first ten minutes here.

The frontend change history for the preceding 24 hours came back completely empty — no deployments, no config or environment edits. That kills a bad frontend build, a frontend-side endpoint retarget, and frontend rollback as a remedy in one stroke, and pushed the investigation downstream.

## shippingservice, the conclusion, and what is still open

shippingservice had 20 changes in 24 hours, all attributed to platform-automation with no human actor. They form a repeating cycle: QUOTE_SERVICE_ADDR set to a nonexistent quote host on port 8090, reverted; container image set to an adservice-tagged demo image, reverted — repeating about five times. This activity is chronic and predates the incident, which also best explains the mild, unsustained frontend error shift with baseline maxima near incident maxima.

Timing is both the crux and the weak point. The nearest change to onset is 19 minutes before it: the revert removing the adservice-tagged image. The address override was reverted 40 minutes prior (set 51 minutes prior). Nothing landed in the minutes immediately before onset, and nothing in the window touched a listening port or any 50050 binding. On that basis three hypotheses were ruled out: a change landing immediately before onset, a dead quote address active at onset, and a port remap. Against the gap sits one hard correlation: the first frontend refusal at ~14:24:18 follows the 14:24:15 image override by seconds.

Conclusion, medium confidence: nothing is listening on shippingservice's serving port because the wrong artifact — the adservice-tagged demo image, which does not bind 50050 — is running in the shippingservice slot. Fix class is rollback to the correct image, plus stopping the automation loop that keeps re-applying the override.

Still open. We never observed shippingservice's actual image and port state at 14:53:45; to keep the conclusion standing we assumed an unrecorded re-application or a stale pod. Verify the running image and its listening sockets directly before trusting this. The late UNAVAILABLE lines identify no callee. And no dispatch ever reached shippingservice or quoteservice logs and metrics, while planner errors dropped the adservice/cartservice/checkoutservice/productcatalogservice/recommendationservice fan-out entirely — a broader infrastructure or network cause remains untested.
