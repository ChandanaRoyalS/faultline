# Uniform latency floor on cartservice traffic; callers alerted in sequence

## What the responder saw first

The page fired from cartservice at 20:00:15 UTC. Within thirty seconds three more services followed: loadgenerator, checkoutservice, and frontend. Severity stayed at warning throughout. The blast radius eventually counted twelve services, with four edges crossed that had no measurement on them at all — meaning the propagation path was partly inferred rather than observed.

The shape of the alert ordering is worth pausing on. Cartservice alerted first, then its callers, in the order you would expect if the callers were queueing behind a slow cart rather than reacting to a cart that had gone away or started returning errors. That reading held up, but it was a hypothesis at T+0, not a finding.

## The change that landed before the page

Working backwards from the alert time, a single change appears in cartservice's history for the window. At 19:56:50 UTC — roughly three and a half minutes ahead of the first alert — platform-automation created a traffic-shaping container attached to the cart-service network namespace. It applied a fixed 300ms delay with zero jitter on the eth0 interface.

Because the shaping is bound to the pod's network namespace rather than to any single connection, it does not degrade one dependency. It puts a uniform floor on every network round trip in and out of cartservice, including calls to its backing cache. The zero-jitter parameter predicts a flat, variance-free penalty rather than a noisy one — a useful discriminator if latency percentiles had been available.

Nothing else landed in that window. No application deploy or image rollout for cartservice. No configuration push touching pools, timeouts, or limits. No feature-flag flip. No cache resize, failover, or upgrade. The change log for the window is one entry long.

## Dead end: cartservice metrics

The obvious next move was to quantify cartservice's error rate from span metrics. The query returned nothing — no series at all for the fifteen-minute window.

The important detail is that the denominator was empty too. The total-calls term carried only a service_name selector with no status filter, and it still matched nothing. So this is not a case of the status label being wrong or the filter being too narrow; the metric family simply has no cartservice series here. An empty result is not a zero, and it cannot be read as "clean."

The practical consequence for the responder: any alert or dashboard built on that call-count family for cartservice was blind during this window regardless of what the service was actually doing. This query also returned nothing on latency percentiles or on saturation of CPU, memory, or connection pools — those questions stayed open.

## Cartservice logs: healthy at both ends, with a hole in the middle

Logs told a different story from the metrics, and a more reassuring one. Every returned line is a routine cart operation — add, get, empty — carrying user and product identifiers. There are no error lines, no exceptions, no timeouts, no connection failures anywhere in the sample.

Traffic is continuous at both ends of the window: normal cadence from about 19:50:16, and a dense multi-request-per-second cluster around 20:03. So cartservice was up and serving through that point. It is not crashing and it is not the origin of the failure in the sense of logging its own breakage.

Two caveats a future responder should not skip. First, the result was truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, leaving roughly 19:50:23 through 20:03:20 unobserved — which includes the alert onset. An error burst confined entirely to that interval would be invisible here. Second, the last line lands at 20:03:43, about ninety seconds before the queried window closes; whether cart stopped emitting or log volume simply tapered cannot be told apart from this evidence.

One loose thread: at least two get-cart calls are logged with an empty user identifier. Not an error line, possibly malformed upstream requests, never explained.

The logs also failed to give the pivot we wanted. No downstream target of any kind — no cache, no store, no peer service — is named anywhere in the returned lines, so there was nothing here to chase outward.

## Dead end: downstream candidates

With no pivot from the logs, we swept cartservice's plausible downstream neighbours directly.

Productcatalogservice came back clean and, unlike the others, actually measurable: error ratio flat at zero across all sixty-one sample points spanning the full window, including everything before 20:00:15. A defined ratio implies a non-zero call denominator, so the service was serving traffic throughout. No pre-alert error degradation, no error-status spans for callers to surface, no hard outage.

Recommendationservice and shippingservice both returned nothing at all — same empty-denominator pattern as cartservice. For both, the absence closes the benign reading as firmly as the alarming one: you cannot call them healthy on this evidence and you cannot call them degraded. No latency was gathered for shippingservice; the executed query covered error ratio only.

So of three downstream candidates, one is affirmatively clean and two are silent. That settles nothing about them, but it also produced no competing explanation.

## Where it landed

The working conclusion is that the traffic shaping attached at 19:56:50 imposed a uniform latency floor on cartservice's network path, and the alerts at frontend, checkoutservice, and loadgenerator are callers waiting on a slowed cart. Cartservice itself is not failing — it is answering, just late.

This is not resource starvation, not a wrong artifact, and not a semantically wrong configuration value. It is waiting on a path that was deliberately made slow. Fix class is a configuration revert: remove the attachment.

Confidence is medium, and the reason is stated plainly in the next section.

## What we never proved

The single largest gap: no latency percentiles were measured for cartservice or for any caller. The predicted ~300ms-per-hop floor comes entirely from the change parameter and was never observed in telemetry. The metrics budget ran out before this could be closed. A future responder facing a similar page should spend the first query here.

Three services — cartservice, recommendationservice, shippingservice — returned no span-metric series with empty denominators. Whether that is a pre-existing collection or label-naming gap, or a second concurrent problem running alongside this one, is unresolved.

The failure mode at frontend and checkoutservice was never examined directly; no query touched them. That they are timing out on cart is assumed from alert ordering, not shown.

Provenance and intent of the platform-automation change are unknown. Whether this was a scheduled experiment that escaped its intended boundary, an accidental invocation, or something else determines whether removal is safe to do unilaterally and whether other services carry similar attachments. Check for that before reverting.

Also still open: the truncated log middle, the ninety-second silence at the tail, and the empty user identifiers.
