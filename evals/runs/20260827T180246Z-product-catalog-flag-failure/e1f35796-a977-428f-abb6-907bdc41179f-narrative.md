# Product catalog GetProduct returning INTERNAL behind an enabled failure flag

## What was visible, in order

Three alerts arrived together: loadgenerator, productcatalogservice, frontend. Critical, seven services in the blast radius, starting at productcatalogservice.

First orienting question was scope. Frontend's error-to-total call ratio was non-zero but modest, peaking near 9.4% over 59 sample points, and touching zero at its minimum — degraded, not down, and starting partway through the window rather than a standing baseline. productcatalogservice looked the same shape: 57 points, peak around 10.7%, also touching zero. Both bursty rather than flat. Internalise that early: roughly one call in ten failing means you cannot reproduce by hand reliably, and a total-outage story is wrong before you begin.

## The dead ends — read this part

**Change history came back empty twice, and it misled.** Both queries against productcatalogservice's change log — deploys, config edits, flag toggles, catalog-data updates — returned nothing. But both covered only 17:56:15–18:11:15, about fifteen minutes, when the interval that mattered ran back to 16:06. Roughly 110 minutes were never looked at. The empty result is true for what it covers and useless outside it. A responder who stops here concludes no change was made and goes hunting for infrastructure problems.

**The service's own logs returned literally nothing** — zero lines, any severity, full window. That is not a quiet service; a live service serving traffic emits routine output. The selector used `product-catalog-service` while the service is named `productcatalogservice`. The hypothesis that the service logged an explicit failure at onset was never actually tested. This is the most consequential dead end here.

**Saturation, timeouts, downstreams, transport, throttling — all considered and dropped.** Failing spans completed in 0.8–2.7ms against sub-0.1ms for successes: slower, but nowhere near any deadline and no queueing signature. No span on cartservice/Redis, currency, shipping, quote, payment, or email carried an error; in the failing checkout trace the cart lookup succeeded first. The failures were application-level gRPC status 13, not UNAVAILABLE or DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, no DNS or TLS text. Frontend's stack traces originate at grpc-js status conversion, so it was relaying a status, not making one. No 429/503/RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED anywhere.

## What mattered, how it fanned out, what is still open

Two independent pieces carried it. Frontend's logs held code 13 INTERNAL errors whose status detail names a ProductCatalogService failure feature flag as enabled — the failing call states the answer in plain text, around T-3m and again near T+4m, sustained across about seven minutes. Separately, trace topology corroborated it structurally: every error span was on GetProduct, and every failing GetProduct carried an accompanying FeatureFlagService/GetFlag lookup that no successful GetProduct in the sample showed. That asymmetry is the tell, and the extra millisecond or two is that lookup. ListProducts succeeded sub-millisecond even in the same trace, so the failure was scoped to one operation.

Fan-out: failures appeared under a frontend browse GET, under a frontend POST into checkoutservice PlaceOrder, and in a trace with no visible root — server-side and caller-agnostic. On checkout, PlaceOrder was marked error and the rest of the pipeline (currency, shipping, payment, email, order publish) was simply absent: it aborted at the catalog lookup. Errors propagated to the frontend root span, which is how a ~10% interior rate became a ~9.4% edge rate.

Conclusion: the flag's enablement is itself the wrong value and is what breaks each request. Turn it off. Not a rollback, not a restart. Fix class: config revert. Confidence high.

Still open before this closes: (1) re-query the catalog service's logs against the correct label — its first-person account was never read; (2) query the full 16:06–17:56 change history to establish when and by whom the flag was set, since errors predate declared onset; (3) explain why the rate is ~10% and bursty rather than total — probability-weighted, product-ID subset, or only some replicas; nothing dispatched distinguishes these. Also unresolved: which HTTP routes and what share of sessions were hit (the frontend metric has no route dimension), whether aborted checkouts left order state needing cleanup, the unmeasured edge crossed in triage was never resolved to a service pair, and whether this flag is a leftover from an intentional exercise — which changes the remediation owner, not the technical fix.
