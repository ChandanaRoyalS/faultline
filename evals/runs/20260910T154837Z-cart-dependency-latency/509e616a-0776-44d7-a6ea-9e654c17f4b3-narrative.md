# Cart latency shift surfacing as frontend errors

## What we saw first

The page came in at warning severity with cartservice named as the origin and a blast radius of twelve services. Four services carried their own alerts: cartservice, frontend, loadgenerator, and checkoutservice, with checkoutservice arriving about fifteen seconds behind the first. Nothing in the initial page said whether this was errors, slowness, or a hard outage, and the first pass through the obvious signals did not settle that question either.

## The first dead end: cartservice metrics

The natural opening move was to pull cartservice's error ratio from the span-derived call metrics and compare the incident window against the preceding three hours. It came back empty on both sides. That is worth dwelling on, because at T+a few minutes the empty result looked like a smoking gun: a pod that had stopped emitting, a collector that had dropped, a crash. It was not. The baseline window was equally empty, so nothing was lost at onset — the series simply was never populated for this service name. Empty is not zero, so this result could not be used to declare cartservice healthy either. Request rate, latency, restarts, and readiness were not covered by this query at all, so those questions stayed open. The practical lesson: this metric family is unusable for cartservice and the investigation had to move to logs and pod-level signals.

## Second dead end: looking for cartservice failing

Cart service logs across the window were dense enough that the result had to be truncated, which itself killed the "log pipeline dead" theory. What came back was uniformly routine: per-request handler traces for cart reads, add-item, and empty-cart operations, spanning many distinct users, with sub-second spacing running right up to the last second of the window. No startup banners, no crash or unhandled-exception lines, no backend connection failures, no auth rejections. So cartservice was alive, serving, and not losing its cache or store. It was not the thing that was broken — it was the thing that had become slow.

Caveat that a future responder should not gloss over: the truncation returned only the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, and the newest block starts well after the alert minute. The alert minute itself was never directly observed. Behaviour at onset is extrapolated from the two window edges.

## Where the symptom actually lived

The frontend told a clearer story. Its error ratio over the incident window averaged roughly 3.2x baseline, mean moving from about 0.018 to about 0.057, with variability around 4.5x baseline — bursty, not a new steady error floor. Peaks reached about 0.42, but the window mean stayed well under ten percent, so this was partial degradation and never anything close to hard-down. Two theories died here: that the frontend was flat and this was purely a slowness problem with no failed calls (it was not, errors clearly moved), and that the elevation was normal noise (the incident maximum far exceeded anything in baseline).

The awkward finding, and the one that cost time, is that the detected change points sit at roughly T-41m and T-22m, not at onset. The frontend was already degraded before the page fired. Chasing "what happened at the alert minute" was the wrong frame.

## The change record

Eighteen changes to cartservice appear in the window, every one of them attributed to the same platform-automation actor. No human actor, no ad-hoc operator edit, no emergency deploy — that whole branch closes immediately.

The change nearest onset, about three minutes before it, attached a traffic-shaping sidecar to the cart-service network namespace imposing a fixed 300ms egress delay with zero jitter on eth0. No matching detach is recorded, so it was still in force when the alerts fired. Zero jitter matters: it predicts a flat latency shift rather than loss or variance, which rules out the "flaky link" reading of the symptom.

Two other artifacts in the same automation cycle were tempting and both proved irrelevant at onset. A cartservice hotfix image tag was applied about twenty-four minutes before onset and reverted about fourteen minutes before, so the baseline image was live when the incident began. A REDIS_ADDR override pointing at a non-default port had been reverted roughly three hours earlier with no re-application. The record shows this triple — image swap, endpoint override, namespace shaping, each set and reverted — repeating at roughly six-hour intervals, which is the signature of an automated harness rather than human activity.

## Conclusion

The cause was the fixed 300ms egress delay attached to cart-service's network namespace about three minutes before onset and never detached. Every cart call paid a flat added wait. Cartservice absorbed it without erroring or restarting; its callers did not. The frontend's bursty error elevation is what a fraction of upstream calls exceeding deadlines looks like, and checkoutservice — another cart consumer — alerting fifteen seconds later fits the same shape. Fix class is a config revert: detach the shaping container. Confidence is medium, for the reasons below.

The earlier frontend step around T-22m is plausibly the hotfix-image phase of the same automation cycle, but nothing collected here confirms that.

## Open questions and what a repeat responder should collect

No cartservice or frontend latency series was measured at all. The 300ms shift is inferred from the change record plus the caller's error shape, never observed directly. That is the largest hole and the cheapest to close.

The frontend change point at roughly T-41m is unexplained; it precedes the shaping attachment by a wide margin and does not map to any recorded change.

The cartservice log gap means the alert minute itself was never seen.

Checkoutservice and loadgenerator were never investigated. Their symptoms are assumed to follow from cart latency, not shown to.

The cleanest available confirmation was not collected: whether the shaping container was detached after its window, as prior cycles did, and whether frontend errors recovered on that detach. Get that next time.

Finally, triage crossed four unmeasured edges. A slow dependency behind cartservice contributing additional latency has not been directly excluded — only rendered unnecessary as an explanation.
