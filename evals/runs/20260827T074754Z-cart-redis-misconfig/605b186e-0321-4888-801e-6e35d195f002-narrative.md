# Checkout order pipeline stops at the cart fetch

## What we saw first

The page came in on three services at once: checkoutservice, frontend, and loadgenerator. Severity was set critical with a blast radius of twelve services. That breadth is misleading in hindsight — frontend and loadgenerator were reporting what they were handed, not what they were doing. The starting point given to the responder was checkoutservice, and the first instinct (mine, and probably yours) was that something on checkoutservice had moved.

The honest summary of the whole record: we located where the failure surfaces with high confidence, and never established why it happens there. Read the open questions section before you start work.

## The first thing checked, and why it went nowhere

Change history for checkoutservice over the fifteen minutes around onset came back completely empty — no deploys, no config pushes, no flag flips. That closed the fastest available remediation path: there is no rollback target on the alerting service. Note the coverage limit honestly, because it bit us later: the query ran only over the fifteen-minute window, not the ninety-minute lookback that was actually wanted, and it covered only checkoutservice. Same story for paymentservice and currencyservice change logs — both empty, both over the same narrow fifteen minutes rather than the intended earlier baseline. Those two queries turned out to be effort spent on services that were never reached by a request, but we did not know that yet.

## Metrics on checkoutservice: real, severe, and unpinnable

The error-to-total call ratio for checkoutservice ranged from zero up to roughly two thirds across 58 sample points in the window. That is useful for three exclusions and one frustration. It excludes checkoutservice being a clean bystander — it is emitting error spans at scale. It excludes low-grade retryable noise; two thirds is an order of magnitude past that. And it excludes a hard total outage or crash-loop, since the floor is genuinely zero and the peak never reaches 1.0, so successful traffic coexists with the failures. It also confirms the scrape was working, so "metrics stopped reporting" is not the story.

The frustration: the result came back summarized as a min and a max over the series, not as a curve. We know a clean state and a two-thirds-error state both exist inside the window. We cannot say which minute the transition happened. Request rate and latency percentiles were not returned at all, and the queried window was narrower than the range we actually wanted, so nothing can be said about before or after it.

## Logs: the shape of the degradation

An unfiltered log pull on checkoutservice returned only info-severity lines — nothing at error or warning anywhere in what we could see. No line names a downstream service in a failure context, which killed the hope of being handed the culprit directly.

What the logs did give us was shape. Early in the window, each request produces a complete pipeline: order start, payment success with a transaction id, confirmation email sent, message write with an increasing offset. Later in the window, from roughly T+9m through T+13m, only the order-start lines appear. The payment, email, and write lines that used to follow every order are simply gone. Orders are arriving and being accepted; they are not completing.

Two exclusions fall out. checkoutservice is not down and not crash-looping — order-start lines continue steadily at several per minute. And the failure did not begin before the window: the oldest lines show healthy, complete pipelines, so whatever happened, happened inside it. Payment rejection is also excluded; the only payment lines we have report success, and the later problem is absent stages rather than logged failures.

The caveat that matters: this result was truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines. Roughly T+1m to T+9m is unobserved and could well contain error lines. Anyone reopening this should pull that interior first.

## Traces: where the pipeline actually stops

This is the load-bearing evidence. Every sampled PlaceOrder trace in the window terminates at the same place: PlaceOrder, into prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart, into the cart fetch — hipstershop.CartService/GetCart — marked ERROR, with no spans beyond it. Around two hundred sampled spans, uniformly in error. This is total for the checkout path, not intermittent.

Durations are the detail that reframes everything. Spans complete in roughly 0.4 to 3.1 milliseconds end to end. The cart fetch is sub-millisecond. That is an immediate error return, not a wait and not a hang. We had been carrying a timeout hypothesis into this query; it does not survive. There is no latency-shaped span anywhere in the set, which also rules out saturation, retry storms, or pool exhaustion — those would show a spread of durations and a mix of successes.

It also explains the two empty change logs. Payment, shipping quote, email, currency: no spans for any of them, because execution never reaches them. They were never invoked. And the frontend is exonerated — its HTTP POST and gRPC spans are in error only as parents of an already-failed child. It inherits the failure rather than producing it.

## The dead ends worth keeping

Three metric queries returned no series at all. For paymentservice and currencyservice, the empty result is consistent with the trace finding — no traffic reached them — but note that the denominator was also empty, so we cannot formally distinguish "no traffic" from "the metric name or label keys do not match for this service." Those two queries were, in the end, wasted motion.

The third empty result is the one that actually hurt. The only query dispatched against cartservice was an error-ratio expression, and it returned nothing. Because the total-call denominator is also missing, that single result cannot tell us whether cartservice emitted nothing at all or whether our label scheme simply does not match it. A cartservice that is dead and a cartservice that is up and rejecting every GetCart look identical from where we were standing — both produce the same fast-fail signature in the caller. We stopped one query short of the answer.

## Where it stands

The locus is cartservice. That much is well supported. The mechanism at cartservice is unestablished, and confidence in the overall picture is low for that reason. No fix class was identified and no remediation was applied.

If you are picking this up: query cartservice directly and stop using span-metrics to do it. Its logs, its change history, and its pod and restart state were never looked at once. Establish whether it is running, crash-looping, or up and rejecting. Then ask whether its own backing store is failing, which would move the mechanism another hop downstream and make cartservice another inheritor rather than the origin. Separately, pin the onset minute — pull the unsummarized error-ratio curve and the truncated log interior around T+1m to T+9m. Finally, widen the change lookback: every change query we ran covered only the incident window itself, and the trigger may sit an hour or more before it.
