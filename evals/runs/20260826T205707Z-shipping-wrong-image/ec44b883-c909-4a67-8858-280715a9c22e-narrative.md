# Checkout failures traced to shippingservice running a mis-targeted image

## What was visible, in order

Three alerts landed together at T+0: checkoutservice, frontend, loadgenerator. Severity critical, blast radius twelve services with four edges unmeasured on the callee side.

The first solid reading was checkoutservice's own error ratio: a populated, continuous series of sixty-one points climbing from zero to a peak of roughly 27% of calls. Two conclusions followed. The telemetry was healthy, so the signal was real rather than a scrape gap. And this was not a hard outage — three quarters of checkouts still completed, and the series starting at zero placed onset inside the window, which is what made a change-driven cause worth chasing.

Traces then broke the deadlock. Every failing checkout had an identical shape, ruling out mixed causes. The error appeared first and only on the checkoutservice client span for ShippingService/GetQuote, then propagated upward through PlaceOrder, the frontend gRPC call and the frontend HTTP POST — those upper errors are inherited, not original. The failing GetQuote spans completed in 0.3–2.4ms and whole failing checkouts in 6–15ms, so these were immediate rejections, not timeouts. Failure occurred inside prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart, before payment or email steps were ever attempted, which is why those spans are absent everywhere. No shippingservice server-side span appeared at all.

## Dead ends worth keeping

The reflex was to look for something that shipped on the alerting service. The checkoutservice change history over T-10m to T+5m came back completely empty — no rollout, no config, no flag. There was nothing to roll back there.

We then worked outward through the dependencies, and all three cost time. cartservice had no recorded changes, and its error-ratio query returned nothing — but the denominator (all calls) was empty too, so that is an instrumentation gap, not a clean zero. Do not read it as health, and do not try to fix it by adjusting the status filter. productcatalogservice had no changes and a genuinely flat-zero error ratio with the series present and populated: a real clean bill of health, no ramp before onset. paymentservice had no changes either, including in the five minutes after onset, so no hotfix was muddying the timeline.

Four change queries, four empty results. The pattern itself was the hint — we were querying the wrong services. Note the recurring coverage caveat: the tooling returned a fifteen-minute band around onset, not the full hour asked for. Anything earlier is uncovered by this record.

A fifth near-miss: shippingservice's error-ratio query also returned no series. In isolation that is ambiguous — equally consistent with zero errors and with the metric family being absent — so it convicts nobody on its own.

## The cause, and what is still open

Logs settled it. Around T-10m shippingservice was serving GetQuote and ShipOrder normally, returning quotes and tracking IDs from a non-JVM binary. From roughly T-2m30s through T+5m the only output is a repeating trio of JVM and OpenTelemetry-Java-agent startup messages every 60–65 seconds across at least eight cycles — a restart loop, in a runtime that is not the one that served requests earlier. No panic text naming a GetQuote failure appears, which is what you expect from a process that never reaches request handling.

The change record closed the loop: exactly one change to shippingservice in the window, an image-reference update at T-3m by the platform-automation actor, pointing at a demo image tag whose name suffix belongs to adservice. shippingservice is running the wrong artifact; it crash-loops, never binds a healthy listener, and checkoutservice's GetQuote calls fail instantly up to the frontend. Confidence high. Fix class: rollback.

Open items for whoever follows. The prior image reference is recorded as unset, so the exact tag to roll back to must be recovered from deployment history or the registry first. All change queries covered only the fifteen minutes around onset, so an earlier change — possibly the thing the automation was reacting to — is unqueried. The ~27% peak rather than near-100% is unexplained: dilution by non-checkout traffic, a partial rollout leaving healthy replicas, or successful retries. The log result was truncated and roughly T-10m to T-2m30s is entirely unobserved, so no start-up error text was recovered. The cartservice metric gap looks unrelated and deserves its own follow-up. Only five of twelve services were dispatched on, so effects on paymentservice, emailservice and adservice are inferred, not measured. And nothing here identifies where the mis-targeted tag entered the automation pipeline, so re-application on the next reconciliation is not excluded.
