# Payment path goes dark in traces while charges keep succeeding

## What was visible, in order

The page named paymentservice, critical, three services in blast radius. Call the alert T+0. The obvious reading — payments are failing — was wrong, and disproving it consumed most of the investigation.

First move was an error-ratio baseline. It returned nothing, and this is the most dangerous dead end in the record. An empty series reads like a service that went silent at onset; it was not. The preceding baseline window is equally empty, so the gap predates the alert and carries no information about it. It is a collection or labelling problem in the span-derived metric path. Worse, that one query was the only metric ever retrieved — no liveness, restart count, CPU or memory was ever pulled, so restarts and saturation here are unmeasured, not disproven.

Logs next, and they were reassuring. Continuous charge-request/transaction-complete pairs every few seconds straight through T+0 and out past T+2m, every line at info severity, no panics, no credential rejections, no downstream refusals. Each charge matched by a success record within half a millisecond. That kills crash, exception, rejection and in-service latency at once. One unresolved oddity: the reporting host identifier differs between the early and late log segments while the process id holds constant, and the middle of the window was truncated away, so a container replacement in that gap is plausible and unobserved.

## Traces, and what turned out not to matter

Queried from the paymentservice side, server-side Charge spans clearly exist — a gRPC Charge server span with a nested charge child, entered from a checkoutservice client span, sub-millisecond and unremarkable, in every trace displayed. But that sample clusters entirely around T-9m to T-6m.

Queried from the checkoutservice side, the picture inverts. Charge appears only as checkoutservice's own client span, one to two and a half milliseconds, with no server span beneath it and no children. A leaf. A black hole. Those traces cluster around T+1m30s to T+2m. Server spans present before, absent after — that is the whole finding, and the reason this looked like a payment outage.

Discarded along the way, all worth not re-chasing: a downstream callee failing beneath payment (impossible, the Charge spans are childless leaves with no room underneath); the payment hop as bottleneck (it is a small slice of thirty-to-forty-seven millisecond traces, and the flagged slow hop is consistently shippingservice's client call to quoteservice, or checkout/frontend self-time); checkout aborting at payment (four of five traces continue to cart emptying, confirmation email, ShipOrder and the orders publish); upstream callees misbehaving (all return fast, none error-marked). One genuine loose thread: a single 27-span trace ends with large unaccounted self-time on checkoutservice PlaceOrder and no Charge span at all. Never explained.

## The change, the conclusion, and what is still open

The change history holds eleven entries for paymentservice across the day and they are all the same thing: one environment variable, the OTLP traces endpoint, set to a loopback address where no collector listens, then reverted, repeatedly. Every entry attributed to platform-automation; no human, no pipeline. The cadence is every three and a half to four and a half hours, and in five of six cycles the set was reverted eleven to twelve minutes later. The sixth set landed about T-6m and has no recorded revert.

Note what distinguishes it: not the change, which had happened five times without incident, but the missing revert. Reasoning "this config is unusual, therefore it caused this" would be reasoning badly. Also ruled out from the same record: no deployment, no release, no image or artifact swap, no flag flip, no convergence of multiple settings.

So: a telemetry failure wearing a payment failure's clothes. Charges succeeded and were logged; spans were emitted but never delivered, and callers' client spans rendered the payment hop as an unresolved void. Fix class is a configuration revert, plus scrutiny of the automation whose corrective step failed once in six.

Confidence medium. Still open: what actually fired the alert is unestablished — if the rule is span-derived the missing exporter explains it fully, otherwise an unmeasured symptom remains. The before/after trace comparison rests on two truncated, non-overlapping samples with roughly eight minutes of no coverage between them, so the cutoff is inferred, not observed. And no non-span metrics were ever retrieved.
