# Checkout orders stalling mid-path; suspect cart artifact

## What we were looking at

Time is written as offsets from the opening of the investigation window (T+0). Alerts fired on checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator at roughly T+30m. Blast radius was recorded as twelve services, severity critical, with checkoutservice as the seed. Four edges out of the checkout dependency set were crossed without ever being measured — keep that in mind when reading the confidence at the bottom.

## First look: checkout is up, but orders stop mid-flight

The most useful signal came from checkout's own logs. Through the early part of the window each PlaceOrder arrival is followed by the familiar per-order sequence: payment confirmation, confirmation email, successful queue write. From about T+28m onward that tail disappears entirely. PlaceOrder entry lines keep arriving at a steady cadence right through T+32m, but nothing follows them for the same order. The process is alive, the logging pipeline is alive, and requests are being accepted — they just never reach the payment or notification stages. That ruled out a crash or an OOM kill immediately, and it ruled out payment and email as the place where orders die, since neither stage emits a success or a failure line after onset.

What the logs did not give us was a name. Across the T+28m to T+32m span there is not a single warn or error record: no status code, no timeout value, no downstream service named. The selector matched and returned dense output, so this is a real property of the logs rather than a query miss. That absence is what forced the rest of the investigation to be indirect.

## Confirming onset in metrics

Checkout's error ratio gave a clean anchor. The pre-incident baseline is exactly flat at zero across 128 samples — mean, min, max and standard deviation all zero — so there is no chronic error floor that merely became noticeable. A single change point lands at T+28m, after which the ratio averages about 7% with peaks near two-thirds of calls failing and a minimum still touching zero. That shape is bursty, not sustained, and it is not a total outage: most calls continued to succeed between bursts.

The limitation matters as much as the finding. The query aggregates only by service name, with no RPC, span-name or peer-service dimension. It tells us checkout is failing; it cannot tell us which callee is failing it. That attribution was never closed.

## Dead end: the broker connection refusal

The one explicit downstream failure in the whole log pull is a message-produce failure to the orders topic, connection refused to a broker endpoint, timestamped at T+0m06s. It is tempting — it is the only error line present, and it names a real dependency. It is not the cause. It sits roughly twenty-eight minutes before the symptom, nothing comparable recurs near onset, and it cannot explain the observed behaviour anyway: the queue write happens after payment, and orders at onset are stalling before payment is reached. Note this one down as a distractor; a responder reading the log tail cold will land on it first.

## Dead end: nothing changed on checkout itself

A change-log query over the full 24 hours preceding the window returned nothing at all for checkoutservice — no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips. That closes the obvious remediation path: there is no recent checkout change to roll back.

The query was scoped to the seed service only, at zero hops. The dependency set named in the original question — cart, productcatalog, shipping, payment, currency, email, ad, recommendation — was therefore not inspected by this call. Their change status stayed unknown until asked separately, and for five of them it was never asked at all.

## Dead end: productcatalogservice

Product catalog looked plausible on the graph and was cleared on two independent axes. Its error ratio is exactly zero across both the baseline and the incident window, 128 samples each, with zero variance — so there is no smoothed-away burst hiding in the aggregate and no pre-existing degradation. Whatever is failing downstream of checkout is not error codes coming back from product catalog.

Its change history is likewise quiet near onset. Two entries exist in the 24-hour window, both from platform automation and both roughly 23 hours before onset: a traffic-shaping sidecar attached to the service's network namespace applying a fixed egress delay, then removed about eight and a half minutes later. The end state restores the pre-change condition, so nothing was left in place. No deploy, image rollout, config edit or flag flip for this service anywhere in the window.

One caveat carried forward: no latency series was ever collected for product catalog. Only the error side was measured.

## The change that stands out

Cartservice has twenty-one recorded changes in the window, all from the same platform-automation actor, no human in the loop. Most of them form a repeating cycle roughly every three to four hours: an image reference update, then a traffic-shaping sidecar on the cart namespace applying fixed egress delay, then an environment override pointing the cart's Redis address at a non-default port — each followed by an explicit revert.

Both recurring environment and network mutations were already withdrawn well before onset: the Redis address override about 6.8 hours prior, the traffic shaper about 13.8 hours prior, neither reapplied. Neither was in effect when checkout started failing.

What is left is the newest entry: an image reference update to a cartservice hotfix build at roughly T+26m30s, about three minutes before onset — and, unlike the three earlier image updates in the window, it carries no matching revert. It was still in effect at onset. Cart sits early in the order path, upstream of payment, which is precisely where checkout's requests stop. That coincidence of position and timing is the whole case.

## Where the evidence runs out

Two error-ratio queries came back with no samples at all — cartservice and shippingservice. For both, the baseline window is as empty as the incident window, which means the gap predates onset and is not something the incident caused. The likely explanation is that the span-derived call counter simply is not produced under the queried service label for these two. Read this correctly: empty is not zero, and neither service was measured. Do not record them as healthy.

Also unmeasured: no latency data was gathered for any service in this investigation, and no cartservice logs were ever pulled to confirm the new build is actually misbehaving. Change history for payment, currency, email, ad and recommendation was never inspected.

## Where it landed

Conclusion: at about T+28m checkoutservice began failing intermittently while remaining up, with PlaceOrder requests entering and stalling before payment and email, no error lines and no downstream status code emitted. Nothing changed on checkout itself, and product catalog was clean on both errors and changes. The only change anywhere in the onset window across every service actually inspected is the unreverted cart image update three minutes earlier. Cart sits upstream of the stall point. Most likely cause: the running cartservice artifact. Fix class: rollback.

Confidence is medium, and the reason is stated plainly above — the stalling callee was inferred from topology and timing, never observed. If you are picking this up again, the first two moves are a per-dependency or per-RPC breakdown of checkout's calls, and a direct log pull against cartservice for the period after the image update.
