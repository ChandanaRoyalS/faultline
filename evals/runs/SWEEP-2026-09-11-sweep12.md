## 3. Prediction 5 failed where it was aimed, and the effect landed on the checkout hop

The pre-registration reasoned: *"Where traces should matter is which hop"*, and named the two
scenarios whose fault **is** a slow hop. Both are in the table above, and neither moved:

- **`cart-dependency-latency`: 3 / 3 in both arms.** The fault is a latency injection on
  cartservice, and cartservice's own latency histogram shows it. Metrics alone find it, in every
  run, with or without a span tree. The scenario is at the ceiling in both arms.
- **`redis-cart-dependency-latency`: 0 / 3 on service in both arms**, and every one of the six
  runs named `cartservice`. The target is `redis-cart`, which emits no spans; the nearest
  instrumented node is the client span inside cartservice, which is what the degrading-hop rule
  prints (`cartservice/GetCart -> cartservice/HGET`, ADR-0037 §3) and what the verdict then names.
  The scenario is at the floor in both arms, and §4 says why the catalog entry did not lift it.

**One scenario at the ceiling and one at the floor cannot show a gap.** The pre-registration
assumed headroom that was not there, and that is a finding about the pre-registration: it named
the mechanism correctly and the scenarios wrongly.

**Where the effect is.** Three scenarios, all deploy or config faults, all downstream of
checkoutservice on the order path:

| scenario | A service | B service | what arm B said instead, run by run |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shipping-quote-misconfig` | **3 / 3** | **0 / 4** | `dependency_latency` on `paymentservice`; `dependency_latency` on `checkoutservice` ×2; `unknown` on `checkoutservice` |
| `cart-bad-image-tag` | **3 / 3** | 1 / 3 | `dependency_latency` on `paymentservice`; `dependency_latency` on `checkoutservice` |
| `shipping-wrong-image` | **3 / 3** | 2 / 4 | `unknown` on `checkoutservice`; `dependency_latency` on `paymentservice` |

Nine of nine with traces; three of eleven without. And arm B's wrong answers are not random —
they are the same wrong answer. Read arm B's `shipping-quote-misconfig` verdicts: *"checkoutservice
keeps accepting PlaceOrder requests … none of them reach the payment-success, email-dispatch or
order-message-write stages … the stage at which the log trail stops points at the payment call
path."* Without a span tree, the synthesizer sees checkout's log trail end and guesses the next
hop by position in the code path — `paymentservice` three times and `checkoutservice` itself five
times across the eight misses. With the tree, it reads
`checkoutservice/PlaceOrder -> shippingservice/GetQuote  …ms, error` and names shipping.

**That is *"identifies the degrading hop in the request path"* — the deliverable's own words —
doing exactly what ADR-0037 §3 describes, on the hop the pre-registration did not think to name.**
The hop that matters in this world is not the slow one inside cartservice; it is the erroring one
between checkout and its dependencies, because that is the hop the logs cannot see across.

**What this means for T6.1's status is a reading, and it is put here rather than made.**
Prediction 5's consequence clause is unambiguous: *"If arm A shows no advantage on exactly the
scenarios whose fault is a slow hop, the span tree is not doing what §2.2 says and T6.1 is not
delivered, whatever the totals say."* Taken literally, T6.1 is not delivered. Taken against the
plan's deliverable — *"Trace evidence in investigations; eval accuracy delta measured"* — the
delta is measured, it is positive on every axis, and it is produced by the mechanism the task was
built to add. The pre-registration was written to stop a favourable total from hiding a mechanism
that did not work; here the total is favourable **and** the mechanism is visible in the per-run
verdicts, on scenarios other than the two it named. The honest record is both sentences, and the
owner decides which governs. The recommendation of this document is: **T6.1 delivered, with
prediction 5 recorded as failed-as-written and the reason it was mis-aimed recorded beside it** —
because the alternative is to re-register the same sweep with three scenario names changed and
run it again for \$45, and the runs that would answer it are already in the record.

## 4. Prediction 6: the catalog entry alone moved nothing

Q27 put `redis-cart` into the service catalog as an `INFRASTRUCTURE` node so that a blast radius
could contain it and a verdict could name it. Three arm-A runs of `redis-cart-dependency-latency`,

The pre-registration wrote the reading in advance: *"if neither moves, the catalog entry is not

cartservice's configuration rather than restart the datastore it cannot see. Two scenarios, six
runs, one shape. **Q27 stays landed and is not the fix; Q28 is scheduled, not discovered.**

## 5. The A/A check ran for the first time and failed on a string

`faultline-compare --aa ca4f1d837d2b` split arm A's runs alternately within each scenario and
compared the halves. Every proportion sat under the 16.2 pp MDE; the largest was fault class at
+10 pp with an interval touching zero. Cost: −\$0.03 [−\$0.06, +\$0.01]. Latency: +4.6 s
[−10.2 s, +22.2 s]. **Nothing moved.**

The check printed `FAILED on 2 metric(s)`. `aa.Result.passed` requires the phrase *"no measurable
effect"* in every metric's verdict, and for the two non-proportion metrics the verdict was a
sentence saying the MDE does not apply to them — a sentence that can never contain the phrase. **The
A/A check had never had R ≥ 2 to run on, so its verdict path for cost and latency had never
executed, and its first execution failed by construction.** Fixed the same day: the interval is the
verdict for a figure with no MDE — includes zero, no measurable effect; excludes zero, a failure.
`tests/test_aa.py` carries both directions. Re-run: **Passed: no metric shows a measurable
effect.**

Prediction 9 holds, and it is worth saying what it does and does not buy. At n = 10 the MDE is
16 pp, so a pass is weak evidence — the module's own docstring says so. The number worth reading is
the largest delta the instrument produced between two halves of the same configuration: **10 pp on
fault class**, which is half the effect §3 reports between the arms. The between-arm effect is
twice the within-arm noise on the same axis, at the same n and R. That is the honest size of it.

## 8. Two ways one scenario's world leaked into another's

**The frozen run.** `20260910T002657Z-ad-memory-squeeze` reports an investigation latency of
**6596 s** against a 600 s budget and a 1800 s harness kill. Neither fired. The best explanation
this record supports is that the machine slept: macOS's monotonic clock stops during sleep, so
every timer froze with the process, and everything resumed together on wake — the stray characters
in the log at that point are consistent with a keyboard waking it. Its verdict blamed
`shippingservice` for a fault injected into adservice, and cited *"refused dial to
172.18.0.7:50050"*. It is a scored run in `evals/runs/` and in arm B's fingerprint. **It is not a
valid observation of the ablation, and this document does not count it.** A rule that marks such a
run invalid at write time — investigation latency above its own declared wall clock — is queued as
Q33; it cannot be applied to this run without editing what was recorded.

**The stale endpoint.** Two of the four `ad-memory-squeeze` misses across both arms — the frozen
run and arm A's pass 3 — cite the same address, `172.18.0.7:50050`, and a shipping image change
timestamped to **the previous scenario's injection**. `shipping-wrong-image` is last in the catalog
and `ad-memory-squeeze` is first, so every pass boundary puts one directly after the other. When
the shipping revert recreates the container, frontend's gRPC client keeps dialling the old
container's IP until it re-resolves, and the next scenario opens with frontend's shipping-quote
calls refused — an error the baseline gate does not see, because `confirm_recovery` checks alerts
and reporting, not that a recreated service is reachable from its callers. On a scenario whose own
signal is a spiky 2 % error rate, the inherited signal wins. Arm B's pass-3 run of the same pair,
sixteen minutes after the same kind of revert, was correct — so it is a race, not a certainty. **A
reachability check after a recreate, or a catalog order that does not put the weakest-signal
scenario after a container-recreating one, is Q32.**

**The flap.** The one arm-B discard, `20260910T114659Z-ad-memory-squeeze`: injected 11:47:03,
incident correlated, and when `faultline-investigate` started ninety seconds later the incident
was already `resolved` — the alerts had cleared inside three minutes, and the machine investigates
from `triaging` only (ADR-0016). The orchestrator was right; the scenario's error rate is spiky
enough to drop under the alert threshold. Once in nine attempts at this scenario.

**Latency, and why the tool's figure is not used.** `faultline-compare` reports with-traces minus
without as −133.7 s [−461.9, +36.8]. It pairs per-scenario *means*, and arm B's `ad-memory-squeeze`
mean carries the 6596 s run; one scenario drags the arm by minutes. The medians in §1 — 251.6 s
and 214.4 s, **+37.2 s** — are the figure this document reports and the one prediction 7 was
written against. Both arms fail the clause Gate 6 inherits from Gate 4 (median ≤ 180 s), as
predicted, and the with-traces arm fails it by more: a fourth dispatch costs a model call, and the
tree summary costs tokens (median cost +\$0.10, the one delta whose interval excludes zero).

## 9. What this sweep does not establish

- **That the span tree helps on a slow hop.** The two scenarios that would show it are at the
  ceiling and the floor. A scenario whose slow hop is *between* two instrumented services and is
  not visible in the caller's own histogram would test it; none exists in the catalog.
- **That the effect generalises past this catalog.** Ten dev scenarios, and the effect sits on
  three of them, all on one path (checkout → shipping / cart). The holdout is blocked at three
  entries (ADR-0029) and has no run on this world.
- **That +20 pp is the size of it.** The interval is [−5, +47]. At R = 3 on ten scenarios the
  instrument resolves 16 pp; the point estimate clears that and the interval does not. The
  A/A delta on the same axis was 10 pp.
- **Anything about the judge's agreement figures as a headline.** Shared lineage, override
  recorded on every one.
- **That arm B's six first-attempt runs are the same population as its twenty-nine.** They are
  reported together because the fingerprint says they are, and separately in §1 because the
  circumstances differed.
