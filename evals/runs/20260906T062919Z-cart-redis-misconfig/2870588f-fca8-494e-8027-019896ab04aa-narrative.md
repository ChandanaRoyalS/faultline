# Checkout aborts at cart lookup after a cart-service environment change

## What was visible, in order

Three services paged: checkoutservice, frontend, loadgenerator. Checkout was the seed and turned out to be relaying, not originating.

T+3m: change history scoped to checkoutservice alone, full day, came back empty. No deploys, no config edits, no flag flips. That closed the easy hypothesis but left the dependency half untouched, because the query never hopped outward.

T+8m: checkoutservice logs showed steady PlaceOrder request-start lines every few seconds, all info severity, no stack frames, no dependency named. What was missing were the completion lines — payment authorization, confirmation email, message-write offset — that accompanied each order half an hour earlier. Orders entered and quietly did not finish.

T+14m: traces gave the shape and it never moved. Roughly thirty-five failing traces, all identical: frontend POST, frontend PlaceOrder, checkoutservice PlaceOrder, prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart, then a client call to CartService/GetCart marked ERROR. Nothing downstream of it existed — no currency, charge, ship order, cart empty, email, or orders publish. A successful trace in the same window exercised that whole chain cleanly.

T+21m: cartservice change history showed an automated environment update at 06:29:24Z setting REDIS_ADDR to a redis-cart endpoint on port 6380, where no override had existed.

T+26m: cartservice logs showed repeated cycles of connection attempt, failure, and an unhandled ApplicationException from RedisCartStore initialization in Program Main — at least six cycles between 06:31:57 and 06:33:40, the last still in flight at the window edge. Every stack trace ended at the process entrypoint. No request-handling lines at all. The port logged matched the change record.

## Dead ends worth keeping

The checkoutservice error ratio was actively misleading. It averaged roughly 6–7% during the incident against roughly 59% in the preceding half hour — read naively, checkout improved going into onset. There is no spike to anchor on. It also aggregated by service name only, so it could never have named a downstream.

The cartservice error-ratio query returned zero samples in both the incident and baseline windows. The tool's "no departure from baseline" verdict rests on nothing on either side and must not be read as an all-clear; the gap predates the incident by hours and is a coverage hole.

Two other cartservice changes near onset looked promising and were not. A container adding fixed egress delay to the cart-service network namespace was removed about eleven minutes before onset. Image activity nearest onset was a revert about thirty-one minutes prior, with no image change in the half hour before symptoms.

Timing also pointed away from a slow dependency: failing checkoutservice spans completed under 1.5ms, far too fast for any plausible RPC deadline. That is connection-level rejection, which is what you get when the callee's process is not up.

## Conclusion and what is still open

Three pieces lock together: the change record puts a new Redis address with a non-default port on cartservice minutes before onset; cartservice logs show that exact port dialed, failing, and taking the process down at startup repeatedly; traces show no server-side cartservice span at all, only the checkoutservice client span erroring. Confidence high. Remedy is reverting the configuration. The same override was applied and reverted at least twice earlier in the day, and all twenty-two changes in the window come from one automation actor with no human involvement — a recurring pattern, not a one-off.

Open: nobody queried redis-cart directly, so whether anything listens on 6380 is unconfirmed; the 10–30s time-to-failure reads as timeout rather than refusal. Crash-loop onset is not pinned — cartservice logs were truncated between 06:02:05 and 06:31:57, so the transition relative to the change was never observed. And the ~59% checkoutservice error ratio from roughly 05:30 to 06:02 is an earlier disturbance this cause does not explain; nobody pulled that thread.
