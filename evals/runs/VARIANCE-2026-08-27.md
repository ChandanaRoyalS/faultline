## The variance read

### How many distinct verdicts did five identical configurations produce?

**One.** All five returned `bad_config` / `config_revert`, all five correct, and the judge scored
all five `same_mechanism` without seeing a label.

A sixth observation exists and was not run for this experiment: the T4.7 sweep's
`cart-redis-misconfig` row (`20260826T185202Z`) used a **byte-identical** configuration — same
stamp, `changes: 8`, same three other bounds. It also returned `bad_config` / `config_revert`.
**Six for six on the verdict, under one configuration.**

### Is target-in-plan stable, or itself variable?

This is T4.9's cause-versus-symptom test. T4.9 found that never planning a dispatch against the
broken service coincided with never answering (5 of 5 abstained), and could not say whether that
was a stable property of a scenario or something that varies run to run.

**For this scenario it is stable in the sense that matters and variable in a way that did not
matter.** `cartservice` entered the plan in **all five** repeats — four times in round 1, once in
round 2. It was never missed.

So on `cart-redis-misconfig`, the planner reliably finds the broken service **despite** planning
5, 7, 8, 8 and 13 round-1 dispatches to get there. Reaching the target is not a by-product of
planning widely.

**This does not generalise from one scenario.** T4.8 observed the opposite case —
`email-wrong-image` planning `emailservice` at position five under one configuration and never
planning it at all under another. What this experiment shows is that target-in-plan *can* be
stable while breadth is not; it does not show that it always is. The scenario where it was not
stable remains untested under repeats.

| quantity | across five repeats | ratio |
|---|---|---|
| **verdict** | **identical** | — |
| judge agreement | identical (`same_mechanism` ×5) | — |
| target ever planned | identical (yes ×5) | — |
| **round-1 breadth** | **5, 7, 8, 8, 13** | **2.6×** |
| tool calls executed | 8, 9, 9, 10, 11 | 1.4× |
| **tokens** | **36,430 – 68,493** | **1.9×** |
| cost | $0.4794 – $0.7017 | 1.5× |
| dispatch rounds used | 1 once, 2 four times | — |
| confidence | high ×4, medium ×1 | — |
| bounds exhausted | 1 of 5 (`metrics`) | — |
| judge: dead ends closed | 3, 4, 5, 5, 7 | 2.3× |
| judge: dead ends missed | 2, 2, 3, 4, 6 | 3× |

**The widest plan is the cheapest run.** Repeat 2 planned 13 dispatches, exhausted `metrics` in
round 1, never got a follow-up round, and finished on 36,430 tokens — 47% fewer than repeat 3,
which planned 8 and used both rounds. It also produced the only `medium` confidence and the only
exhaustion. That is T4.9's pattern reproduced under controlled conditions: **a wide plan under a
tight bound is a plan that will not be executed**, and truncation is cheap.

**Dead-end coverage is the least stable thing measured.** The judge found between 3 and 7 of the
recorded narrative's dead ends closed, and between 2 and 6 left open, on five runs that reached
the identical verdict. `ARTIFACTS.md` calls the dead ends "the most useful thing in the document";
by that standard these five records differ in quality by more than twofold while agreeing entirely
on the answer.

## What the spread does to every earlier single-run figure

**The headline is narrower than it looks.** Verdict stability at 6/6 on one scenario is
reassuring, and it is one scenario — the one with the most prior successes in the archive. It is
the easiest available case, chosen for its ground truth and its history rather than for its
difficulty, and a scenario that answers correctly six times running is not evidence about the
scenarios that abstain.

Three concrete corrections to how prior figures should be read:

1. **Fault-class and fix-class figures were probably not lucky draws.** For this scenario the
   verdict did not move once in six identical runs. Where a scenario answers, the answer looks
   stable; the sweeps' per-class rows are unlikely to flip on a re-run for that reason alone.
   This is measured for **one** of the ten scenarios.

2. **Cost figures are single draws with a ~1.9× spread and should never be quoted as points.**
   Every per-run cost in this repository — $0.27 to $0.70 — is one sample from a distribution
   this wide. Sweep totals ($2.92, $3.27, $3.69) are sums of seven such draws, and the difference
   between two sweeps' totals is well inside what five repeats of *one* scenario produced.

3. **Anything read off a single run's plan is a single draw from a 2.6× spread.** T4.8 concluded
   that `email-wrong-image`'s planner "never asked about `emailservice`" from one run; this
   experiment shows breadth varying 5→13 under identical conditions, so that observation is one
   sample and its complement was already in the archive. **The T4.8 finding stands as reported
   and its n stands too** — it was labelled n=1 there, and this is why that mattered.

**What this experiment does not measure:** variance on any scenario that abstains, on any holdout
scenario, or on any other fault class. Five repeats of one scenario is one cell of a table nobody
has filled in.
