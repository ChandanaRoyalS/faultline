# Checkout and Frontend Fail at the First Cart Hop

## What the page looked like

Four services paged at roughly the same moment: checkout, frontend, frontend-proxy, and load-generator. The declared blast radius was thirteen services, severity critical, with checkout named as the entry point. That breadth was misleading from the start — most of those thirteen were downstream of a single hop that never got taken. The first instinct in the chair was to treat this as a wide multi-service degradation and start fanning out; the better move, in hindsight, was to read one checkout trace end to end before touching anything else.

## The first useful look: checkout traces

Pulling traces for checkout covering the hour before and through onset gave the whole shape of the incident in one pass. Every PlaceOrder span in the sample — ten of ten — errored, and every one of them errored in the same place: the outbound gRPC call to oteldemo.CartService/GetCart. The spans returned almost instantly, single-digit milliseconds, with the name resolver reporting it had produced zero addresses. That detail matters twice over. It tells you this is not a slow dependency (a hanging call would have burned the deadline, not returned in 3ms), and it tells you checkout never got far enough to touch anything else. No payment span, no shipping span, no currency, product-catalog, recommendation, ad, or email span appears anywhere in the sample. Checkout aborts on cart and stops.

## Frontend tells the same story with an address attached

Frontend's logs were queried independently and corroborated checkout without sharing any of its code path. Every error line names the cart service and a single endpoint, 172.18.0.24:7070. More usefully, the logs show a progression rather than a flat wall of errors. Around T-3m the failures are gRPC INTERNAL (code 13) with an i/o timeout on the TCP dial — the connection attempt was being made and hanging. By T+2m the same address is returning EHOSTUNREACH under gRPC UNAVAILABLE (code 14), a network-layer refusal rather than a hang. In the same minute the name-resolver 'zero addresses' error appears in frontend too, matching what checkout's traces showed. That ordering — dials hanging to timeout, then host-unreachable, then the endpoint dropping out of discovery after presumably failing health checks — is the signature of connectivity to cart being cut, with service discovery reacting afterward. It is not an application error surfacing from inside cart, and it is not latency.

## Dead end: looking for a change that preceded onset

The natural second move was to blame a recent change, and two separate queries were spent on it. For checkout and its immediate dependencies, the change log over a full day returned nothing at all — no deploys, no config edits, no flag toggles. An empty window is weak evidence on its own (it only proves nothing was recorded), but combined with the trace picture it removed deploy-triggered regression from the list.

For frontend, the change log did return entries, and for a few minutes they looked promising: CART_ADDR being set and reverted by platform automation. Reading the timestamps killed it. All four recorded changes land roughly 22 hours after onset, not before. CART_ADDR was unset when the incident began. This was automation reacting to the incident, or unrelated churn, not its origin. Worth noting for anyone who repeats this: the change log was the single biggest time sink and produced no cause.

## Dead end: reading checkout's own health signals

Two more lines of inquiry on checkout went nowhere, and both are worth recording because they look alarming if you encounter them cold.

First, checkout emitted zero log lines across the whole window. In isolation that reads like a crashed or silent process. It was not — the traces prove checkout was alive and actively failing requests. Either checkout's logs were not shipping or it simply does not log this path. Do not spend time on it.

Second, checkout's error ratio during the incident window averaged about 0.058 against a baseline mean of 0.333 — roughly a sixth of normal. A caller that sheds work in three milliseconds at the first hop produces fewer errored spans as a fraction of a shrinking total, so the metric moved the wrong way relative to intuition. The baseline itself was already high, which further undercuts the metric's usefulness here. There was no sustained departure from baseline in either direction, and the metric is aggregated only by service name, so it could not have pointed at a dependency even if it had moved.

Frontend's error ratio did roughly double, from about 4.9% to 9.8%, with higher variance — directionally consistent with the incident, but intermittent and not a steady outage, and it named nothing. Treat both services as reporters, not subjects.

## What the evidence supports

The cart service became unreachable to its callers. Every other symptom in the thirteen-service radius follows from that: checkout stops at its first hop, frontend's checkout path fails, frontend-proxy and load-generator report what frontend reports, and the remaining services never receive traffic at all and so never show their own problems. Confidence is medium — the case rests entirely on two caller-side views that agree, with nothing from the far side of the connection. Fix class: reconnect.

## Still open — start here next time

Three things were never resolved, and the first is the big one.

No dispatch ever queried cart itself. Its logs, its metrics, and its process state are entirely unexamined. This is the gap that keeps confidence at medium rather than high. If cart's own logs show it complaining that it cannot reach its own dependencies, that confirms a connectivity cut. If cart is silent, that points instead toward the process being stopped or dead. One query would have decided it.

Second, it is unknown whether 172.18.0.24 was still cart's live address at the time or a stale endpoint left behind after a reschedule. If stale, the whole incident reframes as discovery staleness rather than a connectivity cut, and the fix changes accordingly.

Third, an unexplained port discrepancy sits in the record: callers were dialing port 7070, while the frontend CART_ADDR edits (the ones that landed well after onset and were reverted) referenced cart on 7071. Nothing in the evidence explains the mismatch. It may be unrelated automation noise, or it may indicate someone else was already chasing an address problem on this service.
