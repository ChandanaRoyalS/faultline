# fraud-detection order consumption throttled to one record per second by a runtime flag

## Summary

The alert fired on fraud-detection. By the end of the investigation the mechanism was clear: a runtime flag named `kafkaQueueProblems` was enabled for fraud-detection, and the code path it gates sleeps one full second after each consumed Kafka record. Throughput collapsed while the process kept running and kept logging, which is why nothing looked dead. The fix class is a configuration revert - turn the flag off in the flag store. Confidence is high on the mechanism, but note that this verdict does not explain everything that was visible in the window, and the unexplained parts are written up below rather than smoothed over.

## What was visible, in order

Offsets are from the start of the observation window.

At T+0 the service looked normal: consumption proceeding, order counts incrementing, no delay signature in the logs.

The first thing the metrics surfaced was error ratio. Over the incident window it averaged 0.0206 against a baseline mean of 0.0079 - a 2.6x lift - with the spread blowing out (sd 0.1152 vs 0.0133). Two change points landed, one at T+66m where the ratio jumped to 0.20, another at T+125m at 0.143. The ratio touched 1.0 at one point, far outside the baseline maximum of 0.077. Sixteen sample intervals in the window had no defined ratio at all, meaning no spans were recorded in those intervals; the baseline had zero such gaps.

At T+192m the first log line naming `kafkaQueueProblems` appears. From that point it repeats roughly once per second, continuously, to the end of the window at T+242m.

Traces taken in the last half hour of the window made the mechanism unambiguous. Every sampled `orders process` span sat at 1000-1008 ms, tightly clustered, with self-time equal to total duration and no child spans at all. The `orders receive` root span - the poll step - completed in 3.2 ms. One hundred percent of the trace self-time budget lived inside `orders process`.

Back in the logs, the same order IDs recur across consecutive lines near the end of the window, so the consumer was not advancing cleanly. Record counts went from 1574 at window start to 8645 at window end: still moving, just badly.

## Dead ends - read this part

Several plausible lines were followed and closed. Keeping them here saves the next responder the walk.

A recent deploy. The change log was queried across a full 24 hours around the incident for fraud-detection and returned nothing - no deploys, no config pushes, no flag-binding changes. An empty window is still an answer.

A change to the flag service itself. Same query against flagd over the same 24 hours: also empty. No flag-definition edits, no flagd rollout or restart.

These two empties are the point, not a gap. An artifact and config files that are both unchanged, combined with a failure that lives entirely inside one gated code path, is the signature of state flipped in the flag store at runtime rather than shipped in a release. Do not keep hunting the change log.

A downstream dependency going slow. Ruled out by trace shape: the full second is self-time with zero child spans carrying duration. Nothing downstream was called and nothing downstream waited.

The Kafka poll path. Ruled out: `orders receive` is 3.2 ms.

Intermittent outliers. Ruled out: the spans cluster between 1002 and 1008 ms, every one of them.

Application exceptions. No error lines, no stack traces anywhere in the retained logs.

A consumer group rebalance. No rebalance log lines at all.

Flag evaluation failing. The flag evaluates successfully and logs its enabled state on every record; there are no SDK or resolve errors.

And the most misleading dead end: a query against flagd's own logs for `kafkaQueueProblems` came back completely empty, which initially read as evidence that flagd never served the flag. It is not. That query only covered the last half hour of the window, starting roughly 18 minutes after the flag's observed onset, and flagd most likely does not log per-evaluation at all. Treat that empty result as uninformative, not exculpatory.

## What turned out not to matter

The error ratio is what paged us, and it is the weakest part of the chain. A one-second-per-record throttle explains collapsed throughput; it does not obviously explain a ratio of 1.0 or the two change points. Responding to the error ratio first sent the investigation an hour upstream of where the actual mechanism began. If you see this shape again, go to trace self-time early - it localised the problem in one step.

## Still open

Four things this record does not close.

Timing mismatch. The error-ratio change points at T+66m and T+125m precede the first `kafkaQueueProblems` log line at T+192m by 66 and 67 minutes. The flag was explicitly ruled out as their cause on timing grounds - it simply was not active then. Nothing anyone looked at explains those earlier spikes. They may be a separate, earlier failure or an unrelated precursor.

The traffic gaps. Sixteen intervals with no spans at all. A per-record sleep slows throughput; it should not produce intervals of total silence. Something else may be gating traffic into fraud-detection, and nobody probed it.

Provenance of the flag state. Who flipped it, and exactly when, is unestablished. The flagd log query cannot answer this for the reasons above. If you need an audit trail, go to the flag store's own state history, not to flagd's stdout.

Blast radius. Triage reported one unmeasured edge crossed and no one probed the services on the far side. Consumer lag and producer backpressure from a throttled consumer are unmeasured. Likewise, nobody checked whether this flag is scoped only to fraud-detection or is enabled for other consumers too - worth confirming before declaring the revert complete.
