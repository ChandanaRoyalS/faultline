# Checkout orders aborting at the shipping quote step

## What the responder saw first

The page came in as critical with checkoutservice named as the starting point, and alerts spreading across frontend, loadgenerator, accountingservice, emailservice, frauddetectionservice and quoteservice — fourteen services in the blast radius. The first instinct, and it was the wrong one, was to treat checkoutservice as the source because that is where the alert originated and where the error ratio was measurable.

The first hard number came from the span-derived call metrics: checkoutservice's error ratio roughly doubled between the 34-minute baseline and the incident window (about 12% mean to about 24% mean), with peaks near two thirds of all calls failing. Two things in that same result reframed the timeline. Traffic never stopped — every one of the 122 incident-window intervals had a defined ratio, so the service was alive and serving. And the single detected change point sat at roughly T-27m relative to the reference time we had been handed, not at the reference time itself. Whatever started this began well before the moment we were paged about.

## The logs said nothing, loudly

The checkout log stream carried no error, timeout, or exception lines anywhere in the window. Every returned line was info severity. That is an easy result to misread as "checkout is fine."

The signal was in what stopped appearing. Early in the window each order produced a full sequence: order start, payment confirmation, confirmation email, and a successful queue write with an incrementing offset. From roughly T-4m onward only the order-start lines remained. Payment, email and queue-write completions vanished entirely, with a roughly two-minute gap in emitted lines in the middle. Requests were entering checkout and not finishing, and the service was not announcing why. This ruled out a crash or restart — the process kept emitting — and ruled out any hope that log search alone would name the failing dependency.

## Traces located the boundary

Traces were what actually moved the investigation. In every failing trace after the onset, the ERROR status enters the tree at one specific place: checkoutservice's client span for ShippingService/GetQuote. It propagates upward through checkoutservice PlaceOrder, frontend PlaceOrder, and the frontend root. No other downstream call span carries an error.

Execution aborts there. Failing traces are truncated — ten to fifteen spans against forty-two to fifty in healthy ones — and PaymentService/Charge, CartService/EmptyCart, ShippingService/ShipOrder, the email POST and the orders publish are simply never reached. That lines up exactly with the missing completion lines in the logs.

The shape matters. This is a fast rejection, not a hang. The failing GetQuote client spans run about 3–4ms against 8–9ms for the same call in healthy traces, and the failing roots are shorter than the healthy ones (roughly 10–12ms against 24ms). Nothing is waiting on a deadline. Notably, the shippingservice server span and its outbound HTTP child to quoteservice are not themselves marked ERROR, which reads as shippingservice short-circuiting a failure response rather than the server-side work blowing up.

One detail worth carrying forward: in the pre-incident traces, shippingservice's outbound call to quoteservice was already the single slowest hop, 22–26% of trace time. The path that later became an error path was already the dominant cost.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Several plausible leads were opened and closed, and a responder revisiting this should not spend time on them again.

The checkoutservice change history came back completely empty — no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips. But read the window carefully before trusting it: the query actually ran from the incident timestamp forward about 24 hours, not backward into the hours the question was about. So it does establish that nothing was deployed, flipped, or rolled back during or after the incident, and that no human change muddied the signal. It says nothing about the lead-up.

cartservice looked briefly promising. Its change log showed a hotfix image reference applied at roughly T-25m and reverted at roughly T-19m, both by automation. The apply window sits suspiciously close to the metric change point. But the revert landed before onset, so the pre-hotfix image was what was serving when things broke, and no cartservice change sits within nineteen minutes of onset.

Every other downstream was cleared by the traces themselves, in the same failing traces: cart/Redis GetCart completes in 1.8–2.6ms with sub-millisecond HGETs, currency Convert in 1.4–1.7ms, product catalog GetProduct in 1.0–2.0ms, all error-free. Payment was cleared differently — Charge is never emitted in failing traces at all, because execution has already aborted upstream of it.

Finally, two metric queries came back with nothing at all: cartservice and paymentservice returned no call samples in either the incident or the baseline window. Both windows being empty means this is a collection or labelling gap that predates the incident, not telemetry loss at onset. Those two services are therefore unmeasured by metrics, not cleared by them.

## Where this landed

checkoutservice noticed the failure and is not its source. The boundary is the shipping quote path: checkoutservice calls ShippingService/GetQuote, gets a fast failure back, and aborts the order before charging, emptying the cart, shipping, emailing, or publishing. Confidence in that boundary is good; confidence in the mechanism behind it is not, and no fix class is proposed.

The honest gap is that nothing was ever queried against shippingservice or quoteservice — no changes, no logs, no metrics. That is the single biggest hole in this record. A bad artifact, a wrong configuration value, and load shedding under pressure all remain equally consistent with what was observed. The error-ratio signal is bursty rather than a flat plateau (standard deviation exceeding the mean, range spanning zero to two thirds), which argues somewhat against a hard resource ceiling being pinned but does not eliminate it.

Two other loose threads. The metric change point at roughly T-27m overlaps the cartservice hotfix apply window, and the checkoutservice baseline already carried about 12% errors before the incident window opened — so this was a worsening of an existing degraded state, not a clean break from healthy, and whether the shipping errors began at that change point or earlier is unresolved. And one post-onset trace failed with no GetQuote span emitted at all, the degrading time attributed to checkoutservice's own PlaceOrder span; the shipping call was absent rather than slow. That one outlier was not explained.

## Next steps for whoever picks this up

Start where we did not: pull change history, logs, and metrics for shippingservice and quoteservice, and set the window to open well before the metric change point rather than at the paging timestamp. The change-history window mistake made once in this incident is easy to repeat — check the returned window bounds, not just the returned rows.

Second, fix the missing call metrics for cartservice and paymentservice. Two services in a fourteen-service blast radius could not be evaluated at all, and the gap is older than this incident.

Third, checkoutservice emits no error line when an order aborts. The only evidence of failure in its logs is the absence of completion lines. Adding an error log at the abort path would have cut the time to the boundary considerably.
