# Cart path slowdown traced to fixed per-round-trip cache delay

## What was visible, and the two dead ends

The page arrived as a warning rooted at cartservice, with checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator alerting within seconds. Twelve services in the blast radius, four crossed edges unmeasured. Nothing in the page said whether this was errors, latency, or traffic loss, and that ambiguity cost the first half of the investigation.

First dead end: cartservice error metrics. The error-ratio query returned nothing at all for the incident window, and equally nothing for the preceding baseline. Numerator and denominator both absent. This looks exactly like a traffic halt and exactly like a missing metric series; because the baseline was just as empty, the gap predated the alert and could not mark onset. No baseline mean existed, so no departure could be computed. Do not start here next time.

Second dead end: cartservice logs. Only benign cart get/add/empty lines, no exceptions, no refused connections. The result was truncated — oldest eight lines and newest thirty-two — so the minutes around onset fell in an unreturned gap and were never observed. What the tail did settle: no crash-loop, not down, cache backend not lost outright, serving steadily to the final second. What it did not do was name anything, which pushed the search to a dependency.

## Where the signal actually was

Traces broke it. Across ten sampled cartservice traces, every Redis operation — HGET and HMSET alike — cost roughly 300–313ms of self-time and was flagged as the degrading hop. cartservice's own gRPC handler spans contributed under a millisecond, so nearly the whole duration was waiting on the cache. The cost was near-constant across traces rather than load-varying, identical for reads and writes, and uniform across every sampled span: a fixed per-round-trip delay, not queuing, not write contention, not a tail problem. AddItem makes two sequential cache calls and the frontend then follows with a GetCart, yielding ~900–940ms user-facing POSTs against ~300ms for GET-only paths — which is why the edge services alerted alongside cartservice. Inbound volume had not dropped, no downstream other than Redis appeared in fifty-six sampled spans, and frontend self-time was a few milliseconds.

checkoutservice corroborated the timeline: a flat-zero baseline (129 samples, all statistics zero) moving to ~1.1% mean and ~6.9% peak errors, with a change point at 02:12. It is a partial failure, not total. But that metric groups by service name only, with no downstream target dimension, so it does not attribute a single error to the cart path.

Caveat to carry: all ten traces cluster in the final sixty seconds of a two-hour window. They prove the condition was live at the end; they say nothing about when it began.

## The change log, the conclusion, and what stays open

The change history holds 38 cartservice entries, all from one automated actor, forming a cycle that repeats every four to five hours: a hotfix image tag set then reverted, a traffic-shaping sidecar adding a fixed 300ms egress delay attached to the cart-service network namespace then removed, and a REDIS_ADDR override to a non-default port then reverted. Read naively this exonerates everything — every mutation is paired with a revert, the shaping removal landing ~1.6h before onset, and the log is silent for the last ~1.3h. The specialist ruled out all of it on that basis.

That reading is wrong. The traces show the 300ms penalty still live, and the logged mechanism matches the signature down to the magnitude. Either the removal never took effect on the running instance, or the untrusted log is incomplete near onset. A revert entry is a claim about intent, not an observation of state; when the log and the telemetry disagree, believe the telemetry.

Conclusion: cartservice is slow because every cache round trip carries a fixed ~300ms delay. The service itself is healthy. Fix class is a configuration revert — detach the shaping configuration and verify at the instance, not in the log. Confidence medium.

Open: (1) Is the shaping configuration still attached to the running namespace? Never checked directly; this alone would raise confidence. (2) When did the delay begin? Trace sampling is too narrow to align onset with 02:12. (3) Are the checkoutservice errors cart-path timeouts? No target dimension in the metric, and redis-cart was never queried directly.
