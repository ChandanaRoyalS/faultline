# Checkout order failures traced to a cart service that stopped serving after an image update

## What was visible, in order

The page was wide from the start: fourteen services in the blast radius, severity critical, checkoutservice named as the origin, ten services alerting in the same minute. Five edges in the call graph were unmeasured. All offsets below are relative to onset (T+0 = 11:01:30 UTC).

Because checkout was named as the origin, it was treated as the broken component first. Its change history came back empty — no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips, and nothing in flight during the five minutes after onset. Its span-derived error ratio was populated and non-zero, climbing to roughly two thirds of calls with a floor of zero, which confirmed real errors rather than slow successes and confirmed telemetry was intact. But the series was service-level only, with no peer breakdown, so it attributed nothing.

Traces then collapsed the search. Every failing trace had the same shape: frontend POST, frontend PlaceOrder, checkoutservice PlaceOrder, prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart, then the outbound gRPC call to CartService/GetCart. That cart-fetch call was always the deepest ERROR span; its parent was the only span in the chain not marked ERROR. Checkout's own self-time was sub-millisecond and failures returned in 1.6–4.7ms — fast rejection, not a deadline or saturation. That cleared frontend as origin and cleared a bad checkout build.

## Dead ends worth keeping

The payment hop looked like a second failing component: its error-ratio query returned no series at all, neither numerator nor denominator. Traces settled it the other way — no paymentservice span appeared in 200 sampled spans, because checkout aborted before payment was ever attempted. The empty metrics were a consequence. Whether a genuine telemetry problem also hides there was never separated out.

Checkout's logs were read as a hang, and that was wrong. Early in the window PlaceOrder produced complete sequences: order start, payment confirmation, confirmation email, message write. From T+0 only the request-start lines appeared, with every completion line missing. Missing completions look like blocking calls, but they are equally consistent with a fast abort the service does not log — and the trace timings showed the latter. The logs did establish two firm negatives: checkout stayed alive and accepting requests through T+3m, and stalled orders spanned both USD and CAD, so no currency path was implicated.

## The cart service, and what remains open

Cartservice had exactly one recorded change: a container image reference update described as a hotfix build, applied by platform-automation at 10:56:52 UTC, about 4.6 minutes before onset. No config or flag edits, no cache-backend changes, no human operator. Its logs show routine cart operations at sub-second cadence right up to that same timestamp, then an informational hosting-lifetime shutdown notice — an orderly drain, no exception — followed by total silence for the rest of the window, with the selector demonstrably working. Its span-derived call metrics returned no series at all, numerator or denominator: absence of data, not a low value. The old pod drained; nothing replaced it and served. Checkout's cart fetch therefore failed fast, aborting orders before payment, shipping, or currency were touched. Confidence medium; fix class rollback.

Still open, and load-bearing. No container- or orchestrator-level evidence was gathered, so whether the replacement crash-looped, could not pull its image, failed readiness, or was never scheduled is unknown — and with it whether the artifact is wrong or merely unable to start here. The change record's previous-image value is null, so no rollback target has been identified from this evidence. The 4.6-minute gap between shutdown and alert onset is unexplained; evaluation delay, caller-side connection caching, and a partially-serving replica all fit equally. Cart logs were truncated, with roughly 10:51:36–10:56:37 not returned, so a pre-shutdown cache or auth error in that gap would be invisible and that hypothesis is only partly closed. Checkout's 0.67 error-ratio peak implies surviving successes while every sampled trace failed — unresolved, possibly sampling bias. And all queries covered only 10:51:30–11:06:30 rather than the requested 60-minute lookback, leaving earlier cart changes and adservice, productcatalogservice, recommendationservice and currencyservice unqueried.
