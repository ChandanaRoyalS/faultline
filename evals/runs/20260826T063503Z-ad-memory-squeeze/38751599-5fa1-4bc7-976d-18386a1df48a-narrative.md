# Critical frontend degradation with wide blast radius; single automated memory-cap change is the only near-onset candidate

## What was visible at the start

Two services paged: frontend and loadgenerator, both alerting at the same moment. Call that moment T+0. The declared blast radius was seven services and the severity was critical, which was the first thing that felt off — frontend is normally tolerant of a single downstream going quiet, so a seven-service spread and a critical rating suggested either a shared upstream or something broader than one component misbehaving. No alert inventory beyond those two paged services was available to the responder, so it was never established whether any of the other five services in the radius alerted on their own or were simply named by the radius calculation. The triage note also flagged one unmeasured edge crossed — a dependency link with no observability on it — and that edge was never identified during the investigation.

## First line of inquiry: what changed

With no metrics or traces in hand, the investigation went straight at change history for the services nearest the symptom. Four change-history queries were run, which exhausted the available change budget. Exactly one recorded change turned up anywhere near onset: at roughly T-3m40s, an automated actor named platform-automation applied a resource_limits update to adservice, setting a 256m memory ceiling. The prior value is recorded as unset, so this was not a tightening of an existing limit but the first-ever hard cap on that workload. No code deploy, no image rollout, no release-pipeline activity appears for adservice in the window — one entry, and that entry is a limits edit made by automation rather than by a person.

## The three empty queries

Change history for frontend, cartservice, and productcatalogservice all came back empty. Nothing: no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips, no rollbacks, and no in-window remediation activity that might have confounded the signal. Those empty results are what left the adservice limits edit standing alone as the sole change-side candidate.

The important caveat, and the one a future responder should not skip: each of those queries covered only about a fifteen-minute span centred on onset (roughly T-10m to T+5m), not the two hours that were asked for. The empty results are therefore only evidence that nothing landed in the ten minutes before the incident. Anything that landed earlier — including, plausibly, an earlier pass of the same automated rightsizing action across other workloads — is entirely unexamined.

## Working conclusion, and how thin it is

The conclusion recorded at close was correlation only: the memory cap on adservice is the sole change near onset, and the plausible mechanism is that adservice began running into the new ceiling, started dying or throttling, and that degradation propagated outward to frontend and the rest of the graph.

Nothing in the evidence set actually demonstrates any link in that chain. No pod events, no restart counts, no memory-utilization figures, no logs, no traces were collected. There is no evidence that adservice was unhealthy at all after the cap landed, no evidence it exceeded 256m, and no evidence that frontend's failure originated downstream in adservice rather than somewhere else. If adservice's steady-state footprint sat comfortably under 256m, the cap was harmless and this whole line is a coincidence of timing. Confidence was recorded as low, and that rating should be taken literally. The nominated fix class is a revert of the limits change — cheap and reversible, which is the main argument for it, not the strength of the evidence.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Several hypotheses were closed and should not be re-opened without new information, at least for the ten minutes before onset:

A frontend deploy or rollout immediately before onset — ruled out, no change records of any kind for frontend in that span. A frontend feature-flag flip or config push running concurrently and prolonging the incident — ruled out, the window extends five minutes past onset and is still empty. Rolling back the most recent frontend change as fastest mitigation — there is no near-onset frontend change to roll back, so that effort would have been wasted.

A cartservice deploy or an in-flight cartservice rollout straddling the incident — both ruled out on the same empty-window basis. Likewise for productcatalogservice, including the possibility that an in-window rollback there was muddying the signal.

On adservice specifically: it was not a code deploy or image change; it was not a manual edit by an operator; and it was not an ambiguous pile of overlapping changes needing bisection. One entry, automated, limits only.

## Still open when the record was written

Did adservice actually restart, get killed, or throttle after the cap landed? Unverified — the entire causal mechanism rests on inference. What was adservice's memory footprint before the cap? Without that number the cap may have been a non-event. Why does the radius reach seven services and critical severity, when frontend usually degrades gracefully if ads are unavailable? A shared upstream, an automation sweep touching more than one workload, or a second unrelated failure would all explain the spread better than a single ad backend.

The change lookback covered fifteen minutes rather than two hours; the interval from roughly two hours before onset up to T-10m is unexamined, and an earlier change with delayed effect is still live. No change history was pulled for the remaining three services in the radius or for shared infrastructure — the adservice limits edit may be one visible instance of a wider automated pass. The unmeasured edge was never identified, and the true propagation path may run through it. Finally, the direction of causation between the simultaneous frontend and loadgenerator alerts was never established, and it is unknown whether adservice alerted at all.

## Where the next responder should start

Two cheap moves first. Pull pod events, restart counts, and memory utilization for adservice spanning T-15m to T+15m — that single query either confirms or destroys the working conclusion. Second, re-run change history with the full two-hour lookback and widen it to all seven services in the radius plus shared infrastructure, specifically looking for other edits by the same automated actor. If the automation performed a sweep rather than a one-off, that explains the seven-service spread far better than anything currently in the record. Only after those two should the revert be treated as a diagnosis rather than a low-cost gamble.
