## 3. The finding — a stopped service was not drift

`cart-bad-image-tag` is injected by stopping the container **first** and then pointing compose at
`ghcr.io/open-telemetry/demo:v1.2.1-cartservice-hotfix.2`, a tag that resolves nowhere. Compose
resolves every image before touching any container, so the recreate fails and the world is left
with exactly one cartservice container: the old healthy one, stopped, wearing the declared image.
That is the fault — the service is dark — and it is the shape the scenario exists to teach.

The executor's drift check compared image, environment, memory limit and CPU quota, and **never
asked whether the service was running.** Every field matched. It refused `rollback_image` as
*unexecutable: no drift* — a correct reading of the fields it read, and the wrong answer, because a
declared service that is not running is the most basic drift a service can have. The agent's
proposal was right; the executor's model of the world was one field short.

**The fix**, in this PR: `DockerCli.running_definition` reads `State.Running` and reports a missing
container as `running: False, exists: False`; `ComposeCli.declared_definition` declares
`running: True` (compose has no vocabulary for "stopped"); `running` joins `DRIFT_FIELDS` for both
`rollback_image` and `revert_config`. `tests/test_executor.py` holds a stopped container and a
missing one as drift for both actions.

### 7.2 The proof, second attempt — one property demonstrated, then a correct refusal that crashed the driver

`faultline-replay --only shipping-wrong-image --proof`, 11:10 UTC. The triple executed at 11:15:42
and the replayed token, presented the same second, was refused as **`token c158d4b2… was already
spent by audit row c329551c…`** — the single-use property, demonstrated on the world as §4
pre-registered. Then the driver minted a fresh approval for the kill-switch check and the state
machine refused it: `executing -> awaiting_approval is not a transition this machine has`. **The
machine was right.** ADR-0028 §5 is *one proposal per incident, executed at most once*, ADR-0016's
table has no row for a second approval on an executing incident, and a token that could only ever
be refused is an approval the approver did not get. The driver, minting after the execution, was
wrong; and having crashed on its second check it recorded nothing about the third.

Two consequences, both in this PR. **The executor now enforces the rule itself**: an incident that
already has an executed (or errored) action refuses a second token at the single-use step — after
scope, so a wrong target is still refused as a wrong target — naming the first action and its
audit row. `faultline-approve` refuses to mint for an incident that is `EXECUTING` or terminal, and
is idempotent for one already `AWAITING_APPROVAL` (several tokens, each single-use, no second
transition). **The driver mints the proof's two extra tokens before the execution**, while the
incident is awaiting approval, and records a raising proof step as an `error` entry rather than
dying. The world was left clean: no active injection, the gate admitting. Partial evidence at
`docs/evidence/t6.2-first-execution/shipping-wrong-image.second-attempt/`.

### 7.4 The proof, third attempt — two properties shown on the world, and a wrong target that was in scope

`faultline-replay --only shipping-wrong-image --proof`, 22:25–22:38 UTC, on the corrected driver.
**The triple recovered**: executed at 22:31:03, alerts cleared at **120 s** of 300, the injector
had nothing left, and all three refusals were recorded in the same second the action was executed,
while the incident was `EXECUTING` — which is what §4's correction was for.

| check | presented | refused as | the property it was meant to show | shown? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| replayed token | the spent token again | *token 293f7ecc… was already spent by audit row 9f9c6712… (executed)* | single use, by the ledger | **yes** |
| kill switch | a fresh token, executor switched off | `kill_switch` — *nothing executes while it is. Investigation is unaffected.* | the switch precedes everything | **yes** |
| wrong target | a token for `paymentservice` | *incident … already had an action executed: `rollback_image` → shippingservice … One action per incident* | blast radius refuses a target outside the incident's scope | **no** |

Two of the pre-registered three held exactly as written. The third refused, and correctly, and for
the wrong reason — and the reason is a defect in the *driver*, not the executor.

**`paymentservice` was inside the incident's scope.** `shipping-wrong-image` breaks shippingservice;
checkoutservice, its synchronous caller, alerts with it. `ServiceGraph.blast_radius` takes one
downstream step from every seed — *naming where an error could have come from* — and
checkoutservice calls paymentservice. So the scope of this incident is
`{shippingservice, checkoutservice, frontend, quoteservice, emailservice, cartservice,
currencyservice, productcatalogservice, accountingservice, paymentservice}`, the token's target was
in it, the scope check passed, and §5's very next clause — one action per incident, added in §7.2 —
refused it. The executor applied its order faithfully; the demonstration asked the wrong question.
Choosing the driver's wrong target by intuition, rather than against the graph, is what made it
wrong.

**The fix, and what holds it.** The proof's wrong target is now `frauddetectionservice`, the one
service in the catalog reached only across the `async` edge from checkoutservice — and
`blast_radius` crosses `async` in neither direction, on the measured grounds ADR-0017 records
(frauddetection was dead for 852 seconds while checkout kept completing orders). A test computes
the blast radius of **every** service in the catalog snapshot at the configured hop radius and
asserts `frauddetectionservice` is in none of them, so the proof's target cannot quietly drift back
inside scope when the graph is recaptured.

**Two more driver corrections in this PR, both from this run.** Evidence directories are now named
by `--attempt <label>` (`shipping-wrong-image.third-attempt/`) instead of by hand after the fact —
the first two attempts kept their evidence only because the directory was renamed between runs —
and a run **refuses before it injects anything** if any directory it would write already holds
files. Captured evidence is added to, never rewritten; until now that was a habit, and now it is
the driver's behaviour.
