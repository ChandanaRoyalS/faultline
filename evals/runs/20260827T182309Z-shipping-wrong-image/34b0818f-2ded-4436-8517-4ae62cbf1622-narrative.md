# Checkout order placement failing on the shipping-quote edge after a shipping image update

## What was visible, and the first dead end

Three alerts landed together: checkoutservice, frontend, loadgenerator. The symptom was customer-facing — order-placement POSTs erroring at the frontend. Blast radius was scored at twelve services, which in retrospect was mostly downstream propagation rather than twelve independent problems. T+0 in this record is the platform-automation image change on shippingservice; alerts followed roughly T+2m to T+3m.

The first move was the obvious one: look for a deploy on the alerting service. It came back empty — no deploys, config edits, or flag flips recorded for checkoutservice in a window that brackets onset on both sides. That cleanly excluded a checkout rollout, an at-onset flag flip, and a still-rolling change causing trailing degradation. Keep the caveat with the dead end: only about fifteen minutes were examined, leaving the preceding ~fifty minutes unlooked-at, and the query covered checkoutservice alone with no direct dependencies.

Checkout metrics were real but unhelpful for localization: a variable error ratio across sixty-one points, peaking near a third of calls and touching zero. That ruled out total outage, ruled out a fully healthy checkout, and ruled out a scrape gap — but a one-third peak is also what one failing call in three looks like, and no latency, saturation, or per-dependency series came back.

Checkout logs were more useful, and the useful part was an absence. No error- or warning-severity lines at all. Early on, each placement was followed by payment confirmation, confirmation email, and a message write with an incrementing offset. From about T+2m30s onward only the placement lines remain; every post-placement step vanishes while placements keep arriving steadily through ~T+6m. That killed payment decline, email failure, message-write errors, a checkout crash, and a traffic drop as candidate stories. It also meant checkout would never name the culprit itself. Coverage gap: the result was truncated and roughly T-7m to T+2m30s was not returned, so onset relative to the change is not directly observed.

## Traces localized it, and the change explained it

Traces resolved in one step. Every failing checkout trace terminates identically: the checkoutservice client span for ShippingService/GetQuote is the only child marked ERROR, propagating up through PlaceOrder to the frontend POST. All failing traces abort inside prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart. Failing traces complete end to end in five to sixteen milliseconds and the failing GetQuote calls in 0.3 to 2.5 milliseconds — a fast refusal, not a stall or timeout.

The same traces cleared the neighbours: cart and its Redis HGET, product catalog including feature-flag lookups, and currency conversion all succeed and are fast inside the very traces that fail. Payment and email are never reached. Ad and recommendation services are not on the path. Frontend and checkout carry error status only as inherited propagation.

The change log for shippingservice returned exactly one entry: platform automation updated the container image reference at T+0, about two minutes before the alerts. No config edits, no flags, no human actor, no overlapping changes. Two details matter for the response — the new tag's suffix names a different service, and the prior image reference is recorded as absent, so no rollback target is established by the record itself.

Shipping logs closed it. At about T-7m the service was answering GetQuote and ShipOrder normally. From about T+21s through T+7m the stream contains nothing but repeated process-startup sequences — at least ten, clustered seven to thirty seconds apart at first, then settling into a near-exact sixty-four-second cadence: a restart loop in backoff. No request handling is logged after T+21s. No panic, exception, stack trace, or shutdown message precedes any boot, so the process dies without writing a terminal error. The pre-change lines are Rust-style; the post-change lines are JVM and Java-agent boot output — a runtime change across the change boundary, which is what a wrong artifact looks like. Shipping metrics returned nothing at all, not even an unfiltered denominator; consistent with a dead service, but an empty denominator also points at a selector or ingestion problem, so treat it as corroboration only.

Conclusion: the artifact running on shippingservice is not the one that should be running. Fix class is rollback. Confidence high.

## What is still open before you act

Confirm pod and container identity first. The shipping log stream mixes two apparently different runtimes under one selector, and the specialist explicitly warned this could be a label collision or a sidecar sharing the service label. If the restart loop belongs to a different pod, the wrong-artifact reading weakens substantially. This is the single check that most changes the response.

The restart cause is inferred, not observed. No exit codes, container termination reasons, out-of-memory records, or liveness-probe events were retrieved. An image that cannot start and an image killed for exceeding a limit are indistinguishable in the logs we have.

Recover a rollback target from deployment history — the change record does not name the prior image. Also unmeasured: no server-side shipping span for any failing GetQuote, so the exact failure mode on that edge (refused connection, name resolution, endpoint removal) is unknown; the shipping metric evidence cannot independently confirm the outage; checkout changes were never examined before roughly T-7m and checkout logs are missing a nine-minute stretch across onset; and nobody dispatched on whether the other nine services in the blast radius are independently affected or purely downstream of checkout.
