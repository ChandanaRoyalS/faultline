# Checkout orders failing at the cart hop

## What we saw first

The page came in on three services at once: frontend, loadgenerator, and checkoutservice. That triple is misleading in a useful way — it reads like a broad front-end outage, and the first instinct in the chair was to treat frontend as the origin and work down. Severity was called critical with a blast radius of twelve services, and four edges between services were crossed for which we hold no measurement at all, so the shape of the picture was never as complete as the alert count suggested.

The first look at frontend's own error ratio said something narrower than the alert did. Errors were real and attributed to frontend's own spans — not silently absorbed somewhere below — but they were intermittent, peaking around thirty percent of calls and touching zero at other points across the sampled window. So frontend was on the error surface but not hard down; a crashed instance or a route-wide break would have pinned the ratio near one. Equally, this was not a flat chronic floor or a metrics artifact: the series genuinely moved between zero and roughly a third within fifteen minutes.

## Narrowing to checkoutservice, then past it

Checkoutservice logs were the most quietly informative thing we read, and they nearly sent us the wrong way. Nothing at error or exception severity appeared — every returned line was info. Early in the window each PlaceOrder entry was followed by a clean completion sequence: payment confirmation with a transaction id, the order confirmation email, a message write with an offset. Then, from roughly the eleventh minute of the window onward, the completions stopped while the PlaceOrder entries kept arriving at broadly normal cadence. Orders accepted, nothing finished.

That pattern ruled out several plausible readings at once. The process was alive and ingress was intact, since entries kept landing. The break was not at the final publish or at email delivery, because even the payment confirmation line was missing during the degraded stretch — the failure sits upstream of both. It was not currency-specific: both USD and CAD entries stalled with no completions for either. And nothing was being rejected with a logged failure returned to the caller; the shape was silent non-completion.

That last inference is where the log evidence misleads, and it is worth flagging for whoever reads this next. Silent non-completion in logs looks like a hang. It was not a hang. Coverage was also thin — only the oldest handful and newest few dozen lines were retained, the middle of the window never came back, and the window ended earlier than the period we had asked about.

## The traces that settled the locus

Forty PlaceOrder traces came back and all forty had the same structure, terminating at the same place: frontend HTTP POST into frontend PlaceOrder, into checkoutservice PlaceOrder, into the order-item-and-shipping-quote preparation stage, into the outbound CartService/GetCart call. The cart call is the deepest and last span entered, and it is the innermost span carrying an error status. The error propagates outward unchanged through checkout and frontend, while the intermediate preparation span itself carries no error of its own — the signature of a downstream origin being surfaced rather than generated locally.

This corrected the log reading. Every span sits in single-digit milliseconds; cart calls return errors in roughly two to five milliseconds, root spans under about fifteen. There is no timeout-scale duration anywhere in the sample. It is a fast rejection, not a stall.

The traces also closed several candidate dependencies by absence. No payment span is ever entered, so paymentservice is off the failing path entirely. No shipping quote, product catalog, or currency conversion span appears either — the preparation stage dies at its first child, so those are never reached. No email, ad, or recommendation spans are implicated. Checkoutservice itself is exonerated as origin: its own spans add negligible time and its non-RPC internal span is clean. Frontend is a victim, not a source: its spans successfully create child checkout spans, and the error first appears at the deepest cart span.

One friction remains between the two views. Within this trace sample the failure is total — a hundred percent of sampled PlaceOrder traces error at the cart call, zero successes — while frontend's aggregate ratio peaks near thirty percent. The likely reconciliation is that non-checkout routes are still serving fine, but the frontend metric was aggregated by service name into a single series and cannot distinguish per-route or per-instance concentration. Treat that as unresolved, not eliminated.

## Dead ends worth keeping

We spent real effort on paymentservice before the traces made it irrelevant, and the results there are instructive about how not to read an empty query. A PromQL error-ratio expression scoped to paymentservice returned no matching series at all for the fifteen-minute window. The temptation is to call that meaningful. It is not: the unfiltered denominator — total call rate with no status filter — also matched nothing, so the emptiness is at least as consistent with the metric or label simply not existing as with any change in service behaviour. No latency figure was retrieved for paymentservice at all, and no peer dependency was ever queried, so the comparison across checkout's dependencies that we set out to make was never actually answered. The window sampled was also narrower than the interval of interest, missing roughly eighteen minutes of the leading edge.

We also chased the change-induced theory on two services and came away empty both times. Checkoutservice's change log records no deploys, config edits, or flag flips in a fifteen-minute window bracketing the onset by ten minutes on either side — enough to say no change coincident with onset was recorded for that entity, and enough to say there is no rollback target there. Paymentservice's change history was likewise empty for the same window. But both queries were fifteen minutes wide against a question that spanned roughly ninety, and both were scoped to a single entity, so the earlier period and all nine listed dependencies remain unexamined. The change avenue is not closed; it is only sampled.

## Where this stopped, and why the confidence is low

We have a locus and not a mechanism. The failing hop is checkoutservice's outbound GetCart call to cartservice, established from the trace structure and corroborated from the other side by checkout's logs, which show requests arriving and then producing nothing downstream of the entry line.

What we never did — and this is the single dispatch that would have converted locus into mechanism — is look at cartservice itself. Its logs, its metrics, and its change history were never queried. Not once. The only dependency metrics attempted were paymentservice's, and those came back uninterpretable.

Deterministic sub-five-millisecond rejection is consistent with a wrong backing-store address or credential, an unservable cart artifact, or a refused connection. The evidence in hand cannot choose among these. Naming one would mean importing an answer from a similar past incident rather than reading this one, so no fix class is offered.

Open for whoever picks this up: query cartservice's logs, metrics, and change history directly. Widen the change search beyond fifteen minutes and beyond a single entity — a deploy, config edit, or flag flip on cartservice or its backing store shortly before onset, or well before it, would not have appeared in anything we ran. And break frontend's error ratio down by route to confirm that the thirty-percent aggregate is checkout traffic failing completely against other paths succeeding.
