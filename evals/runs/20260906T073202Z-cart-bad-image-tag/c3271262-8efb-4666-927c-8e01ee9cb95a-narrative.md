# Checkout cart-fetch failures after a cartservice image swap

## What was visible first, and what misled

Three services paged: frontend, loadgenerator, checkoutservice. Severity critical, twelve services in radius from the frontend seed, four edges never measured.

The instinct was to look for an error surge at the paging services. There isn't one. Frontend's aggregate error ratio during the incident ran *lower* than the preceding baseline hour — about three-quarters of baseline mean, no sustained departure — and the series is equally spiky before and during, with brief excursions past 30% of calls in both. checkoutservice looked the same: roughly 0.08 against a 0.20 baseline, same maximum in both windows. Both queries aggregated by service name only, with no route or dependency dimension and no latency metric, so they rule out whole-service elevation but cannot see one broken route among many — which is what this was.

checkoutservice logs were only info-severity PlaceOrder entries, still arriving every few seconds to the end of the window, no restarts, no downstream failure messages. Two traps in that result: it kept only the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, so a mid-window segment was never returned (truncation, not a collection outage — the stream was dense at both ends), and entry lines carry no completion, so per-request outcome isn't recoverable there.

The change record for checkoutservice came back empty, which is weaker than it looks: it was scoped to the seed with zero hops, and its window ran forward from onset rather than back. It legitimately rules out a checkoutservice change at or after onset, and any logged remediation there.

## The line that turned it

Frontend's logs, not its metrics, carried the answer. gRPC code-14 UNAVAILABLE errors ran across the window, and at about T+2m a downstream-failure line named cartservice on the checkout cart-fetch path. The stated reason was name resolution: the cartservice name could not be resolved by the embedded resolver, so the call never got past dialling.

That one detail closes several doors. cartservice was not slow, overloaded, or erroring — no connection ever reached it. TLS, auth and protocol mismatch all come after name lookup. Every stack frame was gRPC client code with no application handler frames, so frontend is reporter, not origin. And cartservice is the only backend named.

Working backwards: cartservice's span-derived error metrics returned nothing in the incident window *or* in a baseline three hours earlier — a missing or mislabelled series, not telemetry dying at onset. Do not read that empty graph as a dead process. Its logs did the work: steady cart operations with no degradation until roughly T-3m, then two hosting-lifetime lines recording an orderly shutdown and nothing for the rest of the window. Orderly matters — no stack trace, no truncated request — so a controlled or signalled stop, not an abrupt kill. A minor oddity, two request lines with empty user ids, went nowhere.

## The change record, and what is still open

Twenty-four changes against cartservice in the preceding day, all from the same automation actor — no human-initiated entry, so no ad-hoc operator push. The change nearest onset, one second after the shutdown line, was an image-reference update to a hotfix tag. The replacement never became resolvable again. That is the conclusion, and the fix class is rollback.

Two attractive dead ends sit in the same record. An environment override pointing cartservice at redis-cart on a non-default port was reverted about 56 minutes before onset and never re-applied. A sidecar imposing fixed egress delay on the cart-service namespace was removed about 1.2 hours before onset. Neither was in effect. No scaling or capacity change appears, and no service or DNS-name change. Note also that the same image tag was applied and reverted at least six times in the window — this is a recurrence, not a one-off release.

Confidence is medium for three reasons. Frontend code-14 errors already appear roughly twenty minutes before onset, naming no backend — same condition with earlier onset, or unrelated spiky behaviour? Resolving that decides whether the image change is the cause or the last of a series. No container status, restart count, or image-pull result was collected for the hotfix tag, so whether it failed to pull, failed to start, or failed health checks is unknown. And no direct name-resolution or service-discovery check was ever run; eight services in the radius and four edges were never dispatched against.
