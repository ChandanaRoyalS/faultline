# Cart unreachable after automation re-applied hotfix image; checkout and frontend errors follow

## What we saw first

The page came from four places at once — checkout, frontend, frontend-proxy and load-generator — which immediately suggested something shared rather than something local. Blast radius was recorded as thirteen services, with checkout named as the seed. The first useful shape came from checkout's error ratio: flat at exactly zero across thirty-two baseline samples through the half hour before onset, then non-zero, with the first threshold crossing at 20:13. That is an abrupt edge, not a slow burn. Worth noting for the next responder: the mean error ratio through the window was only around 4.5%, with a minimum still touching zero and a peak near two-thirds. Checkout was never fully down. If you are hunting a hard outage on checkout you will not find one.

## Dead end: checkout's own logs

The obvious next move was to read checkout's logs. The selector returned nothing — not zero errors, zero lines of any level, and not just during the incident but across the entire window including the quiet baseline. Because the silence is uniform, it is not evidence of anything happening at onset. Three explanations remain indistinguishable from this result alone: a label mismatch, a stalled ingestion path, or a service that genuinely writes nothing to this stream. We spent time here; it yielded no signal in either direction. Treat checkout's log channel as unavailable until someone fixes or re-labels it.

## Dead end: traces, and two red herrings inside them

The trace query for checkout returned ten traces but surfaced only four, and every one of those started between T-14m and T-13m relative to onset — all pre-incident. So the sample names no culprit for the failing period. It did establish something useful negatively: in those healthy traces the PlaceOrder path completes in roughly 11-41ms, with cart, product-catalog, currency, payment, shipping, email and flagd all answering in single-digit milliseconds and no errored spans. Nothing was smouldering beforehand.

Two things in that sample will waste your time if you let them. First, the quote subtree appears with offsets about 6.8 hours before the trace root while its own duration is a fraction of a millisecond — that is host clock skew, not a stall; the parent shipping spans finish in 1-3ms. Second, one trace shows the quote subtree detached from its parent and a checkout HTTP POST carrying all its time as self-time with no child recorded. That is a span-linkage artifact in a trace that completed in under 100ms. Neither is related to the incident.

## Dead end: blaming checkout itself

The change history for checkout came back empty. Read it carefully before leaning on it: the query was scoped to the seed service only, hops=0, so it says nothing about cart, payment, currency, product-catalog, shipping, ad, recommendation, email or product-reviews. Worse, the window it covered actually begins at the reference point and runs forward roughly twenty-four hours, so a change immediately before onset would have fallen outside it entirely. What it does support is that no checkout deployment, config edit or flag change occurred during or after the incident window, which rules out ongoing churn on checkout as a prolonging factor. It does not rule out a pre-onset checkout change — nobody looked in the right window for that.

A second metric pull over a wider span also looked like it cleared checkout: the tool reported 'no sustained departure.' That verdict was computed against a baseline hour that returned no samples at all. It is an artifact of missing data, not a finding of health. Do not move off a service on the strength of a no-departure verdict with an empty baseline.

## The turn: frontend's logs name an address

The break came from frontend. Every returned line in its window is a client-side gRPC transport failure dialing a single peer at 172.18.0.19:7070 — the cart port — with message text attributing it to the cart-retrieval step of checkout. No other downstream appears.

The failure mode evolves, and the evolution is the diagnosis. Around T+0 (≈20:12) the errors are code 13 wrapping an Unavailable transport error from a dial timeout. By ≈20:15:41 and onward they are code 14 UNAVAILABLE stating that no connection was ever established, with the underlying socket error host-unreachable, repeating several times per second across multiple request paths. That eliminates a whole family of hypotheses in one pass: it is not a frontend bug (every frame is a propagated downstream client error), not cart returning application errors or overload responses (no response arrives at all), not name resolution (the target is already a literal IP), and not a handshake or authorization rejection (the failure is at TCP connect, before any exchange). It is also not a pre-existing condition — the oldest retained frontend lines for the full window begin at 20:12:18, nothing before.

## The change that lines up

Cart's change history holds nine entries in the queried span, all attributed to the actor platform-automation, and all of them image-reference updates or reverts of one hotfix tag — no config edits, no flag flips, no human actor anywhere. Eight of those nine form a flapping pattern of roughly 7-11 minute update/revert cycles confined to a morning window about eleven hours before onset, followed by a long quiet stretch.

The ninth is the one that matters: the 2.2.0-cart-hotfix.2 image reference re-applied to cart at 20:11:36, roughly a minute before frontend's first dial timeout and about ninety seconds before checkout's first error crossing. It is the only change temporally adjacent to onset. The morning churn is not the trigger — it ends hours earlier with a change-free gap behind it. Note also that the re-applied tag is not new code; it is the same tag rolled forward and back five times that morning.

## Where it stands and what nobody checked

Working conclusion, medium confidence: cart stopped accepting connections around T+0, roughly a minute after automation re-applied the hotfix image reference. The caller-side signature — dial timeouts escalating to host-unreachable with no connection ever established — is what you see when the container behind that address is no longer running. Fix class is rollback.

Three honest gaps. First, nobody queried cart directly. Its logs, container state and restart counts were never retrieved; the deploy-to-failure link rests entirely on caller-side evidence plus a change record. Second, the underlying digest behind the tag is unknown, and host-unreachable is equally consistent with a container that failed to start and with a connectivity cut at the host level — that is unsettled. Third, no per-dependency error breakdown exists for checkout: both metric queries aggregate by service_name only, with no peer or operation label, so dependency attribution from metrics is structurally impossible and would need a re-query grouped by peer attributes. Nine of the thirteen services in the blast radius were never examined at all.
