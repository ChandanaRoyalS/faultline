# Checkout errors traced to an unreachable cart endpoint

## What paged, and what the first look showed

The page came in on checkout, with frontend, frontend-proxy and load-generator alerting alongside it. Thirteen services were inside the blast radius and the walk from checkout crossed thirteen edges that had never been measured before, so almost nothing in the surrounding graph had a known-good shape to compare against.

The first useful thing was checkout's own error ratio. For the full hour of baseline before onset it was not merely low, it was exactly zero across 249 samples — min, max and standard deviation all zero. That matters because it removes the most common red herring: there was no pre-existing error floor that a threshold or alert-rule edit could have newly exposed. During the event the ratio averaged roughly 8% with peaks near 67% and a standard deviation larger than the mean. That is bursty, not a plateau.

Two further details from the same series reshaped the timeline. The first threshold crossing was about 38 minutes before the page (call it T-38m against a page at T+0). And there were two distinct crossings with a dip back below threshold between them, meaning the condition recovered and recurred rather than ramping monotonically into the alert. Anyone starting their search at the page time is looking in the wrong half-hour.

## Dead ends, in the order we hit them

Change history on checkout was queried twice. The first attempt used a window that started at the incident timestamp and ran forward 24 hours — it returned nothing, but it also did not answer the question we were actually asking, since it covered the period during and after onset rather than the hours before it. The second query was corrected to span the 24 hours preceding and enclosing the first crossing. Also empty. No deploys, no config pushes, no flag flips recorded against checkout in that period. Both queries were scoped to the seed service only, with no dependency hops, so this clears checkout's own change surface and nothing else.

Sampled traces from checkout were the next dead end, and an expensive one. Four of ten matching traces came back; none contained an error-status span anywhere in the tree, and root durations ran a modest 58ms to 172ms — these look like normal completions. Against that sample we were able to rule out payment (server spans consistently sub-5ms), product-catalog and its SQL children (under ~5ms, mostly sub-millisecond), shipping and quote (under ~7ms end to end), currency (around 1ms), and cart's Redis operations plus flag evaluation (single-digit milliseconds, resolution sub-millisecond). In three of four traces the dominant time was self-time in the load-generator root span, meaning no backend dominated at all.

The one thing that looked like a lead here was email: in the slowest sampled trace the order-confirmation send accounted for roughly a fifth of total trace time and was flagged as the degrading hop, though in the other traces the same path cost a few milliseconds. We chased it. Email's error-ratio query returned zero samples in both the incident window and the baseline window — not small values, no values. Because both windows are equally empty, the absence predates the event and is most consistent with email emitting no span metrics at all, rather than with a change in behaviour. No latency query was run against email, so that half of the question remains formally unanswered, but the error-ratio angle is closed: there is no change point at T-38m and no recovery edge at T-2m to correlate with. Email was a distraction.

Also worth recording: checkout's logs. A query for all lines under the checkout label across the whole window returned nothing of any level. Not an error-free log, an empty one. A healthy service still emits access and info lines, so this is either a collection or labeling break or a fully stopped process — and either way it means no distinctive error text or stack trace from checkout is available from this source. Do not spend time looking for it under that label.

## The turn: frontend's outbound calls

The answer came from frontend's logs rather than checkout's. On checkout paths, frontend's gRPC client was failing against the cart endpoint at 172.18.0.24 port 7070, surfacing as INTERNAL (13) wrapping an Unavailable transport error and as UNAVAILABLE (14) with host-unreachable connect failures. The stack frames are frontend's server bundle wrapping promise-based gRPC clients — frontend is the caller, and the error is in its outbound RPC, not in its own request handling.

The mode shifts across the window. Around T-4m to T-3m the underlying cause is a dial failure to the cart IP — no route to host. By T+2m the same call fails earlier still, with name resolution returning zero addresses for the cart target, alongside continued host-unreachable errors citing a cached connect failure. Errors recur over seconds right up to the end of the query window, so the condition was still live when we stopped looking.

That sequence rules out two tempting simplifications in opposite directions. It is not purely a service-discovery problem: the earlier errors resolve the name to a concrete address and fail at connect, so resolution was working then. And it is not purely an address-level routing problem: by the end, resolution itself returns nothing.

With this, checkout reclassifies from suspect to victim. Its bursty ~8% error ratio with a recovery in the middle is exactly what an intermittently unreachable dependency produces at a caller.

## What it was not

Three explanations are positively excluded by the transport-layer evidence, and are worth stating so nobody re-opens them.

It is not an application-level bug or bad response inside cart or checkout code. The connection was never established, or the target resolved to no addresses — nothing reached cart's application logic.

It is not cart being up but overloaded. There are no deadline-exceeded or timeout codes anywhere. The codes are Unavailable and host-unreachable, which is a reachability condition, not latency saturation.

It is not a TLS, certificate or authorization rejection at the cart endpoint. No handshake, certificate or permission-denied codes appear; the failures occur before any session with the peer exists.

And it is not a frontend-side crash or memory problem — every logged exception is a client error about an external endpoint, with frontend frames appearing only as the call site.

## Still open when the record was written

Confidence here is medium, and three gaps are the reason.

First, nobody ever queried cart directly. Its logs, its metrics and its pod and endpoint state are all unexamined. That leaves the central question unsettled: was cart running but cut off at the network layer, or was the process gone (crashed or evicted)? The zero-address resolution result is consistent with both — endpoints get withdrawn when a pod dies, and they also get withdrawn when a pod is deemed unhealthy while still running. This is the first thing a responder should do.

Second, checkout emits spans but zero log lines in both windows, and that gap is itself unexplained. We never got checkout's own account of the cart failure. Worth resolving independently, because a telemetry break that hides a service during an event is its own problem.

Third, change history was only ever run at seed scope on checkout. Changes to cart, or to the network and mesh layer, in the hours before onset at T-38m were never queried at all. That is the most likely place the trigger is recorded, and it is untouched ground.

The indicated fix class is a reconnect: restore reachability to the cart endpoint and let callers re-establish. That addresses the symptom; it does not identify what withdrew the endpoint in the first place.
