# ad loses name resolution to its telemetry collector; metric gaps mistaken for an outage

## What the board showed at the start

Three services alerted together at T+0: ad, frontend-proxy, and load-generator. Severity was called critical with a blast radius of five services, and the investigation was dispatched starting from ad. Only ad was actually worked. That matters when you read what follows — frontend-proxy and load-generator never got a dispatch, and nothing found later explains why they fired.

The first instinct, reasonable at the time, was that ad had broken and the breakage had spread upward through its callers. Most of the subsequent work was spent discovering that the evidence for that story is thinner than it looks.

## First pass on ad's error ratio, and the trap in it

By roughly T+5m we had ad's errored-span ratio against a four-hour prior baseline. It was boringly flat: mean around 0.45% in the window versus 0.46% before, peak slightly lower than baseline peak, and the spread actually narrowed. On that reading ad was not the failing service and was not creeping toward a threshold.

The one odd detail was twenty sampling intervals with no defined ratio at all — intervals where the denominator was empty — against none in the baseline. At the time this was read as a positive signal that ad had periods of no traffic, i.e. that ad was intermittently unavailable. Hold that thought; it is the single most misleading reading in the record.

A second baseline run over a wider, earlier six-hour comparison told a noisier story: ratio roughly doubled, a change point flagged near 01:05Z, and seventy-two undefined intervals instead of twenty. That pulled the investigation hard toward a restart/unavailability hypothesis. It should not have. Both runs returned only the error-ratio series. Request rate, latency, container memory, and restart counts were never measured in any window. The restart theory rested entirely on the shape of a gap in one derived metric.

## The log read that reframed everything

Around T+12m we pulled ad's logs across the full window. Early lines are unremarkable: baggage-absent notices and targeted ad requests for assorted categories, no warnings, no errors. By the end of the window the stream is almost entirely OpenTelemetry Java agent span-export failures, each with a full stack trace through the OkHttp exporter path.

The failure point is specific and it is early in the call: the exporter cannot resolve the collector hostname. It dies at the DNS lookup, before any socket is opened and before any handshake. This recurs over at least several seconds near T+1m and does not clear.

Four things this closes. The collector is not rejecting, erroring, timing out, or failing auth — nothing ever reached it. There are no JVM startup banners, no graceful-shutdown lines, and no AdService-level exceptions, so the process did not crash and restart. The only exception type present originates in the telemetry exporter thread pool, not the request path. And missing telemetry on the dashboards does not, by itself, mean ad stopped serving.

## Re-reading the gaps

With the log in hand, the undefined metric intervals stop being evidence of an outage. ad's span metrics travel through the same collector the exporter cannot resolve. The most economical reading is that those spans were never delivered, not that ad refused requests. That reverses the earlier conclusion drawn from the six-hour comparison.

The cross-check that supports this: frontend's errored-span ratio is present and identically zero for every sample in both the incident window and the preceding half hour. The series is zero-valued, not absent, so the metrics pipeline was accepting frontend spans the whole time. The collector was reachable from frontend. Whatever broke resolution broke it for ad specifically.

This also closed a sequencing line we had open: there is no frontend error rise anywhere in the window, so frontend cannot have preceded ad as the originating service, and ad's trouble did not surface as errored spans at frontend either.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Three change-history queries were run — two against ad, one against flagd — and all three came back empty. No deployments, no image-tag moves, no resource-limit edits, no config pushes, no flag edits. That removes a deploy, a bad build, a tightened memory limit, a config push, and in-incident flag churn as drivers, and it also means no logged remediation produced any apparent recovery.

The caveat a future responder must not skip: all three queries ran forward from T+0 for about twenty-four hours, not backward. They answer "did anything change during or after onset" and they answer it cleanly. They do not answer "what changed in the day before onset," which is what was actually asked. That interval remains unexamined.

Even so, a mistyped endpoint value is a poor fit for the mechanism: the same process was exporting successfully earlier in the same window, so the configured hostname was working and then stopped working.

## Where it landed

The only positive failure signal anywhere on the board is in ad's own log: the telemetry agent cannot resolve the collector hostname, persistently, failing before any connection attempt. The process is alive, its exporter threads are still running, and its request-handling lines are clean. ad is up and saying it cannot reach what it needs. Confidence medium. The fix class is reconnect — restore name resolution for ad and let the exporter re-establish.

## Open when the record closed

Whether ad was in fact still serving requests. Treating the metric gaps as an export artifact is inference built on the log, not an observation. Request rate, latency, memory, and restart counts were never measured. Measure them first if this recurs.

Why frontend-proxy and load-generator alerted at T+0. Neither was dispatched. frontend's zero errored spans does not account for either alert, and nothing in this record does.

Whether ad's loss of name resolution extends beyond the collector hostname to its own upstream dependencies. Only the exporter path was observed failing. If resolution is broken more broadly, the clean early request logs are a coincidence of timing rather than evidence of health.
