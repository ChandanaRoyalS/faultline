# Unreadable product data behind catalog lookups: NOT_FOUND storms reaching checkout

## What was visible, in order

The page came from the edge: frontend-proxy alerted first, then frontend, checkout, recommendation, product-catalog and the load-generator — fourteen services in the blast radius, critical. The proxy being loudest invited an edge-or-routing theory. Its numbers did not support one: error-classified spans roughly doubled (mean ~0.045 to ~0.101, peaking near 40%), but its baseline was already a few percent non-zero and minima still touched zero. The proxy was reporting, not originating.

The locating signal was product-catalog's error ratio. Flat at exactly zero across the prior two hours — 488 samples, no variance — then a single change point at 02:17:46 crossing threshold at ~0.12 and peaking near 0.34. Call that T+0. That one series ruled out a pre-existing floor, a slow earlier ramp, a total outage (peak well below 1.0), and a pure latency story (these were genuine error statuses, not slow successes). No request-rate, latency or saturation series was ever retrieved for product-catalog; that dimension remains unmeasured.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Around T+2m we tried to let product-catalog name its own problem. An unfiltered query over an hour-plus around onset returned nothing — not no errors, no lines of any level. That silence is ambiguous: it fits a service emitting nothing, and equally fits logs not reaching or not being labelled under that identifier. It kept product-catalog in scope rather than clearing it, but onset could not be timestamped from its own output. The work moved to traces.

Three change-history queries followed, twice on product-catalog and once on flagd. All three came back empty. Read the windows before relying on this: each begins at 02:18:46 and runs forward twenty-four hours, so they cover the period after onset only. They legitimately exclude an in-window deploy, flag flip or config edit, and exclude a quiet rollback explaining any recovery. They say nothing about the hours before 02:17:46 — precisely when bad rows would have been written. That is the largest gap in this record.

Also not relevant: the database (the sql.conn.query child of every failing lookup returned clean in single-digit ms); timeouts and saturation (failures were 0.5–4.3ms immediate rejections); cart, currency, shipping, payment, recommendation and flagd (reached-and-fine or not reached at all); and the single-bad-row theory, killed by five distinct product ids.

## Mechanism, conclusion, and what is still open

Traces settled it. Every product-catalog error span is on the GetProduct RPC with 'Product Not Found: <id>'. Its only child, sql.conn.query, never carries ERROR and completes in 0.4–4.1ms — the store answered promptly, and the failure was produced inside product-catalog afterwards. Two callers raise it: frontend (browse, add-to-cart) and checkout, where it surfaces as a PlaceOrder failure naming the same id. Upstream spans carry ERROR only as inherited status with no distinct exception, which is how we knew proxy and frontend were not the source. ListProducts via recommendation kept succeeding, so the service was not down.

The frontend's log named the mechanism at 02:20:39–42: gRPC code 5 NOT_FOUND for specific ids, repeating for the same id within about a second, and separately a code 2 UNKNOWN wrapping a peer INTERNAL raised while scanning a database row because the description column returned NULL. A reachable, responsive store whose contents cannot be read correctly. Fix class: restore_data. Confidence medium.

Still open: (1) the pre-02:17 interval — a deploy, migration or data-loading job on product-catalog — was never sampled and is the likeliest origin; (2) why product-catalog logged nothing for over an hour; (3) whether the per-id NOT_FOUND and the NULL-scan failure are one condition or two, which the log analysis explicitly declined to merge. Note also the frontend log was truncated across 01:18:51–02:20:39, so the 02:17–02:19 onset minutes were never characterized from logs, and an earlier 01:18 EHOSTUNREACH on port 7070 plus a resolver returning zero addresses remain unaccounted for.
