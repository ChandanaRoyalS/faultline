# Checkout outage traced to an unreachable cart endpoint

## What a responder saw first

Pages arrived from four places almost at once: frontend, frontend-proxy, checkout, and the load-generator. That spread is misleading on arrival — it reads like a broad platform failure across thirteen services, when in practice it is one failure reflected up a single call chain. Treat the four alerts as one signal.

The cleanest early orientation came from error-ratio curves rather than logs. frontend's error ratio sat at exactly zero for the full thirty-two minute baseline — zero mean, zero variance, 128 samples — then stepped up at T+1m and peaked near 30% of calls. checkout's curve did the same thing one minute later at T+3m, crossing straight past the alerting threshold to about 13% and climbing to roughly 67% at peak. Both are steps, not ramps. That shape is worth internalising: it rules out the slow-leak and creeping-saturation stories before you spend any time on them, and it bounds onset to within a couple of minutes of the first page rather than somewhere back in the quiet half hour.

Note also that checkout never went fully dark — a third of its spans still succeeded at the worst point. If you are reasoning about whether the checkout process itself died, that number says no.

## The trace that settled it

Sampled checkout traces are where the picture collapsed to a single answer. Ten traces came back from the window, clustered between roughly T+3m and T+4m, and all ten failed identically: PlaceOrder errors wrapping a cart-retrieval failure, and the only downstream span carrying an error is the GetCart child. No spans for payment, shipping, currency, product-catalog, email or product-reviews appear anywhere — checkout aborts on its first dependency call and never gets far enough to touch them. That absence is evidence, not a gap.

The failing GetCart spans last between zero and two tenths of a millisecond and contribute essentially nothing to trace duration. This is a fast failure, not a timeout, and that distinction kills the entire latency line of inquiry. The status is client-side gRPC UNAVAILABLE arising from a resolver that returned zero addresses — the call never reached a cart instance at all, so cart's request handling is not implicated; its availability to the discovery layer is. Both checkout variants, single and multi, fail the same way, so there is no intermittency or variant-specific path to chase. The ERROR status visible on frontend, frontend-proxy and the load-generator is inherited; those spans have small self-times and are carrying someone else's failure.

## What frontend's logs added, and the mismatch inside them

frontend's own log stream shows repeated gRPC code-14 UNAVAILABLE errors, all naming one peer: 172.18.0.19 on port 7070. The stack frames sit in the server bundle, so these are server-side calls out of frontend, not browser-side noise. The errors are sustained across roughly T+0 through T+5m, not a blip.

The interesting detail is that the socket errno changes partway through. The earliest lines carry ECONNREFUSED — something is at that address but nothing is listening. The latest lines carry EHOSTUNREACH with a pinned sub-error timestamp around T+4m — the address is no longer routable at all. Read in order, that is a process going away and then its address being withdrawn.

Worth flagging the seam honestly: frontend's evidence names only an address, while checkout's evidence names only a logical service with a resolver returning nothing. No endpoint or DNS mapping was ever retrieved to join the two. The conclusion that 172.18.0.19:7070 is cart is an inference from timing and topology, not something this record proves. A responder repeating this should pull that mapping first — it is a cheap query and it either locks the story down or breaks it open.

Several plausible hypotheses die on this log alone: it is not DNS (a concrete peer IP was already resolved and the failure is at connect), not TLS or authorization (the failure precedes any handshake), not an application-level error from a live dependency (no handler was ever reached), and not a defect inside frontend itself.

## Dead ends and queries that did not earn their keep

These are the parts of the investigation that produced nothing, and knowing that in advance is the point of writing them down.

Change history on checkout was queried twice, by two different paths, and both came back empty. That sounds like a clean exoneration and it partly is — there was no checkout deploy, config edit or flag toggle at any point in the roughly twenty-four hours covered. But both queries share two defects that make them nearly worthless for the actual question. First, the window starts at or after onset and runs forward a day; the hours *before* the failure, where a triggering change would live, were never looked at. Second, the scope was the seed service only, zero dependency hops, so cart — the service everything else points at — was never examined for changes at all. Two results, same blind spot, duplicated effort.

checkout's log stream returned zero lines for the entire window. Not zero errors: zero lines of any severity. A live service emitting span metrics should be producing routine informational output, so the more likely reading is a collection-side problem — shipping stopped, a label renamed or mismatched, an ingest gap — rather than a genuinely quiet service. This may be entirely unrelated to the incident and should be chased separately. The practical consequence for the responder is that checkout's own logs cannot carry the investigation; traces and upstream logs have to.

Also not retrieved, and still missing: checkout request rate, latency, and any per-dependency error breakdown. Only the aggregate ratio was pulled.

## Where this landed, and what is still unknown

The cart service stopped accepting connections around T+1m and its endpoint left service discovery. Checkout's PlaceOrder flows abort on the first hop; frontend sees the same peer go from refusing connections to being unreachable. Everything else in the blast radius is downstream reflection. The fix class is a restart of cart.

Confidence is medium, and the reason is specific: there is no cart-side evidence of any kind in this record. Cart logs, memory and restart metrics, and cart change history were never queried. Those three would distinguish between a crash-looping deploy, an out-of-memory termination, and a deliberate stop — three different remediations with the same observed symptom. The *location* of the failure is established well; the *mechanism* is not established at all.

If you are picking this up again, the ordered shortlist is: map 172.18.0.19:7070 to a service to confirm the identification; pull cart logs and restart/memory metrics across the onset; re-run change history for cart and its neighbours over the hours *preceding* onset rather than following it; and separately investigate why checkout produces no logs, since that is likely an observability defect that will cost someone else time in a future incident.
