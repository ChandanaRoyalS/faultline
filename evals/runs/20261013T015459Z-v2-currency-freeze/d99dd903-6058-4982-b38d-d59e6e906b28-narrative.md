# Currency goes quiet on checkout's hot path: silence with a reachable socket

## What was visible, in order

The page arrived wide: ten services in scope, seven alerting directly, currency named as the origin, and seven edges between origin and alerting services carrying no measurement at all. That gap shaped the whole investigation.

First move was currency's own logs across the alert window. Nothing came back — not errors-absent, but zero lines of any severity, including at onset. That killed three theories at once: no application exception text naming a dependency, no visible crash loop (which would leave startup banners or panic traces in bursts), and no possibility of reading the cause straight off currency's output. The caveat recorded at the time still stands: zero lines is equally consistent with a silent process and with a broken or mislabeled shipping path.

Second move was span-derived metrics for currency, incident window against baseline. Both empty. Read this carefully — it is not a traffic-loss signature, because the pre-onset baseline is equally empty, so the absence predates the event. It is also not evidence of health; a 'no departure' verdict computed from zero samples on both sides describes missing data, not a flat line. Step-versus-ramp onset shape is simply unanswerable here.

## The dead ends worth keeping

Suspicion moved sideways to the flag-evaluation service, on the theory that a configuration reload had broken evaluations. It went nowhere. Its error ratio ran roughly 0.3% in-window against 0.17% baseline — a sub-2x shift at tiny absolute values, judged no sustained departure — with full continuous sample coverage across the onset minute. That rules out the flag plane as origin, rules out a crash or restart loop there, and rules out an error-producing reload. One limit: this was a single error-ratio query, with no uptime or reload-event series, so a silent non-error reload is not excluded.

We then queried the change log for both currency and the flag service. Both empty — no deployments, no config edits, no rollouts, so no change to roll back or blame, and no tracked remediation to credit for recovery. But the important finding is methodological and is the largest hole in this record: both queries started at the onset timestamp and ran twenty-four hours forward. They cover the aftermath, not the lead-up. The hours before onset were never searched.

Finally, traces. Currency appears in every displayed trace as a callee of a checkout-side gRPC convert call, completing in roughly 0.3–1.8ms, entirely self-time, with no children. Checkout invokes it up to four times per order block, so it is hot by call count but negligible by duration; dominant latency sits in the load-generator root span. That rules out currency as the latency source, rules out it blocking on a downstream, and bounds the fan-out — checkout was the only direct parent in the sample. Limits: ten traces matched, four rendered, no per-span status codes shown, and none confirmed to fall inside the onset minute.

## Where we landed and what is still open

Working conclusion: currency stopped producing observable output at onset while its socket stayed reachable — no logs, no metric series, no recorded change, flag plane healthy. Sitting on checkout's hot path and called repeatedly per order, a hop that accepts connections but never answers stalls checkout and fans out to the seven alerting services. Silence plus reachability points at a stalled process rather than an error-producing failure, which puts the fix class at restart.

Confidence is low. The trace evidence genuinely contradicts this: sampled currency spans complete sub-2ms, and no sampled data is confirmed to cover the onset minute. Worse, checkout's own log stream is also entirely empty over the same half-hour window — anomalous for a request-serving service and pointing at a collection- or labeling-side gap rather than a quiet application. Window framing is not the explanation; the query spanned sixteen minutes either side. Two adjacent services with wholly empty streams is a plausible shared-pipeline problem, which weakens currency's silence considerably as evidence.

Open: is currency's emptiness real silence or a broken shipping path, given checkout shows the same; do any traces fall inside the onset window specifically, and do currency spans there carry error status or unbounded duration; what happened in the hours before onset, which no change query actually covered; and was checkout hanging on currency, or is checkout itself the culprit with currency merely quiet.
