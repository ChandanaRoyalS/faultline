# Cart Redis calls held at a fixed sub-second floor

## What the page looked like

Four services paged together — cartservice, loadgenerator, checkoutservice and frontend — with a blast radius of twelve services at warning severity. Nothing was down. The pages were latency-shaped, not error-shaped, and four edges between the alerting services were unmeasured, so topology alone could not say where the time went. The useful framing for a later reader: ask what got slow and by exactly how much, not what broke.

## Two early dead ends on cartservice

The error-ratio query for cartservice returned nothing — not zeros, nothing — in both the incident window and the preceding baseline. This briefly looked like a service going quiet at onset; it is not, because the baseline was equally empty. The series was simply never populated for this service name, and the tooling's "no departure from baseline" reads like health while meaning absence. Do not re-range it.

The cart log stream was similarly unhelpful but did close doors. Only routine info-level get/add/empty lines, no errors, no startup banners, no backing-store messages, with the service demonstrably serving at both edges of the window. That excluded image-pull failure, crash-looping, and an outright store outage. The caveat worth carrying: the result was truncated to the oldest and newest lines, so the minutes containing onset were never actually returned.

## Checkout: real symptom, no attribution

Checkoutservice's span error ratio ran roughly twenty times its baseline mean with peaks near 6%, but spiky rather than sustained — standard deviation nearly twice the mean, a minimum of zero, no flagged sustained departure. That makes checkout-tier failures real while excluding a hard-down mandatory dependency, which would have pinned the ratio far higher. Time was lost trying to read a culprit out of this series; it aggregates by service name only, with no downstream peer dimension, so it cannot attribute anything.

Checkout's own logs returned zero error lines and complete order flows at both ends of the window, with the queue offset advancing by about 280. Payment, email and the order-publish path are all excluded, and no checkout line names any downstream in a failure context.

## Traces: where the time actually was

Ten sampled traces settled it. All completed cleanly — no error-marked spans, no deadline terminations, no truncated subtrees. In every one, effectively all the time sat in a single Redis leaf span, HGET or HMSET, at roughly 300-311ms of self time, while the enclosing cartservice gRPC handler showed under a millisecond of its own work. Reads and writes were hit identically, excluding anything write-specific. The shape matters more than the size: a tight band with no fast requests underneath, a floor rather than a tail, which is the signature of an interposed egress wait rather than server-side contention.

The cost multiplies per round trip. AddItem runs HGET then HMSET serially and the frontend follows with a GetCart, giving ~910-925ms user-facing POSTs; PlaceOrder absorbs two cart calls inside a ~640-690ms root. Every other checkout dependency in those same traces was single-digit milliseconds, shipping the largest at 10-17ms. Span trees were shallow with no retries, and frontend and checkout self times were a few milliseconds — they were only waiting.

## The change log: right artifact, wrong timing

Eight cartservice changes are recorded, all by platform-automation, clustered between roughly 2.9 and 1.3 hours before onset, forming four matched apply/revert pairs: two hotfix image-tag updates, a REDIS_ADDR override, and a traffic-shaping container on the cart-service network namespace applying a fixed egress delay. The repetition reads as automated exercise activity, not a rollout.

Read strictly, the log excludes all four: every application was reverted, the shaping container about 1.6h and the env override about 1.3h before onset, with no change activity at all in the final 1.3h and no human actor anywhere in the window. Read against the traces, the likelier account is that the shaping rule persisted in the pod's network namespace after the container was detached, or was re-applied without a log entry. That reconciliation is inference, not observation — hence medium confidence, with a config revert as the fix class.

## Left open

Nobody inspected the live pod's network namespace or its traffic rules, so whether the shaping actually stopped when the container was detached remains unverified — that is the first thing to check on a recurrence. Onset is unattributed: the trace window has no pre-onset comparison, so the ~300ms floor may well predate the declared start and imply a different trigger. And the Redis instance itself was never queried, so a genuinely slow server cannot be excluded on its own telemetry, only inferred against by the flatness of the distribution.
