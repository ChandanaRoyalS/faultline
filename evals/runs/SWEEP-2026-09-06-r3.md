## 1. The finding: the disagreement has one cause, and it is not the model reasoning differently

The sweep was built to measure *how often a scenario changes its answer*. It does — three of five
changed. But the runs that changed are not runs that read the same evidence and drew a different
conclusion. **They are runs that never collected the evidence.**

Of the fifteen verdicts, five declined to name a fault class. **All five say, in their own words,
that no dispatch ever queried the service they had already correctly named.**

| run | scenario | the verdict's own words |
|---|---|---|
| `20260906T053012Z` | `ad-memory-squeeze` | *"no adservice-scoped dispatch was ever run"* |
| `20260906T055019Z` | `cart-bad-image-tag` | *"no dispatch queried cartservice's logs, metrics, change history"* |
| `20260906T071156Z` | `ad-memory-squeeze` | *"No dispatch touched adservice — its logs, restart history, and pod state are entirely unobserved"* |
| `20260906T165033Z` | `cart-bad-image-tag` | *"No dispatch touched cartservice: its logs, resource metrics, change history and backing-store health are entirely unmeasured"* |
| `20260906T172751Z` | `cart-redis-misconfig` | *"No dispatch touched cartservice at all"* |

And **all ten runs that named a class cite a change-history result for the origin service in the
root cause.** Fifteen for fifteen, on one variable:

| | queried the origin service's change history | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 10 runs | yes | **named a class, correct every time** |
| 5 runs | no | **abstained, every time** |

**This is not budget starvation, and the numbers say so in the opposite direction.** The abstaining
runs did *more* work and spent *more* money:

| | mean dispatches | mean cost |
|---|---|---|
| the 10 that answered | 6.2 | \$0.6929 |
| the 5 that abstained | **8.2** | **\$0.8269** (+19.3%) |

The clearest single case is `20260906T071156Z`: **12 dispatches, 32 steps, \$1.0489 — the most
expensive run of the fifteen — and not one of those dispatches went to `adservice`, the service its
own verdict named.** It did not report budget exhaustion. It had the calls and spent them elsewhere.

So what varies run to run at this stamp is **planner dispatch routing**: whether the changes
specialist is pointed at the service triage has already correctly identified. It is not variance in
reading evidence. It is variance in deciding what to look at.

**Why this matters more than the consistency rate it was registered to produce.** A consistency
figure describes the pipeline as a noisy oracle and implies the remedy is more repeats. This says
the noise has a single nameable, addressable cause upstream of the reasoning, and that R=5 would
measure the same coin-flip more precisely rather than telling us anything new about it.

**It also does not go in a results table.** n=15 at one stamp, and the routing claim rests on
reading fifteen verdicts, not on an instrumented count of dispatch targets. What it earns is a
queue entry, not a headline.

## 2. Consistency, per scenario

Reported per scenario and **never pooled** — a pooled rate over five scenarios of differing
difficulty is the figure the pre-registration exists to avoid.

| scenario | pass 1 | pass 2 | pass 3 | distinct answers | consistent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ad-memory-squeeze` | `unknown` | `unknown` | `resource_exhaustion` | 2 | no |
| `cart-bad-image-tag` | `unknown` | `bad_deploy` | `unknown` | 2 | no |
| `cart-dependency-latency` | `dependency_latency` | `dependency_latency` | `dependency_latency` | 1 | **yes** |
| `cart-redis-misconfig` | `bad_config` | `bad_config` | `unknown` | 2 | no |
| `frauddetection-memory-squeeze` | `resource_exhaustion` | `resource_exhaustion` | `resource_exhaustion` | 1 | **yes** |

**Every disagreement is an answer-versus-abstention, never answer-versus-wrong-answer.** No run in
this sweep named a fault class that was wrong. What moves is coverage, not correctness — a
distinction no R=1 figure in this repository has ever been able to draw, and one that a single
accuracy percentage would erase.

**Triage is nearly deterministic while the investigation is not.** Blast-radius recall and precision
are identical across all three passes for four of five scenarios (`ad` 1.00/0.43 ×3;
`cart-dependency-latency` 1.00/0.33 ×3; `cart-redis-misconfig` 0.80/0.67 ×3;
`frauddetection` 1.00/1.00 ×3). Only `cart-bad-image-tag` moved, once, from 1.00/0.71 to
0.80/0.67. The variance lives after triage, which is consistent with §1.

## 4. The judge partitions the fifteen exactly as the scorer does

`claude-haiku-4-5`, shared lineage opted into by name, judging only the narrative prose — it does
not see the verdict's class fields or the scorer's answer.

| judge grade | runs | scorer's outcome on those runs |
|---|---|---|
| `same_mechanism` | 10 | **all 10 scored correct** |
| `adjacent` | 1 | abstained |
| `different` | 4 | abstained |

**Zero disagreements across fifteen runs.** Two independent graders — one a deterministic comparison
against the ground-truth register, one a model reading prose — draw the same line in the same place.

This is the first independent check this project has had on the abstention boundary, and it says
the abstentions are legible as abstentions in the narrative itself. They are not scorer artifacts,
and they are not confident wrong answers that the scorer happened to forgive.

The single `adjacent` is run 6 — the \$1.0489 twelve-dispatch run. Its narrative correctly places
the failure at `adservice` refusing connections and stops short of the mechanism, which is a fair
reading of *adjacent* and consistent with §1.

**Traps: two taken across the whole sweep.** Of roughly forty-six planted misreadings evaluated,
`took` appears twice — run 2 (*"cartservice's zero error rate as evidence of health"*) and run 5
(*"empty span telemetry series read as clean bill of health"*). Run 5 took a trap and was still
graded `same_mechanism` and scored correct, which is worth noting rather than resolving here.

Dead ends closed versus missed ran 60/27 across the ten answering runs and 27/20 across the five
abstaining ones. **That is directional only and is not a result**: five runs against ten, with no
pre-registered threshold.

## 5. The six predictions

| # | registered | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ≥1 scenario shows within-scenario disagreement on fault class | **held** — 3 of 5 |
| 2 | `cart-redis-misconfig` and `cart-bad-image-tag` both among the disagreeing | **held** — both show 2 distinct answers |
| 3 | `frauddetection-memory-squeeze` consistent, 3/3 correct — **the control** | **held** — 3/3 class, 3/3 fix, triage 1.00/1.00 on all three |
| 4 | cost between \$9.00 and \$13.00 | **held** — \$11.0636, mean \$0.7376/run |
| 5 | ≥1 of the 15 refused, discarded, or crippled by a failed tool call | **FAILED** |
| 6 | capability and generation unchanged; every manifest carries `repeat_count: 3`, `tier: weekly` | **held** — verified on all 15 manifests |

**Prediction 5 failed, and it was listed in §4 of the pre-registration as something that would
surprise me.** Fifteen runs, zero refusals of a scored run, zero discards, zero `INVALID`, zero
errored tool calls, validity 1.00 throughout. The registered reasoning was that the world had
produced a Jaeger 500 within an hour the previous night and that kafka had forced a recycle twice
that week. Over twelve hours of world time it produced neither.

The two gate refusals that did occur are not counted against this prediction. Both were
settle-window overlaps, both refused **before injection**, both retried and scored, and both cost
\$0.00 — the gate doing its job, not a run lost. Run 1's budget exhaustion is the harness's own cap
rather than a failed call, and is likewise not counted.

**The registered consequence of this failure applies:** *"If all 15 complete cleanly, the world is
more stable than two days of evidence suggests and the \$0.00 refusals in the record are
overweighted in my estimate of it."* They were. That estimate is revised.

**Prediction 3's registered escalation did not fire.** The control held, so the finding in §1 is
about the pipeline rather than about the harness or the world.

**Prediction 1's falsifying branch also did not fire.** Three scenarios disagreed, so the next
experiment is not about Jaeger's reliability.

## 6. Protocol deviations, recorded rather than smoothed

**1. The sweep spanned twelve hours, not the registered four.** Run 8 finished at 07:55 UTC and run
9's gate ran at 15:47 — a gap of roughly eight hours in the middle of pass 2. **The cause is not
established.** Every run on both sides of the gap passed its own baseline gate reading
`clean: 15 services reporting, 0 alerts` at generation `f5bd108f4f70`, so nothing here is
invalidated, but pass 3 ran against a world roughly twelve hours older than pass 1 and that is a
fact about these figures.

It cuts against the registered rationale for interleaving only in one direction: the passes are
*further* apart than intended, not closer, so the concern that three repeats would be measured
against three nearly identical worlds is not the risk that materialised.

**2. The kafka projection was wrong, and the reason is the deviation above.** §5 of the
pre-registration projected the gate's threshold near 59% and a fresh start near 26%, both computed
over four hours at the measured 151MB/h. The sweep took twelve hours and kafka finished at
**74.76% of its 2048MB limit**. The projection was not wrong about the growth rate; it was wrong
about the duration it was projecting over. Any future sweep's kafka figure needs a duration
estimate that survives contact with the actual wall clock.

## 7. What this may not be used for

`variance.TIERS` says `published` (R=5) is *"the only tier a printed comparison may come from"*.
**These runs carry `tier: weekly` and may not back a pipeline-versus-baseline claim.** Stated here
rather than left for a reader to check against the tier table.

The MDE is the reason as well as the rule. At 5 scenarios and R=3 the effective n is 15 and the
minimum detectable effect at 80% power is **22.9pp** — 36.2pp at rho=0.5. Even R=5 needs a 17.7pp
gap to resolve a five-scenario comparison. **R=3 improves the variance estimate, not the
comparison.**

Dev sweep 10's five runs sit at the same stamp, capability and world generation and are a fourth
observation per scenario for anyone who wants them. **They are not pooled into anything above**:
they were taken under `tier: manual` with `repeat_count: 1`, and pooling them would report a repeat
count no run carries.

## 8. What would settle the finding

The claim in §1 is read off fifteen verdicts. The cheap way to make it a measurement rather than a
reading is to count it directly: **record each dispatch's target service in the manifest, and
score whether the origin service was ever dispatched against.** That is instrumentation, not a
prompt change, so it does not move the stamp and it makes every past run in `evals/runs/`
re-scoreable against the same question at \$0.00.

Changing the planner to guarantee a dispatch at the service triage names would move the stamp and
needs its own pre-registration. It should not be attempted before the count above exists, because
without it there is no before-figure to compare against.

Both belong in `docs/QUEUE.md` with a digest lock, not in this document's conclusions.
