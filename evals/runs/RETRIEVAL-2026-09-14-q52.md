## 1. The decision

§2.2 required **both**: flag 2 selected on more than half the splits at `k = 3`, and its mean
out-of-sample `recall@3` advantage above one query of the evaluation half (0.0476).

- Selected on **486 of 1000** — not a majority.
- Out-of-sample advantage **−0.0288** — not merely under the bar, but negative.

Both clauses fail. Flag 2 is rejected and flag 0 stands.

## 2. What the k = 3 column actually says

**The flag chosen on one half is, on average, worse than doing nothing on the other.**

That is not a weak result dressed as a strong one. It is the sharpest statement this data can make
about Q49's six-flag comparison: at `k = 3` the differences between the flags are noise, and
picking the winner **costs** 0.029 rather than gaining the 0.023 the full-set comparison showed.
Shrinkage exceeds the margin by a factor of two — 224% of it — which is what a selected difference
looks like when there was nothing underneath.

Six flags, 43 queries, one-query resolution. Something always wins. §3 of `PREREGISTRATION-Q49.md`
put it in advance: *"six draws from a noisy instrument will always produce a winner."* This is that
sentence with a number attached.

## 3. What the k = 5 column actually says

**Flag 2's advantage at `k = 5` is not a selection artefact.** 0.1628 in-sample, 0.1545
out-of-sample: **95% of the margin survives** being chosen. It is selected on 733 of 1000 splits.

So the 0.605 that clears the registered floor is real in the sense cross-validation can establish.
It is still **not confirmed** — every query in both halves is one all six flags were scored on, as
§2 of the registration says twice — but the specific worry that motivated Q52, that 0.605 was the
maximum of six draws, is answered. It was not.

## 4. Predictions

**Seven held, one failed.**

| # | registered | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | flag 2 selected on a majority at `k = 5` | **held** — 733/1000 |
| 2 | flag 2 *not* selected on a majority at `k = 3` | **held** — 486/1000 |
| 3 | the decision rule rejects flag 2 | **held**, on both clauses |
| 4 | shrinkage at `k = 3` exceeds that at `k = 5` | **held** — 0.0521 against 0.0082 |
| 5 | out-of-sample at `k = 5` stays above 0.10 | **held** — 0.1545 |
| 6 | out-of-sample at `k = 3` within ±0.03 of zero | **held** — −0.0288, just inside |
| 7 | no flag but 0 or 2 selected on more than a fifth of splits | **failed** — flag 1 takes 24.4% at `k = 3`, flag 8 takes 24.1% at `k = 5` |

Prediction 6 held by 0.0012. Registering a band that narrow was closer to luck than to judgement,
and it should be read as a coincidence rather than as calibration.

**Prediction 7's failure is the useful one.** The field is not `{0, 2}` with also-rans. At `k = 3`
flag 1 wins a quarter of the splits, which is another way of saying the same thing §2 says: at that
depth the flags are interchangeable and the winner is whichever noise landed. At `k = 5` flag 8
wins a quarter — and flag 8 reaches the same 0.605 as flag 2 on the full set. Two different
normalisations arriving at the identical ceiling is a hint that what is being hit is a property of
the corpus rather than of either flag.

## 5. What this makes the finding about

Not the flag. **The configuration production runs cannot tell these pipelines apart, and the one
that clears the floor is only distinguishable at a depth production does not use.**

`ContextSettings.retrieval_k` is 3. At 3, six normalisations are indistinguishable and choosing
between them is negative-value. At 5, one of them takes `recall` from 0.442 to 0.605 and keeps 95%
of that when cross-validated.

**The lever is `retrieval_k`, not the flag** — and it is the only one of Q50's four candidates that
moves nothing stored: no embedding, no chunk, no digest. It is also not free. A larger `k` puts more
retrieved text in front of the model on every investigation, and the registration ruled it out of
this task on the same argument that keeps flag 2 unadopted. **Q53** carries it.

Note what Q53 would have to settle that this cannot: whether the extra two chunks *help the agent*.
Every number here says a relevant document is reachable at rank 4 or 5. None of them says the model
reads it and answers better — T6.4 §5's limitation, unchanged through four measurements.

## 6. What this does not settle

- **Whether flag 2 is right.** Cross-validation bounds optimism. A held-out set would settle it and
  cannot be built: 31 non-holdout planner queries exist and the golden set spent 21.
- **Whether the ceiling is the corpus.** Flags 2 and 8 both reach exactly 0.605, which is also
  exactly the `recall@20` Q42 measured under flag 0. Three different routes to 26 of 43 is
  suggestive and is not tested here.
- **Whether the decision rule was the right one.** It rejected a change whose `k = 5` effect is
  measured, survives cross-validation, and clears a floor nothing else has cleared. That is the
  rule working as designed — but it is the third consecutive measurement where the registered rule
  points away from the change, and a reader is entitled to ask whether the apparatus has become
  better at saying no than at deciding.
