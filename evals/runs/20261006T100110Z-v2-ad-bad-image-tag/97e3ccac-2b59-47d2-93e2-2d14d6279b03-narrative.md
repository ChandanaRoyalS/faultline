# Frontend /api/data errors traced to an absent ad service after a repeated image rollout

## What the page looked like

The alerts fired on frontend and frontend-proxy, and triage framed the event as critical with a blast radius of eight services. The first thing worth saying is that the alert framing and the evidence never converged. Frontend's error ratio over the incident window averaged about 4.2% with a peak near 8.6% and repeated returns to zero across 112 samples; more than nine out of ten frontend calls completed normally throughout. The series was spiky, not a plateau, and the baseline-comparison tooling explicitly reported no sustained departure. Treat the "8 services / critical" header as a routing artifact, not a measurement. Note also that the pre-window baseline returned no samples at all, so any statement that frontend "went from zero errors" is an artifact of missing data rather than an observed clean period — do not build a timeline on it.

## Following the errors into the trace data

Trace sampling for frontend over the window returned 10 traces and 170 spans. Every root span finished between roughly 2ms and 34ms — nowhere near any plausible timeout threshold. Exactly one trace carried error status, and the error propagated along a single chain: load generator → proxy → frontend /api/data → outbound AdService/GetAds, with the ad hop terminal. That ad call failed in a fraction of a millisecond. That shape matters: a sub-millisecond failure is a rejected or refused connection, not a client giving up after waiting. Interestingly, the same errored trace contained a second, successful frontend request path alongside the failed one, consistent with an immediate retry or a parallel request succeeding — which fits the intermittent, non-plateau error ratio.

## The ad service's own account

Ad's log stream reads normally right up until it stops. Steady targeted and non-targeted ad request handling continues through roughly T-5m30s, with no errors, exceptions, rejections or retries in the retained lines. Then the stream ends with an orderly gRPC server shutdown driven by JVM shutdown, followed by a clean server-shut-down confirmation, at about T-5m15s. After that: nothing. No startup line, no request handling, no errors, through the alert time and to the end of the window. The service was not erroring at T+0 — it was absent. Caller-side failures at that moment must be explained by the absence, because there is no in-process error to correlate with.

## Change history, both services

Frontend's change log over a full day preceding the window is empty: no deployments, no configuration edits, no runtime toggles, no library bumps. There is nothing on frontend to roll back, which closes off the most reflexive first action.

Ad is the opposite. Three entries, all from the same automated actor, all image-reference updates, all inside roughly thirty minutes before onset. The hotfix build went out at about T-27m, was reverted at about T-16m, and was re-applied at about T-5m15s — coincident, to the second as far as the records allow, with the orderly shutdown in ad's logs. The revert did not stick. The identical image having been live for an earlier ~11-minute interval is a useful comparison period if someone wants to separate "the image is bad" from "this particular rollout got stuck".

## Dead ends worth keeping

Several lines of inquiry were opened and closed; a future responder should not re-walk them.

Slow-dependency timeout on the frontend path: ruled out. No sampled trace contains a slow span at any depth. product-catalog, the most frequently called service, held every server span and SQL query under about 6ms including the five-call recommendation fan-out. The recommendation fan-out itself completed in roughly 14ms end to end. Cart's Redis operations were sub-millisecond; the largest cart cost was a few milliseconds in a flag resolve inside EmptyCart. product-reviews dominates its own trace only relatively, inside a ~12ms total. The deepest trace — a 49-span checkout flow — finished in ~34ms with every downstream leg under ~7ms.

Ad as a high-error service: ruled out by metrics, and this is the trap. Ad's error ratio over two hours means under 0.5% with a peak of 6.25% and no sustained departure. Read alone, that metric says "ad is fine, look elsewhere." It says that because a shut-down process emits no error spans — 44 of 216 intervals had no defined ratio at all, meaning no ad traffic. The traffic gaps, not the error ratio, are the signal.

Ad crash or out-of-memory kill: ruled out. The termination was orderly and tied to JVM shutdown, not abrupt.

Frontend logs naming the failing downstream: nothing. A query for the literal "AdService" token in frontend logs across the window returned zero lines. Whatever frontend emitted about failing ad fetches used different wording or a different label. That source cannot anchor onset timing or give a failure rate.

## Conclusion

The automated re-application of the hotfix image to ad at about T-5m15s took the running ad container down cleanly, and nothing came back into service behind it. Frontend's /api/data route lost its ad dependency and returned fast rejections on the calls that needed it, which surfaced as an intermittent single-digit-percent error ratio rather than an outage, because only one route depends on ad and retries frequently succeeded. Frontend itself changed nothing. Confidence is medium — the causal chain is coherent and well-timed, but one link (the gRPC status on the errored span) is inferred rather than read.

## Open questions at close

Three gaps, listed in the order that would most change the response.

First, and decisive for remediation: no one collected pod events, container restart counts, image-pull status, or readiness series for ad. We know the old container stopped; we do not know whether a replacement started and crash-looped, failed to pull, or never scheduled. That distinction is the difference between rolling back the image reference and unwedging a stalled rollout. Whoever picks this up should gather that first.

Second, the gRPC status code on the errored GetAds span was inferred from its sub-millisecond duration, not read off the span. Frontend logs offered no corroboration.

Third, triage reported eight services and critical severity, but the collected evidence explains one route losing one dependency while over 90% of frontend calls succeeded. The other six services were never dispatched to. Either the severity model is over-reading intermittent errors, or there is impact nobody looked for.

## Suggested remediation

Fix class is rollback: return ad to the image that was live between the revert at T-16m and the re-application at T-5m15s, and confirm an ad container reaches readiness and resumes emitting request-handling lines. Before or alongside that, check whether a replacement container exists and what state it is in — if a rollout is stuck rather than a build being bad, a rollback of the image reference alone may not dislodge it. Separately, the automation that re-applied a build fifteen minutes after that build was reverted deserves its own follow-up; the revert carried no signal that prevented the re-apply.
