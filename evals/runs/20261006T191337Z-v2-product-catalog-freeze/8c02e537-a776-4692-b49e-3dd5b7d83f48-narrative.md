# Cart-touching requests hang at the client while cart answers in milliseconds

## What was visible, in order

The page opened with three alerting services: frontend-proxy, load-generator, and frontend. Severity critical, nine services in the blast radius, seed at frontend-proxy, and seven of the edges crossed during the walk carried no measurement at all. The first instinct was that the proxy or the frontend HTTP layer was breaking, and the first stretch of the investigation went into proving that wrong.

Around T+3m we pulled frontend-proxy's aggregate error ratio against the preceding baseline. The incident window averaged slightly lower than baseline (roughly 0.058 against 0.065), with no step change at onset. A single change point appeared late, near 19:18:31, peaking around 0.25 against a baseline maximum near 0.15 — a short late spike, not the condition present from the start. Two cautions: both windows had standard deviation comparable to or larger than the mean with minima at zero, consistent with bursty volume making the ratio noisy, and the query aggregated by service name only, with no route, upstream, or latency dimension. It could never have identified the failing route. It did one useful thing — it killed the theory that the proxy was failing a large share of requests, and reframed the failure as a wait rather than an error.

At about T+11m the traces turned the picture over. The frontend's gRPC client span for CartService/GetCart hung roughly 418 seconds, far past the 15s proxy ingress timeout that was actually surfacing the error, while the cart server span it eventually reached completed in about 2ms. Checkout showed the same shape: its own cart call stalled about 360 seconds with a connection-draining error before a retry succeeded in roughly 12ms. Every cart server span and its Redis HGET finished under 3ms, including inside the hung traces. The time was spent before the request reached cart, on client-side connection acquisition. Impact was confined to cart-touching journeys; browse, ads, recommendations, and the AI-assistant path completed in 5–38ms.

## Dead ends worth keeping

product-catalog appeared as the labelled degrading hop inside otherwise healthy recommendation traces — exactly the kind of signal that pulls an investigation sideways. Its durations were 0.4–7ms with no error status and cannot account for 15-second user-visible failures. ad (~3ms), recommendation (3–6ms) and product-reviews (~34ms end to end) were clean. Checkout's own self-time was a few milliseconds and its shipping leg ~4ms; the whole checkout duration was the stalled cart call. The proxy and frontend HTTP spans had near-zero self-time and merely terminated at the timeout.

Change history, queried around T+18m for cart and frontend, returned nothing at all. The trap here: both windows were built starting at the incident timestamp and extending ~24h forward, so they never covered the hours preceding onset. What they fairly close is the in-incident period — no rollout still progressing, no scaling action, no remediation that could explain a recovery. Only those two services were queried.

Log searches at ~T+24m were the least productive branch. A drain-substring search on cart and a draining-substring search on frontend both matched zero lines over the full window. These are filtered empties, not confirmed-silent streams; no unfiltered baseline was collected. They close only the narrow case that either service emitted a graceful-drain message in that exact wording.

At ~T+29m cart's span-metrics error ratio returned no samples in either the incident window or the six-hour baseline. Because the baseline is equally empty, the gap predates onset — cart's telemetry did not go dark at onset, it was not being collected at all. That emptiness is not an all-clear.

## Conclusion and what is still open

Callers of cart hang acquiring a gRPC connection, not on cart's work. We attribute this to cart as the dependency whose reachability degraded — serving endpoints going away, or connections torn down or exhausted — with frontend and frontend-proxy merely the places the latency surfaced. Confidence medium; indicated fix class is a restart.

Still open for the next responder: cart's unfiltered logs were never read, so whether it logged connection-limit, shutdown, or unreachability errors is unknown, and a partial loss or stall of some cart replicas is not excluded. No cart-side resource, replica-count, connection-count or churn data exists. Change history should be re-queried backwards from onset, and for frontend-proxy, checkout and the rest. Finally, the connection-draining error text seen in the checkout trace has no established origin — replica shutdown, mesh or load-balancer configuration, and pool limits are all still live possibilities.
