# Cart disappears mid-window; frontend checkout fails on an empty endpoint list

## What was visible, in order

All offsets are relative to T+0, when frontend, frontend-proxy, checkout and the load-generator alarmed together. The window opens around T-30m and closes around T+2m.

Frontend's error ratio was the first clean signal. The 32-minute pre-onset baseline is exactly zero across all 55 samples — no background error floor at all. It breaks that floor at T-2m, crossing the detection threshold near 6.6%, then runs through the incident at a mean around 2.2% with a peak near 27%. Two calibration points: frontend was already erroring before anyone was paged, and it was never fully down — three quarters of requests succeeded even at peak, with the ratio touching zero between bursts.

Frontend's logs carry two distinct signatures. The oldest retained lines, about T-17m, are already gRPC status 14 UNAVAILABLE with connections being dropped. By T+15s the dominant form is UNAVAILABLE where no connection could be established, with a host-unreachable error against 172.18.0.24 port 7070. Separately, the checkout path throws status 13 INTERNAL wrapping the useful detail: the cart lookup failed because the name resolver returned zero addresses. Dropped connections, then no connection, then no address to connect to — a backend being withdrawn, not degrading.

That progression closed several doors. No panic, restart or resource error in frontend's own logs; every error is client-side against a dependency. No DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, so "up but slow" does not fit. No auth or certificate failures. The checkout INTERNAL explicitly wraps a transport failure, so the checkout handler is a victim rather than the origin.

## Dead ends worth keeping

The frontend change log returned nothing at all — no deploys, config edits or flag flips. That genuinely rules out a frontend rollout as the trigger and rules out a quiet corrective change landing later. But the query was scoped to the seed service only, zero hops, and the window ran forward roughly 24 hours from onset rather than backward into the interval shortly before it. The empty answer is real but narrow.

The frontend trace sample was the most misleading artifact here. Ten traces, 115 spans, not one error-marked span. Root durations of 4ms to 40ms. Cart server spans and their cache operations sub-millisecond; product-catalog SQL under a couple of milliseconds; the proxy contributing almost no self-time; the ad service present in only two traces. Taken at face value this says the system is healthy, which contradicts both the error ratio and the logs. The resolution is sampling — these are the calls that still had a reachable backend. The traces are good evidence that the failure was not latency-driven and not uniform, and nothing more. Do not let a clean trace sample close an investigation when metrics and logs disagree with it.

## Cart, the change, and what stays open

Cart logged routine info-level store operations at normal cadence until T-3m40s, then at T-3m37s began an orderly shutdown: hosting lifetime, feature lifecycle, feature provider. Graceful termination, not a crash. Nothing followed for the remaining ~5.5 minutes — no restart, no startup banner. That rules out a panic (staged teardown, no stack trace), an out-of-memory kill (which leaves no in-process teardown), a cache connection failure (every Redis-path line is informational, including those just before shutdown), and a restart loop (exactly one lifecycle transition).

Cart's error ratio is flat zero in both baseline and incident windows — it emitted no error-coded spans at all. The discriminating detail is that four incident-window samples are undefined rather than zero: intervals with no cart span traffic whatsoever. A service that is gone, not one that is broken.

Cart's change log is busy where frontend's was empty: nine events, all from the same automation actor, all image-reference updates or reverts of one hotfix tag, flapping apply/revert across roughly 5.5 hours. The last event is an application of that image at about T-3m36s, immediately alongside the shutdown. Conclusion, at medium confidence: a bad deploy on cart; fix class is rollback; frontend, frontend-proxy and checkout are downstream victims.

Still open for whoever picks this up. Cart's logs end at shutdown with ~5.5 minutes of window left, so whether the image failed to start, crashed unseen, or was never scheduled is unsettled — as is whether the artifact itself is broken or the apply/revert churn simply left cart down. Nothing confirms that 172.18.0.24:7070 is cart; if it is another backend, only the zero-address resolution error ties cleanly to cart. No availability, replica-count or restart-count metrics were returned for cart, so its liveness was inferred rather than measured. And eleven of the thirteen services in the blast radius were never examined.
