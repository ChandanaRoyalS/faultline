## 3. Prediction 3 failed exactly where it said it would

The registration set the range at 4–5 and named the reason it was not 5:

> two of these targets are unlike anything the service axis has been scored on. `featureflagservice`
> is the stub that stands in for the retired flag service, and `redis-cart` is a datastore rather
> than a service… **A miss on either is a finding about the service axis's name space… A miss on the
> other three is the first plain miss on this axis.**

The other three were correct. Both misses were the named pair:

| scenario | target | verdict said | where the target lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `product-catalog-flag-failure` | `featureflagservice` | `productcatalogservice` | in the catalog, **not in the graph** — ADR-0006's stub emits no spans |
| `redis-cart-dependency-latency` | `redis-cart` | `cartservice` (**`redis-cart` ranked second**) | **not in the catalog at all** — ADR-0017's marked decision |

So the registered consequence fires and the plain-miss clause does not. **Neither target is a name
this pipeline can say**, and the first verdict says so itself: *"if the flag lives on
featureflagservice the right target is not even in the legal blast radius."* The second ranked the
right answer at position two — a top-3 hit — which is the same fact seen from the other side: the
name was reachable as a hypothesis and not as a blame.

**What was done about it, and what was deliberately not.** T5.7 landed
`evalharness.visibility`, which records on every scored run whether the target was `in_graph`,
`in_catalog_not_in_graph`, or `absent_from_catalog`, and prints it under a missed service. **It
changes no score.** ADR-0027's bar for a second correct answer is measurement — acting on it clears
the fault — and reverting config on `productcatalogservice` does not clear a flag held in a stub,
nor does restarting `cartservice` clear a qdisc on redis-cart's interface. ADR-0017's Addendum 3
settles the marked decision (infrastructure belongs in the catalog) and queues the execution as
**Q27**, because a catalog node changes what the graph tool answers and moves `cap:c4d52d00`;
**Q28** queues the prompt-side ablation, which moves the stamp and which no task in the execution
plan proposes.

**The honest reading of the axis at this stamp: 24 of 26 answered, two misses, both structural.**

## 4. `redis-cart-dependency-latency` returned `config_revert` for a third time

Registered: *"if this sweep returns `config_revert` again, the question is T7.17's — whether
clearing the netem qdisc fixes this one durably too — and the answer is a measurement, not a scorer
edit."* It did. On the current world this scenario has now returned `config_revert` **three times in
four scored runs**, against a `restart` label with no `also_correct_remediation`.

The sibling scenario `cart-dependency-latency` was in exactly this position until T7.17 measured it:
deleting the qdisc cleared the delay durably, 3/3, without a restart, and `config_revert` became a
recorded second-correct answer (ADR-0027, evidence in `docs/evidence/t7.17-fix-class/`). **The same
experiment on `redis-cart` has not been run.** Until it is, the label stands and these runs are
scored wrong on the fix axis — which is the correct treatment of an unmeasured hypothesis, and is
not the same as believing the agent is wrong.

## 5. The B0.2 arm was run twice, and nothing is deleted

**Arm A** (`20260907T191641Z` … `202206Z`) is the registered arm. **Arm B**
(`203723Z` … `214310Z`) is an unregistered repeat: the arm was run a second time by mistake, an
operator error rather than a decision, and it is recorded rather than removed (ADR-0022 §3.3). It
cost \$0.0000 — B0 makes no model call — and it injected five more faults into a world that was
quiet before each one and confirmed recovered after.

**It is also the first R=2 observation of the baseline at a fixed stamp, and the two arms agree
scenario for scenario:**

| scenario | truth | arm A | arm B |
|---|---|---|---|
| `payment-telemetry-blackout` | `bad_config` | `bad_config` ✓ | `bad_config` ✓ |
| `product-catalog-flag-failure` | `bad_config` | `dependency_latency` ✗ | `dependency_latency` ✗ |
| `redis-cart-dependency-latency` | `dependency_latency` | `bad_config` ✗ | `bad_config` ✗ |
| `shipping-quote-misconfig` | `bad_config` | `dependency_latency` ✗ | `dependency_latency` ✗ |
| `shipping-wrong-image` | `bad_deploy` | `bad_deploy` ✓ | `bad_deploy` ✓ |

**2 of 5 in both arms, 5 of 5 agreement, no service named on any of the ten runs.** Prediction 8
held on the registered arm and holds again on the repeat.

**One property of the baseline this exposed, worth a line.** On both `redis-cart-dependency-latency`
runs B0 reported *"suspect: service of the most recent pre-onset change in the window (10:39:55)"* —
a change from that morning, hours before an onset at 19:54 and 21:15 respectively. The heuristic
takes the most recent change *in its window* without asking whether the change is near the onset,
so on a scenario whose fault is injected by traffic shaping rather than by a recorded change, it
attributes to whatever change is oldest-but-nearest. That is not a defect to fix — B0 is a control,
and the control is meant to be dumb in legible ways — but it is why B0 called this scenario
`bad_config` twice with no configuration having moved.

**How the two arms are counted.** Arm A is the sweep's B0 column. Arm B is reported here and is not
added to it: doubling one arm's n while the pipeline arm stays at R=1 would make the comparison
between them a comparison of two different designs.

## 6. Finding thirty-five: the proposer produced nothing on one run, and the contract is why

On `redis-cart-dependency-latency` the proposer failed schema validation twice and was escalated:

```
FAILED DISPATCH proposer: schema validation failed twice (1 validation error for Proposal
confirm_within_seconds_note
  Extra inputs are not permitted [type=extra_forbidden, input_value=None, input_type=NoneType])
```

The model emitted one extra key — `confirm_within_seconds_note`, valued `None` — beside a proposal
that was otherwise well formed. `Proposal` is a `REQUESTED` contract and forbids extras, so the whole
proposal was refused, twice, and that run has a verdict and no remediation at all. The run is
flagged, the flag is reported beside the score and never inside it, and nothing else about the run
moved.

**This is Q25b's finding, one contract over.** Q25b measured that two runs on `cart-bad-image-tag`
were destroyed by unexpected verdict keys, changed the `Verdict` policy to accept-and-record, and
one of the keys that policy accepted — `remediation_class` — turned out to be worth adding as a real
field. The same asymmetry now stands between the two contracts: **a verdict may carry a key nobody
asked for and it is recorded; a proposal may not and it is discarded.** Both B0 verdicts in this
sweep carried an unexpected `summary` key and were accepted and recorded, in the same runs.

**Queued as Q29, not fixed here.** Changing the policy mid-record would make the last runs of this
sweep a different pipeline from the first, and the change deserves the same measured argument Q25b
made rather than a reflex.
