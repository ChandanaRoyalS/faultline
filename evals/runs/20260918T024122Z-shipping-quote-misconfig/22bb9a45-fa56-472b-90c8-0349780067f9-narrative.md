# Checkout order failures traced to the shipping quote boundary

## What was visible, in order

The page named checkoutservice at the centre, twelve services in the blast radius, critical. Alerts from accountingservice, emailservice, frauddetectionservice, loadgenerator and quoteservice made it look broad. Five edges in the region carried no measurement at all.

Checkout's logs came back truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, so the onset minute itself was never returned. Early in the window checkout ran complete PlaceOrder lifecycles: request start, payment success, confirmation email, message write. In the final stretch, roughly T+2m to T+4m40s, there were about thirty PlaceOrder start lines and nothing after them. Every line was info severity — no error text, no timeout, no dependency named. Checkout was alive and accepting traffic to the last second, and the entry line with resolved user and currency was still normal, so the break sat downstream of request admission.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Checkout's error-ratio metric flagged nothing. The incident-window mean was slightly below baseline, maxima identical, variance unchanged, and the only two change points sat hours earlier. This series is pre-existing spiky and is a poor onset detector; do not use it to pin timing here.

cartservice consumed disproportionate effort and produced nothing. Its error-ratio query returned no samples in either window, so the gap is missing series data rather than a symptom. Its logs were routine cart operations at steady cadence through the end of the window, no cache errors. Its change history looked alarming — thirty-eight automation-authored entries in a repeating three-to-four-hour cycle of image swap, an egress-delay sidecar attach, and a REDIS_ADDR repoint — but every mutation had a matching revert, the last one about 1.6 hours before onset, and nothing was left standing. A trap for anyone reading that log cold.

The checkout change log was empty, ruling out a deploy, flag flip, or in-flight config push at or after onset. Note the window starts at onset and runs forward, so the hours before onset were never covered.

## What the traces showed, and what remains open

All twelve sampled PlaceOrder traces aborted at the same hop: checkoutservice to shippingservice GetQuote, the only consistently ERROR-marked downstream call, with the error propagating up through frontend PlaceOrder and the frontend HTTP POST. It is a fast failure, not a wait — GetQuote spans ran 2.5–3.5ms and whole traces finished in 6–11.6ms. That reframes the orphaned checkout log lines as rapid aborts rather than hangs.

The rest of the fan-out was clean: currency Convert under 2ms, GetCart and its Redis lookup sub-millisecond to 2.2ms, product catalog and its flag lookups fast, all unmarked. Payment and email appear in no sampled trace because the request dies during shipping-quote preparation — their alerts were downstream noise.

Shipping's own server span and its HTTP child are short and unmarked; only the checkout-side span carries the bad status, so the failure surfaces at the call boundary. Checkoutservice is the reporter, not the cause. Confidence low, no fix identified.

Still open: shippingservice's logs, metrics and change history were never queried at all, so the mechanism is unestablished. We cannot tell whether shipping returned a non-OK status or checkout mishandled a valid response. Onset timing is unpinned. And shipping's own upstream — the HTTP client target, plausibly quoteservice, which also alerted — was never checked as a source of bad data.
