# Cart-path slowness surfacing as checkout error bursts

## What was visible, in order

Take T+0 as the page: cartservice, checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator alerting together, warning severity, twelve services in the blast radius, cartservice named as origin, four unmeasured edges crossed. Frontend and loadgenerator are pure waiters and told us nothing beyond the pain reaching the edge. The first move — cartservice's own error ratio — returned nothing at all, and returned nothing in the two-hour baseline too, so the series is simply absent from the backend rather than broken at onset. cartservice logs then showed a service that was fine on its own terms: clean start about two hours prior, Redis connected and self-tested, and steady GetCart/AddItem/EmptyCart traffic at several per second right up to roughly T+2m with no errors and no second startup banner. The measurable signal lived on checkoutservice: error ratio ~1.4% against a 0.17% baseline, peak near 10%, change points at 07:05:15 and 07:11:15 — onset about twenty minutes before the page. Window minimum still zero and standard deviation nearly twice the mean, so spiky bursts, not a sustained break. checkoutservice logs carried no error-severity lines whatsoever; every retained order completed payment, email and queue write. What changed was spacing: early orders finish within ~30ms of the PlaceOrder line, late ones take several hundred milliseconds. Finally, the cartservice change stream: 26 changes, all from platform-automation, all applied-then-reverted pairs on a ~3.5 hour cycle, including a traffic-shaping container imposing 300ms of eth0 delay on the cart network namespace.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Do not re-walk these. A cartservice deploy still in effect: every image change has a matching revert, the last at 05:33. A lingering non-default Redis endpoint: reverted at 06:09. A crash or restart: one startup sequence only, two hours before onset, with continuous service through the window edge. Loss of the redis-cart connection: initial connect and read/write self-test succeeded and no failure lines appear anywhere retained. A human or ad-hoc change: all 26 changes are automation. Total checkout outage or zero throughput: orders complete to the last minute, ~90% of calls non-error even at the worst sample. Checkout's error level as ordinary noise: baseline mean 0.17%, max 1.8%, both under the incident mean. A measurable cartservice error spike: unprovable either way, the series is empty. Metrics collection breaking at onset: the baseline is equally empty. Also filed here: recurring GetCart calls with an empty userId, which looked suspicious for a few minutes and produce no error output.

## Where it landed, and what is still open

The symptom is latency, not failure. checkoutservice completes every PlaceOrder stage while per-order duration grows tenfold and errors rise eightfold in bursts — the signature of a caller intermittently breaching deadlines against a slow callee. cartservice looks like a pod with delay on its network path rather than trouble inside its process, and the recorded 300ms delay arm matches the observed magnitude. Best call: slow cart-path calls, with the other three services alerting only because they wait. Fix class is a configuration revert, and confidence is low for one blunt reason: every relevant manipulation was already reverted — delay container removed at 05:52, last cart change reverted at 06:09 — over an hour before the 07:05 onset, so nothing recorded was in force. Three gaps for the next responder. There is no cartservice-side latency or request-rate measurement at all, so the 300ms is inferred from checkout timestamp spacing and never measured at the source. Both log results are truncated to the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines, leaving 07:05–07:27 — the interval containing actual onset — uncovered on both services. And no result attributes checkout's errors to cartservice specifically: the metric carries no downstream-peer dimension, and with four unmeasured edges crossed, paymentservice, emailservice or productcatalog remain live candidates for the slow dependency.
