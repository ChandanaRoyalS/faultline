# Unresolved warning across 14 services, starting at cart — investigation closed without a culprit

## What the alert looked like from the chair

The page arrived at T+0 as a warning with a blast radius of fourteen services and cart named as the starting point. Five services were alerting together: cart, checkout, frontend, frontend-proxy and load-generator. Fourteen edges between affected services had no measurement attached to them, which is worth noting up front because it meant the graph could not be used to narrow the search; the starting point was effectively chosen by the alert, not by evidence.

The investigation window ran from roughly T-30m to T+2m. Everything below was gathered inside that span. Read the rest of this record knowing the ending: seven dispatches went out, all seven looked at cart, and all seven came back clearing it. No other service was ever examined. The actual origin of this incident is not identified here.

## First look: cart's error ratio

The opening move was the obvious one — compare cart's errored-span ratio against its own baseline. It came back flat at exactly zero, 128 samples in the incident window and 128 in the preceding baseline, with no variance on either side. There is no step at T+0 and no ramp leading into it. Both runs of this comparison agreed.

Two caveats are worth carrying forward. First, the denominator stayed populated throughout, which independently confirms cart was still receiving and recording calls — so this is not a case of traffic silently going to zero. Second, the query template returned only the error ratio; request rate and latency percentiles were never actually retrieved, despite being asked for. Anyone picking this up should not assume cart's latency was checked. It was not.

## Second look: cart's logs

Cart's logs across the same window are unremarkable. The retained lines are informational store operations — get, add, empty — against the Valkey-backed cart store, for a steady stream of distinct user ids, several per second right up to the window edge at T+2m. No errors, no warnings, no stack traces.

Two limits on this evidence matter. The result was truncated to the oldest eight lines (around T-30m) and the newest thirty-two (around T+1m to T+2m); the middle was dropped, and T+0 itself falls squarely inside the dropped section. And the selector was unfiltered by level, so this is a traffic sample rather than a targeted search for errors. What it does establish firmly is that the process was alive and serving at both ends of the window, and that its backing store was not wholly unreachable — a total store outage would have surfaced as failures in exactly these code paths.

## Third look: traces, and the one thing that did not fit

The trace query is where something genuinely anomalous appeared, though not the thing we were hunting.

The content of the traces that exist is reassuring and dull. Cart's own handler spans (GetCart, AddItem) run sub-millisecond to low single-digit milliseconds, with self-time a fraction of a millisecond. Every Valkey leaf span — HGET, HMSET, EXPIRE — is sub-millisecond, including in the slowest trace in the set. Product-catalog spans including their SQL stay under 4ms; flagd resolution completes in well under a millisecond. The slowest end-to-end trace is under 41ms and its cost sits in the frontend/product-catalog path, not cart. The longest frontend-proxy ingress span is about 19.6ms — three orders of magnitude short of the 15s route timeout, so there are no timeout-shaped spans anywhere in the sample.

The anomaly is the timestamps. Only ten traces came back, 146 spans, and every single one started between roughly T-28m and T-27m. From T-27m onward — through the alert at T+0 and out to the window edge — cart has no trace data at all. The result was explicitly not truncated, so narrowing the query will not surface more. Set that against the metrics and logs findings: span-metrics kept a non-zero denominator and logs kept flowing through T+2m. A service that is alive, logging, and counted in span metrics but producing no traces for the last twenty-seven minutes of the window is describing a telemetry or export gap, not a service failure.

This is the most interesting fact in the record and it was never followed up.

## Dead ends: the change-log queries

Three separate change-history lookups went out — cart, flagd, product-catalog — and all three came back empty. No deploys, no config edits, no flag changes recorded for any of them.

These are the clearest dead ends in the record, and not only because they were empty. All three queries were bounded from the incident timestamp forward for roughly twenty-four hours rather than backward. They therefore cover the period during and after onset and say nothing whatsoever about the hours leading up to it. Read narrowly they rule out a post-onset release, a mid-incident config push, a staged flag ramp, and a quiet rollback masquerading as recovery — all legitimate eliminations. Read as an answer to "what changed before this started," they are worthless. Anyone re-running these must flip the window direction. The one thing the flagd query did confirm is that the service name resolved correctly, so the emptiness reflects an absence of records rather than a mistargeted query.

## What turned out not to matter

Several plausible shapes were tested and found absent, and they can be skipped on a re-investigation of cart specifically: cart as the error origin propagating to callers; cart handler logic as the in-process bottleneck; Valkey as slow or saturated; a downstream dependency (product-catalog, flagd) adding large latency to cart paths; cart hanging into the proxy's route timeout; cart having crashed or stopped logging; gradual pre-onset degradation visible as a ramp.

All of those eliminations carry the same asterisk: the trace-based ones rest entirely on a sample that ends twenty-seven minutes before the alert, and the log-based ones rest on a sample with the alert moment cut out of the middle. They are real for the periods they cover. They are not evidence about T+0 itself.

## Where this was left

Low confidence, no fix applied, no class of problem established. The honest summary is that the investigation exonerated the service it was pointed at and never looked anywhere else.

Three things are open, roughly in the order I would take them. First: why cart traces stop at T-27m while its logs and span metrics continue — broken export, a sampling change, or a real loss of sampled requests. That gap is the only signal in the record that does not have a benign explanation. Second: checkout, frontend and frontend-proxy all alerted alongside cart and none of them was dispatched to. Their error ratios, latencies and logs at T+0 are entirely unexamined, and given that the slowest observed trace put its cost in the frontend/product-catalog path, that is where I would send the next pass. Third: re-run all three change-history queries with the window pointing backward from onset, since the lead-up is currently uncovered.

The fourteen unmeasured edges remain unmeasured. Until some of them are instrumented, an alert like this one will keep nominating a starting service that the evidence then clears, and the search will keep having nowhere obvious to go next.
