# The change log the benchmark writes to itself — Q57, 2026-09-14

**What this is.** [`PREREGISTRATION-Q57.md`](PREREGISTRATION-Q57.md), run. **$0.00** — no model
calls, no injections, no world; 244 scored investigations and 734 change records already on disk.

**The registered result.** Accuracy is **19.6 points higher** in the noisier half —
`−0.196, 95% CI [−0.304, −0.090]`, excluding zero, in the **opposite direction to prediction 4**.

**It is not reported as a finding about the agent, because it is not one.** §3's comparison
controlled for scenario and not for world generation, and stale count carries the generation with
it. The registered number is a comparison across worlds wearing a residue label, which is the exact
boundary ADR-0014 exists to police.

**What the run does establish**, and these are the results:

1. **The density is real, large and now measured.** On the world that exists, the median
   investigation reads **12 stale change records**; the worst reads **37**. Every one of them is a
   fault injection, because `injector.changelog` is the only writer.
2. **There is no uncontaminated baseline left.** Within the current generation, every scenario's
   *low* half still saw up to sixteen stale records. The archive cannot answer what residue costs
   because after twenty days of sweeps on one world **there is no control group**.
3. **Five of seven predictions failed, from one defect.** §3's design.

Evidence: `docs/evidence/q57-change-residue/q57-residue.json`.

---

## 1. What was measured

Per investigation, over the union of its `change_history` calls, reading the window each call
actually recorded rather than recomputing the policy:

- **own** — records for that service inside `[injected_at, reverted_at]`. This run's fault.
- **stale** — records inside the queried window and **before** `injected_at`. Everything the
  session left behind.

Distinct record ids, so two queries covering one service do not count a record twice.

| | |
|---|---:|
| scored investigations with a change query | **244** |
| median stale, whole archive | 0 |
| median stale, **current world** (`90e9f29e`) | **12** |
| maximum stale | **37** |
| median own | 1 |
| saw zero stale, whole archive | 134 of 244 |

## 2. The registered comparison, and why it says nothing about the agent

§3 halved each scenario's runs at that scenario's own median stale count and took
`accuracy(low) − accuracy(high)`, paired across scenarios. Result: **−0.196, CI [−0.304, −0.090]**.

A twenty-point accuracy *advantage* from a noisier change log is not a mechanism anyone can state,
and this write-up does not try to state one. Here is what the halves actually were:

| generation | n | median stale | accuracy | abstentions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `90e9f29e` — the world that exists | 89 | **12.0** | 0.75 | 12 |
| `(none)` | 76 | 0.0 | **0.93** | 19 |
| `f5bd108f` — the previous world | 70 | 0.0 | 0.65 | 13 |
| `90e9f29e@L` | 8 | 0.0 | 0.38 | 0 |

**Only one generation has any residue at all.** So "high stale" was very nearly "the current world"
and "low stale" was "every other world, plus the runs that predate generation recording". The
number is a cross-generation comparison, and ADR-0014 forbids exactly that.

**It is not a time trend either** — that was the first explanation tried and it is wrong. Accuracy
does not climb with the calendar: `(none)` is the most accurate group at 0.93 and `f5bd108f` the
least at 0.65, with the current world between them.

**The tell was in the abstentions.** §5 predicted more abstentions in the noisy half; the run has
**32 in the low half against 10 in the high**. A reversal that large is not noise responding to
noise, it is two different populations — which is what they are.

**−0.147, 95% CI [−0.342, +0.033].** The interval includes zero.

**This is a diagnostic and not a result, and the reason is a rule rather than a preference.** It was
computed while working out why prediction 4 failed — that is, *after* the registered number was
read — so registering it now would be choosing a cut with the answer in hand. It is reported here
because concealing it would be worse, and it is not promoted to the headline because this repository
does not promote post-hoc cuts.

**What it is good for is the structural point, which does not depend on its value.** Look at the
thresholds: 20, 18, 24, 16, 15. On the current world a scenario's *quietest* half still reads
sixteen stale records. **There is no zero-residue population on this world at all** — so even a
correctly stratified version of §3's comparison is comparing *some* residue against *more*, and the
contrast it can offer is a fraction of the one the registration assumed.

## 4. The predictions

**One held, five failed, one untested — and all five failures have the same root.**

| # | registered | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the median investigation sees more stale than own | **failed** — 0 against 1 across the archive. **Holds on the current world**: 12 against 1 |
| 2 | fewer than one in five sees zero stale | **failed** — 134 of 244, 55%. Near zero on the current world |
| 3 | stale rises with position within a sweep | **untested** — the artifact records no sweep position, which §2 should have noticed when it specified the instrument |
| 4 | accuracy lower in the high-stale half | **failed, and reversed** — +0.196 the other way |
| 5 | the 95% CI includes zero | **failed** on the registered comparison; **holds** on §3's diagnostic |
| 6 | abstentions higher in the high-stale half | **failed, and reversed** — 32 against 10 |
| 7 | at least one investigation saw more than twenty stale | **held** — 37 |

**Predictions 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 fail for one reason: the archive spans four world generations and
only one of them has residue.** Each was written as though the 244 investigations were one
population. They are not, and §3 of the registration said the word "scenario" where it needed to say
"scenario and generation".

**Predictions 1 and 2 are the instructive pair.** Both are false about the archive and both are true
about the world that exists. A reader who wanted to know *what an investigation reads today* would
have been served by them; the instrument answered a different question because it was pointed at
every run ever recorded.

## 5. The decision

§4 of the registration fixed this in advance and it applies unchanged: **the density is reported as
a measured property of the benchmark, with an accuracy effect this archive cannot resolve.**

- **Nothing is cleared, filtered, or narrowed.** The three declined options stay declined for the
  reasons §4 gives.
- **`RESULTS.md` carries the density as a standing term**: on this world the median investigation
  reads **12 stale change records against 1 of its own**, and every entry in the log is an
  injection.
- **No accuracy penalty is published**, because none was measured. The registered figure is
  confounded and the diagnostic is post-hoc and spans zero.

## 6. What this leaves open

**The measurement is still worth making and this archive cannot make it** — there is no
zero-residue population on the current world and will not be one while sweeps continue against it.
That is **Q59**: a control needs a world with an empty change log, which means either clearing the
table for one registered comparison or standing up a fresh generation.

**§2's other gap is Q60.** Prediction 3 was untestable because run manifests do not record position
within a sweep. The residue an investigation reads is a function of what ran before it, and nothing
in the record says what did.

**And the finding Q53 started with is unchanged.** An agent read a real log correctly and built a
false root cause from it, because a service with fourteen changes in a day is ordinary in this
world and extraordinary in any other. This run measured how ordinary — it did not measure what it
costs, and it now knows why it could not.
