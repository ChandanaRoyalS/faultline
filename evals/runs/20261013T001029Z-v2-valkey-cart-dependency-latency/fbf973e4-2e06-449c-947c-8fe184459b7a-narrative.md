# Five services alerting with no errors anywhere: an unresolved latency symptom on the checkout path

## What was visible, and cart cleared four ways

The page named five services at once — cart, checkout, frontend, frontend-proxy, load-generator — with a declared blast radius of fourteen services across fourteen never-measured edges. Severity warning. Triage origin was handed to me as cart, which is an artefact of alert routing rather than a finding.

Cart refused to be guilty from every angle. Error ratio was exactly zero for all 128 samples of the window and exactly zero in the preceding baseline, with min, max and standard deviation all zero — which excludes a brief burst hidden by averaging as well as a sustained shift. Because the ratio is only defined when the call denominator is non-zero, that same continuous series proves cart was serving traffic throughout: no stall, no restart. Logs showed only routine info-level store operations (get, add, empty) interleaved across multiple users right to the final seconds, with no connection errors and no startup or shutdown banners. Traces showed cart server spans mostly under 5ms and backing-store calls under roughly 2ms with no retries.

If you are reading this because cart has been named origin again, assume that naming is wrong.

## The dead ends — change history, and the slow flag call

The change-history branch consumed real time and produced nothing. For cart the log held exactly two entries, both from platform automation, both touching only the container image reference: a roll-forward to a hotfix tag and a revert of that same reference about ten minutes later, leaving net state unchanged. Both landed roughly an hour after the alert boundary, so neither preceded the symptom; the hotfix was not running at onset, no config or flag edits appear, and nothing is attributed to a human. For checkout the log returned nothing at all — but note the trap I walked into: that window starts at the alert and runs forward a day, so it says nothing about the minutes before. For the flag service the query came back empty too, leaving no correlation path through it.

The second dead end was more tempting. In the slowest sampled trace (a 401ms checkout root) cart's EmptyCart handler took about 29.5ms, of which roughly 22.5ms was its outbound flag-evaluation call and about 18ms was self-time in cart's own client span. The obvious reading — a degraded flag service — is wrong: the server-side span nested inside that same slow call completed in about 1ms, so the delay sits on cart's client/network side. It is also intermittent; the identical path in a second trace ran at about 6.4ms. Either way, ~30ms of a 401ms trace cannot account for the symptom.

## Where it concentrates, what was never measured, and what is open

The only trace-level localisation puts the weight of the 401ms and 172ms roots in checkout's own work plus its fan-out to shipping/quote, currency, payment and email, with cart contributing at most ~30ms. Checkout's own error ratio was identically zero through the window and had moved down from a baseline background near 1.9% (excursions to ~7%) rather than up; there is no step at the alert moment in that signal. I name checkout as where the symptom concentrates, and name no mechanism.

Three coverage gaps shaped this and must not be mistaken for findings. The metric work returned only the error-ratio template — latency percentiles, request rate and CPU/memory saturation were requested and never returned for any service, so every clean verdict here is about errors, not about time or pressure. The log pull was truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, leaving roughly 31 minutes in the middle — almost certainly including onset — unseen. The trace sample covered only the alert window with no baseline spans, so no before/after latency comparison was possible.

Confidence low, no fix class. Open: what signal actually tripped the alert, since the quantity that crossed a threshold was never retrieved; where in checkout's fan-out the 401ms sits, since none of those four services was ever dispatched to — the single largest omission of this investigation; and whether the intermittent client-side gap on cart's outbound flag call reflects a shared condition across callers or is sampling noise.
