# Checkout orders aborting at the cart hop; failing service never directly measured

## What we saw first

The page arrived naming three services: frontend, loadgenerator, and checkoutservice. Blast radius was tallied at twelve services and severity was set critical. From the responder's chair this looked like a frontend problem — frontend was the seed, frontend was alerting, and the loadgenerator's complaints are always a frontend-shaped signal. That framing held for the first stretch of the investigation and turned out to be wrong in the only way that mattered: frontend and checkoutservice were both erroring purely by propagation.

First instinct was to look for a change. Both change-history queries came back completely empty: nothing recorded for frontend, nothing recorded for checkoutservice, no deployment, no config edit, no flag flip. Worth flagging for whoever reads this later — the change log itself was healthy. It answered cleanly with an explicit empty result rather than an error, so "change data unavailable" is not an available excuse. What is a real gap is the window: both queries ran forward from onset for roughly a day instead of backward into the hours before it, and both were scoped to the seed service alone with zero dependency hops. So the honest statement is "no post-onset change on these two services," not "no change caused this."

## Metrics: real but bursty, and the clock didn't line up

Frontend error ratio moved from about 2.9% baseline to about 4.1% in the incident window, with peaks near 36%. Checkoutservice roughly doubled, 0.047 to 0.098. Both are real degradations and both rule out the two comfortable extremes: this was never a total outage (minimum zero, most traffic succeeding throughout) and it was never a clean step change either.

Two details cost time and are worth keeping. First, the shape: at checkoutservice the standard deviation (0.19) exceeded the mean, the minimum stayed at zero, and the peak error ratio was *identical* to baseline at 0.6667 — which on inspection is two failed calls out of three, i.e. very low volume in the affected buckets. So what changed was the frequency of error bursts, not their depth. That killed the working theory of a constantly-wrong value or a bad artifact serving a fixed fraction of traffic.

Second, the timeline did not agree with the alert. Frontend's only two threshold crossings were at 20:52 and 20:56, roughly half an hour *before* the 21:29 alert timestamp. Checkout's change points landed at 20:53 and 21:29. Treating 21:29 as onset was a dead end; error elevation predates it, and baseline peaks match incident peaks, which leaves open whether this is a step-up of chronic behaviour rather than a new failure.

The frontend metric query also gave no per-dependency breakdown and no latency percentiles, so it could not answer which dependency degraded first. That question had to wait for traces.

## Logs: alive, talking, and saying nothing useful

Checkoutservice logs were queried unfiltered over the final half hour. Every line returned in the last three minutes was info severity. No exception, no stack, no named failing dependency. This ruled out the fastest path to an answer — reading the culprit off checkout's own error text — and it ruled out several tempting explanations at once: the process had not crashed or restarted (order-entry lines kept arriving at a steady cadence right to the window edge), it had not stopped receiving traffic (new distinct user ids throughout), and the log pipeline was not broken (logs were flowing continuously).

The useful observation was a gap rather than a line. In the oldest slice around 20:59, each order-entry line was followed by downstream completion milestones — payment authorization, confirmation email, a message-write offset. In the newest slice the order-entry lines still appear, and the completions never do. Requests enter the order path and vanish. Not failing at the first step, and not failing loudly: a silent abort somewhere after entry and before the first completion milestone.

## Traces: where the shape gave it away

Ten sampled PlaceOrder traces spanning 21:30:03 to 21:31:27 shared one identical five-span shape. Every one of them terminates at checkoutservice's call to CartService GetCart. That leaf span does not hang — it completes, in roughly 0.5 to 1.0 ms, in an error state, and the error propagates up through prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart to the PlaceOrder span and on to the frontend root. End-to-end duration was 1.4 to 2.8 ms.

That single fact retired a whole family of hypotheses. There are no payment, currency, email, or shipping spans in any trace — execution never gets that far, so none of those can be the stall. There is no productcatalog span either: prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart has exactly one child, the cart call, so the abort happens before catalog lookups. And the sub-millisecond completions rule out waiting on a slow dependency entirely; you cannot blame timeout budgets or deadline tuning for a rejection that arrives in under a millisecond. Finally, checkoutservice spans show negligible self time (~0.1 ms) and are in error only by propagation, which removes checkoutservice itself as the origin — and by the same argument removes frontend.

One caution about these traces: all ten of ten failed at the same hop, which reads as uniform within that ninety-second slice, but the metrics say the failure is bursty over hours. The traces sampled a burst.

## Where it landed

The failing hop is cartservice. Frontend and checkoutservice, the two services that alerted and the two services we spent the investigation querying, are both erroring by propagation only.

The mechanism is inferred, not observed. Immediate sub-millisecond rejections, appearing in bursts rather than as a constant fraction, with healthy periods interleaved, fits cartservice periodically depleting some resource — memory, or connections/threads to its cache backend — and rejecting calls outright while depleted. That is a signature match, not a measurement. Fix class is scale. Confidence is low, and the reason is the next section.

## Open threads for whoever picks this up

The largest gap is the obvious one: cartservice was never queried. No metrics, no logs, no change history, at any point. Four unmeasured edges were crossed to reach this conclusion. The specific depleted resource is unverified, and so is the basic question of whether cartservice restarted during the window. Start there — cartservice memory and its cache-backend connection/thread pool, plus restart counts.

Second, both change-history queries ran the wrong direction in time. A pre-onset change to cartservice or its cache backend remains unqueried, which is not the same as clean. Re-run change history backward from onset and expand past zero hops.

Third, true onset is unresolved. Error elevation predates the alert by roughly half an hour and incident peaks match baseline peaks, so it is genuinely open whether this is a new failure or a step-up in the frequency of pre-existing bursts. Pulling the baseline window further back would settle it.

Fourth, no latency percentiles, request rates, or per-dependency client-side latency were ever retrieved for either service. Given the traces, latency is unlikely to be the story — but it was never checked.
