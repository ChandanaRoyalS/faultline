# Checkout failures traced to a cart dependency that stopped serving after an image swap

## What was visible, and the first dead ends

Three alerts arrived together at T+0: checkoutservice, frontend, loadgenerator. The seed was named as checkoutservice, which shaped the first hour of work and turned out to be misleading - the seed was a witness, not the suspect.

A change-history query scoped to checkoutservice over the preceding day came back empty: no deploys, config edits, flag flips, or partial rollouts. That closed the bad-build-on-the-alerting-service story, but note the scoping - seed only, zero hops, so none of the nine direct dependencies were covered.

The error-ratio metric nearly sent us the wrong way. A single sample just before the alert hit roughly two-thirds errors and tripped a detector. It was noise: the incident-window mean sat below the at-rest baseline mean, the minimum was zero, the standard deviation exceeded the mean, and the peak was an exact 2/3 - a handful of requests in a two-minute bucket. Two hypotheses could not be closed at all here, because only the error-ratio template was returned: no throughput and no latency series, so a p95 regression and a traffic surge were never actually measured.

checkoutservice's own logs showed only informational PlaceOrder entries across the alert, with requests still being accepted. The loudest line in the file - a fatal Kafka broker connection refusal on port 9092 - is timestamped about T-30m and is immediately followed by a full startup sequence. That is an earlier, self-healed restart, not the cause.

## Traces reframed it as an absence

Every error trace from about T+45s onward terminates at the checkoutservice client span for CartService/GetCart. It is the only ERROR-marked leaf, carries essentially all of its 2-4ms as self-time, and - decisively - has no cartservice server-side child span beneath it at all. The error propagates unchanged up through PlaceOrder to frontend's HTTP POST, which explains why three services alerted at once.

The failing traces are truncated to about five spans: checkout aborts in cart preparation. That retired a shelf of candidates at once - payment, currency, accounting, shipping/quote, and email were eliminated because the request never reached them, not because they measured healthy. Cart-side Redis work was cleared too: no HGET span is emitted, so the break is on the edge into cartservice. checkoutservice's own logic was cleared at 0.1ms self-time. A separate, non-error observation from healthy traces ten minutes earlier - the shipping-to-quote hop at 18-22% of self-time - is unrelated latency and a dead end here.

## The cause, and what remains open

Re-run against cartservice, the change log was the opposite of empty: 33 entries in 24 hours, all from platform-automation, in a repeating cycle of image update, revert, a 300ms interface-delay container attach, detach, and a non-default REDIS_ADDR. Two tempting entries are dead ends: every REDIS_ADDR edit was reverted, the last about 2.8 hours before onset, and the delay container was detached about 3.1 hours before onset. What survives is one entry - an image reference update to a hotfix tag at T-3m30s, the only change in the window never reverted.

cartservice's log stream ends at that exact moment with an orderly .NET hosting-lifetime shutdown banner, after serving normal traffic to the last second, and no startup banner ever follows. The old container stopped for the swap; the replacement never came up serving. Its metrics returned no samples at all, but the at-rest baseline is equally empty - that series was never present, so the silence is neither an onset signal nor evidence of health.

Conclusion, high confidence: the artifact now referenced for cartservice is not running and serving. Fix class is rollback. Still open: no orchestrator evidence, so whether the new container is stuck pulling, restarting, or failing readiness is unverified; the roughly 3.5-minute gap between shutdown and alert is unexplained and alert lag is only plausible; and one later error trace contains frontend spans only, with no checkoutservice span, hinting at a second failure mode at the frontend-to-checkout hop that was never examined.
