# Frontend gRPC connections failing to an unnamed downstream dependency

## What was visible, in order

Offsets run from the page (T+0). Three alerts landed together: frontend, loadgenerator, checkoutservice. Twelve services in the blast radius, severity critical, four unmeasured edges crossed — so much of what follows is inference across gaps.

First look was frontend's error ratio against the prior hour. Real shift: mean roughly 1.8% to 7.3%, about fourfold, incident mean above the baseline maximum, so not an alerting artifact. The shape mattered more than the size — bursty, peaking near 0.42, meaning most requests still succeeded and frontend was never down. Three threshold crossings: first about fifty-six minutes before the page, largest about thirty-seven minutes before, and one more roughly two minutes before the window closed. It had not self-resolved.

Frontend's logs gave the one solid finding. A single repeating signature dominates: client-side gRPC status 14 UNAVAILABLE with a detail that no connection was established, followed by a grpc-js stack trace, present at the oldest retained line about thirty-two minutes pre-page and still firing every one to three seconds at the window edge. That places the failure on an outbound dependency call, not internal logic, and makes it a connection-establishment problem rather than a slow callee — a deadline-exceeded would look different. But no service name, host, port, or method appears anywhere in the retained lines. The callee is anonymous and cannot be recovered from this evidence.

## Dead ends worth keeping

checkoutservice drew real effort and is not the source. Its error ratio moved 1.31x in the mean — inside one standard deviation of either window, against a baseline that was already noisy at ~9.4%. It does show two discrete spikes reaching two-thirds of calls erroring, one adjacent to the page, plus two intervals with no traffic at all. Read those as a caller that could not connect, not a callee that broke. Its logs back this up: only info-severity order-intake lines through the retained tail, right to the window edge, zero errors or warnings after the top of the hour. The single email-service 500 warning is timestamped roughly thirty minutes pre-page and never recurs — chasing it is wasted time.

Both change-log queries returned nothing at all for frontend and checkoutservice. The trap is the window: both start at the page and run forward about a day. They rule out a concurrent or later change, and rule out crediting any recovery to a rollback. They say nothing about the hours before onset. Do not read them as 'no change was involved'.

Also ruled out along the way: frontend fully down (peak under half), degradation predating the window (baseline stayed below threshold), and a reachable-but-slow dependency (the status detail contradicts it).

## Still open — start here

Which service is frontend failing to reach? This is the whole investigation and it needs frontend's environment and connection config, traces spanning the failing calls, or connection-level logs. None were pulled.

Is the candidate callee running at all? Pod status, restarts, listener and port state, and its own saturation metrics were never queried. Given the signature is connection establishment, this is cheap and probably decisive.

Was anything changed in the hour before onset? Re-run the change history pointed backwards. Two smaller threads: checkoutservice logs intake with no payment or queue-write completion in the final minutes, silently at info severity — trace one order end to end. And both log queries were truncated to the oldest and newest lines, so a lower-volume second signature in the middle of the window remains unexamined.
