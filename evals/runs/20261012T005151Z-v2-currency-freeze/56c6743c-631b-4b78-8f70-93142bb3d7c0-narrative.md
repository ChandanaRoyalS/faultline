# Critical multi-service alert storm seeded at fraud-detection, no culprit established

## What the responder saw first

The page arrived as a critical, eleven-service blast radius with eight services alerting at once: fraud-detection, accounting, currency, email, frontend-proxy, payment, quote and shipping. Triage seeded on fraud-detection because it sat upstream of most of the alerting set and because eight edges between it and the alerting services had never been measured. Treat that seed as a convenience, not a finding — nothing in the alert payload itself pointed at fraud-detection as the origin. Onset is taken as T+0; all offsets below are relative to it.

## First line of inquiry: is the seed service itself broken?

The obvious hypothesis was that fraud-detection started erroring at T+0 and pushed failure outward. It does not hold. Span-derived error ratio over the incident window came in below the preceding baseline window — roughly 0.10 against 0.17 — with no sustained departure flagged. The ratio is noisy in both windows (values swinging across the full range, standard deviation near 0.3), which is what you expect when per-interval span volume is small enough that one bad span moves the number; that noise is a reason not to over-read the improvement, but it is not a reason to read an increase that is not there. A secondary worry — that fraud-detection looked healthy only because it had gone silent — also failed: intervals with no spans at all were *less* frequent during the incident (5 of 128) than during baseline (12 of 121). So the metric view says neither "started erroring" nor "went dark."

## Logs: clean, and then nothing

The fraud-detection log stream over the same window contains only routine Kafka consumption lines, one per consumed order, with a running counter. No exceptions, no stack traces, no timeouts, no connection failures, no startup banner. The counter advances monotonically from about 11714 to about 11900 with no reset and no sequence gap, which rules out a restart or an out-of-memory kill during the window and rules out a broker disconnect, consumer-group eviction or rebalance. Throughput looks steady in both the oldest and newest retained segments, around 0.1–0.14 records per second. The one thing that stands out is where the stream ends: the newest retained line is at T-5m45s, roughly nine minutes before the end of the query window, even though the query keeps the newest lines. That is a silence, not an error.

## Traces: the same silence, earlier

Ten sampled fraud-detection traces all root at fraud-detection itself with a receive operation and a single short in-process child. No inbound parent span exists in any of them, so the caller cannot be identified from this side — attribution of the eight downstream alerts has to come from the callers' own traces, which were never collected. No span carries an error status. Root durations spread from about 7ms to 100ms, but all of that spread is self-time in the receive span; there are no outbound dependency spans at all, which kills the "fraud-detection was slow because something it calls was slow" line. Trace activity stops at T-8m and never resumes through the end of the window. There is no latency creep approaching onset and no inbound surge; durations vary non-monotonically, short traces appearing both early and late.

## Dead end: flagd

flagd was pursued as a plausible common-mode cause for a wide, simultaneous blast radius. Three separate looks came back empty or clean. Its error ratio over the incident window was about half baseline (≈0.27% vs ≈0.47%), with a lower peak and lower variance and no sustained departure; sample counts matched baseline exactly, so flagd was not silently failing to report. Its log stream returned zero lines for the entire window — but note this is an absence across the whole period, not a cutoff at onset, so it most likely reflects a label or shipping mismatch rather than a flagd problem. And its change history was empty. flagd should be considered off the table for this incident, with the caveat that its request-rate and latency series were never retrieved.

## Dead end that is really a gap: change history

Change history was queried for both flagd and fraud-detection and both came back empty. The important detail — and the one a future responder should not repeat — is that both queries ran a window starting at the onset timestamp and extending roughly twenty-four hours *forward*. They therefore answer "was anything changed during or after onset?" (no, for these two services) and say nothing whatsoever about the hours preceding onset. A pre-onset deploy or config push, to these or any other service, remains completely unexamined. The empty result is real; the question it was meant to answer was not asked.

## Where this landed

No culprit was established. The seed service is not failing by any measure taken: error ratio down, consumer offset monotonic with no errors, traces clean and dependency-free. The only positive signal is the silence — logs stop at T-5m45s, traces at T-8m, while other telemetry continues. A process that stops producing work, never errors, logs nothing, and is not recorded as restarting or killed is most consistent with fraud-detection being stopped or stalled while still appearing present, with the eight downstream services alerting a couple of minutes later as their calls hung to deadline. Confidence in that reading is low. The fix class, if it is right, is a restart.

## Why the conclusion is weak, and what to do differently

Three things undercut it. First, the timing does not fit: a genuine stall around T-8m should have tripped callers sooner than T+0, and the six-to-nine minute lead is unexplained. Second, the silence may not be real — Loki retention and Tempo sampling can truncate the newest portion of a window, producing exactly this shape. Confirm by re-running the same queries with a shifted or widened window and checking whether the apparent end-of-stream moves with it. Third, no caller-side evidence exists at all: accounting, payment, checkout, frontend-proxy and the rest contributed no traces, logs or metrics, so whether they were hanging on fraud-detection or failing independently is unknown. Also unretrieved: any resource metrics for fraud-detection — memory, CPU, file descriptors, thread counts — which means a stall from internal exhaustion cannot be separated from an external stop. Next responder: start with caller-side traces into fraud-detection, then re-query change history over the hours *before* onset.
