## Reading it

**The clearest practical result of the series.** Putting the specialists on the smaller model
cut the total cost by about a fifth with an interval that stays well away from zero, and cut
the time to report too, while accuracy showed no measurable effect on either axis - culprit
service moved up, fault class did not move. **The registered prediction (cost 20-45 % lower,
accuracy inside the MDE) held.**

**What it does not license.** "No measurable effect" at an MDE this size is not "no effect": a
loss smaller than the MDE could hide in it. Making Sonnet specialists the default would be a
stamp-free change to production behaviour, and by this project's rules it needs its own scored
comparison - at the registered scale, with holdout untouched - before it lands. This report is the
reason to run that comparison, not a substitute for it.

## What this does not establish

- **A size.** Ten dev scenarios at R = 1 resolve nothing smaller than the MDE above, and the
  interval beside every difference says how little is pinned down. The plan asked for the full
  catalog at R = 5; the owner's scale (12 scenarios, then 11, then 10 after one was removed) is
  registered in `docs/DEVIATIONS.md`.
- **Anything about holdout.** Dev scenarios only, by ADR-0008: an ablation's result is what tuning
  reads.
- **A control without noise.** F is one run per scenario. The A/A table in `docs/ABLATIONS.md`
  shows the same configuration changing its mark on half the scenarios between two runs nine days
  apart, so any single scenario's difference here can be noise; only the paired difference over
  all ten is read.
- **Anything about another world.** OTel Demo 2.2.0 on the Mac, at `cap:91279a09`; nothing here is
  about SREGym.
