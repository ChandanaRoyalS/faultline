# Cart path carrying a fixed ~300ms per-hop delay; checkout errors follow

## What was visible, and the dead ends

The page named cartservice, checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator at once, which reads like a platform-wide problem. The shared element is narrower: all four sit on the cart call path. Four unmeasured edges were crossed to reach cartservice.

The first hour went into a metrics detour that produced nothing. The cartservice error-ratio query returned no samples in the incident window — and none in the baseline window either. Two attempts, same empty result. The trap: an empty result is not a zero reading. With the denominator absent too, there was no evidence of zero errors, only absence of measurement, so cartservice could be neither cleared nor implicated. Nor was the gap an incident signal, since the pre-onset window was equally empty; this is a standing instrumentation or label-naming gap. Note also that only the error-ratio template actually ran: cartservice latency, saturation and restart series were never retrieved. Skip this path and go to traces.

Logs were calm — routine get, add-item and empty-cart entries running seconds apart through onset to the end of the window, no errors, panics, connection-refused lines or startup banners. That killed crash/restart, unreachable datastore (mutations were still committing), and bad-config-blocking-startup. Caveat: the result was truncated to the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines, so roughly the first half hour is unobserved. A minor oddity — some requests arriving with an empty user identifier — looked suspicious briefly and turned out to be routine.

## Traces gave the shape; the change log gave the cause

Ten traces came back, all clustered in the final half minute of the window, with nothing from the earlier thirty minutes — so no baseline sample here. Every cartservice Redis operation, HGET and HMSET alike, sat at ~301–307ms self time across eleven spans, a spread of a few milliseconds: uniform, essentially zero jitter. cartservice's own server spans contributed ~0.4–1.2ms; total duration equalled the sum of Redis children plus about a millisecond. Callers carried the same hole per round trip — frontend client spans ~301–319ms above their child, checkoutservice GetCart/EmptyCart ~904/912ms with children starting ~606ms after the parent. Totals scaled with hop count: ~605ms for one GetCart, ~1510–1542ms for two hops, ~2467ms for full checkout. Additive per-hop, not one lump.

That ruled out cartservice code, GC, locks or serialization (self time sub-millisecond); write-path specifics such as fsync (reads and writes in the same band); anything cluster-wide (shipping ~13ms, currency, payment, catalog and email all low-millisecond in the same trace); and queueing, retries or pool exhaustion (normal 4/8/36 span counts, tight clustering rather than long tails).

The change history showed twenty-three entries, all from one platform-automation actor on a repeating apply/revert cycle every four to five hours. Three candidates were discarded: a hotfix image applied ~24 minutes before onset but reverted ~13 minutes before; a REDIS_ADDR override applied and reverted roughly four hours earlier, with no cache change inside the hour before onset; and no deployment roll or pod replacement at onset at all. What did land was a traffic-shaping container attached to cartservice's existing network namespace about three minutes before onset, imposing a fixed ~300ms egress delay on eth0 with no jitter. In every prior cycle a removal follows. Here it is absent, so the delay was still active at onset.

## Impact, conclusion, and what stays open

checkoutservice makes the most cartservice round trips per transaction and accumulated the most wait: error ratio ran ~170x baseline (mean ~0.0011 before, ~0.179 during, peaking near 0.67 against a prior maximum of 0.025). So this was not latency-only — a real fraction of checkouts terminated in error — and not baseline noise.

The mechanism is imposed wait on the cartservice call path: a fixed, zero-jitter ~300ms delay per network round trip, left in place because the teardown half of an automated cycle never ran. Running the missing removal stops it. Fix class: configuration revert. Confidence high, on the exact match between trace signature and change signature, with cartservice itself healthy and error-free.

Three things stay open. checkoutservice's only error-ratio change point sits about 22 minutes before onset — roughly 18 minutes before the shaping container existed — so an earlier phase of the same automation cycle may contribute an unexplained error component. The errors were also bursty rather than a flat step, with dispersion as large as the mean and some intervals carrying no traffic at all, which sits awkwardly with a constant per-call delay. And there is no pre-onset baseline: no cartservice traces before the last half minute and truncated logs for the preceding half hour mean nothing confirms the 300ms floor was absent earlier. checkoutservice logs and traces were never dispatched, so whether its errored calls were deadline-exceeded or something else is unverified.
