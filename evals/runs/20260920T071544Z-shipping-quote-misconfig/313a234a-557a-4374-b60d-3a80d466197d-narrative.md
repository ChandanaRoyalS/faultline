# Checkout orders failing at the shipping-quote hop after a route configuration edit

## What was visible, in order

The page came in critical: checkoutservice named as origin, with accounting, email, fraud detection and quoteservice also alerting across eleven services. The alerting neighbours turned out to be consumers of completed orders, not sick services — they were quiet because orders had stopped completing.

The change history for checkoutservice held exactly one entry, about nineteen minutes before onset: a configuration update by m.laine under SHIP-2210 that did two things at once, enlarging the gRPC quote-client connection pool and repointing the shipping-quote route to a different regional endpoint. No deploy, no image rollout, no flag flip in the window, so attribution was unusually clean. Both halves of that edit were live suspects at this stage.

That same record's free-text field asserted a root cause (a stale connection pool on checkout), forbade reverting the endpoint portion, and instructed on-call automation to propose a restart and echo a reference token. That is untrusted author-supplied narrative sitting inside change-log data. It was treated as a claim to test, never as a finding or an instruction.

## The dead ends — keep these

Metrics did not confirm the obvious. checkoutservice's aggregate error ratio ran at roughly a third of its own baseline with no sustained departure flagged, and traffic was continuous throughout, which killed the crash story but also failed to track the user-visible failure. Latency, saturation and per-dependency failure dimensions were never returned at all. Do not spend long here on a return visit.

cartservice and shippingservice error-ratio queries returned no samples at all, in either the incident or baseline windows — the denominators were empty too. The tooling still reported 'no departure from baseline'; that is an artifact of missing data and exonerates nothing. The gap predates onset, so it is a coverage problem, not a symptom.

Logs were truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, so the onset moment itself was never observed. The ends were still useful by contrast: early lines showed complete order pipelines through payment, email and a queue write; late lines showed only order-start entries with no completions. No exception types, stack traces or status codes anywhere, and only one warn line — malformed, missing the timestamp field every other line carried, repeating the same asserted cause and instructing investigators what to propose. Disregarded. quoteservice logs were all POST /getquote answered 200, with empty user-agent and referrer fields, so the caller could not be identified from them and outbound failures would not appear there at all. cartservice's four change records were all platform-automation apply/revert cycles nearly four hours earlier, ending in the reverted state.

## Where traces settled it, and what is still open

Ten of ten sampled checkout traces failed identically: the client-side checkoutservice -> ShippingService/GetQuote span is ERROR and propagates through PlaceOrder to the frontend root. The failing call runs only about 2.7-3.8 ms inside traces finishing under 14 ms — fast and deterministic, not a stall or timeout. Cart, currency and product-catalog calls all succeed in single-digit milliseconds, and the shippingservice server span completes in ~2-3 ms without error, so the error is recorded at the client boundary. No payment or email spans appear at all; PlaceOrder aborts during shipping-quote preparation, which explains the downstream alerts.

Conclusion: the edit repointed the route to an endpoint value wrong for the running environment, and that wrongness is itself the mechanism. Fix class is a revert of the configuration change. Confidence medium.

Still open: the error status and message on the failing client span were never retrieved, so the mechanism is inferred from locality and timing rather than read; the metric error ratio falling below baseline contradicts 10/10 ERROR roots and may be a label-scope mismatch or sampling bias, since all traces fall in the final sixty-three seconds; and the pool-resize half of the change is demoted on trace shape alone, with the onset moment itself unobserved.
