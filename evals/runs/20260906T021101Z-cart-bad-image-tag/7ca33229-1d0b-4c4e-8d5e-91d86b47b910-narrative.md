# Checkout orders failing at the cart lookup

## What was visible, in order

Alerts fired at frontend, loadgenerator and checkoutservice; twelve services in the blast radius, severity critical. All offsets below are from that first frontend alert (T+0).

The first pass measured the alerting services and found nothing. Frontend's error ratio in the half-hour around onset was statistically indistinguishable from the half-hour before it — same mean, same spread, same peaks, bursty in both. That result is aggregated by service only, so it could not attribute errors to any downstream path; it is why frontend looked clean early and stayed only-the-caller at the end. The same short-baseline comparison run against checkoutservice was actively misleading: its error ratio was slightly lower in the window than in the preceding 32 minutes, with an identical maximum. We briefly wrote checkoutservice off on that basis. The comparison was wrong because the failure had already begun inside the period being used as baseline, and three of the four requested dimensions (latency, per-dependency counts, saturation) came back empty without our noticing.

Widening the baseline to two hours reversed the read. Across 488 baseline samples checkoutservice's error ratio was flat zero with zero variance; in the window it averaged about 6.4% with peaks near two-thirds of calls. Crucially the shape was wrong for a single cutover — three separate threshold crossings at roughly T-83m, T-54m and T-2m, low mean with high spread. That rules out a hard-down period (successful traffic continued) and a one-shot event, and it means the first onset predates the alert by well over an hour.

Traces are the load-bearing evidence. Forty sampled PlaceOrder traces share one five-span shape and all terminate at checkoutservice's CartService/GetCart span with ERROR status, which propagates up through checkoutservice PlaceOrder to the frontend HTTP POST. No payment, shipping-quote, currency, email or message-publish span appears anywhere. Durations matter as much as statuses: GetCart 2-5ms, PlaceOrder 2-5ms, frontend POST 3-15ms. Nothing accumulates. This is an immediate error return, not a hang, and checkoutservice has no self-time of its own.

Logs showed the process alive and still accepting orders every few seconds, with no error lines, no exception types and no named downstream target — info severity only. In the healthy portion each order produced four lines (initiation, payment with transaction id, confirmation email, message write). From about T-1m only the initiation line appears. Read from logs alone this looks like a silent stall; the traces prove it is a fast error that simply writes no log. Both USD and CAD orders appear before and after, so currency does not partition success from failure.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Four change-history queries were run — checkoutservice, frontend, productcatalogservice, paymentservice — and every one came back completely empty: no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips. On that basis we closed out concurrent-deploy, flag-flip and config-edit explanations for each, plus the possibility that any recovery came from a logged rollback.

That is weaker than it looks, and it is the most important caveat here. All four windows *start* at onset and run about 24 hours forward. None cover the hours before onset, which is precisely where a triggering change would sit. The emptiness is evidence about the period during and after, not before.

Other paths that went nowhere: payment as the stuck dependency (its span is never entered); email or the order-published message holding PlaceOrder open (zero traces reach them); shipping quote or currency conversion as a latency source (the enclosing preparation span holds only the cart child and returns in milliseconds); a partial outage from one bad replica or particular cart contents (every sampled trace fails identically); upstream collapse of order intake (initiations continue at normal cadence throughout); and timeout or thread-starvation theories, which the millisecond durations kill outright.

## Where we landed, and what to do next

We attributed this to cartservice rejecting GetCart calls fast and intermittently. The supporting shape: errors are fast rather than slow, new rather than chronic, partial and recurring across three crossings rather than one clean transition. That fits a capacity limit — connection pool, threads, or cache memory — being reached under load and released. With no change record anywhere to support a bad artifact or a bad value, that inference was the best remaining fit. Frontend is only the caller; checkoutservice is healthy on everything actually measured.

Confidence is low, bluntly because cartservice was never queried. No logs, metrics, change history or saturation signal exists for the service we blamed. The exhausted resource is inferred, not observed, and the proposed restart is a guess consistent with that inference rather than something the evidence demands.

If this recurs: first dispatch directly on cartservice, since everything above rests on a service nobody looked at. Second, capture the gRPC status code and message that GetCart returns — that one field separates a capacity limit from a connection refused to a wrong endpoint, and we never obtained it. Third, re-run change history with a window that *ends* at onset. And do not baseline against the thirty minutes before an alert; here the failure was already inside that window.
