# Cart store endpoint override leaves cartservice crash-looping; checkout orders hang silently

## What was visible, and the dead ends

Three services paged at once: frontend, loadgenerator, checkoutservice. Take T+0 as declared onset. The first move — comparing error ratios against the preceding hour — pointed the wrong way. Frontend's aggregate error ratio came back around a third of baseline with no sustained departure; checkoutservice gave the same answer, with identical peak ratios in both windows, meaning its spikiness predates the incident. That is the trap of this record: two of the three alerting services were quieter than usual. Both queries were aggregate-only, with no per-endpoint or per-dependency split and no latency series at all, so what they genuinely exclude is narrow — a broad error surge, and a downstream that hard-fails with errors. They say nothing about a downstream that hangs.

Second dead end: change history for frontend, checkoutservice and productcatalogservice all came back completely empty. But every one of those queries ran from onset forward roughly twenty-four hours. They establish that nothing landed at or after onset, that no rollout was worsening things, and that no silent remediation was applied. The hours before onset were never queried for any of the three; they are change-free after the fact, not exonerated.

Third dead end: cartservice's error-ratio query returned zero samples in both the incident window and the three-hour baseline. Because the baseline is equally empty, this is not a series going dark at onset — the metrics simply are not emitted for that service. An empty result was briefly mistaken for a healthy one. Adjudication had to move to logs.

## What the logs showed

Checkout's log stream carried the shape of the failure. About an hour before onset, each order request is followed within tens of milliseconds by a full set of downstream completion lines — payment authorization, confirmation email, message-write offset. From roughly T-1m30s onward only the request-entry line survives; the completion lines vanish. Entry lines continue at steady cadence past onset at volume comparable to or above the early window, which rules out checkout crashing and rules out an upstream traffic collapse. Nothing fails loudly: there are no error, warning or exception lines anywhere and no callee is named. Requests enter and hang. Note the stream was truncated to oldest and newest lines, leaving roughly the middle hour unobserved, so onset at checkout is only loosely bounded.

Cartservice's logs gave the mechanism. Half an hour before onset it was serving cart operations cleanly. From about T+30s to the end of the window it is in a continuous crash-restart loop: dial the redis-cart host, fail after five to twenty-five seconds, throw an unhandled ApplicationException out of RedisCartStore.EnsureRedisConnected inside InitializeAsync, terminate. The dial target is port 6380 with TLS disabled. Because the throw comes from startup initialization rather than request handling, this is not pool exhaustion with the service staying up — it is not serving at all. No handshake or auth error text appears, so it fails at connect; the messages are too generic to separate refusal from timeout. Cart being unreachable is why checkout stalls without erroring, and why the upstream error ratios fell rather than rose.

## The change, the conclusion, and what is still open

Cartservice was the one service with change activity: five records, all from platform-automation, all touching a single environment variable naming the cart store address. No image rollout, no flag flip, no other config key, no human actor. The last record is an environment update applied about two and a half minutes before onset, setting the address to the non-default port that the cart logs show being dialed. The other four are the same variable set and reverted three times over the preceding couple of hours; neither state held more than about half an hour, and the service entered onset in the overridden state — the last action was not a rollback to something known good. Each revert unset the variable rather than restoring an explicit value, so the two states are different resolution paths, not two addresses.

Conclusion: the configuration value names a cart-store endpoint that will not accept the connection, cartservice cannot finish startup, and every cart-dependent request stalls behind it. Confidence high; fix class is a configuration revert.

Still open. Nobody checked the Redis side — it is unknown whether redis-cart listens on 6379, on 6380, or is down; if the server genuinely moved and is unhealthy, the value was right and this is a store outage with a different fix. Why the automation flapped the variable five times is unexplained, so a naive revert may be re-applied by the same loop within the hour, and since reverts unset rather than restore, the compiled-in default it would fall back to is unknown. No latency series was ever pulled; the hang hypothesis rests on absent log lines. Triage named twelve affected services and crossed four unmeasured edges, but only four were dispatched — impact on the other eight is unverified.
