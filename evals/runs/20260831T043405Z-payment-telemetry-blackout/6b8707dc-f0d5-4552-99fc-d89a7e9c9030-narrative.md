# Payment service alert with no established cause: container replacement hidden inside a truncated log interval

## What the responder saw first

The page named paymentservice, and that is where every dispatch in this investigation went. The stated blast radius covered three services and crossed two edges that were never measured, but only paymentservice was ever queried. That asymmetry is the single most important thing to carry away from this record: the conclusion is not that paymentservice was healthy and therefore the incident was imaginary, it is that we spent the whole investigation looking at the one service that kept coming back clean and never looked at the other two.

Offsets in this record are relative to the start of the investigation window; the alert point sits at roughly T+10m.

## The obvious candidate, and why it collapsed

The change history for the window returned exactly one entry: at about T+4m, roughly six minutes ahead of the alert, platform-automation set an environment variable pointing the OTLP traces exporter at a loopback address, where no value had been set before. Machine-applied, telemetry-configuration only, no image or version bump, no secret or certificate rotation, no library upgrade, no human operator edit. For a responder arriving cold this looks like the answer — a fleet-wide automation rollout landing minutes before a critical page.

It was not the answer, at least not here. Both plausible mechanisms were tested directly and both failed. If the exporter had stalled against a loopback address with nothing listening, spans would have gone missing; instead paymentservice spans appear in every sampled checkout trace across the change boundary, and an OTLP export span itself completed in about 1.5ms. If export had back-pressured the request path, latency would have stepped up after T+4m; instead the Charge server spans stay flat at roughly 0.2ms with internal charge work at effectively zero, and the caller-side view from checkoutservice agrees at 1.4–1.7ms with no bimodality.

Worth preserving as a live thread rather than a closed one: export worked *for paymentservice*. That says nothing about whatever else that automation touched, where a local collector may not have been listening.

## Metrics: two passes, same flat line

The error-to-total call ratio for paymentservice was queried twice over the window and came back flat at zero — 57 sample points on one pass, 61 on the other, minimum and maximum both zero. Because the ratio was defined rather than absent at every point, the denominator was non-zero throughout, which means the service was continuously receiving and completing traffic. Three hypotheses die on that evidence: paymentservice was not returning errors to callers, it was not down or crash-looping to the point of serving nothing, and there was no brief error burst being smoothed away by averaging.

The dead end worth recording is what did *not* come back. Both metric dispatches asked for latency percentiles, CPU and memory utilization, connection-pool saturation, absolute request volume, and the container restart counter. None of those were returned on either pass. The queries also narrowed to a fifteen-minute slice rather than the full hour requested. So a resource-starvation story around the restart is unfalsified, not excluded — nobody actually looked.

One consequence to note before moving on: since the error series never left zero, whatever fired the alert was not an error-rate threshold. The triggering condition remains unidentified.

## The logs, and the hole in them

Two log dispatches, and both returned the same shape of answer: the oldest eight lines and the newest thirty-two, with everything between them discarded. The dropped interval runs from roughly T+1m to T+12m — which contains the alert point at T+10m entirely. Whatever a shutdown message, a startup banner, an out-of-memory notice or an exporter error would have looked like, it falls inside the part that was thrown away. The silence in these results is an artifact of truncation and carries no information about health.

What the retained ends do show is the one genuinely unexplained fact in the whole record. The pre-gap lines are emitted by a container whose hostname ends 213d; the post-gap lines, resuming at about T+12m, come from a hostname ending 2c0f. The process id is identical across both, which is what a fresh container start looks like rather than a process restarting in place. The pod was replaced somewhere inside the eleven minutes we cannot see.

Either side of the gap the service looks fine: paired charge-request and transaction-complete records at info level, sub-millisecond service times, multiple card numbers, both USD and CAD, continuous through roughly T+14m. No error or warning lines, and no line naming any downstream target — though that last observation is bounded by the same truncation and should not be read as proof that no dependency call was ever logged.

## Where the traces actually point

The trace dispatch did more than exonerate the payment path — it handed the next responder a direction. End-to-end checkout latency in this window sits at roughly 21–46ms for frontend PlaceOrder, and the bulk of it is in the cart, catalog and currency preparation step (about 14–18ms) and the shipping leg out to quoteservice (about 7–11ms). Payment is around 1.5ms of that total. Every sampled trace shows a completed Charge span followed by downstream ship, empty-cart, email and order-publish spans, i.e. successful checkouts.

That leg was never dispatched. If the incident had a user-visible latency component, this is where the returned evidence says to look, and it is outside the service that was paged.

## Conclusion and what is still open

No cause established; confidence low; no fix class identified. Every dispatch targeted paymentservice and paymentservice comes out exonerated rather than implicated. The one change on it was tested and contradicted. The one anomaly on it — the container replacement — sits precisely inside the interval that truncation destroyed.

For whoever picks this up:

1. Recover the T+1m to T+12m log interval. Re-query with a narrower slice or pagination so truncation cannot eat the middle. That range holds the alert point and any shutdown, startup or exporter lines.
2. Determine why the container was replaced. An out-of-memory kill, a liveness-probe failure, an eviction and a routine reschedule are all consistent with a hostname change and none is distinguished by anything returned here.
3. Get the signals that were asked for and never came back: memory and CPU utilization, connection-pool saturation, restart count, p50/p95/p99.
4. Dispatch on the other two services in the blast radius and characterize the two edges triage crossed without measuring. Neither service was touched.
5. Follow the trace evidence to the quoteservice leg and the cart/catalog/currency preparation path.
6. Check whether the loopback exporter endpoint landed on services beyond paymentservice.
7. Identify what condition actually fired the page, given the error series never left zero.
