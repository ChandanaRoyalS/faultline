# Cart-dependent routes failing at connection setup; cartservice never examined

## What was visible first, and the baseline trap

Three services paged together: frontend, loadgenerator, checkoutservice. Blast radius was tallied at twelve services with critical severity, which set an expectation of something platform-wide. That expectation was wrong and it cost time — loadgenerator paged because it drives frontend, and frontend paged because of a single downstream.

First measurement was error ratio on the two alerting services. Frontend averaged roughly 22% across 18 samples, swinging between about 1.7% and 32.6%. checkoutservice was worse — around 49% mean, peaking near 67%, with samples dipping to zero between bursts. Two readings were tempting and both were wrong: frontend was not down (most requests succeeded throughout), and the wide spread is better read as intermittency than as a ramp or rollout in progress.

The trap worth remembering: both queries reported 'moved from 0' and 'no sustained departure from baseline'. The comparison window returned no samples at all. There was no baseline. Anyone taking those verdicts at face value would have dismissed both services and walked away. Consequently pre-incident state and onset ordering between the two services remain unknown. Both queries also aggregated by service name only — no dependency label, no route label, no latency metric — so they could not name a callee or settle whether latency was involved.

## Two dead ends: change history and the logs' silence

Four change-history queries were run (frontend, checkoutservice, featureflagservice, productcatalogservice) and all four came back empty. That genuinely closes some branches: no rollout in progress, no mid-incident config edit or flag toggle, no silent rollback explaining a symptom shift, no churn-driven instability. But there is a defect not to repeat — every window ran *forward* from onset for about twenty-four hours and never covered the hours *before* it, which was the actual question. 'Nothing changed beforehand' is unqueried, not established.

Frontend logs gave mechanism but not identity. Repeated gRPC client exceptions, status 14 UNAVAILABLE, with a connection-establishment detail, first visible around T+27m and still recurring in the final seconds of coverage. Stack frames sat entirely in the client path and response metadata was empty. That rules out application-level errors, other non-retryable statuses, deadline expiry, and any internal failure in frontend itself. What it does not do is name a host, port, or method — and the output was truncated at both ends, so the callee could not be read off these logs at all.

Checkout logs carried zero error or warn lines; everything retained was info severity. Their value was an absence: early in the window each order start was followed by payment authorization, confirmation email, and a message-write offset. Late in the window only order starts appear. That closed the crash reading (checkoutservice was alive and accepting several orders per minute to the end) and the upstream-break reading (requests were arriving fine).

## Traces named the callee, and what is still open

Traces turned the shape into an answer. Every failing frontend client-side edge in the sample went to hipstershop.CartService — AddItem or GetCart, six traces, all error — each terminating in roughly 0.1 to 0.4ms with no cartservice server span anywhere in the trace. The single failing checkout trace says the same from another angle: its erroring leaf is its own call to CartService/GetCart, while the sibling span preparing order items and shipping quote is clean. That is why checkout logged order starts without completions. Route-level confirmation: failed HTTP spans map exclusively onto cart add, cart view, and checkout, while catalog, ads, and recommendation routes complete.

This is where the remaining suspicions died. Not platform-wide or frontend-side (all other frontend edges clean). Not productcatalogservice, adservice, recommendationservice, or featureflagservice (their spans complete with full fan-out). Not checkoutservice as root — it is a victim of its cart child. And not saturation: calls failing in a fraction of a millisecond with no server span are refused at the door, not exhausting a budget.

Conclusion: cartservice is not accepting connections, intermittently. Confidence medium; expected fix class is a restart. The honest limit — no dispatch ever examined cartservice. Its logs, restart and resource metrics, pod state, and change history are all uncollected, so crash loop versus unstartable artifact versus wrong listen address is unresolved. The health of its backing store (redis-cart) is unchecked and could crash it at startup and present exactly this way. And why the failures come in bursts rather than steadily is unexplained, with no baseline to anchor onset.
