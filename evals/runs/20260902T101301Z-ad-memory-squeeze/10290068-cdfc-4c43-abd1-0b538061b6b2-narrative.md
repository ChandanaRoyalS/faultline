# Frontend error bursts traced to a memory ceiling on the ad service

## What was visible, in order

The page came from frontend and loadgenerator; triage called it critical across seven services with one unmeasured edge that we never identified. T+0 is the reported onset.

The frontend error ratio was the first useful measurement. Its two-hour baseline was exactly zero — zero mean, zero max, no variance — so there was no pre-existing error floor to argue about. During the incident it averaged under one percent but peaked above eleven. That shape mattered more than the peak: a low mean with a high max and high variance is intermittent bursts, not a hard-down dependency, and the error status codes ruled out a latency-only reading. The same series moved our clock, crossing five percent about twenty minutes before the page and again near T-2m, so the trigger did not live in the minute we were handed.

Frontend logs then pointed outward: repeated gRPC code 14 UNAVAILABLE, detail naming a name-resolution failure against the ad service on port 9555, with stack frames in the client receive path rather than frontend's own handlers. Resolution failure means no request ever reached an upstream, so "the ad service errored on our call" was off the table, as were deadline and auth codes.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Change history for frontend, cartservice and productcatalogservice all came back empty, which felt like progress. The trap is the window: every one of those queries ran from the incident timestamp forward about twenty-four hours, never backward. They say only that nothing landed during or after onset, which was not the question. Read them as unasked, not answered.

We also queried the ad service's span-derived error ratio hoping to time its dropouts. Zero samples in the incident window — and zero in the baseline too. That symmetry is the finding: the series does not exist for this service, rather than having vanished at onset. We briefly read the emptiness as telemetry dying with the pods; that reading is wrong. The query carried no readiness, endpoint or restart series, so it neither supports nor refutes restarts.

Both log results were truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, leaving the long middle of each window unobserved — including the reported minute on frontend and the earlier threshold crossing on the ad service.

## What it was, and what is still open

Ad service logs were the turn. Around T-30m it was serving ad requests normally. From about T-3m30s the stream is almost nothing but JVM and OTel startup banners — six sequences inside one minute, then a steady one-per-minute cadence for five minutes. Not silence, not a single death, not one rollout: a restart loop. Notably absent are any kill notice, crash stack or clean-shutdown line.

Change history for that service held three entries, all from platform automation, all the same knob: the container memory limit. It was lowered to 256m about three minutes before onset, and the identical lowering had been applied roughly twenty-one minutes earlier and reverted fifteen minutes before onset — a flap matching the two threshold crossings. No deploy, config edit, scaling or DNS change is recorded.

Mechanism: under the 256m ceiling the process cannot hold its working set, the endpoint set empties during each restart, and only the ad-bearing fraction of frontend requests fails — the burst pattern we saw. Fix class is reverting the change, though the automation loop reapplied it once already.

Open: the kill itself was never confirmed — no kill event, working-set or restart-count series was queried. An earlier code 14 roughly forty-five minutes before onset fits no recorded change. And change history for the blast radius still needs re-running backward from onset.
