# Checkout failures traced to a stopped cart tier after an image reference swap

## What we saw, and the first dead end

Three alerts fired together: checkoutservice, frontend, loadgenerator. Blast radius scored twelve services with checkoutservice as the seed. That framing misled the first half of the investigation — the seed is where alarms surfaced, not where the failure lived. Offsets below are relative to the alert edge (T+0).

The first move was to look for a change on checkoutservice. Empty over a ~24-hour lookback: no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips. That ruled out a self-deploy regression and also killed the tempting quick fix of rolling back checkoutservice, since there was nothing to roll back. The trap: the query ran at zero hops, so it said nothing about cart, payment, currency, email or the rest. Reading it as "nothing changed anywhere" would have been fatal. Widening the radius is what produced the answer.

Checkoutservice's own error-ratio metrics were a second dead end. The incident-window mean sat *below* baseline (about 0.076 vs 0.098), both windows noisy enough that the deviation rivalled the mean. One useful signal fell out — a change point at T-1m15s where error ratio jumped to roughly two-thirds of calls, the highest value in either window, with no recovery after. Good onset marker, but no per-dependency breakdown, no rate series, no latency series. It told us when, never which.

## Logs: alive, accepting, never finishing

The checkout-service stream around T+0 held no error or warning lines at all — every retained line an informational PlaceOrder request-start, arriving every few seconds from T-1m47s to T+1m50s. Process up, traffic arriving, no panic or startup entry. That closed off a local crash, an OOM stop, config or validation rejection, and an upstream traffic outage in one pass.

The signal was negative. Earlier in the window each PlaceOrder was paired with completion entries — payment authorization, confirmation email, queue write. In the late window those companions are simply gone. Requests taken in, never completing, silently. Note the result was truncated to oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, so roughly T-30m to T-2m was never returned; the retained lines do cover T+0.

## Traces: the shape that settled it

Ten sampled failing traces were one identical 5-span tree — a single deterministic failure mode. The deepest ERROR span in every one was the leaf: checkoutservice's outbound GetCart call, holding essentially all of its own duration as self time, with the error propagating upward unchanged through PlaceOrder to the frontend HTTP span.

That eliminated three hypotheses at once. The shipping-quote span was not itself ERROR and held only fractions of a millisecond of self time. Checkoutservice's own spans carried near-zero self time, so it was not stalled in its own handler. Frontend spans were ancestors inheriting the error from two levels down.

The four edges the blast radius flagged as unmeasured — accounting, currency, payment, email — produced no spans at all. Checkout aborted inside cart retrieval before any was attempted, so none could be causal. End-to-end latency was 3.0–6.7ms with the cart hop taking 63–74%: a fast fail, not a timeout or saturation.

## The cart tier, and three convincing distractors

The cart-service log stream ends. Last line at T-3m26s, an orderly application shutdown notice; nothing follows. Everything before it is routine get/add/empty handling — no cache or connection failure, no deserialization error. The final record being a lifecycle shutdown rather than a stack trace argues for a managed stop, not a crash.

One second after that notice, at T-3m25s, platform-automation updated cartservice's image reference to a hotfix build tag. That is the change.

The surrounding history is where an hour can go. Twenty-nine entries over ~21 hours, all from the same automation actor, forming a repeating five-step cycle that recurs about every five hours. Two steps look exciting and both were checked against onset and cleared: the last REDIS_ADDR override to an alternate port was applied and reverted ~2.9 and ~2.8 hours before T+0, so the default address was live; a traffic-shaping container on cart's network namespace was attached and removed ~3.2 and ~3.1 hours before T+0, never re-attached. Added egress delay also cannot produce 3ms fast failures. In the cycle before onset only the image step had run.

Cart's own metrics were unmeasurable — no samples in the incident window and none in a baseline three hours earlier. Since the absence is not incident-correlated, it reads as an instrumentation or label gap, not a dropout. So no independent onset time for cart exists, and any claim that cart errors preceded the T-1m15s change point cannot rest on metrics.

## Conclusion and what stayed open

The artifact now referenced for cartservice is not serving after the old process was stopped for the swap. Checkoutservice is healthy throughout — continuous traffic, no error lines, no restart, no change in 24 hours — and the frontend and loadgenerator alarms are the propagated form of the same cart error. Fix class: rollback of the cart image reference. Confidence medium.

Three things were never established. No pod or container lifecycle evidence was collected for cartservice after the shutdown, so whether the new image failed to pull, crash-looped, or came up under a different log label was never observed directly — that is the first thing to gather next time. Cart's rate, latency and error onset remain unmeasurable for want of the metric series. And the ~3.5 minutes between the shutdown and the alerts is unaccounted for; earlier iterations of the recurring automation cycle were never checked, so it is unknown whether prior image steps behaved differently.
