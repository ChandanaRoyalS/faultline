# Partial GetProduct errors surfacing through frontend

## What we saw first

Pages started alerting almost together: frontend, loadgenerator, and productcatalogservice. From the responder's chair the loudest thing was user-visible — the load generator's HTTP parents were going red, so the first instinct was to treat frontend as the offender. The frontend aggregate error-ratio series settled that quickly and in a slightly confusing way. Over the half hour leading to the page, the window mean was about 1.2%, marginally *below* the preceding baseline of ~1.3%, which at a glance reads as "nothing wrong here." The mean was misleading: the series contains a single detected step at T-0m15s where the ratio jumps to roughly 8.5% and peaks near 10%, above anything in the baseline window. Because the excursion only occupies the last few minutes of the window it barely moves the average. Two things came out of that: the excursion is real, and it is a step, not a ramp — no slow climb, no progressive degradation preceding it.

## Narrowing to the edge

Traces from frontend did the real work. Errored traces all ran over one gRPC edge, frontend → productcatalogservice, operation ProductCatalogService/GetProduct. The deepest span carrying error status is the productcatalogservice server-side GetProduct span; the error is then mirrored upward through frontend's client span, frontend's HTTP handler, and the loadgenerator parent. That ordering is the whole argument for the origin being downstream rather than in frontend or the client.

Two properties of the failure were unusually informative. First, it is partial: most GetProduct calls in the same window complete cleanly, and in one recommendation-page trace three of four sibling GetProduct calls succeeded while one errored. Second, there is no timing component at all — every span on the failing edge, errored or not, is single-digit milliseconds, and the slowest frontend work observed was a fully successful ~27ms checkout. Nothing is slow; things simply return errors, a stable minority of the time.

The last detail: every errored GetProduct span contains a child call to FeatureFlagService/GetFlag, and that child never errors. So the failing step sits inside productcatalogservice's own handler, immediately after the flag read.

## Dead ends — read this part

Three lines of inquiry produced nothing, and two of them produced nothing for procedural reasons rather than substantive ones. Keeping them straight matters because an empty result looks like an answer.

Logs: a Loki query across the full window for productcatalogservice returned zero lines. The selector used a hyphenated service name that may not match the label value actually stored. The result was empty, not truncated, and the window comfortably covered the lead-up — so window width is not the limiting factor, but a label mismatch is at least as likely an explanation as a genuine absence of logs. Treat this as unanswered, not as evidence of quiet logs.

Change history: two queries, one on productcatalogservice and one on frontend, both empty. Both were scoped to the seed service only at radius 0, and — importantly — both windows *begin* at onset and run roughly 24 hours forward. They therefore say nothing about the hours before onset, which is exactly the interval a step change would live in. What they do legitimately rule out is any recorded change on those two services during or after onset, including remediation or repeated churn. Neighbours such as featureflagservice were never queried.

Productcatalogservice error metrics: an initial run against a six-hour window starting at onset returned no samples in either the incident or comparison window; the equally empty comparison window suggested a collection or labelling gap rather than a behavioural change. A later run over the tighter window did return data — ~7.2% mean error ratio across 20 samples, peaking near 9.9% — but its baseline window was still empty, so the "rise from zero" is not a measured comparison. That non-zero ratio is enough to keep the service firmly in scope and enough to reject a hard-down reading: nine calls in ten completed normally.

## Where we landed

Requests fail inside productcatalogservice's own GetProduct handler, after a successful feature-flag lookup, at a roughly 7–10% rate, with no latency signature and an abrupt onset step rather than a ramp. The most economical mechanism for a stochastic, fixed-fraction, zero-cost error occurring immediately after a flag read is that a product-catalog failure flag is set to an incorrect value, and each request that reads it as enabled is deliberately failed. Suggested fix class is a configuration revert. Confidence is medium, and the reason it is only medium is stated plainly below.

## Still open

The flag's identity and its value were never actually read. The configuration attribution rests entirely on the shape of the errors, not on an observed setting — a responder picking this up should go look at the flag store first, because it either confirms this in one step or destroys it.

Productcatalogservice logs remain unretrieved; re-query Loki with the correct label spelling. That is the cheapest remaining move.

Change history was only ever examined forward from onset and only at radius 0. Pre-onset changes, and any change on featureflagservice or other neighbours, are unexamined.

Latency, restart counts, and CPU/memory saturation for productcatalogservice were never queried. They are open, not cleared — the trace evidence argues against a timing story but does not substitute for those series.
