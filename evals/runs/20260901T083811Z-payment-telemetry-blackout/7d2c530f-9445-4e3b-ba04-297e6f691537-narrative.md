# paymentservice telemetry goes dark while charges keep succeeding

## What the responder sees first

The page fires on paymentservice, severity critical, with a stated blast radius of three services and two edges crossed that nobody had measurement on. The obvious first read — payments are broken, checkouts are failing — is wrong, and the record is mostly the story of how long that took to establish.

Take T+0 as the critical alert on paymentservice. The only recorded change anywhere near it landed at T-6m: platform-automation, not a human, set paymentservice's OTLP traces exporter endpoint from unset to a loopback address on port 4317. It is an environment-variable update, nothing else. No image rollout, no flag flip, no secret rotation, no repoint of a business dependency. That single record is the spine of the whole incident.

## The signals that looked damning and were not

The span-derived error ratio for paymentservice returns nothing over the fifteen-minute window. The instinct is to read that as catastrophe, but read it carefully: the denominator is empty too. A service serving traffic normally still populates a total-calls series. Absence of both halves means either no span metrics reached the backend from this service, or the service is carrying a different label value or metric name than the one queried. Those two readings were never separated, and the log stream matched service="payment-service" hyphenated, which is direct proof that naming inconsistency exists in this environment. Do not treat the empty series as measured zero traffic.

On the checkoutservice side, every sampled PlaceOrder trace contains a client Charge span of roughly 1.4-2.4ms against a 20-43ms PlaceOrder — and that Charge span is a leaf. No paymentservice server child beneath it. Read alone, a leaf client span looks like the callee never answered. It answered fine; what is missing is the callee's side of the telemetry, not the callee's response.

## What actually held up

The charge path never stopped working. Logs on either side of the interesting interval show paired charge-received and transaction-complete lines, sub-millisecond apart, every one of them info level. No error lines, no validation rejections, no connection-refused text. The same card identifiers that charged before the gap charge after it, across multiple currencies.

Traces on paymentservice do contain server-side gRPC Charge handler spans with their internal charge children, sitting inside end-to-end PlaceOrder traces that continue through ShipOrder, EmptyCart, the confirmation email and the orders publish, with accounting and fraud-detection consumers picking the message up. Nothing aborted at payment.

Where time genuinely accumulates in checkout is the pre-payment leg: prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart at 13-21ms, dominated by ShippingService/GetQuote and its onward HTTP call to quoteservice. That is steady-state shape, not incident. One trace also shows an 18.1ms client ShipOrder against a ~0ms server span — a second, unrelated asymmetry worth a separate look, not part of this.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Changes on checkoutservice: queried, returned completely empty for the full window and for all change types. That closes checkoutservice as a source and also argues the endpoint change was not a fleet-wide push — a fleet push would have left a record here. It says nothing about frontend, which was never queried despite being inside the stated blast radius.

"Nothing changed locally, so this must be an external surge or an upstream failure" — closed by the T-6m change record.

"The problem is downstream of paymentservice, at a card processor" — there is no span subtree past the Charge span in any trace, and the Charge span is short. Nothing to blame down there.

"Cart, catalog or currency broke earlier in checkout" — GetCart, GetProduct and Convert are all sub-3ms with matching server spans present.

"paymentservice stopped emitting spans entirely" — it did not; server spans exist somewhere in the window. But note the trace result was truncated at 200 spans with no per-span timestamps, so it cannot show whether those spans are before or after T-6m. That limitation is what keeps this conclusion at medium confidence rather than high.

## Conclusion and fix

The exporter endpoint value is wrong on its face: it names an address that terminates nothing inside the pod. Everything the incident consists of sits downstream of that — empty span metrics, leaf client spans, a critical page on a service whose business path never faltered. The alert is best read as loss of visibility, not loss of function.

Fix class: revert the configuration value to its prior state (unset) or point it at a real collector endpoint.

## Still open — read this before you trust the above

Both log queries truncated away the interval from T-6m through roughly T+12s to T+2m. The alert-time behaviour — errors, shutdown or start lines, connection-refused text, exporter messages — is unretrieved, not absent. Nothing directly attests what happened at T+0.

The container replacement's cause is unknown. If it was a memory kill driven by an unbounded exporter retry queue blocking on a dead loopback target, the operative mechanism is saturation rather than a wrong value, and the fix class changes. No memory, CPU, exporter-queue or restart-reason evidence was collected.

Nobody verified whether a collector or sidecar actually listens on loopback:4317 in that pod. The endpoint being dead is inferred.

The empty metric series may be partly a label or metric-name mismatch; the two readings were never separated, and hyphenated-versus-unhyphenated naming demonstrably exists here.
