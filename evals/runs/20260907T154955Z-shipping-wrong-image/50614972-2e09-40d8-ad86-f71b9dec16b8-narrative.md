# Checkout orders dying at the shipping-quote step

## What was visible first, and the dead ends

The page arrived critical: eleven services in the radius, six alerting, checkoutservice named as origin. The shape of the alert set was the real clue and was read last. Accounting, email and fraud detection sit downstream of a completed order, so their silence meant orders were not completing, not that they were broken. Shipping and quote alerting a few minutes later was the signal that mattered.

The first move was to ask whether checkoutservice had started erroring. The aggregate error ratio said no: the mean actually fell slightly (~0.82x), ranges overlapped heavily, and the in-window maximum matched baseline. This cost time and was misleading. A flat aggregate was read as excluding both a checkoutservice-side surge and any broad dependency failure. That inference was wrong in a way worth remembering: PlaceOrder is a small share of this service's call volume, so a deterministic 100% failure on one endpoint hides inside a flat ratio. The query also covered neither latency nor a per-dependency breakdown.

The logs half-pointed the right way and were misread. Entry lines continued steadily to the end of the window, so the process was alive and serving; crash-looping and traffic loss were off the table. But the successful-order sequence (payment confirmation, confirmation email, message-write offset) stopped entirely after 15:53. No error or warning lines appeared anywhere, and nothing named a dependency or RPC method. This was taken as evidence of hanging, and a fast status-code rejection was explicitly ruled out on that basis — which the traces later contradicted. Entry-only logging plus silence looks identical whether a request stalls or aborts in two milliseconds. Truncation kept only the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, erasing 14:53–15:53 and with it the onset moment.

A whole branch went into cartservice and returned nothing. Its change log held thirteen entries, all from one automated actor, all in matched update/revert pairs: a Redis address override set and reverted six times, an image reference pointed at a hotfix tag and reverted, a traffic-shaping sidecar attached to the network namespace and removed. None was in effect at onset — image and sidecar were undone 22–23 hours earlier, and the nearest change of any kind was 5.2 hours before onset. The cartservice metric query returned no samples in either the incident window or a baseline three hours prior, so that gap is missing data, not a measured zero, and cannot be a symptom of onset. cartservice logs carried only request-entry lines with no Redis timeout or dial-failure text; no completion lines exist for any request in any period, so 'receipt without completion' is this service's normal log shape and carries no signal. Change history on checkoutservice and productcatalogservice came back completely empty across a window extending a day past onset — no deploys, no config edits, no flags, and no rollback masking the original state. Note that every change query here began at the onset timestamp rather than before it, so none of them can speak to the lead-up.

## The traces, which settled it

The trace query was the turn. Across every PlaceOrder trace, the cart, product catalog and currency child spans completed cleanly in roughly 0.1–2.1 ms with nothing left open — the GetCart span and its Redis HGET child both closed well under a millisecond, and currency conversion closed at about a millisecond on both sides. The hung-child-span premise the log reading had suggested simply did not exist.

The only child span carrying an error status under PlaceOrder was the ShippingService/GetQuote call, and it was fast: 0.3 to 2.5 ms. No payment span, no ShipOrder span, no email span appeared in any trace. PlaceOrder aborts inside the order-items-and-shipping-quote preparation step, before payment is attempted. The error propagates cleanly upward through checkoutservice PlaceOrder, frontend PlaceOrder and the frontend HTTP POST, with end-to-end latency still in the 7–15 ms range. Nothing waited, which rules out timeout and stall outright; this is a deterministic, immediate rejection from shippingservice. A minor distraction: some traces carried extra product catalog lookups with a nested feature-flag call, but they add well under a millisecond and appear in erroring and unremarkable traces alike.

## Where it was left

Conclusion, held at low confidence: PlaceOrder fails at the shipping-quote step, and the mechanism sits on the shipping quote path — shippingservice's own quote computation, which calls quoteservice. That placement fits those two services alerting at 15:56 and the downstream silence on accounting, email and fraud detection, which are never reached. The failure is not attributable to any observed change on checkoutservice, productcatalogservice or cartservice. No fix was identified.

The crucial gap: neither shippingservice nor quoteservice was ever dispatched. Five unmeasured edges were crossed, and the mechanism inside the quote path is inferred from trace shape alone.

Three things for whoever picks this up. First, go straight to shippingservice and quoteservice: what does GetQuote return, and does the error originate in shippingservice or in its outbound call? Second, query change history on both — it was never done — and start the window well before suspected onset, since every change query in this record began at the onset timestamp and can only exclude post-onset activity. Third, onset is unpinned: log truncation removed 14:53–15:53, so the degraded state is only confirmed to exist by 15:53, not to have begun then.
