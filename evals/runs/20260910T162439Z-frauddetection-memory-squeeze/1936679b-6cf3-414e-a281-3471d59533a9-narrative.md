# frauddetection-service silent crash-loop after memory ceiling lowered

## What the page said and what was visible first

The page named a single service, frauddetection-service, at critical severity, and triage put the blast radius at that one service with no unmeasured edges crossed. Treat T+0 as the moment the alert fired. The first thing worth knowing, and the thing that shaped the rest of the hour, is that the alert arrived several minutes after the service had already stopped working: the log record shows the failing behaviour beginning around T-6m. If you are reading this while paging on the same service, do not anchor your search window on the alert timestamp — walk backwards at least ten minutes before you start correlating.

## The metrics dead end (start here, so you can skip it)

The first two moves were span-derived call metrics for the service — error ratio over the incident window against a short pre-incident baseline, then again against a six-hour daytime baseline. Both came back completely empty. No samples in the incident window, and none in either baseline.

That emptiness is not a finding about the incident. Because the baselines are just as empty as the incident window, the series was never there; nothing dropped out at onset. The most likely explanation is a service-label mismatch or missing instrumentation for this service in the metrics backend, but that was never chased down. The practical consequence is severe and worth stating plainly: request rate, latency percentiles, and error ratio were unmeasurable for this service for the entire investigation. A second attempt also used a window that did not even contain the alert timestamp, which cost time for nothing.

Also note what these queries structurally could never answer. The query template carried no container memory, memory-limit, restart-count, or termination-reason dimensions. It was never going to settle the eventual hypothesis, and re-running it against wider windows did not change that.

## What the logs actually showed

Logs were the only signal that carried information. Early in the window the service is healthy and doing its normal work — record consumption from the broker is present through roughly T-29m. In the newest lines that work is entirely gone.

What replaces it is a repeating startup sequence and nothing else: the JVM picking up its tool options, a benign class-sharing warning, and the OpenTelemetry javaagent printing its version banner. Then silence, then the same three lines again. After a burst of tightly spaced restarts around T-6m, the cadence settles into a metronome — one cycle roughly every 62 seconds — and holds that rhythm through the end of the window at about T+1m. At least ten distinct startup sequences appear. There is no recovery inside the window.

Two details matter more than the rest. First, there is no error, exception, stack trace, panic, or fatal line anywhere in those cycles, including at and around T+0. From the application's own point of view the failure is completely silent. Second, every cycle stops at the same place: immediately after the agent banner and before any application framework output. No config load, no broker connection, no pool initialisation, no readiness line. The process is dying before it has done anything it could fail at.

One gap in the evidence: the middle of the log window, roughly T-29m to T-6m, was not returned. The transition from healthy consumption to the loop is bracketed inside that gap, not pinpointed.

## The change history

The change log for this service in the surrounding day contains exactly one kind of entry: a memory-limit adjustment on frauddetection-service, lowering it to 200m, applied and reverted repeatedly by the same automated actor. Five entries total, all attributed to platform-automation, none to an operator or a release pipeline.

The shape of the pattern is what makes it interesting. The same lowered limit was applied roughly 10.2 hours and 3.6 hours before onset, and each of those was reverted within about ten or eleven minutes. The application that landed about six minutes before onset has no matching revert after it — it was still in force when the page fired and remained so.

That asymmetry is the whole argument for the earlier applications being harmless: they existed for ten minutes each and were undone. This one was not.

## What was ruled out along the way

Worth recording so nobody re-walks these.

No code deployment or image rollout. The change history for the service across the window contains only resource-limit entries — no deploy, release, or image-change events at all.

No feature-flag flip. None appear in the window.

Not a shared or platform-wide change. Every recorded entry targets the single object frauddetection-service; nothing multi-service is present.

Not "nothing changed, so look outside." That framing was tempting given the empty metrics, but a change did land six minutes before onset and stayed active.

Not the OpenTelemetry agent. It logs its version successfully on every single cycle. The class-sharing line next to it is a routine JVM warning, not a load failure. Easy to misread when it is one of only three lines you have.

Not degradation. The service is not up-and-slow; the repeated init banners with no work output mean the process is being recreated, not limping.

Not a single restart or deploy blip that self-healed. Ten-plus cycles, and the last one is at the window edge with no recovery.

Not an in-application initialisation error. Death occurs before any application wiring logs, which points at something external terminating the process rather than the code rejecting a value it read.

## Where the investigation landed

A JVM that is killed silently at the very beginning of heap allocation, on a fixed restart delay, with no output of its own, immediately after its memory ceiling was cut to 200m and left cut — is a process being terminated for exceeding the resource it needs. It is not a process failing on a bad value. The bracketing lines up: healthy consumption early in the window, un-reverted limit change at about T-6m, loop onset in the same few minutes, still looping at T+1m.

Confidence is medium, not high, and the reason is stated in the next section. The fix class is a configuration revert: restore the previous memory limit.

## Still open when this was written

The central inference is unconfirmed. No container termination-reason, restart-count, memory working-set, or memory-limit time series was ever retrieved. The kill is inferred from the crash-loop shape plus the change record, not observed directly. If you are on this again, that is the first query to run and it closes the case in one step.

Why the page fired at T+0 when the loop appears to start around T-6m is unexplained. The alert may carry a sustained-condition threshold rather than firing on onset. Worth checking before you trust alert timestamps on this service again.

Whether the automation will re-apply the lowered limit after a manual revert is unknown, as is what drives that automation. A revert may not hold. Watch it, or disable the automation first.

The missing call-metric series for this service is unexplained and predates the incident. Until it is fixed, this service is effectively unmonitored for rate, latency, and saturation.

Downstream impact was never investigated. No consumer of this service was examined. The blast radius of one service is triage's claim, not a verified finding.
