## 1. The decision

§4 registered `recall@3` as the deciding metric (ADR-0040), a one-query band as "no measured
difference", and that **within the band the simpler form stands**.

`recall@3` under flag 1: **0.395 before, 0.395 after. Zero queries.** Inside the band. Flag 0 stands
and flag 1 is not adopted.

That is the whole decision, and it was reached without reference to the rest of the table — which
matters, because the rest of the table is where the temptation is.

---

## 2. What the other flags show, and why they change nothing today

§3 registered that the non-candidates would be **reported and not decisive**. Reported:

**Flag 2 is the first configuration ever measured here that clears the registered floor.**
`recall@5` = 0.605 against a floor of 0.60, argued in T6.4's pre-registration before any
measurement existed and missed by every reading since. It also restores synthesizer `recall@5` to
**0.273** — exactly its pre-Q39 value, which is to say it undoes the regression this row was opened
for.

**And 0.605 is a number already in the record.** Q42's falsification measured `recall@20` = 0.605
under flag 0 — 26 of 43 queries, the same 26. Length normalisation pulls every answer that was
anywhere in the twenty-candidate list into the top five. A perfect reranker over those twenty
candidates would have reached the same place.

**Flag 2 cannot be adopted on this measurement.** Choosing it means measuring six flags against one
43-query golden set and taking the winner: selection on the test set. §3 forbade it in advance,
in these words — *"six draws from a noisy instrument will always produce a winner"* — and ADR-0018
records the same practice as the mistake behind its four unmeasured parameters. The fact that the
winner is large and clears a floor is an argument for confirming it properly, not for skipping the
confirmation.

**Q52** registers the confirmation. The golden set was drawn by taking *the first* 3 planner and 2
synthesizer queries per scenario from a deterministically sorted harvest — a prefix, not a sample —
so the remainder was never drawn and never read. A second draw by the same rule is a genuine
held-out set, and it exists only because the original draw happened to be a prefix.

### 3.1 §1's falsification rule was wrong, and that is the most useful thing here

§1 registered: *"If the synthesizer does not improve, the mechanism is not length, and this row
should be struck rather than retried with another flag."*

Under flag 1 the synthesizer did not improve. By that rule the row should be struck. **But flags 2
and 8 improve it substantially** — 0.182 to 0.273, four queries of 22 to six — so the mechanism
**is** length, and flag 1 was simply too weak a version of it.

The rule conflated *this flag* with *the mechanism*. A falsification attached to one strength of an
effect does not test the effect, and writing it that way would have struck a row whose hypothesis
the same measurement supports. The failure of prediction 7 — `MRR` moving while `recall` did not —
is the same shape: flag 1 reorders without promoting anything past the k = 3 cut.

**What a falsification should have said**: if no flag improves the synthesizer, the mechanism is not
length. That version survives contact with this table.

## 4. What this does not settle

- **Whether flag 2 is real.** That is Q52's whole purpose. 0.605 on the set that chose it is not a
  measurement of 0.605.
- **What happens to Q42.** If flag 2 is confirmed, `recall@5` reaches the ceiling Q42's
  falsification measured for `recall@20`, and a reranker over twenty candidates has **no headroom
  left at k = 5**. Q42 survived its first falsification; it would need a second, against a new
  ceiling measured under the adopted flag.
- **What happens to Q50.** Q50 was opened because nothing above 0.605 can come from ranking. Flag 2
  reaching 0.605 *by ranking* does not raise that ceiling — it exhausts it. Q50's size is unchanged
  and its urgency is higher, because after this there is nothing left on the ranking side.
- **Whether the floor means what it said.** `recall@5 ≥ 0.60` was argued in the abstract before any
  number existed. A configuration now touches it. Whether 0.605 is "the pipeline works" or "one
  flag on one arm happens to land there" is exactly what a held-out set decides.
