# Fraud detection service restart loop after an unreverted memory-limit reduction

## What was visible, in order

The page named one service, frauddetectionservice, critical, no unmeasured edges crossed. I anchor T+0 at the moment platform-automation lowered the memory limit on frauddetection-service to 200m; the alert did not arrive until roughly T+6m. Treating the alert time as onset was my first mistake and cost me minutes in the wrong window.

The service's own logs were the first thing that spoke. Ordinary Kafka order-consumption logging runs normally to T-1s (last consumed record, running count 566) and then stops for good. From about T+17s the JVM and agent startup sequence repeats three times in quick succession, then roughly every 60 seconds through T+7m14s, including at the moment the alert fired. No instance after the first ever reaches record consumption.

The shape of each cycle matters more than the count: every cycle ends silently, with no throwable, no shutdown message, no dependency complaint. That is what a process terminated from outside before application logging is established looks like, not an in-process crash. The near-fixed ~60s spacing also argues against timing that varies with workload.

Change history for the service contains nothing but five resource-limit edits, all by platform-automation, alternating lower and revert. The most recent lowering landed about five minutes before the alert and, unlike the two earlier pairs that day (each reverted within 11-12 minutes), was still in effect.

## Dead ends

Metrics. I expected an error-ratio departure. The span-metrics error-ratio query returned zero samples in the incident window, in the 32-minute baseline before it, and in a six-hour baseline earlier in the day. That symmetry is the useful part: the series does not exist in this range, an instrumentation or label gap, not a collapse at onset. Worse, no container memory working-set, memory-limit, restart-count or termination-reason data was ever retrieved; a later pass aimed at exactly that returned the same empty error-ratio result. Run those queries first next time.

Logs. Four hypotheses dropped: a malformed message on the orders topic (no deserialization or handler errors, and the restarting instances never handle a record); a failing downstream broker, database or peer (no connection-refused, timeout or retry lines; death occurs during startup, before any client would complain); the service merely lagging (consumption logging is replaced by startup banners, which is teardown-and-recreate); and a logged application exception (there is no error output at all, which is why pod termination reasons, not stdout, hold the answer).

Traces. Fraud consumer spans are present and healthy before onset (~0.1ms, no errors), which rules out both absent instrumentation and fraud being the source of checkout latency. The distraction was the newest sampled trace at ~T-1m22s, where the whole async leg is gone - publish span, fraud consumer, accounting consumer, and an unrelated emailservice call. I first read that as a consumer-only outage; it is not, the producer span is missing too. Span counts decline monotonically (49, 48, 42, 36, 25) while root duration stays flat, and this begins before T+0. Likely a separate collection or sampling problem. The sample was truncated and its newest trace predates onset, so there is no trace coverage of the restart period at all.

## Conclusion, remedy, and what is still open

At T+0 the memory limit was lowered and not put back. Within about 17 seconds the service entered a restart loop and never processed another order record. I believe the container cannot hold its working set inside the 200m ceiling and is killed for exceeding it before startup completes. Confidence medium, because the memory and termination telemetry was never in hand. Remedy is a config revert: restore the prior limit and stop the automation loop re-applying the lower one. Verify by watching a startup banner be followed by resumed order-consumption logging rather than another banner 60 seconds later.

Still open: whether the kills are cgroup-level memory terminations or a startup/liveness probe timeout caused by a memory-starved JVM coming up too slowly - both fit silent ~60s restarts, and pod events would separate them. Why the two earlier lowerings were reverted and this one was not, and whether the automation will re-lower after a manual revert; until that is answered a revert may not hold. The pre-onset span-count decline and whole-leg loss, plus the unmeasured impact on checkout during the loop, and the persistently degrading shippingservice-to-quoteservice hop that nobody has looked at.
