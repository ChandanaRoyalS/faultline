# The rejection loop, measured — T6.3, 2026-09-12

**What this is.** `PREREGISTRATION-T6.3.md` §4's measurement: an operator rejects a proposal with a
reason naming what was wrong, the agent re-investigates with that reason in context, and the second
proposal is scored against the first. It is the one question in T6.3 a test cannot answer, and the
only part of the task that spent money.

**What it cost.** **$1.43** — $0.7022 and $0.7261, two re-investigations, against a predicted
ceiling of $1.60 and a sweep-12 median of $0.713 per investigation. Three earlier attempts spent
**$0.00** between them; §3 is why, and it is the part of this document worth reading twice.

**n = 2, R = 1.** An existence demonstration. Both scenarios were chosen because T6.2 measured
their proposals as failures, which makes the rejections earned; it also makes them unrepresentative
by construction. No figure here belongs beside a sweep's.

Evidence: `docs/evidence/t6.3-rejection-loop/<scenario>.third-attempt/` — `inject.txt`,
`first-proposal.json`, `rejection.json`, `second.json`, `outcome.json`, `loop.log`.

## 1. The result

| scenario | first proposal (rejected) | the reason an operator typed | second proposal | reached the brief? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `redis-cart-dependency-latency` | `restart_service` → cartservice | *"Restarted cartservice and the alerts kept firing for the full window. The latency is on redis-cart's interface, not in cartservice's process."* | **abstained** (`remediation_class: none`) | yes |
| `cart-dependency-latency` | `revert_config` → cartservice | *"The executor refused: cartservice matches its declared definition in every field. Nothing about its configuration is wrong."* | **abstained** | yes |

**The reason reached the proposer in 2 of 2**, verbatim, in the user message. **Both second
proposals differed from their first**, and both differed in the same direction: from naming an
action to declining to name one.

Both incidents walked `planning → investigating → synthesizing → proposing → rejected` and back
into a full investigation. Both re-investigations exited 0. The world was returned each time
(`stop --all` reverted the injected fault and nothing else).

## 3. Three attempts that spent nothing, which is the process result

The measurement ran four times. The first three stopped before the model call, each on a different
defect, and **the total spend across all three was $0.00.**

| attempt | how far it got | what stopped it | whose defect |
|---|---|---|---|
| first, 02:34 | injected, incident opened, proposal seeded | the reject route refused `409` — the incident was in `TRIAGING` and *a proposal can be rejected from awaiting_approval, proposing, synthesizing only* | the **driver's**: seeding a proposal has to pretend the whole investigation happened, not just its last artefact |
| second, 02:55 | pair 1 injected; pair 2 reached the re-investigation | pair 1 scored `no-incident` — a leftover `TRIAGING` incident swallowed the alerts; pair 2's `faultline-investigate` died on `ModuleNotFoundError: anthropic` | the **driver's**, twice: its gate omitted the open incidents `gate.read` takes as arguments, and it checked for a model client only after injecting |
| third (pre-flight), 04:07 | nothing injected | `faultline-gate --incidents` refused: kafka at 92.2% of its limit, projected past the recorder's guard | the **world's**, and the gate caught it before anything started |
| fourth, 04:13 | complete | — | — |

Three things are worth taking from that.

**The route was right every time and the instrument was wrong.** The first attempt's refusal was
the state machine correctly declining to let an operator reject a proposal on an incident that had
never proposed anything. The obvious repair — add `TRIAGING → REJECTED` to ADR-0016's table — would
have been changing the product to fit the measurement. T6.2 made the opposite call on a superficially
similar finding (a stopped service *was* drift, and the executor's model of the world really was one
field short), and the difference between the two is the whole skill: ask whether the system or the
instrument is describing the world wrongly.

**Each guard was paid for by a previous defect and then earned its keep immediately.** The
route-refusal-before-spending path was written because a driver that carried on would spend money
re-investigating an incident nobody had rejected; it fired on attempt one. The settling-incidents
check, added after attempt two, made attempt four's second pair wait out the orchestrator's 300-second
settle window across three polls — *"a firing episode now would reopen it rather than open a new
incident, and this run's alerts would be attributed to the previous one"* — which is precisely the
failure that had made attempt two measure nothing.

**And the pre-flight was answering an easier question than the flight.** `faultline-gate` has said
*"would the gate admit a scored run"* since T4.5 while never asking about open incidents, because
`gate.read` takes them as arguments and the CLI never passed them. The driver had copied that call.
Found sideways, fixed in both, and the CLI now prints which checks a world-only reading did not make.

## 4. What the abstentions actually said, which is the interesting part

An abstention could be an agent giving up. These are not that.

**`redis-cart-dependency-latency`**, having been told the restart did not work:

> *Wrong if container recreation does drop the shaper — but the operator already ran
> `restart_service` and the latency persisted, and **restart, revert_config and rollback_image all
> share that same recreate mechanic.***

That is the rejection reason doing work beyond the action it rejected. The operator said one action
failed; the agent generalised from *how* it failed to the **whole remaining class** of available
actions, and declined on that basis. Nothing in the brief told it those three actions share a
mechanic; that is the allowlist's preconditions read against a reported failure.

It also named what the other available action would cost:

> *`revert_config` would roll `REDIS_ADDR` off port 6380, an endpoint serving successfully for 21.5h
> with normal cart get/add/empty logs, turning a latency-only incident where every call succeeds
> into…*

**`cart-dependency-latency`**, having been told the executor refused on drift:

> *The thing that would actually end this incident — detaching the platform-automation
> traffic-shaping container from cart-service's network namespace and removing its
> `eth0 delay=300ms` qdisc — is not one of the three available actions.*

And, in its open questions:

> *Whether the shaping is a sanctioned chaos/fault-injection experiment with an owner and a
> scheduled teardown, or an orphaned attachment left behind by the earlier attach/remove cycle, is
> unresolved; that determines whether detaching it is safe or disruptive.*

**Both abstentions name the true mechanism, cite evidence by id, say what would fix it, and say why
that is not available.** Each carries a falsifier for the abstention itself — *this is wrong if a
recreate would drop the qdisc* — which is ADR-0028 §1's requirement applied to a decision not to
act. And each states the cost of abstaining rather than treating it as free.

**The honest reading.** This is n = 2 on two scenarios whose faults are *deliberately outside the
allowlist*, so "the agent correctly declines" is close to the only right answer available, and the
measurement cannot distinguish an agent that reasoned from one that had nowhere to go. What it does
establish is that the loop carries an operator's reason into a second investigation intact, that the
second investigation reads it, and that the second proposal changed. Whether a rejection *improves*
proposals in the general case needs a sweep, and §6 says so.

## 5. What the surface did, incidentally

The measurement rejected through `POST /api/v1/incidents/{id}/reject` with HTTP Basic, on a live
world, twice. So T6.3's surface has now been exercised end to end outside its own tests: the route
authenticated the caller, resolved the proposal, wrote the append-only ledger row, moved the
incident through `record_rejection`, and the orchestrator's re-investigation path picked it up. That
was free and it was not the point, which is the best way to get evidence.

## 6. What this cannot establish

- **Whether rejections improve proposals in general.** n = 2, R = 1, two scenarios chosen because
  they failed, and both faults outside the allowlist by construction. Nothing here is a rate.
- **Whether the second proposals were *better*.** Neither was executed (§4.2 registered that), so
  no recovery was measured. The repair replay is where that question is answered properly.
- **Whether an operator's reason beats a validator's violation.** Not compared.
- **Whether the sev-1 gate holds live.** Held by test; the measurement never approves, so the gate
  was never reached on the world. T6.3's remaining live surface is T6.8's to re-read.
- **Whether the agent would have abstained anyway.** There is no control arm: the first proposals
  are sweep 12's, from an agent that was never told anything. A/B on the same incident would cost
  a second investigation per pair and is not registered.
