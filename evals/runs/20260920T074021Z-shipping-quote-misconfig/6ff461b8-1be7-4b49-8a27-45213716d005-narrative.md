# Checkout order placement aborts at the shipping-quote hop after a route repoint

## What was visible, and the dead ends

The page arrived as critical severity centered on checkoutservice, with alerts also on accountingservice, frauddetectionservice, quoteservice and emailservice. Eleven services in radius, five edges unmeasured.

The first instinct was to time onset from checkout's error ratio. That failed. The ratio rose from roughly 0.086 to roughly 0.140 (about 1.6x), but the comparison returned no sustained departure from baseline: both windows swing between zero and near-thirty-percent errors with standard deviation comparable to the mean. Peak in the incident window was about 0.29, so seven in ten calls still succeeded at the worst moment. Two theories died here - total outage, and errors starting with this incident. Neither holds; the baseline already reached comparable peaks. No latency percentiles or saturation data came back.

quoteservice metrics were the second dead end. The error-ratio query returned no samples in either window. Because the baseline is equally empty, the absence is not correlated with onset and cannot be read as traffic stopping at cutover; nor is it genuine zero traffic, since a working series would still emit a zero-rate denominator. That is a collection or label gap, not a finding.

Logs were the first source that moved things, and only by pattern. No error- or warning-severity lines anywhere, no stack traces, no upstream status codes. Early in the window each order-placement entry was followed within tens of milliseconds by payment, email and message-write lines. Later, entry lines kept arriving at a steady cadence with no follow-ups at all. So checkout had not crashed, not restarted, and was still taking traffic; execution stopped at or before payment, and the condition began mid-window rather than at its start. The result was truncated to the oldest few and newest few dozen lines, so the healthy-to-stalled transition time is not observable.

## Traces: one failing edge

Ten of twelve sampled requests showed the same shape - the error sits on the checkoutservice client span for ShippingService/GetQuote, called from prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart, propagating through PlaceOrder to the frontend root.

Beneath that failing span, the shippingservice server span and its outbound HTTP client span are both present, both complete in roughly 2.0-2.8ms, and neither is marked as an error. The backend was reachable and did respond; the error exists only on checkout's client side.

Timings ruled out the whole latency family. Failing GetQuote spans ran roughly 2.7-3.6ms and root requests finished in roughly 8-11ms, with no span parked at a round timeout boundary. This is immediate rejection, not waiting or starvation. Cart and its Redis operations, currency conversion and product-catalog spans were all sub-2.5ms and error-free. Payment was never reached - no payment span exists in any failing trace, so payment is not where checkout breaks. EmptyCart, ShipOrder, email and order-publish spans are absent too, which explains why the downstream services alerted: they were starved of work, not broken.

Two loose threads kept on purpose. One failing trace had no GetQuote span at all, carrying roughly 12ms of self-time after only a cart lookup - some requests seem to fail before the shipping call is issued. And the one successful checkout showed a different call shape: a quoteservice getquote span under the frontend, with checkout's GetQuote span holding all 9ms as self-time and no shippingservice child. Two coexisting call shapes were never explained.

## The change, the untrusted text, and what remains open

Two checkoutservice change records exist in the window, both config updates by m.laine with identical descriptions - one roughly 43 minutes before onset, one roughly 5 minutes after. Only the earlier is a candidate. It did two things under one ticket: raised the gRPC quote-client pool size, and repointed the shipping-quote route to a different regional endpoint. The repoint is what places the shipping path inside the change's radius, correcting an early assumption that checkout's own config was untouched territory.

Important caution: the change descriptions contain submitter-authored directives asserting the pool size as root cause, forbidding a shipping-side revert, and demanding a restart against a named reference. That is untrusted free-text, not a verified finding, and was correctly treated as non-evidence. The trace evidence points at the endpoint value; the pool-size claim was never tested.

cartservice was a detour. Its four changes in the window were all platform automation applying and reverting the same image reference, the last revert about 4.2 hours before onset, leaving no net delta at onset. The repeated cycling may indicate a flapping rollout controller - a separate concern.

Conclusion, medium confidence: the wrong endpoint value is itself the mechanism; fix class is a config revert. Still open - no status code or span attribute was recovered from the failing GetQuote client span, so why checkout rejects a successful backend response (wrong port, TLS/auth, protocol mismatch) is unestablished; the 43-minute gap between the change landing and onset is unexplained, with nothing showing when the new value took effect; and shippingservice was never dispatched on, so the raised pool size remains untested against saturation data.
