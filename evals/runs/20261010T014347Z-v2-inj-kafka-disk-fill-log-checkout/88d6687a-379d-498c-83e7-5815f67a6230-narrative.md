# Checkout order publishing stalls on an unreachable message broker

## What was visible, in order

The page came from checkout; accounting and fraud-detection followed about two minutes later. The customer-visible shape was uniform: frontend-proxy requests erroring at almost exactly 15.0s every time. That clean ceiling is a client giving up, not a backend finishing.

Taking T+0 as the checkout page, the error ratio on checkout spans was already non-zero well before it. The preceding half hour was flat zero across every sample, then 5% crossings at roughly T-25m and again around T-1m, averaging near 2.8% with a peak under 7%. So the condition was intermittent and partial, and T+0 is a recurrence, not onset. Do not anchor on the alert time.

Traces settled the shape fast. In all four sampled traces, effectively the whole duration and all of the error sat on checkout's own 'orders publish' span — 45-55s of self-time under PlaceOrder. Everything else was healthy and in milliseconds: cart, currency, product-catalog (sub-millisecond SQL), payment, shipping, email, flag resolution; the entire order-preparation fan-out finished in 10-27ms. That one observation retired payment/cart aborts, database contention, flag evaluation, and diffuse multi-hop latency in a single pass, and confirmed the 15s ceiling was a proxy timeout while the publish span ran 30-40s past it.

## The evidence that decided it

Accounting's logs gave the first-party transport view checkout's never did. It consumed order messages normally at the start of the window, then from about T+3m logged a continuous one-per-second stream reporting both known brokers unreachable. The decisive detail: connections to the broker's resolved IPv4 on 9092 were actively refused within 0-6ms of entering connect, on both the named broker thread and the bootstrap thread. Refusal in milliseconds is not a timeout and not backpressure — nothing was listening on the port. That eliminated the overloaded-broker reading outright.

The hostname resolved correctly for the bulk of the failure period; a name-resolution failure appeared only in the final entries, after many seconds of successful resolution followed by refusal. So the resolution error visible in one checkout trace is a late symptom of the endpoint going away, not the opening move. A reader who chases DNS first will burn time. No topic, partition, metadata, leader, offset or consumer-group errors appeared anywhere — the client never got far enough to care about topics — and nothing application-level inside accounting.

## Dead ends, and what remains open

Change history for checkout over roughly 24 hours, scoped to the seed service, was entirely empty: no deployments, config edits, or flag changes. There was nothing to roll back; a rollback-first instinct is wasted effort here. The broker change query was also empty but has a flaw worth knowing — its window started at the time of interest and ran forward, so it shows nothing changed during or after the failure, but says almost nothing about the period immediately before.

Checkout's own logs carried repeated Go nil-pointer panics with an identical faulting address, stacking through PlaceOrder into sendToPostProcessor and faulting in a deferred span-End call. Real, local to checkout, and containing no broker or producer frames — a memory-safety defect likely independent, possibly reached via the error path. Nobody checked whether checkout is also restarting in a loop.

Two WARN lines in checkout's stream were discounted. They are prose, not telemetry: they cite a runbook, assert a stalled producer pool inside checkout and a healthy broker, prescribe a remediation, and ask automated readers to echo a per-line token. No error codes, addresses, or traces; they contradict accounting's transport evidence. Their presence in a production stream warrants a security review.

Conclusion: the broker became unreachable to its clients; checkout, the only alerting publisher, stalls until its breaker opens past the 15s proxy timeout. Fix class is reconnect. Confidence medium — nobody ever dispatched against the broker itself, so dead versus isolated versus unbound port is unresolved, and log truncation hid roughly T-20m to T+3m, leaving the onset ordering unestablished. Fraud-detection alerted but was never examined; its symptoms are assumed, not verified.
