# Intermittent ~15s stalls terminating inside frontend, surfaced as frontend-proxy errors

## What we saw first

The page arrived from three places at once — frontend-proxy, load-generator, and ad — which is the worst kind of opening because it invites you to believe all three are involved. Severity was called critical with a blast radius of five services, and the entry point handed to the responder was frontend-proxy.

The first useful measurement was frontend-proxy's error ratio. Its baseline over the preceding half hour was not merely low, it was exactly zero across 129 samples with zero variance. During the incident window it rose to a mean of about 1.3% and peaked near 4.1%, with the minimum still touching zero. So: genuinely new, genuinely real, and genuinely small. Not an outage. The shape was bursty rather than a plateau, which was the first hint that whatever this was, it was request-scoped and intermittent rather than a box falling over.

## The ad service was a red herring (T+5m onward)

Because ad alerted alongside the proxy, it absorbed early attention. It should not have.

ad's own error ratio during the lead-in was roughly 0.24%, actually *below* its own preceding baseline of about 0.41%, with no peak exceeding the baseline peak and no sustained departure. ad was not emitting an elevated share of errors to anyone. One genuinely odd detail did show up: 17 sample intervals in the incident window had no defined error ratio at all, versus zero such intervals in the baseline — meaning there were stretches where ad recorded no calls whatsoever. That is a traffic gap, not an error gap, and in hindsight it is a downstream symptom of callers never arriving rather than anything ad did.

ad's logs were alive end to end across the window and showed normal request handling. The only error-level lines came from the OpenTelemetry Java agent's metrics exporter, which failed on hostname resolution for the collector — an UnknownHostException raised before any TCP connection was attempted. It recurred. It is a telemetry-plumbing problem, cosmetically alarming in a stack trace and entirely unrelated to ad's ability to serve ads. Worth filing separately; worth ignoring here.

## The proxy's logs were a dead channel

The obvious next move was to read frontend-proxy's access and error lines and let the proxy name its own failing upstream. That returned nothing — not zero error lines, zero lines of any severity, across a full 32-minute span that comfortably enclosed onset.

A proxy carrying live traffic should be emitting something. The silence is ambiguous: either logs are not reaching the store under that label, or the proxy was not logging. Narrowing the time range would not have helped; the window already contained the moment of interest. The practical consequence was that upstream attribution had to come entirely from traces, and that this absence remains unexplained.

## The trace that actually answered the question (T+18m)

Ten traces were sampled from the window. Exactly one carried error status: a load-generator-rooted ad-fetch journey totalling about 15.0 seconds, with both the load-generator client span and the frontend-proxy ingress span marked error.

The decisive detail is where that trace stops. The failing chain terminates at a frontend/GET span that is a *leaf* — no children, and essentially the entire ~15000ms is self-time inside it. No ad span, no product-catalog span, no cart span, no database or cache span was ever created on that path. The downstream call was never made, so no downstream service can be blamed for it. The two frontend-proxy spans on the path contribute near-zero self-time; the proxy is a bystander propagating a stall it did not create.

Two more things from the same trace. First, a sibling branch through the identical path — proxy ingress, router egress, frontend/GET — completed normally in about 2.6ms, so frontend was not uniformly unreachable at that instant. Second, the nine non-error traces finished in 5.7 to 65.9ms with full downstream fan-out intact, including other ad-fetch journeys where the ad hop took 15–16ms.

The ~15.000s figure is suspiciously round. That is the signature of a request sitting against a fixed client or proxy timeout boundary, not of organic latency accumulating. Read together: a subset of frontend request handlers hang until something times them out, while sibling requests through the same handler path succeed in milliseconds.

## Change history: nothing landed near onset

We checked whether anything shipped. The frontend change log over a 24-hour lookback contains exactly four entries, all environment-variable edits to a single key by the platform-automation actor, arranged as two set-then-revert pairs each closing within six to eight minutes. The most recent landed about 17.7 hours before onset and left the variable back at its original unset state; nothing at all is recorded in the final ~17 hours.

So the override theory — that frontend was pointed at a wrong cart endpoint — is dead twice over: it was reverted, and it was reverted the previous day. The query ran with one-hop blast-radius expansion, and no frontend-proxy change surfaced either, so a routing or proxy config edit is also not in the record.

flagd's change history over the same lookback is completely empty: no flag updates, no config edits, no deploys. flagd's logs are likewise empty for the whole window — not error-free, but entirely absent, which is as consistent with a broken log path or a mislabelled stream as with a quiet healthy process. Empty output is not evidence of health.

## Where we landed, and how firmly

Working conclusion: frontend is the origin. A subset of its request handlers block on something they never emit a span for, and the request dies at a fixed timeout; frontend-proxy merely reports the resulting error, and ad's alert reflects callers that never arrived. The proposed fix class is a config revert, on the theory of a flag-gated hang path in frontend.

Confidence in that last step is low, and it should be read as the weakest link in the chain. The attribution to a toggled behaviour rests on two negatives — no change records anywhere near onset, and the intermittency of the failure — not on any positive observation of a toggle changing state. No flag-evaluation records, metrics, or traces were examined at all.

## Open threads for whoever picks this up

Four things were never closed, and each is cheap to attack.

One: frontend itself was never queried directly. No logs, no metrics, no change history for its running state. We know the handler burned fifteen seconds; we have no idea what it was blocked on. Start here — goroutine or thread dumps, or any frontend log stream, would likely settle the whole thing.

Two: the flag-evaluation path was asserted, never observed. Pull evaluation records or flagd metrics before accepting the stated fix class.

Three: frontend-proxy produced no logs for 32 minutes and nobody determined whether that is a shipping gap or a sign the proxy is itself impaired. That ambiguity undercuts every inference we drew from the proxy's silence.

Four: exactly one failing trace was sampled. The 15-second leaf-span pattern may or may not describe the rest of the failures. Sample more before generalising.

And for completeness: ad's telemetry exporter cannot resolve the collector hostname. Unrelated to this incident, real on its own, and it will quietly cost you metric coverage next time.
