# Checkout path hard-fails at the cart lookup

## What was visible first

Three alerts arrived together: frontend, loadgenerator, and checkoutservice. That reads as an edge outage on the order path, with the load generator confirming it was reproducible rather than one unlucky user. Blast radius scored twelve services, severity critical, seed at frontend.

The first measurement was frontend error ratio against the preceding hour. The mean was up about 1.4x, which looks like something until you read the shape: standard deviation exceeded the mean in both windows and the incident peak was essentially identical to the quiet-hour peak. No sustained departure, equal sample counts in both windows, so not a collection gap. Frontend is bursty by nature and was bursty before onset. It is reporting the failure, not producing it.

The cost of that query was that it covered only frontend and only one metric family. For a while the nine downstream dependencies were treated as if they had been checked and found clean. They had not been touched.

## Checkoutservice, and a wrong turn worth keeping

The service named in the alert had an error ratio around 40% of baseline during the incident — it went down, with no sustained departure. Confusing to sit with mid-incident, and it also meant this series could not be what fired.

Logs were more useful. Early in the window each request produced the full healthy sequence: request start, payment success with transaction id, confirmation email, message write with advancing offset. In the tail, only the request-start lines remained. No errors, no warnings, no panic, no restart banner, and arrivals continued at a steady several-per-minute rate to the end. So checkoutservice was alive and inbound traffic was intact, and the break sat at or before the payment step. The reading at the time — that orders were stalling mid-flight in a downstream call — was wrong, and it steered the next round of questions. Coverage was also truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, so the half hour containing onset was never returned.

The missing payment line sent two queries toward paymentservice, both dead ends. Its error-ratio series returned zero samples in the incident window and zero in the baseline before it, so the emptiness predates the incident and is a selector or instrumentation gap, not a symptom. Change history for paymentservice plus one-hop neighbours over 24 hours was genuinely empty, as was a parallel change query on checkoutservice — but that one was scoped to the seed with zero hops, so cart, catalog, shipping, currency and email were never covered.

## The trace that ended it, and what is still open

Every PlaceOrder trace terminates at the same span: checkoutservice's call to CartService/GetCart errors. The ERROR status on prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart, on checkoutservice PlaceOrder, on frontend PlaceOrder and on the frontend HTTP POST is inherited from that one child. No payment, shipping, currency, catalog or email span appears anywhere, because execution never reaches them.

The durations overturned the stall theory outright. Nothing hangs — root spans run one to eight milliseconds, the GetCart child under one. This is a fast failure. The absent payment and email log lines were never evidence of a stall; those steps were simply never attempted. An absent completion log looks the same whether work hung or never started, and only the trace separates the two. That is the most useful correction in this record.

Blame sits on cartservice, hard-failing every GetCart. Confidence medium: the mechanism is unestablished. No log, metric, or change query was ever run against cartservice — everything asserted about it is inferred from its caller's traces. The actual error returned (refused, OOM, auth, deadline) is unknown, a cart backing store behind the service remains an untested candidate for the real origin, and no change record was sought for cartservice or that backend. Expected fix class is a restart, which is a guess consistent with a total, uniform, fast failure rather than a conclusion.
