# PlaceOrder failures at checkout: error returned from the shipping quote hop

## What we saw first

The page arrived for checkoutservice and, within the same minute, for accountingservice, emailservice, frauddetectionservice and quoteservice. Blast radius was eleven services, severity critical, with checkoutservice named as the seed. The user-visible symptom was orders not completing.

The first thing that held up was the shape of the error output. checkoutservice was emitting error-status spans at a mean ratio of roughly 0.22 across the window, ranging from about 0.02 to 0.275 over 31 consecutive samples. That told us two useful things immediately: this was sustained, not a single spike, and it was partial — around three quarters of calls were still completing. Anyone arriving with a "checkout is down" mental model needed to drop it at this point.

One trap here, worth naming because it cost time: the metric tool reported "no sustained departure from baseline." That verdict is worthless in this case. The baseline window (about four hours earlier) returned zero samples, so the reported jump from 0 to 0.22 is a comparison against nothing. Do not treat that verdict as reassurance. The collector itself was fine — the same query returned dense samples during the incident window.

## Reading the logs, and where they failed us

Around T+3m we pulled checkout-service logs over a wide window. The result was truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines, and that truncation turned out to matter more than anything in the lines themselves: everything between roughly 01:44 and 01:56 was dropped, which is exactly the onset. We re-queried with a narrower intent and got the same truncation behaviour — the interior was again unobserved, and no severity filter was actually applied. Whatever error text checkout emitted at first failure, we never saw it.

What the retained slices did show was a clean before/after contrast. In the early lines each order-placement entry was followed within about 20-25ms by a payment-succeeded line, a confirmation-email line, and a message-write line — full end-to-end flows. In the late lines, from about 01:56:37 through 01:59:59, there were roughly thirty order-placement entries and nothing else. No payment line, no email, no message write. Orders were being accepted and were not reaching completion.

Every retained line was info severity. There were no exception traces, no timeout text, no named downstream target. If you go looking for a loud error line in checkout's logs, you will not find one.

## What the logs ruled out

The steady intake cadence through 01:59:59 killed the crash/restart/OOM hypothesis outright — the process was alive and serving traffic to the edge of the window.

The intake line itself, complete with resolved user id and currency, was still being emitted for every request. So the break was not at checkout's own entry point: not request parsing, not auth, not currency resolution.

We briefly entertained that payment was fine and only a post-order side effect (email, order publish) had broken. The absence of the payment-succeeded line, not just the email and message-write lines, closed that off. The break sat at or before payment.

## Traces: the one hop that breaks

Sampling ten PlaceOrder traces after 01:59 gave the clearest picture of the whole investigation, and they were unanimous. Frontend HTTP POST, frontend gRPC PlaceOrder, and the checkoutservice PlaceOrder span were all marked error. Inside checkoutservice, exactly one downstream call carried the error marker: the client span for ShippingService/GetQuote, raised inside prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart.

The hop was fast. GetQuote took roughly 2.6-3.8ms and whole traces closed in about 9-16ms. This is a prompt error return, not a stall and not a deadline expiry. It was also deterministic across all ten traces, accounting for 22-35% of trace time in each.

The oddity, and the thing still unresolved: the shippingservice server span and its own downstream HTTP client span complete clean in every trace. The error appears only on the caller's side of that boundary.

## Dead ends we walked, in order

cartservice. We pulled its change history, its error-ratio metric, and its logs. Change history was empty. The metric query returned no samples at all — and, importantly, none in the baseline period either, so this is a standing instrumentation gap, not telemetry lost to the incident. Do not read that empty result as a clean bill of health; it carries no information. The logs showed routine GetCart/AddItem/EmptyCart handling continuing steadily through 01:59, with AddItem and follow-up GetCart pairs milliseconds apart, so neither a stall nor a backing-store failure fit. A recurring GetCart with empty userId appears both at the start and end of the window; it is baseline noise, not a signature. The traces settled it properly: GetCart and its Redis child are present, unmarked, sub-millisecond in every failing trace.

productcatalogservice. Change history empty over the queried window. Traces show multiple GetProduct spans per trace completing normally, one of them with a feature-flag child call. Not involved.

currencyservice. Every Convert span returns at effectively zero milliseconds with no error marker. Not involved.

paymentservice. No payment span exists in any failing trace. Checkout aborts before payment is ever invoked, so the charge path cannot be the origin.

A bad checkoutservice release. The change log for checkoutservice was empty across a full 24-hour window — no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips. Rollback has no target here. Note the scoping limitation: that query covered checkoutservice alone, hops=0, and says nothing about its dependencies or shared infrastructure.

checkoutservice as the origin. Self-time on the PlaceOrder span is around 0.1ms. It is propagating, not originating.

## Where we landed

Every sampled failing order breaks at the same hop: checkout's client call to ShippingService/GetQuote returns an error in a few milliseconds, aborting the order before payment. Cart, product catalog and currency all succeed in the same traces. checkoutservice's own work is negligible. quoteservice alerting at the same moment as the dependent services is consistent with the problem living on the quote side.

Best reading of the class: a fast error return from the shipping quote path. Not a timeout, not saturation, not a bad checkout artifact. Confidence is low, and the reason is scoping, not ambiguity in what we saw.

No fix class was established and no remediation was applied.

## Open questions for whoever picks this up

First and most important: no dispatch ever touched shippingservice or quoteservice. Not their logs, not their metrics, not their change history. The single most load-bearing conclusion in this record rests entirely on what checkout's client spans reported about a service we never queried directly. Start there.

Second: why does the shippingservice server span complete clean while checkout's client span errors? Candidates worth separating are a status-mapping discrepancy, a non-2xx quote response that shipping does not consider an error on its side, and a transport or deadline mismatch between the two.

Third: the 01:44-01:56 log gap. Truncation dropped the onset. The error text at first failure is unknown and may still be retrievable with a tightly bounded query over that interval plus a severity filter that actually applies.

Fourth: roughly 78% of checkout calls still succeeded. Is the failure keyed to particular carts, item sets, or currencies? Answering this would sharpen the mechanism considerably.

Finally, a methodological warning for the record. Two separate empty-versus-empty metric comparisons in this investigation produced "no departure from baseline" verdicts that mean nothing. Check whether the baseline window has samples before you let such a verdict clear a service.
