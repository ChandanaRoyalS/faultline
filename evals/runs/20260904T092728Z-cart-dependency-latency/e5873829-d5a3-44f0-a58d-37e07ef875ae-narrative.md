# Cart path slowed by a fixed 300ms per-hop network delay

## What the responder saw first

Four alerts arrived close together: cartservice, checkoutservice, frontend, and loadgenerator. Blast radius was tallied at 12 services, severity warning, with the walk starting at cartservice. Four edges in the graph had no measurement attached, so part of the map was drawn from traces rather than from metrics.

The first instinct — read cartservice's own error ratio and request rate — went nowhere, and it is worth knowing that before you try it. The span-metric series for cartservice returned nothing at all, and it returned nothing in the two-hour comparison window before onset either. That symmetry matters: an empty incident window plus an empty healthy window means the series was never being collected for that service name, not that traffic went to zero. Do not read the blank panel as a silent service, and do not read it as a clean bill of health; it clears nothing.

## cartservice's own logs: clean, and that was the clue

The cart-service log stream over the ~2h window carried only routine request handling for get, add, and empty. No exceptions, no connection failures, no startup or shutdown banner. Request pairs kept arriving every few seconds right through the tail of the window, so the process stayed up and kept serving. One early request was handled with an empty user identifier and logged normally — a curiosity, not an error path, and it went nowhere.

Caveat on coverage: the query returned only the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines, so roughly two hours in the middle were never inspected. What the retained head and tail do establish is that whatever cartservice was doing wrong, it was not saying so. That pushed the investigation to caller-side traces.

## Where the time actually went

Traces resolved it. Inside cartservice, nearly all the added time sat in the Redis client child spans: HGET and HMSET each ran ~300-311ms, and the enclosing gRPC server span exceeded its children by only 1-2ms. Handlers cost whole multiples of that unit — GetCart and EmptyCart ~301-307ms with one cache operation, AddItem ~602-613ms with two. Variance across dozens of samples was a few milliseconds.

The same unit showed up on the caller side, above and beyond the cartservice server span: frontend GetCart ~605-627ms against a ~301-307ms server span, frontend AddItem ~905-937ms against ~602-612ms, and checkoutservice GetCart/EmptyCart ~1206-1213ms against a ~301-302ms server span — three quanta of gap. At the top of the tree, frontend cart POSTs ran ~1512-1568ms and PlaceOrder ~2466-2476ms, the latter dominated by two cartservice-bound legs.

## The change that was still in effect

The change log for cartservice held nine entries, all attributed to platform-automation, none to a human. At T-3m a traffic-shaping container was attached to cartservice's network namespace, applying a fixed 300ms delay with zero jitter on eth0. No matching removal appears afterward in the window, so it was in force at onset. That is the only change in the window still live when symptoms began, and its magnitude matches the quantum seen in traces exactly.

The log also shows this is not the first time: the same attachment had been applied ~2.0h before onset and removed ~1.9h before, an attach/remove cycle from the same automation.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Several plausible leads were followed and closed.

A cartservice hotfix image tag. It was rolled out and reverted twice — the second rollout ~23m before onset, reverted ~14m before. cartservice was running its prior image when symptoms started, so the code change was not live.

A misconfigured Redis endpoint. A REDIS_ADDR edit pointing at redis-cart on port 6380 was applied ~1.8h before onset and reverted ~1.7h before, with no later edit reintroducing it. Not in effect.

A different downstream dependency. Every span for productcatalog, currency, shipping, quote, payment, email, accounting, and frauddetection was under ~25ms, most under 3ms.

A cluster-wide or mesh-wide slowdown. Non-cartservice hops in the same traces — frontend to productcatalog, checkout to shipping, shipping to quote, checkout to email — ran at normal single- to low-double-digit milliseconds. The penalty was confined to cartservice call paths.

CPU saturation or slow handler logic. The server spans exceeded the sum of their children by 1-2ms, leaving no room for in-handler compute.

A payload-size or cart-item-count effect. The delay was near-constant per cache operation regardless of operation type or trace.

Read-only or write-only impact. HGET and HMSET both paid the same toll.

An unreviewed manual edit by an on-call engineer. Every change in the window is machine-attributed.

## Why downstream alerted, and the timeline that does not fit

checkoutservice's error ratio is genuinely new: the baseline period is flat zero across every sample with zero variance, while the incident window averages roughly 5-6% with excursions as high as two-thirds of requests. It is bursty rather than a sustained step — the standard deviation runs about three times the mean. That is consistent with multi-second cart-bound legs pushing past some deadline intermittently.

But the timeline does not close cleanly. The first threshold crossing lands at 07:16, more than two hours before the network change, with further crossings at 07:44 and a severe one at 09:10. Whatever happened at onset is not the first error episode of the morning. Two intervals in the incident window also have no defined ratio at all, meaning checkoutservice served no traffic in them — which argues against any assumption that request rate stayed flat, though it does not measure rate directly. Note also that this series is an overall error ratio, not per-dependency latency, so it cannot itself confirm the 300ms step.

## Conclusion and fix class

Every network round-trip leaving cartservice paid a fixed 300ms toll applied by a traffic-shaping attachment on its network namespace, landing ~3 minutes before onset with no removal recorded. cartservice was waiting, not failing — up, error-free in its own logs, and adding only 1-2ms of its own work per call. The waits multiplied through frontend and checkoutservice and produced the downstream alerts about fifteen seconds later. Confidence: high. Fix class: revert the configuration — detach the shaping and confirm the delay is gone.

## Still open for the next responder

Three loose ends.

First, the earlier checkoutservice error crossings at 07:16, 07:44, and 09:10 all predate the attachment. Nothing dispatched tied them to the earlier automation churn on cartservice. It is plausible that onset is simply the latest episode of a repeating pattern rather than a discrete event.

Second, there is no cartservice latency or request-rate metric anywhere — the series is absent in both windows. The 300ms step rests entirely on traces, and there is no telemetry confirmation that the delay has since been cleared. Verify by direct inspection of the namespace, not by dashboard.

Third, the exact error mechanism at checkoutservice — deadline exceeded versus something else — was never read from its logs or timeout configuration. Worth doing before assuming the fix is sufficient.
