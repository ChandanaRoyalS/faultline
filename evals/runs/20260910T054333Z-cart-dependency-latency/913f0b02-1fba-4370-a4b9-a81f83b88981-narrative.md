# Checkout error bursts traced to lingering egress delay on cartservice

## What we saw first

The page named four services at once: cartservice, checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator, severity warning, with a blast radius of twelve services and cartservice given as the origin. Triage also flagged four edges it could not measure, which mattered later.

The shape of the alert set was the first useful hint. Frontend and loadgenerator alerting seconds behind checkoutservice is the signature of an error travelling outward toward the caller rather than four independent problems. That pointed the first hour of work at cartservice and at whatever sits between cart and checkout.

## First dead end: cartservice's own error metrics

The obvious first move was to pull an error ratio for cartservice and compare it to a two-hour baseline. It returned nothing at all — not a low value, an empty result.

This burned time because an empty series looks like a signal. It is not. The baseline window, two hours before onset, was equally empty, so the absence predates the event and cannot be a symptom of it. The honest reading is that this metric and label combination is simply not emitted for cartservice — an instrumentation or label-naming gap, not a service that went dark. A responder hitting this again should stop immediately: with both windows empty, this query cannot even tell you whether cart is serving traffic.

## Second look: cartservice logs said it was healthy

With metrics unavailable, logs were the fallback. Everything returned was routine INFO-level request handling — cart adds, gets, empties — with no exceptions, no panics, no backend connection errors and no startup banners. Traffic was dense and continuous at the tail of the window, multiple distinct sessions per second through 05:48:57, with no gap that a restarting process would leave behind.

That let us discard several plausible stories in one pass: cart was not crash-looping, had not thrown a fatal error, had not lost its cache backend, and had not been cut off from upstream traffic. A recurring pattern of GetCartAsync calls arriving with an empty userId looked suspicious for a few minutes, but it appears at both ends of the window and is pre-existing background noise, not an onset marker.

One real limitation: the result was truncated to the oldest handful and newest few dozen lines, so the onset minute itself was never actually returned. Cart's behaviour at the exact moment of onset remains unobserved.

## Where the failure was actually visible

checkoutservice, which sits directly on the cart call path, was the service with a measurable problem. Its error ratio went from a near-zero baseline — maximum under one percent across the preceding half hour — to a mean roughly 354 times higher, with bursts reaching about two-thirds of requests failing. The dispersion was as large as the mean, which reads as intermittent bursts rather than a clean sustained plateau: consistent with requests tipping over a deadline some of the time rather than a hard break.

Change-point detection found exactly one transition, so there was no second escalation to chase.

## The change history, and what in it did not matter

Change records for cartservice showed twenty-three entries in the window, all attributed to platform-automation with no human actor anywhere. They follow a repeating cycle roughly every three hours: image update, image revert, traffic-shaping attach, traffic-shaping remove, environment override set, environment override revert.

Three candidates were examined and dropped:

- A cartservice image reference set to a hotfix tag about 23 minutes before onset. It was reverted about 13 minutes before onset, so that image was not what was running when things broke.
- A REDIS_ADDR override pointing cart at a non-default Redis port. Applied and reverted several times across the day, but most recently reverted roughly 7.2 hours before onset and not re-applied in the cycle preceding the event.
- The idea that this change activity was novel or the work of an operator doing something ad hoc. It was neither; the same sequence had repeated at least four times in the prior day.

What survived: a traffic-shaping container attached to cart's network namespace about three minutes before onset, applying a fixed 300ms egress delay on eth0 with no jitter. Unlike every prior turn of the same cycle, the matching removal step never ran. The delay was still in place at onset and after it.

## Conclusion, and how much to trust it

The working verdict is added latency in the cart call path: cart keeps answering correctly but slowly, checkout waits on it and starts breaching its own deadlines in bursts, and the errors surface at frontend and the load generator. Cart's own logic is not implicated. Fix class is a configuration revert — remove the lingering shaping attachment.

Confidence is medium, not high, and the reason is stated plainly in the next section.

## Open questions a future responder should attack first

The strongest evidence against the verdict is a timing mismatch and it was never resolved. The single change point in checkout's error ratio lands at 05:25, roughly eighteen minutes before the shaping container was attached at 05:43. Either the checkout metric window (starting 05:17) was too short to place that change point reliably, or something degraded checkout before the shaping was applied. If you pick up this record, widen the checkout metric window first — an hour or more of baseline before 05:17 — and see whether the change point moves.

Other gaps, roughly in order of value:

- No latency measurement for cartservice was ever obtained. The effect of the 300ms delay on cart response times and on checkout's p99 is inferred from the shaping configuration, never observed.
- No checkoutservice logs were pulled. It is unconfirmed that checkout's errors are deadline-exceeded against cart rather than failures against payment, email or another downstream. This is a cheap check and it would settle the mechanism.
- Cart's logs at the onset minute are missing due to truncation; re-query with a tight window around onset.
- Whether the shaping attachment has since been cleaned up by a later automation turn, and why the removal step did not fire in this cycle, is unknown. The un-removed step is the only anomalous thing in an otherwise routine cycle and deserves its own follow-up.
- Triage reported four unmeasured edges. No service between cart and the frontend was examined, so an intermediate amplifier cannot be excluded.
