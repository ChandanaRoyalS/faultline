# Constant ~300ms network delay on cartservice inflates checkout to 2.5s

## What paged and the first two dead ends

Offsets are from the incident timestamp on the alert (T+0). Four services warned within about fifteen seconds of T+0: cartservice, checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator. Blast radius was twelve services, origin named as cartservice, and triage crossed four edges nobody had measurement for.

First move was cartservice's own logs across a fifteen-minute bracket. They held nothing but routine per-request handler lines — cart add, get and empty, with user and product identifiers — at a steady multi-request-per-second rate, with no error- or warning-severity entries at either end of the retained output and no downstream endpoint or datastore named anywhere. That disposed of three theories: no explicit dependency error pointing at a backing store, no traffic stop or crash-loop (requests continued well past onset), and no exception flood. Two oddities surfaced here and neither mattered: the newest retained line stopped roughly two minutes short of the window end, and a couple of requests carried an empty user identifier. Both remain unexplained and untied to the latency.

The second move cost the most time. A span-derived error-ratio query for cartservice returned no series at all — not a zero, an empty set, with even the denominator absent. An empty result is not a healthy zero: with no total-call denominator it is equally consistent with a traffic stop or a metrics gap. That query path cannot measure cartservice and needs replacing, not retrying.

## Ruling out an error cascade

With four services alerting, the reflexive hypothesis was failures rippling outward. Two measurements closed it.

frontend's error ratio was flat at zero across all sixty-one sample points, both sides of onset. That excludes 5xx responses, deadline breaches, breakers tripping and connection resets — all of which would appear as errored spans at frontend. Sixty-one defined points also mean the counter was advancing, so frontend never stopped serving. Usefully, that query confirmed the working label schema for this deployment: calls_total carrying service_name and status_code. Nobody re-ran cartservice against it, so cartservice's own rate, percentiles and error ratio stay unmeasured.

productcatalogservice, checked as a candidate seed, was likewise flat at zero for the whole window and cannot have started a cascade. Read together: this event is latency-only.

## Traces, then the change

Sampled checkoutservice traces broke it open. End-to-end PlaceOrder ran 2454–2488ms, and two cart calls — GetCart and EmptyCart — accounted for nearly all of it at ~1205–1220ms each. Everything else was noise: currency, payment, product catalog, email, accounting and fraud detection all under 10ms; shipping and quote under ~27ms. Inside cartservice the server spans were only ~302–309ms, almost entirely the nested Redis operation (~301–308ms), leaving under ~2ms of application work. Durations clustered tightly across every trace and across both reads and writes, which is the signature of a fixed delay rather than queueing, and rules out a write-path-only story. The unresolved part: client spans ran roughly four times the server spans, leaving ~900ms per call outside cartservice entirely.

The change history for cartservice held exactly one entry. About three and a half minutes before onset, platform automation attached a traffic-shaping sidecar to the cart-service network namespace, applying a fixed 300ms zero-jitter delay on eth0 in both directions. No code deploy, image rollout, config or flag change, dependency bump, or datastore schema/failover/resize/credential event appears in the window, and the onset is not organic. The mechanism fits: every hop crossing that interface pays the toll once per direction, which is why Redis spans sit at ~300ms and why the checkoutservice-to-cartservice call pays twice more outside the server span. Fix class is a revert of the change; why automation applied it, and whether it will re-apply, is not established.
