# Checkout order placement failing on cart reads

## What paged us and the dead ends

The page came from the user-facing checkout path at T+0, naming nine services and eight unmeasured edges with checkout as the seed. The first instinct — read checkout's logs — produced nothing at all: not zero errors, but zero lines of any severity under that service label, while the service was demonstrably alive and returning errors. That silence is a second, unexplained problem (broken shipping or a label mismatch) and the eventual conclusion does not account for it. Next came metrics: two pulls of checkout's span error ratio. The short baseline showed a ~1.25x mean shift swamped by a standard deviation the size of the mean; the long pull showed an intermittent ratio averaging ~2.3% and peaking ~8.3%, with the two-hour pre-onset baseline returning no samples at all, so the apparent rise from zero is missing data rather than a quiet period. Neither pull contained latency or request-rate series, so that half of the question was asked twice and answered never. Useful negative: this was never a hard failure mode.

## Traces: the trap

Four sampled checkout traces in the onset window completed in 35-56ms with the full PlaceOrder fan-out intact and not one error status across roughly 200 spans. Because the failing slice was only a few percent of traffic, every sampled trace was a success — reading them closely would convince you nothing was wrong anywhere. Each candidate slow hop fell in turn: payment Charge 1.4-2.8ms, cart GetCart 0.6-1.0ms with store operations under 0.3ms, product-catalog queries 0.2-0.5ms, shipping ship-order ~0.2ms, currency's own span at most 1.6ms. These eliminations are correct but answer the wrong question; this was never a latency story. Two incidental notes to carry forward: checkout's own self-time reached 7.1ms in one trace with 1-2ms of wrapper overhead, and quote-service children appeared at negative offsets relative to their shipping parent, meaning fleet clocks disagree. The skew was never examined.

## The break: read the neighbour's logs

The turn came from querying frontend rather than checkout. Frontend logs gRPC 13 INTERNAL on its checkout calls, with a detail string relayed verbatim from checkout naming exactly one failing step — retrieving the user cart — wrapping an upstream gRPC 9 FAILED_PRECONDITION from the cart service's Valkey-backed store. The innermost cause is a protobuf parse failure on a message that ended mid-field. The chain is frontend to checkout to cart, and the break is in the bytes returned, not in reaching the store. This also rules out transport trouble (no UNAVAILABLE or DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, server-side frames present), a defect in frontend itself (its frames are only the thin server chunk), checkout's other steps (no other dependency named in any line), the store being unreachable (it returned bytes), and a transient blip (the same signature appears at both ends of the window).

## Change history: all negative, one near-miss

Checkout, payment and email all show no releases, config edits or flag flips. Caveat on email: the window queried runs forward from onset rather than back, so the period actually asked about was never examined. Caveat on checkout: the query was scoped to the seed with zero hops, so currency, product-catalog and shipping were never searched. Cart is the near-miss — eight entries, all platform automation, all traffic-shaping attach/detach on cart's network namespace, forming four complete pairs applying and withdrawing a flat 300ms eth0 delay. The last event is a removal roughly seventeen hours before onset. A responder arriving cold will want these to be the answer; they were not in force at onset, and a flat link delay does not truncate stored payloads.

## Conclusion and what remains open

Cart data in the cart service's store is malformed or truncated; the read path retrieves it, fails to parse, and returns FAILED_PRECONDITION to checkout, which cannot complete GetCart and relays INTERNAL to frontend. Only requests whose keys hold corrupt payloads fail, matching the intermittent 2-8% ratio. Fix class is data restoration; confidence medium. Open: cart's own logs and metrics were never pulled — the budget ran out at four of four calls — so there is no cart-side confirmation of parse failures, affected key count, or store memory and eviction state. Start there. The mechanism of corruption is unidentified (a truncated write versus a producer writing bad bytes), so a flush may not hold. Onset timing is unestablished: the same signature is present from ~T-29m through the end of the window, so the condition may predate the alert by an unknown margin. Checkout's total log silence remains unexplained, as does the clock skew.
