# Checkout order flow stalling on shipping quote calls

## What was visible first

The page came from checkoutservice, and within a couple of minutes ten other services had joined the alert list, including accountingservice, emailservice, frauddetectionservice, quoteservice and shippingservice. Severity was set critical on the strength of the blast radius alone. From the responder's chair the initial picture was simply "checkout is erroring," which is exactly the shape that pulls you into checkoutservice itself and keeps you there longer than it should.

First useful confirmation: checkoutservice's error ratio in the incident window averaged around 0.137 against a baseline near 0.010 — roughly thirteen times normal, peaking near 0.3. Importantly it dipped back to zero at points, so this was partial and intermittent, not a hard down. That also killed the "it's only slow" reading: the error ratio genuinely moved, an order of magnitude outside baseline spread.

## Onset does not match the page

The same metric work turned up something that reframed the whole timeline and was never fully resolved. The earliest change point in the error ratio sits about 24 minutes before the page (T-24m), with a second crossing about two minutes before it (T-2m). The elevated-error regime therefore predates the alert. Whatever you find at T+0 is inside an existing degradation, not at its start.

This matters because the trace sample later collected clusters near T+4m, nowhere near T-24m. Nothing in the evidence gathered ties a specific event to that earlier onset. Treat any narrative that starts at the page time as unsupported.

## The logs: orders start, orders never finish

checkoutservice logs at the start of the window show complete, healthy order flows per request — order start, payment confirmed, confirmation email, successful queue write, all info severity. In the tail of the window (from about T+1m through T+5m) only the order-start lines remain. The payment, email and queue-write lines that used to follow each order are simply absent.

Two things this rules out cleanly. checkoutservice did not crash or lose traffic: order-start lines continue at a steady cadence of roughly one every few seconds right to the window edge. And the problem is not at intake — requests are arriving and beginning. The break is between order start and the downstream steps.

One honest limitation: the query returned only the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines. Everything between roughly T-30m and T+1m was truncated away, including the period immediately before the page. Across everything returned, there was not a single error- or warning-severity line and no dependency exception text. If checkoutservice was shouting about a dependency, it was shouting in the part of the window nobody retrieved.

## Where the traces actually pointed

The traces resolved it. In all ten sampled failing traces the deepest span carrying an ERROR is checkoutservice's outbound client span for the ShippingService GetQuote call. The PlaceOrder span above it and the frontend root span above that are both ERROR only by inheritance; their own self time is minimal. So checkoutservice is the noticer, not the origin.

Two details sharpen this. The failing GetQuote calls produce no shippingservice server span at all — the whole call is recorded as checkoutservice-side self time. And they return in about 2-3ms, with whole traces finishing under 14ms. That is an immediate refusal at connection or handshake, not a wait. Anything framed as saturation-driven latency or a timeout is contradicted by the durations.

The failure lands inside the order-preparation phase, which is why checkout aborts before payment or fulfilment are ever invoked — and why the logs show order starts with no completion lines. The two signals agree.

## Dead ends worth keeping

cartservice absorbed real effort and produced nothing. Its change log for the preceding day holds 38 entries, all from a single automated actor, forming a repeating ~4-hour cycle: an image reference set to a hotfix tag then reverted, a traffic-shaping container attached to the cart network namespace then removed, and a REDIS_ADDR override to an alternate port applied then reverted. Every apply has a matching revert. The last of them completed about two hours before onset, and nothing is recorded in the final two hours. The cycle repeats across the whole window, so none of it is unique to this period. It looks damning at first glance and is entirely routine.

cartservice logs were likewise clean — routine cart retrieval and item-add lines, alive and serving sub-second requests right to the window edge, no Redis connection or pool failure text. Same truncation caveat as checkout: the interior of the window, including both suspected change points, was not returned. And the traces independently cleared cartservice anyway: every failing trace contains a successful GetCart with a completed server span and a sub-millisecond Redis HGET child.

currencyservice and productcatalogservice were cleared the same way — their calls complete without error inside the very traces that fail. paymentservice never appears in a failing trace at all, because checkout aborts before reaching it.

Two metric queries were wasted. Both the cartservice and paymentservice error-ratio queries returned no samples whatsoever — and critically, no samples in the baseline window either. That pattern means the series was never populated for those service labels, an instrumentation or naming gap, not something that started during the incident. Do not read empty as zero: those results neither implicate nor exonerate either service.

Finally, the checkoutservice change history came back empty, which is weaker evidence than it looks. The window queried began at the page time and ran forward 24 hours, so it does not cover the hours before onset at all. What it does support: no checkoutservice deployment, config push or flag toggle occurred from onset onward, and no undocumented remediation on checkoutservice explains any later recovery.

## Where blame sits and how confident to be

Blame sits on shippingservice. It alerted alongside checkoutservice, and it is the callee on every erroring span. Fix class is a restart.

Confidence is low, and the reason is specific: the mechanism inside shippingservice is not established. No shippingservice logs, metrics, change history or resource signals were collected. Whether the refusals come from saturation, a bad artifact, or a misconfigured endpoint or connection limit is unmeasured. The record stops at "shippingservice is refusing a fraction of GetQuote calls" rather than guessing past it.

## Open threads for the next responder

Three things a follow-up should pick up.

First, collect shippingservice directly — logs, error metrics against a baseline, change history, and resource signals. Every conclusion here about shippingservice is inferred from the caller's side.

Second, quoteservice alerted at the same moment and shippingservice's GetQuote path depends on it, but quoteservice was never dispatched to. The origin may be one hop deeper than this record places it.

Third, resolve the onset. The error regime began roughly 24 minutes before the page while every sampled trace clusters near T+4m. Pull traces and logs from the T-30m to T-20m band specifically; the truncated middle of the log window is the most likely place the missing evidence lives.
