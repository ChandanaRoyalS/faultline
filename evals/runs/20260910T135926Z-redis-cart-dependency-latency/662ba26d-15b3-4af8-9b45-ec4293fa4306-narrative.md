# Partial error regression visible at frontend, origin unidentified

## What we were handed

The page arrived as a warning-severity event with cartservice named as the starting point and a blast radius of twelve services. Four services carried alerts of their own: cartservice, frontend, loadgenerator and checkoutservice. Triage flagged that four edges of the service graph were unmeasured, which turned out to matter more than anything else in this record. The timestamp of interest attached to the cartservice alert was 14:03; checkoutservice alerted at 14:03:30.

Read this record expecting no conclusion. It ends with one confirmed symptom, a onset time that contradicts the alert time, and three open questions.

## First look: the change log for cartservice

Because the page pointed at cartservice, the first move was to ask what had changed there in the previous 24 hours. Twenty entries came back, and they looked promising for about a minute: an image reference moved to a cartservice hotfix tag, a traffic-shaping container attached to the cart-service network namespace adding fixed egress delay on eth0, and an environment override pointing REDIS_ADDR at redis-cart on a non-default port. Any of the three would be a satisfying story.

The timing killed all three. Every entry was attributed to the same automated actor, and every apply had a matching revert eight to ten minutes later. The cycle repeats at least four times across the window — roughly 22h, 15h, 8h and 2h before onset. The final cycle ran between about 2.0h and 1.3h before onset and finished its reverts inside that span. The most recent change of any kind is a revert of the REDIS_ADDR override about 1.3h before onset; nothing is left outstanding after that. The image had been reverted ~1.9h before onset, the traffic-shaping container detached ~1.6h before.

So: no deploy immediately before onset, no hotfix image running at onset, no live Redis port override, no delay sidecar attached, and no human actor anywhere in the window. This is routine repeating automated activity, not a unique event. T+ this point, the change log was a dead end, and worth recording as one — it is the branch a future responder will be most tempted to take.

## Second look: cartservice metrics came back empty

With changes ruled out, the next question was whether cartservice's own numbers showed anything. They showed nothing at all. The error-ratio query over span call counts returned no samples in the incident window and no samples in the preceding baseline window either.

The empty baseline is the useful detail. If collection had broken at onset, the baseline would be populated and the incident window would not be. Both being empty says the series was never populated for this service. The denominator — all calls, not only errors — is also empty, which points at the label value or metric name not matching what this service actually exports rather than at any outage of the pipeline.

The consequence is that cartservice has no measurable error rate, request rate, latency, restart count or saturation figure in this record. There is no confirmed error spike on cartservice anchoring the timeline, and no way from here to say whether the alert was error-driven, saturation-driven or restart-driven. Do not read the empty result as a signal; read it as a hole in instrumentation that needs closing before the next occurrence.

## Third look: cartservice logs, healthy at both edges

Logs were the fallback. The selector matched and returned populated lines, which at least confirms cartservice ships logs under that label and that ingestion was working — so the empty metrics are not part of a broader telemetry blackout for this service.

What came back was routine: cart reads, add-item and empty-cart operations. No error-level lines, no exception traces, no startup or restart banners, no cache or Redis connection failure messages. The service was serving normally at the start of the window (13:33:00–13:33:10) and was still serving normally, at sub-second spacing, through 14:05:09.

That pair of healthy edges rules out a sustained crash loop running through the incident, and rules out the failure still producing logged errors by the window's end. But the result was truncated to the oldest eight and newest thirty-two lines. The interval from roughly 13:33:10 to 14:04:54 was never returned — and that interval contains 14:03. A brief restart hidden entirely inside it remains possible. This query is worth re-running with tighter bounds or a line cap next time; the truncation, not the content, is the finding.

## The one thing that was real: frontend error ratio

Pivoting to the caller finally produced a confirmed symptom. Frontend's aggregate error ratio rose from a baseline mean around 0.017% of calls erroring to an incident mean around 2.3% — roughly a 141x move. Baseline maximum was about 0.28%, well below the incident mean, so this is not variance being over-read.

Two things about the shape matter. First, the single detected change point sits at 13:41:30 — about 21 minutes before the 14:03 cartservice alert. The incident-window mean is already elevated before 14:03, so whatever happened at 14:03 is not the onset of frontend errors. That reordering is the most important line in this record: the alert time is downstream of the actual onset. Second, the pattern is bursty and partial. The ratio still touches zero at its minimum and peaks near 11.5%, with a standard deviation larger than the mean. That is a fraction of requests failing, not any dependency being hard down — a total failure of a dependency carrying meaningful frontend traffic would not leave the floor at zero or the ceiling at 11.5%.

The limitation is severe: the query was an unfiltered frontend-wide error ratio with no per-dependency or per-route dimension, and no request-rate or latency series was retrieved. So the regression is confirmed and located at the frontend vantage, and the hop actually producing the errors is not identified.

## Where this stopped

Not established. One confirmed failure — a caller-visible error regression at frontend with onset at 13:41:30 — and no identified erroring hop. Confidence is low and no fix class is proposed, because proposing one would mean guessing.

Nothing implicates cartservice. Its logs are dense and error-free at both window edges, its change activity ended over an hour before the frontend change point, and its own error, latency and saturation telemetry is unmeasurable at the labels queried. That is an absence of evidence rather than evidence of health for the truncated middle interval, but it is not support for cartservice as the origin.

Still open, in the order I would pursue them:

First, break the frontend error ratio down per dependency and per route. This is the shortest path to the erroring hop and it was simply never queried.

Second, re-query cartservice logs for 13:33:10 to 14:04:54 without truncation. That interval contains both 14:03 and the 13:41:30 change point and remains wholly unexamined.

Third, checkoutservice alerted at 14:03:30 and was never dispatched to. Its errors, latency and dependency edges are entirely unmeasured, as are the four graph edges triage flagged at the outset. Four unmeasured edges plus one uninvestigated alerting service is a plausible home for the missing hop.

Separately and not as an investigation step: the cartservice metric labels need reconciling against what the service actually exports, or the next responder will hit the same empty result.
