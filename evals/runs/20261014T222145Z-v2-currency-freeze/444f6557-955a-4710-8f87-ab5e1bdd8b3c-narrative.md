# fraud-detection goes quiet; seven peers alert three minutes later

## Order of events as seen from the chair

The page was broad rather than deep: critical severity, ten services in the blast radius, rooted at fraud-detection, with seven of the edges in that radius unmeasured. At roughly T-4m40s fraud-detection emitted its last log line. Up to that moment it looked entirely healthy — a steady stream of order-consumption records at second-to-tens-of-seconds spacing, a running count advancing to 12,051, trace and span IDs attached, and no error, warning, or retry severity anywhere. Then nothing. At T+0 the incident was declared, by which point the service had already been silent for about four and a half minutes, so no returned line covers the onset instant itself. At about T+3m seven peers alerted as a group: accounting, currency, email, payment, quote, shipping.

The critical negative is what does not follow the silence. No JVM or agent startup banner, no process-restart marker, nothing resembling a container coming back. The only startup sequences in the whole result sit a week earlier at the far edge of the window, two normal process starts a minute apart. So the service did not die and did not loop — it stopped speaking while apparently still resident. Note the log result was truncated to the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines, so the middle of the window is genuinely unobserved.

## The dead ends, kept deliberately

Looking for something that landed produced nothing. The change record for fraud-detection holds exactly five entries, all resource-limit mutations by one platform-automation actor, clustered in a ~45-minute burst about 167 hours before onset, ending on a revert that restored the limit. No rollout, image change, config, or environment edit anywhere in the window. A responder arriving at a similar page will be tempted by that automation burst; it does not connect.

flagd was the second instinct and failed on every axis. Its change log is completely empty for the window, and its own logs are nothing but routine INFO file-watch WRITE notifications — no parse failures, no reload errors, no restart markers. The last line sits ~83 minutes before onset, which reads as a gap until you see the baseline: this service logs sparsely, with multi-hour and near-24-hour quiet intervals throughout.

Metrics failed four times over. Two attempts against fraud-detection and two against checkout all returned HTTP 400 Bad Request with zero samples, using the same error-ratio comparison template. An empty result here reads like a negative but is not one: a 400 is the backend parsing and rejecting the request, so it says nothing about whether the service was scraping or whether checkout moved ahead of onset. A service with no series would have returned a successful empty result.

## Where we landed, and what is still open

Traces sharpened the picture and undercut part of it. Ten fraud-detection traces came back, two spans deep — a Kafka-style orders receive root plus a child process step. No error statuses, no flagd or other dependency span to absorb time; duration is self-time on the receive span (57ms of 57ms, 78ms of 78ms), with the child under 9ms against roots of 14–96ms. But none of the ten fall near onset, and every trace is consumer-initiated with no upstream parent.

The surviving signature is a live-but-wedged process: a service that lost its broker would log that it cannot reach it, and a full disk would say so; this one said nothing. Seven peers alerting three minutes later fits callers burning down deadlines against a socket that accepts and never answers. Confidence medium. Fix class: restart.

Three gaps. Nobody probed the process or socket directly — liveness is inferred from absent restart markers, not checked; hit the health endpoint yourself, and capture a thread dump before restarting, because the restart destroys the only evidence that separates GC thrash from thread-pool deadlock from FD exhaustion. That mechanism is entirely unmeasured because every metric query failed at the query layer. And the link from fraud-detection to the seven alerting peers is asserted by timing, not shown: a consumer with no upstream parent is a poor candidate to synchronously block payment, currency, quote, and shipping, so a shared upstream cause is not excluded.
