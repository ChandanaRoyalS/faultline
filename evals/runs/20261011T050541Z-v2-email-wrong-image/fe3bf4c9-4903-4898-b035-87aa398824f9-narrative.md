# Order confirmations fail at the last hop: email stops binding its listener

## The checkout detour

The page came from checkout, with a second alert on email. The instinct was that checkout had broken, and that instinct cost the first part of the investigation.

The change record was pulled first across checkout and its immediate dependencies. Eight entries came back, all on checkout, all the same environment variable — a currency service address — set and unset four times over, every ten to seventeen minutes, attributed to platform automation rather than a person. The loop ended about 22.4 hours before onset, leaving the variable unset. Nothing landed near onset. Notably, none of cart, currency, payment, product-catalog, shipping or email had any recorded change, which later reads as a gap rather than reassurance.

Metrics were the second dead end. Checkout's aggregate error ratio in the window is indistinguishable from the preceding hour: mean moved about three percent, ranges overlap, standard deviations near-identical across 256 samples each side. The service runs a low spiky error floor, zero to seven or eight percent, in both windows. This rules out a service-wide spike, not a single failing endpoint — which is what was happening.

Third: an unfiltered query for checkout logs over the whole window returned nothing at all. Read at the time as possible evidence of a crash loop. It is not — no startup lines, no traces, no routine request lines either, while traces show checkout actively serving. The log path or label is broken, and the silence stays unexplained.

## The trace that reframed it

Checkout's error traces were the turn. Across four of them the pattern is identical. Within PlaceOrder, cart GetCart, currency Convert, product-catalog GetProduct, shipping get-quote, payment Charge, cart EmptyCart and shipping ship-order all succeed in sub-millisecond to low single-digit milliseconds; whole traces finish in 41 to 75ms. The only error span is checkout's own outbound HTTP POST to email, failing at name resolution against the container resolver in roughly 2.8 to 3.9ms. That speed matters — a fast negative answer, not a timeout — which kills the saturation and pool-exhaustion theories. No email span sits beneath it, so the request never left the pod.

Ordering matters too: email is the last call, after payment is charged and the cart emptied. Orders completed; only the notification failed.

One red herring: quote spans carry offsets of roughly -137ms to -170ms against their shipping parents. That is clock skew on the quote host; those spans are successful and carry no error. Ignore them.

## Email, and what remains open

Email's metrics returned nothing — in the incident window and the baseline hour alike. Nearly over-read as "email went dark at onset"; it is not, since the absence is equally total beforehand. No error rate, no request rate, no latency on either side.

Email's traces then argued it was fine: three of four show checkout's POST with a proper email server span child handling order-confirmation, fanning into send-email and template-render work, six to ten milliseconds inside traces of 62 to 196ms. Propagation works; email is checkout's only caller and no bottleneck. One trace lacks an email span, but a sibling was elided, so likely presentation truncation.

The logs reconcile this temporally. Early in the window email serves confirmation POSTs with 200s under ten milliseconds. Later it logs only one fatal error, repeating: during bootstrap it builds its listen address with an empty port, producing an invalid TCP bind URI, and the socket-server constructor throws an uncaught argument-validation exception at the entrypoint, line 102. The process dies before binding a listener or contacting any dependency, and is restarted roughly every minute to the end of the window. No listener means the endpoint is withdrawn, which produces checkout's three-millisecond negative resolution. The healthy email spans are simply from before the break. Middle log lines were truncated, so the exact changeover is unrecoverable.

Conclusion: an empty port in email's listen address. Fix class is a configuration revert; confidence medium. Open: there is no change record for email at all, so what emptied the port — a push outside the log's coverage, failed templating, or an orchestrator environment regression — is unknown and the revert has no target. Start there. The bind-to-resolution link is inferred from endpoint withdrawal; no DNS or endpoint evidence was gathered. And both telemetry gaps remain unexplained, with the other seven services in the reported radius never examined.
