# Recommendation stops serving while its process stays up

## Timeline anchor and what the responder saw first

Offsets in this record are measured from the moment recommendation's inbound request logging stops. T+0 is that cut.

The page did not arrive at T+0. Three alerts fired together at roughly T+6m45s, on frontend-proxy, load-generator and recommendation. From the responder's chair the picture at that moment was an edge-first one: the proxy and the synthetic traffic generator were complaining about the user-facing path, and recommendation was named alongside them without any obvious reason why. Blast radius was counted at six services, severity critical, with four edges crossed that had no measurement on them at all.

The instinct from that opening - that the problem started at the edge and worked inward - is wrong, and discarding it early saves time. The edge alerts are a lagging symptom of callers hanging on a backend that stopped answering about seven minutes earlier.

## The one signal that actually located the problem

Recommendation's own logs are the spine of this record. Application-level request lines (ListRecommendations receive entries, INFO, routine, sampled traces attached) run continuously up to T+0 and then stop dead. The tail of the returned data is contiguous from about T−2m20s onward, so the silence after T+0 is a genuine gap and not an artifact of the query cutting off.

The process did not die with it. Starting at T+43s and continuing on a regular ~70-second cadence all the way to T+7m43s, the OTLP gRPC exporter logs failures to ship metrics and traces to the collector, each with a deadline-exceeded status. That unbroken timer is the strongest evidence available that the same process stayed alive through the whole window - a crash and restart would have broken the cadence.

So: alive, logging, complaining only that it cannot reach anything, and serving nothing. There are no application exceptions, no stack traces, no flag-evaluation errors, and no logged timeout against any business dependency anywhere in the window.

## Corroboration from the metric side

Recommendation's error ratio toward its callers is essentially flat against the prior half hour - a fraction of a percent in both windows, maxima under one percent, overlapping variance. On its own that reads as "recommendation is fine," and that reading is a trap. The service is not returning errors because it is not returning anything.

The telling detail is in the gaps rather than the values: 16 of 112 incident-window samples have no defined value at all, against 0 of 128 in baseline. For this ratio an undefined sample means no recorded traffic in the interval. Those gaps exist only in the incident window.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Four lines of inquiry were run and closed. Keeping them here is the point of the record.

First, downstream blame. product-catalog was the obvious suspect as recommendation's dependency. Its error ratio is exactly zero across all 128 incident-window samples, with zero variance, identical to baseline, and the series is fully populated - meaning it was receiving and serving traffic the entire time. No error-propagation path from product-catalog upward, and no gap suggesting it stopped serving. Close it.

Second, a recent change. Change history for recommendation was queried twice over the full 24 hours preceding onset and came back completely empty both times; the same query against flagd was also empty. No deploy, no scaling action, no image bump, no config or flag push. There is also no remediation entry in the day following onset, so nothing corrective is confounding the timeline. A rollback has no target.

Third, traces. The sampled traces show a clean, healthy call graph: load-generator through frontend-proxy to frontend to recommendation, then out to product-catalog and back, with recommendation's own spans in the single-digit milliseconds and no erroring or truncated spans in its subtree. The hop flagged as costly in most traces is frontend to product-catalog GetProduct, which is outside recommendation entirely. That is a distraction. The real information in the trace evidence is coverage, not content: only ten traces exist across the roughly 32-minute window, all clustered in a ~60-second burst ending about ten minutes before T+0, and nothing at all lands between T−5m and the end. The spans after the cut are absent, not truncated mid-subtree, which rules out the "accepts the call then dies mid-span" pattern from this source.

Fourth, the caller's view. The natural next move after establishing recommendation went quiet is to ask the frontend what it saw - connection refused versus timeout would settle the mechanism. That query returned nothing. Zero frontend log lines across the entire window, not just the incident portion. A total absence across 32 minutes is more consistent with frontend logs never reaching the store under the queried label than with frontend falling silent at onset. It cannot be used to timestamp onset and it cannot distinguish the failure modes.

## Where the evidence lands

At T+0 recommendation stopped serving inbound requests while its process remained alive. Its only outward complaint, repeated on a metronome for the next seven and a half minutes, is that it cannot reach the collector. Its callers therefore hang rather than receive errors, which is exactly why frontend-proxy and load-generator alerted before anyone looked at recommendation, and why recommendation's error ratio stayed innocent.

The mechanism most consistent with this shape is network isolation of the recommendation instance. Confidence is medium. The indicated fix class is reconnect.

## Open questions for whoever reads this next

Four things this record does not settle, listed in the order I would attack them.

No CPU, memory, file-descriptor or connection-count data was collected for recommendation. A resource ceiling that stalls request handling while a background exporter thread keeps ticking would produce a very similar signature and is not excluded. This is the most important gap.

The caller-side failure mode is unverified. Connection refused versus timeout would confirm or break the isolation reading, and the frontend log path produced nothing under the selector used. Try a different label value or a different source.

Whether the collector-unreachable errors indicate broad network isolation or only a broken telemetry path is unproven. Recommendation logged no failed attempt against product-catalog or any business dependency - but it also logged no attempt at all, so silence here is ambiguous.

Finally, the roughly seven-minute offset between recommendation going quiet and the alerts firing is unexplained by anything in hand. It may be nothing more than alert evaluation windows, but nobody checked.
