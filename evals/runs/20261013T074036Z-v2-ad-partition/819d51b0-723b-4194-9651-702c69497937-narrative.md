# Intermittent edge errors traced to an ad service that could no longer resolve its collector

## What was visible from the proxy, and the first dead end

The page arrived critical, naming frontend-proxy, load-generator and ad, with a blast radius of five services. The responder was handed the outermost hop as a starting point, which is the usual and worst place to begin.

The proxy's error ratio gave the first real shape: roughly ninety times baseline in the mean (about 1.6% against near two hundredths of a percent), but peaking only around 4.4% and dipping back to zero repeatedly, with no sustained departure detected. Bursty and partial, not an outage — which already argued against a dead upstream or a uniformly bad release behind the proxy.

The obvious follow-up was to read proxy access logs for the failing upstream and status codes. That failed completely: zero log lines of any kind for frontend-proxy across the incident window. The absence was total rather than selective, which points at a label mismatch or a broken shipping path, not a quiet healthy proxy. Since the error-ratio metric carried no route or status dimension either, route-level attribution was never recovered and remains open.

## What turned out not to matter

flagd was eliminated twice over. Its change history across the full day covering onset was empty — no deploys, no config edits, no flag definition changes — and the query returned empty rather than truncated, so nothing was hidden by result limits. Its error ratio was statistically indistinguishable from baseline, flat and under one percent in both windows. There was no flagd change to roll back; effort aimed there would have been wasted.

ad's change history held exactly two entries, both from platform automation, both touching the memory limit, and they form a closed pair: lowered, then reverted about half an hour later, roughly sixteen hours before onset. At incident time ad ran under the restored unconstrained configuration. No deploy, no config push, nothing within hours of the page.

Sampled traces through ad looked clean — ten traces, no failing spans, latency in single-digit milliseconds, ad rarely the dominant hop, and no flagd span anywhere. Read carefully: with errors peaking at a few percent, a ten-trace sample passing cleanly proves nothing. This is not exoneration of ad.

## Where the mechanism appeared, and what is still open

ad's own logs are the only place a service explains itself here. Early in the window ad handled requests normally. By the minutes before the page, its telemetry agent was repeatedly failing to export, with a name-resolution failure on the collector hostname raised during route planning — before any socket opened. That detail kills a family of alternatives: no rejection, no 5xx, no TLS failure, no refused connection, no backpressure, and no bad path or port, since neither is reached. Nor is it an instrumentation gap; data was generated and never delivered. The process stayed alive and writing throughout, which separates lost reachability from a stalled process. Failures recurred periodically rather than once.

ad's own error ratio fits: a discrete step to roughly 37%, about twenty times its baseline maximum, against an otherwise tight baseline — bursts, not drift, and never total failure. Traffic gaps that initially looked like the incident were pre-existing and proportionally more common in the baseline.

Best reading: ad lost network and name-resolution reachability, silencing its telemetry and producing intermittent failures that surfaced at the proxy. Fix class is reconnect. Confidence is low, deliberately. Three edges were crossed unmeasured: neither frontend nor load-generator was examined, so the propagation path is inferred. Nothing tested whether ad's resolver failed for all hostnames or only the collector — that distinction decides lost reachability versus a telemetry-only outage. And the proxy's driving upstream and status codes are still unknown.
