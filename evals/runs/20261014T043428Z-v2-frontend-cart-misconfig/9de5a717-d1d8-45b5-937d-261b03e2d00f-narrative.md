# Checkout PlaceOrder failing fast on the shipping-quote leg

## What we saw first

The page named four services at once: checkout, frontend, frontend-proxy and load-generator. That breadth was misleading. Treat the alert moment as T+0; the three non-checkout services were alerting because they sit on the same request path and inherit whatever checkout returns. The blast radius counted thirteen services, but nearly all of that is propagation, not participation.

The first useful measurement was checkout's error ratio. Over the window it averaged roughly 0.22 against a prior-window baseline near 0.024 — about nine times normal, with a maximum around 0.67 and a far wider spread. Importantly the elevation began as a step at roughly T-23m, already at ~0.21 at the first change point rather than climbing toward it. A second change point just above threshold near T-1m is a re-crossing inside an already-elevated, bursty series, not a second event.

## What the shape of the curve ruled out

Two early theories died here. The first was slow degradation — a leak, a filling queue, a resource ceiling. The curve does not ramp; it steps, landing at double the crossing threshold in a single interval. The second was that the alert timestamp is the trigger. It is not: onset predates the page by about 23 minutes, so any search for a trigger must look before T-23m, not at T+0.

It is also not a total outage and it is not noise. The mean sits far outside the entire baseline range, so the alert is real; but the minimum is zero and the mean is 0.22, so the majority of requests still succeeded throughout. This is a partial, request-dependent failure.

## Traces: where the error actually originates

Ten sampled checkout traces in the window all failed, and all failed the same way. In every trace the deepest errored span is checkout's own outbound HTTP POST to the shipping quote endpoint. Everything above it — frontend, frontend-proxy, load-generator — carries an inherited error status, which settles the question of whether the edge services are an origin. They are not.

Two details narrowed things further. The shipping server-side get-quote span, the child of that failing POST, is not marked as an error at all and completes in under 2ms. And the whole trace finishes in roughly 11–78ms, with the failing hop accounting for only 3–18% of it. So this is a fast rejection, not a stall and not a deadline being exhausted. The checkout PlaceOrder span's error description names a non-200 — a 400 — from the email service.

## Dead ends worth keeping

Cart was cleared early: checkout GetCart and the cart span with its Redis HGET succeed in every single trace, sub-12ms, on the failing path. Payment, currency and product-catalog were never reached — checkout aborts in prepareOrderItemsAndShippingQuoteFromCart, and none of those services appear anywhere in the 150 spans. Ad, recommendation and product-reviews are simply not on this path.

Shipping as the broken component was also eliminated, twice over. Its handler span is clean and fast, and a log search for any shipping line mentioning email across the full window returned nothing — no rejection warnings, no 400 text, no onset signal. That absence is weak-to-moderate evidence that the email handling does not live in shipping, and it pushed the search back toward the caller.

Change history was queried for checkout, shipping and email. All three returned completely empty: no deploys, no config edits, no flag flips. That removes rollback as a remediation path and removes any in-window operator change as a confounder for recovery or persistence. Note a real limitation: those queries ran over the 24 hours *after* T+0, not before onset, so a pre-window change is not excluded.

Finally, checkout's logs. A severity-unfiltered query for all checkout lines over the ~32-minute window returned zero lines — not zero errors, zero lines. That is almost certainly a label mismatch or a shipping/ingestion gap rather than a behavioural signal; it cannot be dated, since the window is empty end to end, and it means no checkout-side log narrative exists for this incident.

## The email gap

The hypothesis that email shows a matching step could not be tested. Its span-metrics error-ratio series returned no samples in the incident window — and none in the baseline window either, so the series is absent in this environment rather than going dark at onset. No re-slicing or threshold change will recover a signal from it. Correspondingly, email's request rate is unknown.

This compounds a stranger observation from the traces: no email span appears anywhere in the 150 spans, despite checkout naming email in its error description. Either the call is made out of band from the traced path, or the client is untraced, or the service reports under a different name.

## Where we landed, and how much to trust it

Working conclusion: checkout's PlaceOrder fails fast on a subset of requests because the email service returns a 400 on the shipping-quote leg. Nothing in an artifact or file flipped — no deploy, config or flag is recorded for any of the three services — so the changed behaviour sits in email's runtime request handling, a rejection or invalid-input path. The step onset and the bursty ~22% ratio are consistent with a partial, request-dependent rejection rather than a hard outage. Fix class is a restart.

Confidence is low, and the reasons are specific. No email logs or traces were ever queried, so the actual mechanism behind the 400 — input validation, a bad downstream address, a corrupted template or store — is unestablished. The email call's absence from every span is unexplained. The pre-onset period was never searched for changes. And checkout's total log silence is itself an unexplained telemetry gap that may share a root with email's missing metrics. A responder picking this up should start with email's own logs and traces, and with a change query covering the hours *before* T-23m.
