# Cart path uniformly slowed by a 300ms egress delay attached to cart-service

## What was visible, and the dead ends

The page was a warning-severity latency alert naming cartservice, checkoutservice, frontend and loadgenerator, with a twelve-service blast radius originating at cartservice and four unmeasured edges crossed. Nothing suggested errors.

The first line of work was the least productive. Two attempts to baseline cartservice in Prometheus both ran the same error-ratio template over the span-metrics call counter, and both returned no samples at all — in the incident window and in the preceding baseline window alike. The one useful conclusion is narrow: because the baseline is equally empty, the gap is a standing condition of that metric family, not something that began at onset, so "telemetry died when the incident started" is dead. The unuseful part is that this template was the only query ever run; rate, latency percentiles, restarts and readiness were never asked for. Go to a histogram or trace-derived source directly.

Logs were the second dead end, though a reassuring one. Cart-service logs showed only routine add/get/empty handling at both window edges, no exceptions or connection failures, and continuous traffic at one to three calls per second through the final seconds — killing crash-loop, outage and dead-pipeline theories. A few cart reads with empty user ids looked suspicious and went nowhere; no error follows them. Checkout-service logs were info-level only, with every PlaceOrder followed by payment, email and an advancing queue offset, but with pacing that stretched from tens of milliseconds early to about a second per stage late. Both pulls were truncated to the oldest 8 and newest 32 lines, so the interior of the window — including onset — was never observed.

## Traces gave the shape; change history gave the cause

Inside cartservice handlers, almost all time was a single Redis command: GetCart ~301-308ms, essentially one HGET; EmptyCart ~301-303ms, essentially one HMSET; AddItem ~603-614ms, being both. Handler logic contributes nothing, so cartservice application code is ruled out.

Callers paid an additional gap. Frontend GetCart client spans ran ~603-616ms against ~301-308ms server handlers, roughly one extra 300ms unit; checkoutservice client spans ran ~1205-1221ms against ~301-307ms handlers, roughly three. Every span clustered tightly rather than forming a tail, read and write paths degraded identically, and both callers saw the same per-call gap — so outliers, an operation-specific defect, and a checkout-only client misconfiguration are all eliminated. At the edge, frontend cart POSTs ran ~1511-1534ms and PlaceOrder ~2467ms, while every other checkout dependency stayed between sub-millisecond and ~20ms.

The change log held fifteen entries, all from platform-automation. Three looked promising and were eliminated: the cartservice image was cycled five times to a hotfix build but reverted ~13 minutes before onset and never re-applied; a REDIS_ADDR override to port 6380 was applied and reverted 1.2-1.3h before onset; and the idea that nothing changed dies against the entry closest to onset. That entry is a traffic-shaping sidecar container attached to cart-service's network namespace ~3 minutes before onset, imposing a fixed 300ms egress delay with zero jitter on eth0, still in effect at onset. An identical attach/remove pair ~1.5h earlier shows it is a repeated, reversible automation action.

## Conclusion and what remains open

Every packet leaving cartservice paid a fixed 300ms cost: Redis commands outbound to redis-cart, and gRPC responses back to callers. The trace arithmetic matches exactly, and the tight clustering in multiples of 300ms is the signature of a fixed added delay rather than contention. Nothing errored — cart operations and checkout orders both completed throughout, only slower. Confidence high; the fix is to revert the configuration and detach the shaper.

Still open for the next reader: the ~300ms step and its onset boundary were never confirmed against any time series, since no latency or rate metric was queried. Why the checkout gap is ~3×300ms while the frontend gap is ~1×300ms is unsettled — connection-pool serialization is a hypothesis, not a finding. It is also unconfirmed whether the shaper is still attached; redis-cart was never dispatched on directly, and ten of the twelve blast-radius services were never investigated.
