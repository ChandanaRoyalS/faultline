# Checkout order flow aborts on unreadable cart records

## What the responder saw first

The page came from checkout, critical, with a blast radius eventually drawn at nine services. The entry point was therefore checkout and nothing else — no cart alert, no store alert, no platform alert. That matters for reading what follows: everything about cart in this record was reached by walking downstream from checkout, not by being told cart was unwell.

The first instinct was the usual one: something shipped. That line was closed almost immediately (see the next section), and the investigation then spent its productive time in traces.

## The deploy theory, closed early (dead end)

At roughly T+2m the change log was read for checkout and its immediate dependencies across the preceding 24 hours. It was empty. No deploys, no config edits, no flag flips touching checkout or its direct neighbours. Three separate shapes of the "something shipped" hypothesis — a code regression, a toggle, a config or secret edit — all died on the same empty answer.

Keep this dead end. It is the reason the investigation turned to traces rather than to a rollback, and an empty change window for the alerting service is a real finding, not an absence of work.

## Checkout's own metrics said nothing was wrong (dead end)

Around T+5m checkout's span error ratio was compared against baseline. It averaged about 0.13 in the window against a baseline of about 0.19 — slightly *lower* than normal, with no step and no ramp. On this signal alone checkout looked healthier than usual.

This is the most misleading artefact in the record. A responder arriving fresh and checking the alerting service's error rate first will conclude there is no incident. The user-visible failure is a hard abort on a subset of requests; it does not express itself as a sustained error-rate departure on the aggregate. Do not let this metric talk you out of the page.

## Checkout produced no logs at all (unresolved)

At about T+8m checkout's logs were queried for the whole window and returned nothing — zero lines of any severity. Three sub-hypotheses were marked closed on this basis: no error text to find, no dependency complaint recorded, and (more aggressively) that checkout could not have been running normally because a live process would have emitted something.

That last inference is the weakest step in the whole investigation and should be treated as open. Zero lines is equally consistent with checkout simply not shipping logs to the log store — a pipeline or labelling gap — and nothing gathered here distinguishes a silent process from an unobserved one. If checkout really was silent as a process, that needs its own explanation and nobody has given one. First action for a future responder: confirm whether checkout logs reach the store at all, on a normal day.

## The trace that carried the answer

Traces from checkout were pulled around T+12m and this is where the record turns. In one trace, checkout's GetCart call to cart returned a gRPC FailedPrecondition, and the underlying cause attached to that span was a protobuf parse exception — a truncated or corrupt message encountered while reading cart storage. That error propagated up and aborted the entire order-placement flow for that request.

The same trace set cleared a lot of ground. Payment completed in roughly 0.3–1.4 ms with no errors. Currency, product-catalog and shipping all completed in single-digit milliseconds, stable and error-free. None of them explains either the failure or the spread in checkout durations.

One loose thread was left hanging here. Email was the single largest and most variable downstream call in checkout's path — roughly 21 ms in the slowest successful trace, 7.5 ms in the next, 4.9 ms in the fastest. It was noted and never investigated. It is not implicated in the hard failure, but it is the only latency contributor of any size in the path, and it went unexamined.

## Cart was alive, which is the point

Cart's logs across the window were all info-level — ordinary GetCartAsync and AddItemAsync activity on both sides of onset, no connection complaints, no warnings, no error lines. Two hypotheses died there: that cart was down, and that cart was shouting about storage connectivity. Neither is true. The service is up and serving.

A side observation: repeated GetCartAsync calls with an empty userId appear throughout the visible lines. Nobody followed this up. It may be ordinary anonymous browsing; it may be relevant to which records are readable. Unresolved.

Cart's span error ratio tells the complementary story. Baseline was a perfectly flat zero across every sample. In the window it rose to a mean of roughly 0.5% with peaks around 4.5%, explicitly intermittent rather than sustained — high variance against a tiny mean. That is exactly the shape you get when the store is reachable but only *some* stored keys cannot be decoded.

Two things in that metric were not explained. Twelve samples in the window had no defined ratio at all, implying intervals of zero traffic to cart, against a baseline where undefined samples were proportionally far rarer. And the absolute error volume is small — 4.5% at peak — while the page was critical across nine services. Whether that volume alone accounts for the severity, or whether some other edge is contributing, was never established.

## Where the bad bytes probably came from (plausible, untested)

Cart's change history was the last thing read. Sixteen platform-automation changes in the preceding 24 hours, all on cart, all cycling one image reference — a hotfix build — deployed and rolled back eight times over roughly eleven hours. The final rollback landed about 17 minutes before onset, so at onset cart was running the reverted image, not the hotfix.

The story that fits: a version that wrote cart entries in an incompatible or partial encoding left records behind that the currently-running image cannot parse. The rollout is not what is failing now; the decode on read is.

This link is plausible and untested. Nothing verifies that the hotfix build is actually the writer of the unreadable records. Note also that the change log shows no edits to cart's storage or cache backend at all — only image references — so the store's own configuration is not implicated.

## Conclusion and what is still open

Cart's backing store is returning records that cannot be deserialized. Checkout's GetCart call fails hard, and that hard failure aborts order placement. The store is reachable and healthy; its contents are not decodable. Fix class is data restoration, not rollback. Confidence: medium.

The gaps, in rough order of how much they should bother you:

Nobody queried the cart store directly. The corruption is inferred from a single exception in one trace — not from inspecting the stored bytes, not from knowing how many keys are affected, not from knowing which users are hit. That is a thin foundation for a data-restoration decision.

Seven of the eight unmeasured edges crossed during triage were never dispatched against. Email in particular was flagged as the dominant variable latency in checkout's path and was never looked at on its own.

Checkout's total log silence was read as process silence without evidence that it was not pipeline silence.

The modest cart error volume versus the critical, nine-service severity is unreconciled, as are the zero-traffic intervals to cart.
