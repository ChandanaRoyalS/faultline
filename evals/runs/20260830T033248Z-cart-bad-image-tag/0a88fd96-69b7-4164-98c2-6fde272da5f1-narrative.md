# Checkout order failures traced to a cart backend that never came back after its image update

## What was visible, in order

Pages arrived from checkoutservice, frontend, and loadgenerator at roughly the same moment; call that onset T+0. Blast radius twelve services, severity critical, investigation seeded at checkoutservice. The symptom was simple: orders were not completing, and the load generator noticed first.

The obvious first move was to ask what had shipped to the service that was paging. The change log for checkoutservice came back empty — no rollouts, config edits, flag flips, or dependency bumps in the ten minutes before onset or the five after. That closed the rollback-the-last-deploy path for this service immediately. One caveat: the lookback that actually executed covered only about T-10m to T+5m, not the two hours requested, so anything landing earlier anywhere in the graph was never examined.

checkoutservice's own error ratio was episodic rather than a steady background level — touching zero somewhere in the window, peaking near two-thirds of calls carrying an error status, with continuous coverage. That ruled out both 'healthy all along' and 'hard total outage.' No latency percentiles and no saturation data were collected, so those remain unmeasured rather than cleared.

## Two dead ends worth keeping

The checkoutservice logs pointed the wrong way. The stream carried info severity only — not one error or warning line at or after onset. Early orders showed a complete lifecycle: placement, payment confirmation, confirmation email, a message write with an advancing offset. From onset forward, only order-placement start lines appear, with no matching completions. Read alone, that reads as requests hanging downstream and never returning. The newest line sits around T+2m28s, well before the window closes, so the stream itself appears to have stalled. What the logs did settle: no panic, no crash banner, no restart loop, and inbound traffic still arriving steadily.

The more expensive dead end was cartservice metrics. Its error ratio is flat at zero across the entire window, before and after the rollout, with sixty-one continuous sample points and no gaps. Taken at face value that exonerates the new artifact completely and pushes the investigation toward latency or saturation — numbers that were never gathered. A defined ratio needs a non-zero denominator, so the metric appears to assert the service was still serving. That reading turned out to be wrong, or at least incomplete, and the contradiction was never resolved.

A smaller red herring: several cart-fetch calls shortly before the cutoff carry an empty user identifier. It appears intermittently among normal traffic for minutes beforehand — pre-existing, not a boundary event.

## What actually happened, and what is still open

cartservice had exactly one entry in the window: a container image reference update pushed by platform automation at about T-4m25s, tagged as a hotfix build. Its logs show normal request handling up to a second before that, ending in an orderly hosting-lifetime shutdown notice — and then nothing at all for the remaining nine and a half minutes. No startup banner, no listener-bound line, no repeated startup attempts, no stack traces. The replacement artifact never came up.

The traces confirm it from the client side. Every sampled checkout trace has the identical five-span shape ending at the cart fetch, and that deepest span carries ERROR while completing in 1.6–4.2ms, consuming nearly the whole parent duration. No spans exist for payment, email, shipping, currency, or catalog, because the request aborts before reaching them. checkoutservice self-time is a fraction of a millisecond. A millisecond-scale error on the first outbound call is what a client sees when nothing is listening — not slowness, not an erroring server. Fix class: rollback. Confidence medium.

Still open. First, the cartservice metric contradicts total log silence and nobody determined whether those samples are client-side spans, a surviving replica, or a stale series — a surviving replica would change this from total outage to partial. Second, why the new pod never started is unestablished; no pod events, container status, or registry evidence was gathered, and the change record carries no prior image reference, so the rollback target is not identified. Third, the checkoutservice error ratio peaks near two-thirds while every sampled trace shows the same failure, and four unmeasured edges were crossed with no specialist examining frontend, loadgenerator, or the other nine services — a second concurrent failure is not excluded.
